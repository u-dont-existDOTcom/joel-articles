## Candidate text

Part of it is what happens afterward. You can have an amazing experience that makes your life suddenly seem clear, only to find yourself back in the same old environment, with the same old cues, surrounded by people who have no idea what you've just been through. If the people who sat with you through it are also part of your community, they can still notice what's happening to you in the months afterward.

The deeper you go, the more alienated you can feel. After my original ayahuasca ego deaths at 25, I had trouble finding anyone in the physical world who seemed to have experienced anything similar. I couldn't even find them at Buddhist monasteries despite all the preaching about enlightenment. That helped give me a God complex, a Savior complex, or both, which then attracted exactly the right people to show me my limits. 😂 I could have used somebody around who'd been through similar nights and wasn't afraid to tell me when I was getting carried away.

Do you trust the people you're relying on for support? Some paid sitters are great, but you're often paying somebody you don't know to look after you for a night. The person with the feather might have met you four hours ago, and somehow already have an entire cosmology to explain everything you say.

In a community, your sober sitter might be someone who's known you for years. They've seen you on ordinary days, and you won't have to explain your whole life to them while you're in the middle of an intense experience. With iboga or other long, medically risky experiences, you still need proper screening beforehand and continuous monitoring.
