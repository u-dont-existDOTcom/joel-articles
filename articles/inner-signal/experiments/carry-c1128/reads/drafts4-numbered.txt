## SH1, carrying A2 Imagery Rescripting (somatic) and IC1 (inner child); new paragraph at the end of "Do These With a Clinician", after:
> The main clinical practice guidelines for the treatment of major trauma recommend one or more of the trauma-specific variants of cognitive behavioral therapy (often referred to by specific names, such as cognitive processing therapy, prolonged exposure, etc.) or eye movement desensitization and reprocessing, depending on the person and the guideline. These approaches in turn can be further supported by techniques such as self-hypnosis and inner child work. But high-risk trauma work needs a bigger container than that.
(and before: ## Find a Hypnotist Who Fits)

With links: Another container is a clinical therapy called [Imagery Rescripting](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/201523223/imagery-rescripting), which I go into in my somatic guide. It works a lot like the way I [re-do trauma memories](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/189761959/re-do-trauma-memories) in my inner child reparenting guide, where you imagine a painful memory going differently. In Imagery Rescripting, though, the therapist is in the scene with you, stepping in as the ideal adult who protects and nurtures your inner child, so it's what I'd go to for a memory that feels too big to re-do on your own. Over the sessions, you learn to be that adult yourself.

## SH2, carrying A3 Pain That Won't Quit? (somatic) and IC2 (inner child); new paragraph at the end of "What It's For, and Not For", after:
> Don’t use self-hypnosis to: force yourself to remember trauma, force yourself to prove trauma through memories, push symptoms down instead of getting medical attention, destabilize yourself, or replace appropriate clinical care.
(and before: # Forget Left Brain/Right Brain)

With links: If the symptom is pain that has lasted months, and doctors have found no injury or disease to explain it, I'd keep up the self-hypnosis and also look for a therapist trained in one of the two talk therapies I go into in [my somatic guide](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/201523223/pain-that-wont-quit), Pain Reprocessing Therapy or Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy. They've helped people with long-lasting back, muscle and joint pain, by changing their relationship to the pain without pretending it's not there.

## SH3, carrying A1 Can't Stay With a Feeling? (somatic); new paragraph at the end of "Too Analytical?", after:
> “What if you didn’t try to improve the practice?” Put one hand on your heart and the other on your solar plexus, as in [the two-hand practice](#§the-two-hand-practice), and say, “I don’t have to do this perfectly. I’m here for a relationship with something deeper than my surface mind.” “What can you do to make this a communion, not a performance?” Maybe it’s silence, resting, or doing something. Whatever it is, just do it.
(and before: ## Too Blissful or Diffuse?)

With links: The same habit can follow you into therapy sessions: you start explaining a feeling as soon as it comes up, or you agree with the therapist just to get past it. If that keeps happening, look for someone trained in Intensive Short-Term Dynamic Psychotherapy, where the therapist catches you each time you slip away and asks what you're feeling right then. I go into it in [Can't Stay With a Feeling?](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/201523223/cant-stay-with-a-feeling), a section of my somatic guide, along with two other therapies for staying with feelings.
