What I Mean by Hypnosis

P1APA Division 30 commonly defines hypnosis as focused attention, reduced peripheral awareness, and increased responsiveness to suggestion. That’s basically true, but there’s a lot more than meets the eye. In fact, hypnosis is such a broad and common type of altered state that it’s hard to confine it to one use. But I’m using hypnosis here as a therapy.

P2And hypnosis as a therapy is as old as time itself. While the modern technique of it was discovered by Franz Anton Mesmer in the 1770s, shamans have been using dancing, music, and drugs to induce hypnosis for therapeutic purposes forever. We enter into trance states every night which resemble hypnosis as we go to sleep and as we wake up as well. Those states can also be used for deep therapy.

Affirmations Done Right

P1My dad always said: “Always, and in everything, expect the very best. But be prepared to take what you get. P2Another quote I use all the time from my dad… when someone asks me “How’s it going?” I say “Better & better!” This really confuses a lot of people. Some think it’s amazing that I’m always doing better. Some ask if I was sick or something. But it’s doing multiple jobs. Hypnosis Evidence

P1Is there real (not magical) evidence for hypnosis? Yes. In 2024, there was an overview of 49 meta-analyses containing a total of 261 distinct primary studies. P2Is there evidence for self-hypnosis? Yes. There has been a systematic review of Randomized Controlled Trials of self-hypnosis, finding benefit in studies of pain, childbirth, children, stress, anxiety etc. Self-hypnosis seemed more useful when people learned it as a real skill and practiced it several times than when they just listened to an audio file.

The Inner Adult’s Three Jobs

P2Secondly, here are some of the ways people use hypnotic language to manipulate: you consent to something without realizing it, you only get one choice, or someone slips something you never agreed to into a sentence. For example:

P3“You will soon realize that I am right…” Or “You may, in time, decide to admit that…” Or “The only reason you do not agree is…” Or “Part of you already knows…” Or “Your higher self wants…” Or “Everyone who is awake knows…” Or “The reason you do not believe that is true is because your ego does not want you to…”

P5What are they asking me to agree to? What choice are they giving me? What are they assuming that I may not agree with? Who benefits if I comply? Simple Beats Confusing

P1"Keep it simple, stupid!" (KISS principle) actually can apply to hypnosis!

P2It's really easy to take a whole bunch of techniques from all of this and make something so complicated that you "hypnotize" yourself to the point where you don't even know if it worked or not. But keep in mind that especially with self-hypnosis, we can't go so deep that we are unable to continue the session! And even if it were a hypnotist bringing you under, you still need to be at an "access level" of awareness in which you can respond to questions.

P3So start off with setting one anchor/image to rest your mind's eye on. Decide on a hypnotic tone (warm, indirect, direct, neutral witnessing, etc.) for the session. If you're going to use metaphors, just start with one, and if you switch metaphors midway, understand the purpose of the switch. P4In fact, you don't even need to use metaphors nor imagery. You can use whatever form of inner signal that you notice. Let the Response Guide You

P1The induction and the inner meeting get you to where you can listen. From there, you answer whatever comes up, and you can't know ahead of time what every part of you is going to say. Read through these sections first, and then put the screen away and try the inner meeting.

P2Respond to each thing that comes up with an accurate notice and a small reasonable invitation (or action or taking more time etc). The thing might be a sensation, image, gesture, feeling, words, or anything. The notice and invitation don't need to cover all those forms, just notice and invite what happens.

If Your Mind Objects

P1Allow your analytical part to make comments and/or tell you that it doesn’t like what you are doing. Just tell it that you will check things out, and make any commitments etc, when you are back. If it says no to going deeper, or tells you to stop doing the exercise, I’d stop there. You can stop whenever you want to. Just because you see an image, or feel a body release, doesn’t mean you are right about what is going on.

Finding Your Own Words

P1An example notice might be, "I notice my chest tightening when I imagine asking for help". Don't add interpretation like "this must be the abandoned child part of me, or a protective mechanism" - just notice what you notice. An example invitation might be, "And as I notice that, I can take some time before deciding what it means, and notice if I want to say anything, or change it, or just stay like that".

P2Then just listen. You don't need fancy language - often it will be better to just ask what happens next, or whatever. If you notice a feeling developing, it might be best not to say anything while it's developing (if you can help it), but some days you need to talk to understand, so just do that.

P3You can be pretty straightforward when that's the help you chose - "Let the explanation wait a moment. What do I feel as I say that?" can be a perfectly valid thing to say to yourself, and help you focus. As long as you're still present, and able to change if you want to, and happy to carry on, a sad feeling or tension or whatever doesn't require an exit.

Understand the Meaning

P1It's worth trying to separate the notice, the meaning, and your thoughts on the meaning, but in practice it will often be pretty fuzzy. For example, "I noticed... this seemed to mean... here's why that might be true / false" or something like that.

P2Is this a notice or meaning? "My stomach went tight" is a notice, "He's a complete bastard" is a meaning, even if you didn't think it in words. You can then go on to find out what he did, notice what it was that gave you the idea, and consider if there might be other explanations. It's also ok to put a meaning into words now, to look at it, even if it came to you without words. Nonverbal thoughts and early thoughts aren't necessarily any deeper, truer, or more reliable than other thoughts.

Invitation Feels Wrong?

P1Your shoulders tightening, or 'hmm, that feels kind of wrong', is a signal to investigate further, but 'No! That is Not Right!' is a signal to stop what you're doing and change it. If you don't feel like it fits, don't keep doing it, and don't try saying it again more persuasively. You don't need any other reason.

P2"I notice the tightness, and I can just leave it like that. Then again, is that what I really want?"

P3Sometimes the invitation just isn't right or helpful, even if it sounds like something a hypnotist might say. Each time we play around with this, we explore new possibilities which bring up new inner signals for us to feel out.

P4Doubting is sometimes a guard to listen to, and sometimes simply part of normal consideration. Lack of doubt doesn't necessarily mean the process is working in the best way, either.

Look at the Situation Too

P1If there is another person involved in the situation, it may be a good idea to look at what they are doing as well. Can they hear you disagree, take responsibility, and make room for what you're going through? Try and be specific in what you conclude about them, and be willing to change it if you find out that it was wrong. Just because someone is disagreeing with you or trying to set boundaries with you does not mean they are in their emotionally immature parts. You don’t need to subject yourself to something that may be dangerous before protecting yourself.

P2Look at your own side too. You may find that you feel guilty. Having feelings of guilt does not mean that you have done something wrong or that you have been manipulated. Have you done something that was wrong or that caused harm? If so, what can you do to fix it? On the other hand, did you give in to someone else just to keep them from being disappointed in you? What do you want to do or achieve the next time you interact with this person? Do you need them to understand what you are feeling/thinking? Do you just need to communicate information to them? Do you need to set boundaries with them? Do you need to end the interaction? You don’t have to make them understand before you can act.

P2You can say, "I can let this much be here, and notice what's supporting me right now." And when you've had enough, "Ok, I think that is enough for now."

If you have trouble orienting yourself and it lasts for a prolonged period of time, please get some human help rather than trying a stronger induction.

Urge to Move?

P1If you feel an urge to move, turn, reach, push, or change your body’s position, feel free to either actually do something that will not cause your body pain or discomfort, or just imagine doing what you feel you need to do, and see what happens next. If you do move and you feel that you are causing yourself pain or other symptoms that you are concerned about, stop moving.

Offer the Care They Want

P1If any younger parts come up, offer them the opportunity to spend time with you, or some space if they would rather. If one of them says “don’t come closer”, stay at the distance they choose. You don’t have to consider them literally younger, nor do you have to ask them why they are there or what happened to them. If you can identify any guards (childhood protector parts), talk to them in the same Nurturer voice you meant for your little one, and find out what they are concerned about. You don’t have to get rid of any of them.

P2If you feel that it would be useful to bring in your Nurturer, Protector or Guide to help you either change the tone of the interaction, set boundaries, or find your way toward the life you want, feel free to do so. You don’t have to talk to them, just use the one whose job you need, and if you can’t do it yet, borrow it. You only need to use the Witness if you need help getting started with a step.

P3Try some different ideas as to what might be helpful for this inner part to feel better (being welcomed by you, visualizing someone else, giving them the sort of words they would like to hear, you placing yourself in a different body position, you moving further away etc). Only try one idea at a time to see if it works.

P4It is possible that when you try and give the part what it wants, you might feel very upset. It may be that it never got what it needed at the time and now that you are giving it what it wants, it is processing the upset of not getting what it wanted before. That doesn’t mean your love isn’t working. If this happens, allow yourself to respond for as long as you want to do so, and for as long as you can stay there (ie remain in the process), but don’t push for more tears, since crying more doesn’t mean you’re healing more. If you feel that the voice, distance, touch, image etc is too much, then change it to something that is less.

Remember that whatever you find out is likely to be metaphorical. It may be a good hypothesis for what you need to do, but it isn’t a record of what actually happened.

P6Alternatively, you may want to use the session to build up something you want more of (eg love, play, courage, curiosity etc). You can also use it to rehearse something that you have decided to do.

Finish Without a Revelation

P1Don’t feel that you have to end the session with a great revelation (those are often flaky, not standing the test of time). When you have done as much as you want to do, ask yourself what would help you leave things where they are for now. This might include thanking parts for their help, or making sure that you do something in real life (put a boundary in place, get some rest, repair a relationship, keep a promise etc). You can tell your little one, "That’s enough for today, choochoo. Aren't you excited to get back to the life we're building?"

P2When you have finished, even if you stopped part way through, use the ‘Come All the Way Back’ procedure to bring yourself back. When you are back, it is a good idea to write a few lines in a notebook about what you have done. Just jot down the essentials (what was there before you started, what you have done to change things, what happened, what you are unsure about, any phrases that were said, any body signals, any information that the inner child said that it needed, anything that a guard wanted you to know, any boundaries that you put in place, any repairs that you did, anything you learned about what not to force etc). You don’t have to list every part of you that might have been involved, only what you actually noticed.

The 15-Minute Daily Practice

P1You don't need to learn a second script for this. Just do the same practice, from the induction to coming all the way back, for fifteen minutes or so. That is long enough to induce yourself (it only takes a few minutes), practice and let it unfold at its own pace, and come out again (that takes a few minutes too). Fifteen minutes is just a handy appointment with yourself, though, and your feelings don't have to keep to it. Five minutes is plenty to see what happens when you simply sit in silence, listening, and you can interrupt yourself at any time to change direction if that feels better.

Capacity Before Content

P1Note: if it is medically unsafe, or you are in a place of panic, sleep deprivation, nausea, deep destabilization, mania, psychosis-like symptoms, feeling overwhelmed by chemicals etc., focus on stabilizing first, with help if you need it (see “When to Get Outside Help”), and only then look for what your subconscious needs you to know. P2Sometimes, it feels like your subconscious knows something that your conscious self is not prepared to know. It may be something related to your sexuality, grief, trauma, family issues, betrayal, shame, vocation, a relationship, illness fears, spirituality, addiction, identity, moral issues etc.

P3What you don’t want to do is bully your subconscious into telling you what it knows by saying things to yourself like:

P4“What is it you won’t tell me? What are you hiding? Tell me NOW! I am telling you to tell me!”

What Not to Do

P1Don’t view your inner experiences as commands or proof. They’re internal happenings that need to be reality tested at all times.

P2Don’t go seeking “downloads” of information, or make a hobby of demons/jinn/Unattached Burdens or the like. Stabilize your life first, and get help to do so if you are not reality testing well, or not functioning well in your life.

P3Don’t do self-hypnosis while driving, bathing, swimming, operating any equipment, supervising children, or doing anything else that requires active attention.

P4Don’t try to “force” memories, to use trance to “prove” something happened to you, or to skip medical attention for symptoms just because you can “force” them down using hypnosis.

P5Don’t force yourself to ignore your body’s needs, such as eating when hungry, resting when tired, responding to pain, etc., or your emotions, such as letting yourself grieve when someone dies, feeling anger when it is appropriate, acknowledging your fears, etc., until you know more about what the signals are telling you.

P6Don’t “extend” the duration of altered states indefinitely.

P7Don’t allow your inner roles to get out of proportion (e.g., letting your inner child run your adult life, the Protector shut out all love, the Nurturer disregard consequences, or the Guide ignore the needs of your inner child). You are capable of analysis, as well as tenderness.

P8Don’t use NLP techniques to force down guard parts, to give yourself certainty you do not have, to manipulate others, or to heal yourself when your body is telling you not to.

P9Don’t consider negative indicators (doubt, distraction, resistance, lack of response, saying “No”) as good signs that the induction is working.

P10Don’t rely upon the safety of your unconscious mind as the only safety factor in any session. Be sure you have set up clear limits, are in a safe place, and have a tested way to end the session and get out.

P11Don’t believe that normal physical sensations are indications of hidden trauma, illness, toxins in your body, possession, “attachments,” “transformations,” or information from your “subconscious mind.”

P12Don’t believe that anything is “healed” in your body (neurologically, cellularly, etc.) simply because it is “healed” in your imagination.

P13Don’t put hidden commands in yourself, nor allow anyone to do so. B: Ethical NLP

P1NLP stands for Neuro-Linguistic Programming.

P2You do not need to know this stuff to use the main path, but if you want to freshen up on your skills in the art of working with imagery, body states, behavioral rehearsal, talking to yourself in effective sentences etc., then it might be useful, as long as it helps you listen. Using the techniques can be an easy way to fall into the trap of thinking that NLP is a bag of tricks rather than something that needs to be done with love and truth and contact and consent.

The Four Layers

P1The four levels of NLP are:

P2Sensory Level of NLP: working with the form of an inner experience (where do you see or hear it, how far away, how big, how bright, how loud, how do you hold your body, where is the pressure etc.)

P3State Level of NLP: making useful states (warmth, adult presence, courage, love etc.) easier to get to.

P4Strategy Level of NLP: practicing a better response.

P5Language Level of NLP: pacing what is true; opening up people’s awareness; presuming only that you can do something safe; linking cause and effect carefully; embedding invitations; asking de-hypnotic questions to break the effects of vague, absolute, manipulative or grandiose language.

Milton Model: Soft Words

P1We are going to use a model that’s called the Milton model. It’s all to do with sentence architecture. When we tell ourselves to ‘be better’, ‘relax now’, ‘stop feeling anxious’ or ‘trust me’, it doesn’t work, does it?

P2What we need to do is use indirect language to go around the part that wants to argue with us, so that we can actually get to where we want to get to.

P3So let’s start with something we know to be true, let’s take the pressure off, presume that we can only do a tiny bit and let’s invite one thing:

P4‘As my body begins to notice the sensation of the bed underneath it, it may be possible for it to begin to realize that it is not alone in carrying the burden of the day.’

P5We can then test this out with the body to see if it was possible.

1. Pacing and Leading

P1So how might we pace and lead in a way that works with anxiety?

P2It might go something like this: ‘It is completely true that the sensation of ________ (or the emotion of _____) is coming up for me. It makes complete sense that my system is trying to react and respond to that by wanting to do or think ________. While that is completely true, there is another thing that is equally true, which is that my adult self is here, breathing in the room right now, and wanting to listen to what comes up for me next.’

P3Truth, Invite, Truth, Invite etc. is all you need to do.

2. Awareness Verbs

P1Open up awareness by using awareness verbs e.g. notice, realize, recognize, sense, feel, wonder, imagine, picture, listen to, allow, discover, remember, consider etc.

P2Example: ‘Notice how the answer comes to you… maybe as an image, or a word… maybe as a pressure, or some warmth… or a silence, or a sense of what to do next.’

3. Presuppositions

P1A presupposition hides an assumption inside a sentence, and whoever hears it usually just goes along with it. That’s a lot of power, so only use it to assume good things. It should give you more agency, not less. It should make you more safe (not just feeling more safe when you're not). It should help you feel the love that's there (even if it's only available beyond the conditions). We don't use this to assume obedience, the way a less ethical hypnotist does when they say something like, ‘As you go deeper, it gets easier and easier to just do what I say.’

P2I'd switch that up to the safe version: ‘As you go deeper, it gets easier to commune with any part that needs your understanding before it can change.’

4. Cause-Effect Bridges

P1Bridge words, like ‘as’, ‘whilst’, ‘because’, ‘when’, ‘after’, ‘before’ and ‘which means’, link one event to the next, so the first can seem to come with the second, or even cause it. That doesn't mean it does, of course. This is another kind of more subtle presupposition technique.

P2We can use this technique to support communion and agency: “As I breathe, my adult self can allow itself to become present enough to hear a bit of what my wounded inner child needs.”

5. Embedded Invitations

P1An embedded invitation tucks a small direction inside a bigger sentence, like when you put a pill inside a little food to swallow it easier. In communion hypnosis, we choose not to use embeds like this to hide commands, but rather to soften them into nudges. Nudges give more agency, and they keep the conversation flowing without a need for a strong decision. For example, rather than, "As I sit here, I will become aware..." we can say, "As I sit here, I may begin to allow myself to become aware..."

P2With your inner child, it could be: "My little one doesn't have to trust me all at once. I invite them to just notice that right now, I'm still here."

P3Invitational language can also feel super fake though, especially when it's overused. Fakeness doesn't feel so safe. And if it's just chunking the manipulation into easier bite-sized portions without reducing the overall size of the demand, it could be less ethical, not more.

6. Ethical Double Binds

P1This is also an actual parenting technique. Children love choices, even when they're both designed for the same pre-decided outcome. Our subconscious still works like that, too. A manipulative double bind is the name for that, although hypnotists don't call it manipulative, that's my framing. Nimja demonstrates this a lot in his hypnosis: "Do you want to go deeper now, or in a moment?"

P2An ethical double bind also gives you two choices, but both of them are designed to be genuinely safe, and they also give a bit of actual agency, even if both choices serve the same end. Example:

P3"Welcome to McHappyness. Would you like some warmth first, or some space?" And would you like fries with that?

7. Questions as Requests

P1A conversational postulate is a sneaky little yes or no question that's actually a way to ask for something indirectly. People use it all the time casually, like, "Can you pass the salt?" In its most egregious form, it could turn into a dare. Do you recall how hard it was as a child to ignore a dare?

P2I dare you now to practice this ethical postulate! "Can my subconscious show me the next healing stepping stone, sparing me any more painful memories than I can handle right now?"

8. Tag Questions

P1Tag questions, like "isn't it?" or "doesn't it?", can soften a sentence, but they get old very quickly, so go easy on them.

P2"Your body knows how to relax, doesn't it?"

9. Nominalizations

P1A nominalization turns something you do into a noun: you heal, and it becomes 'healing'. Others are 'safety', 'trust', 'integration', 'communion', 'clarity' and 'rest'. They're a bit vague, and sometimes that's useful, because it lets the listener's subconscious fill in something very specific for them.

P2"A bit more safety can come to me in whatever way my body understands."

10. The Meta Model

P1Milton Model language opens things up, and Meta Model questions ask what exactly someone means.

P2You need both to be ethical with people, so when something sounds true but vague (like the new-age slogans: e.g. "everyone is your mirror"), ask: "What do you mean, specifically, my mirror to what extent? According to whom, Madame Blavatsky, AI, or...? How do you know that, and what evidence would change your mind?"

P3So when someone is starting to tense up in their body, use the Milton Model to help them soften. And when you feel you're losing track of what's really going on, that's the time for the Meta Model.

H: Demons, Jinn & Presences

P1I speak here from experience.

P2When you’re doing hypnosis, meditation, lucid dreaming, loveyhuasca-ing, going through a period of intense grief, praying, or whatever else may alter your consciousness, it’s possible you may run into something that you perceive to be a demon, a jinn, a parasitic energy, or whatever other dark entity or terrifying archetype you may encounter – it may be a voice or an image, and it doesn’t just feel like an emotion or a younger part of you, but something that feels intrusive, hateful, seductive, mocking, etc.

P3First of all, it doesn’t matter if they’re demons or not. They may be literal demons, for all you know. Or they may be manifestations of your mind, burdens you’re carrying from previous relationships, whatever. What matters is finding the best way to deal with them, so you end up safer, clearer, more loving, more grounded and less hooked.

Start With Basic Safety

P1So what do you do when you encounter a demon, or a jinn, or something else that’s bad and intrusive?

P2First of all, do the usual things – make sure you’re not in danger from a medical standpoint, check your environment, check your body, make sure you’re not overstimulating yourself, make sure you’re not opening your mind too much. If you need to, open your eyes, ground yourself in your body and your environment, remove yourself from any bad influence you’re surrounding yourself with, stop stimulating your mind. Don’t go further into whatever it is you’re doing – don’t be curious, don’t try to figure out what it is.

P3Tell yourself, “If this can be integrated in this moment, it’s welcome. If not, it can wait until later. The adult part of me is still here, and my body can return to safety.”

P4If you feel like you’re in any danger of doing anything harmful to yourself or others, if you feel like you’re going psychotic and you’re afraid of it, if you feel like you won’t be able to sleep when the self-hypnosis session is over, if you feel overly paranoid, if you feel physical pain in your chest, if you feel like you’re going to have a seizure, if you feel like you’re too hot in a bad way, if you feel like you’ve taken too much of something, if you feel like you won’t be able to function in the world anymore – then the self-hypnosis session is over, and you should get help in the real world.

Warmth or Distance?

P1If, however, you feel like you’re stable enough to stay present, check in with yourself and see what’s best to do. Put your hand on your heart if that feels okay, breathe comfortably, and stay aware of the room.

P2Find out if it helps to be warm towards it or to keep it at arm’s length, and keep your boundary either way. Keep in mind that you don’t have to do what it tells you to do.

P3If you feel vulnerable, if you feel tightness in your throat, if you feel sadness or fear or like you’re trying to protect something, call upon your Nurturer. If you don’t feel better, or if it continues being hostile towards you, stop trying to deal with it and return to your body and the room.

Should You Fight It?

P1Your first instinct may be to fight it, banish it, hate it, show it that you’re more powerful than it is, argue with it, panic, turn completely into the frightened part of you, etc. Some people may want to use traditional prayer or mantras or protection or banishing or exorcism techniques, and those may well work for them. But if you’re fighting it out of fear, it can backfire:

P2It becomes a center of attention if you fear it, and if you hate it, you give it a hook in you.

P3So the next time it comes up, you can just say, “I see that there is no reason to hate and revile what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to merge with or become what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to obey what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to try to defeat what I am experiencing out of fear. I choose to return my attention to loving awareness.”

Compassion, Door Closed

P1You can also tell the presence that while you have compassion for it, the door is closed.

P2This is especially a good idea if the presence you’re experiencing seems even the slightest bit hostile, cold, alien, parasitic, etc. It’s not really helpful to approach it with the kind of soft, sentimental love that you’d use if you were inviting it to stay for tea.

P3Sit up, make sure your feet are grounded on the floor, straighten your spine, pull your shoulder blades back a little, open your chest a little, feel the boundary of your torso’s skin, feel the perimeter of your body, and tell the presence, “While I have compassion for you and any suffering you may be experiencing, this suffering needs to go back where it came from. The boundary of my skin is the boundary of an adult now, and you’re not welcome to come inside the house anymore.”

Could It Be Psychosis?

P1Everything above assumes that the “hostile presence” is symbolic, the kind that settles down when you ground yourself and meet it with love. What you’re going through might instead be psychosis, or your mind becoming unstable in some other way.

P2Get help right away if you feel under attack for days at a time, or you can’t sleep or function normally. The same goes if you hear voices telling you to do things, feel you need to hurt yourself or somebody else, or feel you’re going manic. And get help if you can’t communicate normally, or can’t tell the difference between what you’re experiencing symbolically and normal reality. This isn’t something to deal with in hypnosis.

P3The general rule is the one in “Daily Life Is the Feedback”: if what you're doing is making you more paranoid, sleepless, grandiose, isolated or scared, or things feel more and more unreal, stop going deeper and get help.

I: An Induction Library

P1Preview the sequence. Work out in advance which one you want to do and what you want to use it for (pick just one) and whether or not you want to do it at all. Any of these families of induction can be done as light inductions, with eyes open, or you can just do them as a no-trance inner contact.

P2Once you start an induction, you will want to notice what’s going on (even if nothing changes) and work out what to do next: move, imagine something else, change the sensory channel you’re using, do nothing, or stop the induction sequence and do something else… Whatever you do, make sure you bring yourself fully back to waking at the end of an induction.

1. Stretch and Release

P1Work out what will be supporting you, and focus on the feeling of support. You can also focus on where one of your hands or feet is. Either actually, or in your imagination, stretch a little bit, either stretching that part of your body slightly or pressing gently down into the support, and then let go of the effort, just relax. See if there is any difference (it’s fine if there isn’t). Do this for no more than 3 places on your body. Let releasing guide you as you go through the sequence. Which bit of your body, or inner voice, needs this? What do you need to support it? Bring your body back to normal movement and return.

2. Supported Floating

P1Work out what is supporting you and where your weight is. Imagine that the support is holding a tiny bit (1%) more of your effort. It’s like floating but you don’t have to lose contact, or you can just keep it at the same level, with just enough weight/pressure. See what happens. You can fractionate this if you want, perhaps noticing your normal awareness when you breathe in and the support when you breathe out. Tell yourself you are going to fractionate before you do it. What can you hear more easily now? Keep the support, come back to your normal weight and firm contact, and return.

3. Breath or Pulse Rhythm

P1Use your breathing or a slight hum in your throat for a natural rhythm. You could also tap your finger, or follow a sound outside. You can even do it by counting to yourself (silently) or with no rhythm at all. Say “here, and here again” over and over, and notice what you pay attention to in between the phrases. Then stop saying it. Notice what you become aware of in the first beat – you don’t need to make your mind blank, just notice what comes up. Does it make any words, feelings, needs or actions more clear? Then let everything go back to its normal rhythm, and come back.

4. Unwinding Object

P1Unwinding an object is good for when you have a few different strands to one thing you want to work on. Imagine some yarn unwinding, some bubbles settling, or some knots that you place beside you. Once you’ve placed them all, give yourself a minute to look at them all. You don’t have to change anything, but you can label them if you need to (just a word or two). How many did you put down? Which one do you need to work on, and what does it need – warmth, protection, action? Keep one strand in your attention and put the others somewhere you can see them and return.

5. Sensory Journey

P1Pay attention to the sounds in the room you’re in and how your body feels, then imagine yourself walking somewhere. Where do you want to go? Listen to the sounds of your footsteps and take in your surroundings as you walk. What do objects feel like if you touch them? What landmarks do you see? When you feel you have gathered enough of your attention, imagine the scenery changing until you are in a place where you can talk to whoever you need to (or you can just cut to the chase, if you prefer). Pay attention to something without worrying about what it means. Allow yourself to be curious about what’s going to happen next, but don’t make it a “what are they going to do to you?” kind of thing. When you are ready to finish, imagine yourself walking back the way you came and close the space.

6. Gradual Wave

P1Start with a very small neutral area, feel into it a little and then imagine a wave of warmth, steadiness, or attention spreading out to a new area that is still ok. Sense into the new area (or continue using the old). See if there is anything different at each step. Does the body want any contact, space, words, other care? Let the mental image go, sense your body, sense your contact points, come back.

7. Care Workshop

P1Prepare the workshop (time, place, tools, boundaries, etc). Invite the signal/feeling/young part to come only as close as is welcome. Try to avoid any language that makes you feel you're not one person. Care for it as best you can, by asking what it wants and giving that if you can, or sensing into it and responding, or just being with it. Take breaks, get back in contact if that's ok, take more breaks, etc. Keep bringing in adult functions (or borrow someone else's function). Have the adult part of you run the show. The goal is to make it "good as you" rather than "good as new", not to erase history. Close the workspace, reassure yourself that you are one whole person, that you're an adult now, and come back.

8. Resource Embodiment

P1Pick a quality you understand and want more of, like warmth, being able to hold a boundary, steadiness, curiosity or dignity. Imagine yourself with just 1% more of it. (If you'd rather not step into it, you can watch an imagined you have it instead.) What's different? How do you sit, and how does your voice sound? Try it a few times; each time, it gets a little easier to find. Come back, keeping the quality and a positive action that you would do with it, but leaving the imagined you behind, saying "I am me".
