# Reading a table of contents

You're testing the table of contents of a long online guide on Substack, "Inner Signal Guide to Self-Hypnosis (& APP),
including Reparenting, Conscious-Subconscious Spiritual Communion", by Joel. It teaches self-hypnosis the way he does
it: what he means by hypnosis and how to keep from being hypnotized by others, affirmations, a map of the mind, inner
child work (the inner child, the protective parts, and the jobs of the adult in you), noticing whose voice you're
following, inductions and coming back, the actual inner meeting, a daily and a weekly practice, troubleshooting, what
not to do and when to get more support, his Inner Signal app, and appendices (asking the subconscious questions, NLP,
anxiety, daydreaming, self-trust and oracle-like states, altered states, hostile presences, sleep hypnosis, an
induction library, sources). It's about 29,500 words. You'll see only its table of contents, the way the post's narrow
contents sidebar lists it: each heading on one line at its level (#, ##, ###, ####), with how many words are under it,
and a heading too long for the sidebar cut off with "…". You won't see the text, and you shouldn't guess at it beyond
what a reader skimming the contents would.

Read the table of contents file and the situations file your task line names, then write your answer file as Markdown,
in two parts:

1. **Where you'd look.** A table with one row per situation: the situation's number; the heading you'd go to first,
   exactly as the contents shows it ("…" included); "sure" or "guess"; and, if it's a guess, the heading you'd try next.
   Answer quickly, as someone in that situation would, scanning a sidebar.
2. **What gets in the way.** Headings that don't say what's under them or could be read two ways; headings that say or
   ask the same thing as the one just before them or their parent (in the sidebar a parent and its first child sit on
   two lines in a row, and the parent's text doesn't show); sections that seem to be in the wrong place, at the wrong
   level, or under the wrong parent; headings that are notes to the reader rather than sections. Say why for each. Then
   list your hardest headings, one per line, in exactly this form: `HARD: <line number> | <why>` (the line numbers are
   at the start of each line of the contents file).

The writer writes playfully on purpose ("NLP without turning yourself into a sales seminar", "Forget the
left-brain/right-brain cartoon"): a playful heading isn't a problem by itself, only one a reader can't use to find
their way.
