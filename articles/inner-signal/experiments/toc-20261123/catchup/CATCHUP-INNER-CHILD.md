# Self-Hypnosis guide: where its inner-child sections have fallen behind the Inner Child guide

Compared: the Self-Hypnosis guide's sections built on the Inner Child guide (live post, unchanged since 2026-11-20) against the Inner Child guide as live on 2026-11-24 22:39 UTC. 33 items: 2 high, 16 medium, 15 low. Substance only; nothing here is rewritten. Section ids are each file's own.

Some of the caveats below may already be said in parts of the Self-Hypnosis guide that weren't in the file compared (only its inner-child sections were); each such item says so.

## High

**C01: dropped in source** (high)

Hypnosis guide [S019]:

> The longer version of this exercise can be found in my Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide .

Inner Child guide [S003, Chicken-and-Egg Problem]:

> Maybe you copy one small thing you’ve seen them do, or imagine them there when they’re not, and try one piece yourself while they’re still helping with the rest. That’s what I mean by borrowed adulthood.

Why it matters: The reader is sent to the Inner Child guide for a longer version of the hat exercise, but that guide no longer has the adult hat, the apprentice adult or the step of returning the hat; it now describes borrowing as someone doing part of the adult role with you first while you copy one piece, and as five stages (S004).

Suggestion: ask the author. The pointer sentence in S019, and whether the hat steps stay as the hypnosis guide's own.

**C02: caveat** (high)

Hypnosis guide [S136]:

> I’m not saying you should use substances, but many people have used them when they first did self-hypnosis, contacted their inner child, or talked to their Higher Self.

Inner Child guide [S102, If a past trip went badly]:

> If you go manic, psychotic (by which I mean losing your sense of reality to the point of posing a risk to yourself or others), get super paranoid, or end up worse afterward than before, those are signs to reconsider trying the same thing again, and try to figure out why that happened. Don’t try again until you understand what would make it safe.

Why it matters: A reader who mixes substances with this work is told to start sober and what not to use self-hypnosis for, but not the signs that mean stop and don't repeat it (mania, psychosis, strong paranoia, ending up worse), nor when to see a professional, all of which the source now spells out.

Suggestion: link to the source section. After the first paragraph of S136; point to source S101 to S108 (Losing Touch With Reality). Check the hypnosis guide's own safety sections first; they weren't in the file I was given.
