When your inner child does show up, spend some quality time with them before taking off the kid gloves, so to speak, and jumping into the healing work.

They might like to hear something like, “Baby boopaloopa, I’m here with you now. You don’t have to work to explain anything or impress me with how fast you can get better. Can I help you feel less alone?”

What does your inner child need? It can be anything: warmth, to play, to be protected, to mourn, to be angry, food, rest, boundaries, better relationships, to stop fantasizing, to stop working their butt off, that you keep a promise to them today, etc. etc.

When a younger you, a guard, a feeling or a signal comes up in your body, take it slowly:

A. Prepare yourself to tend to them with some privacy, but still able to access support if needed. If being alone was a big part of what hurt during your childhood, I’d call somebody now, mess and all, since reaching toward people is part of taking care of your little one too. Even if you don’t have someone you know who wants to listen, there are warmlines for this (not hotlines unless it’s an emergency). Check how much grown-up you is really in the room first. Can you take a moment before you react? When you decide something, do you follow through? If you answered "No," because right now you’re mostly still stuck identifying as the frightened little boo, hold off on the deeper conversation. Do what makes you feel adult before you try to talk to little you.

B. Look at what's there before judging or deciding what it is.

C. Ask them what you can do or if you can move closer to them. Even if you feel like you already know what they need, ask if they'd prefer warmth, space, verbal reassurance, silence, distance, whatever.

D. Give them what you can: pick a need and do the smallest thing that would help.

E. Give yourself some time to let what you did sink in- it may take sleep, etc.

F. Reconnect with them if they're willing- don't expect them to merge, go away, forgive you, become happy, etc.

G. Stop and rest before the caring turns into another performance, and reconnect with them later to see what was helpful.

Remember the goal isn't to make yourself good as new but to make yourself good as you- more supported and connected to yourself. If these exercises start feeling like a ritual you have to complete before you’re allowed to live, stop there. If you don't like the idea of part work or it makes you feel more divided, you can work with a signal or a feeling instead of a part, or with an imaginary younger you that you feel embodies the issue you're having.
