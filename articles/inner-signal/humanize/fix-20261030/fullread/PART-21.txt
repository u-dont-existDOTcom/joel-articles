## Learning, entering, and returning

Maggie Phillips and Claire Frederick, Healing the Divided Self (1995), especially pp.24–27, 45–50, and 83–90, discusses interactive hypnosis, sensory learning, self-hypnosis, and a definite return. It is a professional clinical book, not a self-help manual; its case procedures and historical claims shouldn’t be imported wholesale into solo practice. Adam Eason and Benjamin Parris’s ISH article on self-hypnosis discusses self-directed learning and its relationship to another hypnotist and recordings.

## Indirect language and re-entry cues

Phillips and Frederick, pp.53–57 and 96–100, explain pacing, utilization, metaphor, and associational cues. Nimja’s hypnotic-language tutorial and trigger tutorial describe language and cueing techniques. They are technique sources, not evidence that every NLP claim is established or every example suits trauma work. The original wording and deliberately chosen re-entry sequence here are adaptations; Nimja did not write or endorse this guide.

## Body awareness and changing the amount of contact

Peter Levine, In an Unspoken Voice (2010), chapters 5 and 7; Levine and Maggie Phillips, Freedom from Pain (2012), chapters 3–4; and Levine, Healing Trauma, chapter 4, inform bodily tracking, resources, titration, pendulation, and attention across sensation, imagery, behavior, emotion, and meaning. The invitation to try a movement or image is not a claim that a particular physiological repair has occurred.

## Inner-child care and adult capacities

The companion Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide gives the fuller treatment of borrowed adulthood, love without compulsory trust, practical and relational repair, spiritual struggle, differentiation, and mutual peer support. The corresponding practices here follow that account while retaining the self-hypnosis entry, responsive language, and complete return.

Healing the Divided Self, pp.148–153 and 171–174, discusses renurturing, somatic work, and developing nurturing capacities. Levine and Maggie Kline’s Trauma Through a Child’s Eyes, especially chapters 4–5 and 10, discusses attunement and individual responses in actual children and caregiving. Applying those ideas to an adult’s younger feeling-states is an adaptation. My three-adult arrangement and borrowed-adulthood sequence are not presented as those authors’ tested protocol.

## Inner speech and putting experience into words

Nedergaard and Lupyan’s Not Everybody Has an Inner Voice (2024) studied people reporting different amounts of inner speech, not self-hypnosis adaptations. The questions in Quick start are a practical self-check, not a validated screening instrument or a test of hypnotic ability.

## Memory, support, and choosing a practitioner

Levine’s Trauma and Memory (2015), chapter 7, is useful on the difference between compelling experience and historical verification. The VA’s hypnosis overview includes cautions about strong reactions and false memories. ASCH’s explanation of certification distinguishes professional preparation from a guarantee of someone’s competence. The existing links in the evidence and specialist sections point to their particular claims rather than validating every use of hypnosis.

## Learning, entering, and returning

Healing the Divided Self, Maggie Phillips and Claire Frederick (1995), pp 24-27, 45-50, 83-90, covers a lot of aspects of this post, such as interactive hypnosis, helping the subject learn how to learn by using other senses, learning self hypnosis, making sure the subject comes back at the end, etc. However, this is a professional clinical book, not a self help book, so be careful about trying to use the procedures used with the cases, etc in this book for solo work (the same applies to historical information it contains). Also, Adam Eason and Benjamin Parris's article about self hypnosis for the International Society of Hypnosis should be a useful thing to read (especially the parts that talk about how to learn to learn when you're directing it yourself, and about whether you need another person to help you, and using recordings to help you, etc.)

## Indirect language and re-entry cues

For information about using indirect hypnotic language patterns, such as pacing, utilizing, metaphor, and associational cues, etc, see Healing the Divided Self, Phillips and Frederick, 1995, pp 53-57, 96-100, as well as Nimja's tutorial on hypnotic language patterns, and his tutorial on triggers. The information on these techniques comes from these sources, but that doesn't mean they validate every NLP claim, or that every example suits trauma work (Nimja did not write or endorse this guide). I adapted the language for my own purposes, and I deliberately used a sequence that would be easy to use for re-entry.

## Inner-child care and adult capacities

For information about borrowed adulthood, love that doesn't require trust, practical and relational repair, spiritual struggle, differentiation and mutual peer support, see Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide. The practices in this post are designed to match with those in the guide, but include some elements of learning to get into self hypnosis, as well as other helpful language, and making sure you come back at the end, etc.

Some of the information in this post is taken from Healing the Divided Self, Phillips and Frederick, 1995, pp 148-153, 171-174, about renurturing work, and how it can be combined with somatic work, and how to develop capacities for nurturing, etc. For information about attunement, and how every child is different and needs to be responded to differently, see Trauma Through a Child's Eyes, Levine and Maggie Kline, especially ch 4, 5, 10, etc, as it talks about actual kids (and this guide is applying this to how to respond to younger feeling states within an adult). The sequence for doing this with borrowed adulthood and the arrangement of 3 functions of adulthood, etc, is not from these authors, however.

## Inner speech and putting experience into words

Not everybody has an inner voice (Nedergaard and Lupyan 2024) is not about self hypnosis adaptations, but about people with different amounts of inner speech. The questions in the quick start are a practical way of seeing for yourself how much inner speech you have.

## Memory, support, and choosing a practitioner

Chapter 7 of Trauma and Memory, by Peter Levine, 2015, is useful on the difference between compelling experience and historical verification. This US Veterans Administration website contains useful general information about hypnosis, such as the fact that it can be very strong and should be carefully used, and that it may result in false memories. This ASCH website contains information about the usefulness of getting certified in hypnosis, and why it doesn't guarantee competence. Links are available for these and other useful groups in the evidence and specialist sections, but they point to their particular claims rather than validating every use of hypnosis.
