## Sober induction: 3–5 minutes

Read this slowly into a recorder, speak it aloud, or learn its movement and use silent words if that comes easily. Choose one anchor—a sound, bodily contact, or your two-hand practice—and an opening phrase. Keep using the same anchor where the words refer to it. Pause between paragraphs; don’t read the delivery directions aloud.

Let your eyes settle somewhere in the room, somewhere they can rest for a little while, and notice the difference between looking for something and letting what is already there come into view.

There is a surface beneath you. Notice where you meet it … the contact, the pressure, whatever you can actually feel. And while you notice that, a sound may reach you, or a breath may draw your attention, without needing you to arrange either one.

You can give this a little time.

A thought about the day may still be here. A thought about whether you’re doing this properly may be here too. There is room to notice a thought and return to the anchor you chose, without finishing the thought first.

And as you return, you may find one detail that holds your interest … something about the sound, or the contact, or that particular way of listening … and let attention stay with it long enough to become curious.

Perhaps you can give yourself the time you would give someone you care about who has begun to speak and hasn’t yet found the words. You can remain nearby, listening, without finishing their sentence for them.

Let your chosen phrase come to mind now. Say it as an invitation, and notice how it is received.

As you continue, your eyes can close, soften, or stay open. Let that be a choice you make. Listening may deepen while a sound from the room remains available in the background.

Count slowly from five to one. Give each number its own moment.

Five. Notice your chosen anchor again. Four. Let attention gather around what you find. Three. Leave a little space after the words. Two. Listen into that space, without preparing an answer. One. Stay here for a moment.

Bring your chosen concern lightly to mind, or remain with your wish for contact. Let it be present without rehearsing its whole story.

What am I noticing now?

Give the response time to take its own form. It might be a sensation, a few words, an image, a feeling, or an impulse to move. There may be nothing clear yet.

Leave room to notice, with words or without them.

## Already open? Contain before you deepen

Use this when loveyhuasca, meditation, lucid dreaming, grief, music, prayer, erotic energy, exhaustion, or intense beauty has already opened the state. Stabilize before making it stronger.

“The state is already open. I do not need to intensify it. I need to aim it, stabilize it, and keep it kind.”

Look around the room. Name where you are. Name the date, or at least the day. Feel your feet, hands, and spine. Press your thumbs gently but firmly against your index fingers. Focus on the physical friction of skin meeting skin.

“Only what can be integrated is welcome now. Anything too much can wait. The body may stay safe. The adult self remains present. The inner child does not have to carry this alone.”

Once you’re oriented, choose one intention:

“Please show me the next useful truth, symbol, feeling, question, or action — only in the amount I can live well with.”

## Stay awake when your mind likes to escape

Sit upright. Open the eyes. Feel the feet. Let the breath be natural.

Say: “I am not going away. I am coming more fully here.”

Look at one object. Feel the spine.

“Is this daydream guidance, emotional digestion, or avoidance?”

“If it is guidance, show me the action inside it. If it is avoidance, bring me gently into the present and show me what I am avoiding.”

If stillness makes you sleepy, diffuse, or escape-prone, hypnosis can become more alert rather than more relaxed. Keep the eyes open and pair a small repeated hand movement with increasing clarity; stop the movement and notice what remains without trying to disappear inward.

“Eyes open. Press thumb and forefinger together gently. Release. Again — clearer, not sleepier. One more time. Now stop the deliberate movement. Notice the first second after the rhythm ends. Is there an echo in the fingers, the breath, the attention, or nowhere at all? Stay upright and work from exactly that level of alertness.”

## The two-hand induction

Place one hand on the heart. Place the other hand on the solar plexus.

Let the heart hand represent the Nurturer. Let the solar plexus hand represent the vulnerable child.

Breathe gently.

“Love above. Child below. Adult present.”

“I send love to the child. I receive the child’s signal. I send love again. I listen again.”

## Sleep induction

Sleep induction has a different job. In inner work, the adult returns, writes, and acts. At bedtime, analysis can remain off duty. Revelation can wait; the task is rest.

For sleep hypnosis, see Nimja’s sleep playlist.

## Optional fractionation: practice coming back

Fractionation means returning toward ordinary awareness and then deciding whether to enter absorption again. Used openly, it teaches that trance has a door in both directions. Name the technique before using it; every answer cannot secretly mean “deeper.”

“Open the eyes and return toward ordinary awareness. Feel the room and decide whether to stop here. If you choose to continue, close or soften the eyes and notice what changes as attention gathers again. You may stop at either point.”

## Deepening, if useful

“If it is useful, attention may become more gathered. If it is not useful, I remain exactly as awake as I need to be.”

Choose one simple scene: a room in the heart, a garden, a temple, a fire with the Protector nearby, a clear lake, or a chair where the adult waits. One scene is plenty.

“Who or what needs to speak first?”

## Optional trance depth check

“As I wait, I give up the responsibility of making anything happen.

If it is useful, the wise subconscious may let one finger twitch, pulse, lift, warm, tingle, or become subtly noticeable when the inner meeting space is ready.

I will not force it. I will not fake it. I will simply wait with love and notice.”

A finger may move, a hand may warm, the breath may change, or nothing may happen. None proves truth; at most, something may be listening.

## Sober induction: 3–5 minutes

You can read this slowly into your recorder, you can read it out loud or say it to yourself if you are good at that. Choose a sound, or a contact, or the two hands thing (above) or something to anchor yourself, and choose the thing you will say to open it up. Use the same anchor for everywhere that anchor is mentioned. Don't read the directions, just pause between paragraphs.

Script:

You can allow your eyes to rest on something in the room for a bit, and let what's already there come into view.

You can allow yourself to notice something you can feel, like the sensation of something under you, and notice the sensation of the contact, the pressure. Or maybe there is a sound, or something else to notice.

Allow yourself some time to do this.

You might notice yourself thinking, and whenever you notice yourself thinking, you can allow yourself to re-engage with your anchor and leave the thought unfinished.

You might notice something that holds your interest, something about the sound, or sensation, or listening or something else.

Allow yourself some time to do this, as much time as you would for someone you care about who has started to talk and hasn't gotten to what she wants to say yet, so you are going to stick around till she gets it all out.

Repeat the phrase that you chose, if you want to. Say it as an invitation and notice how it's received.

You can close your eyes now if you want to. If not, it's fine to keep them open, or soften your gaze. The listening might go deeper while there's still a background sound you can hear.

Count 5 to 1, taking your time if you like.

5. Let your attention come back to your chosen anchor. 4. Let more of your attention come to what you've found. 3. Leave a little space. 2. See if you can listen to a space after the words without preparing an answer. 1. Take your time.

Bring to mind, if you want, the thing that you want to talk about, or just the wish to have inner contact. Just let it be there for now. You don't need to tell the whole story yet.

What do you notice now?

Let yourself notice what comes to you, if anything. It may not be clear yet what it is. It may be sensations, words, pictures, feelings, movements, or anything else.

Let yourself take your time to notice, with or without words.

## Already open? Contain before you deepen

This is for when you're already open for some reason (loveyhuasca, meditation, lucid dreaming, going through grief, listening to beautiful music, prayer, erotic energy, exhaustion, seeing something you love, etc) and want to aim and stabilize it.

Know that you're already open, you just need to aim and stabilize it, and keep it kind.

Orient yourself by looking around. Where are you? What's the date or day? How does your body feel? Where are you grounded? Can you feel your feet/hands/spine/etc? Make some contact with yourself, eg press your thumbs against the tips of your index fingers gently but firmly so that you can feel friction. Focus on that.

Say something like, "Only what I can integrate, please. If there's too much it can wait. My body may stay safe. I'll be here as an adult, this won't just be my inner child."

When you feel oriented, make an intention.

Something like, "Please show me the next truth/symbol/feeling/question/action that is useful for me, that I can integrate."

## Stay awake when your mind likes to escape

Say to yourself, "I'm not going anywhere, and I'm coming more fully here." Sit up, open your eyes, feel your feet, let your breathing be natural.

Look around you and choose something to look at. Feel your spine. Ask yourself why you're daydreaming. Is it to get guidance, or because you're working through something, or are you avoiding something?

Say something like, "If I'm meant to be getting guidance, please show me the action I need to take. If I'm avoiding something, please bring me back to now and show me what I'm avoiding."

### Make the hypnosis more active

If being still tends to make you sleepy, zone out, or wish you were out (escapism), no worries, there's a trick for that. Instead of getting more relaxed with the hypnosis, try getting more alert (it's still relaxed-alert, not paranoid-alert). Keep your eyes open. Use a small hand movement each time you make the auto-suggestion that you're gaining increasing clarity. Then stop the hand movement and see where your mind is at. That hand movement is the mental escape into the body's flow. Say something like,

“Starting with your eyes open, press your thumb to your forefinger. And as you press your fingers together gently and release, you begin to bring yourself into a place of clearer awareness… again you press your fingers together and release, bringing yourself into an even clearer state of awareness. And again as you press your fingers together and release, you bring yourself into a clear state of awareness. And now you stop pressing your fingers together, and in the first second after you stop pressing your fingers together deliberately, you can notice if there is any sensory echo of the pressing in your fingers, in your breathing, in your attention, or nowhere at all. Just notice, sit up straight and work from this level."

## The two-hand induction

One hand on your heart, one hand on your solar plexus

Let the hand on your heart be your Nurturer and let the hand on your solar plexus be your vulnerable child

Start to breathe gently….

Imagine a divine Love from above, with the inner child below, in the solar plexus area. The inner adult is present. You can also use the hand on the solar plexus to massage that area. Say:

“Sending my love down to my Child. If something comes back up, I let it, and send my love on down again. If nothing comes back, I just keep sending mine, and listening.”

## Optional fractionation: practice coming back

Fractionation means returning toward ordinary awareness and then deciding whether to enter hypnotic absorption again. It gives trance a revolving door, so that the approach can be evaluated at different levels rather than "flying blind." Going in and out repeatedly also helps to make those transitions easier, quicker, and more intuitive. At some point in time you can add this with intention.

“Ok, open your eyes and come back up, feel the room around you, you can decide if you want to stop the work at this point or if you want to continue, if you want to continue soften your eyes or close them and notice the change that comes over you when you do, if you want to stop, that is ok too, either way is ok and you can stop at either place.”

## Deepening, if useful

Allow your attention to gather, if it will be of use to you. If it won’t be of use to you at this time, you can remain as awake as you need to be.

Pick a place to go to when you go inside. It might be a room in your heart, a garden, a temple, sitting in front of a fire with your Protector, down by a clear lake where you can see the bottom, in a comfortable chair where your adult is going to be, whatever you want. Just pick a place.

Who or what needs to talk first?

## Optional trance depth check

“As you wait, you release the responsibility of making anything happen.

It might be useful for your wise subconscious to twitch a finger in one of your hands, or make a finger pulse, or come up by itself, or to heat up a finger and make it noticeable to you, or tingle in a noticeable way to let you know that your inner meeting space is ready for you to go into.

You don’t have to make it happen, or pretend that it is happening, just wait with love and notice what happens.

Sometimes a finger will move, sometimes an entire hand will heat up, sometimes you will notice a change in your breathing, sometimes you won’t notice anything at all. Even if something happens, it doesn’t mean it is true, it just might mean that something is listening.
