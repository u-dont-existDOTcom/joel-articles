## What I will not use NLP for

To talk myself out of a real signal.

To override fear before asking what the fear protects.

To recover memories.

To manipulate anyone else.

To convince myself an intuition is true.

To erase pain, hunger, grief, anger, or a medical symptom.

While panicking, destabilized, manic, psychosis-like, chemically overwhelmed, or too dissociated to reality-test.

To make another person’s no feel like resistance.

To make someone feel spiritually inferior for disagreeing.

To hide a command inside soft language.

To create urgency where time is needed.

To bypass consent by calling something “subconscious agreement.”

To use trance, fatigue, attraction, shame, grief, or altered states to get compliance.

## Running the whole NLP sequence

Induce or contain. Gather attention, or stabilize first if you are already wide open.

Ask permission. Is it useful to work with this image, feeling, or pattern today?

Represent the pattern. How does this show up — image, voice, sensation, posture, distance, size, color, movement?

Ask what it protects. What is this trying to do for me?

Adjust the form, gently. Move it back, lower the volume, add space, change posture, slow the scene.

Bring in the three adults. Nurturer warms, Protector makes it safe, Leader points a direction.

Anchor the useful state. Heart, fingers, breath, posture.

Future pace. If the old cue shows up, what is the smallest new action?

Check the fit and the evidence. What changes in the body? Is the response kind and doable? What supports the interpretation beyond how it feels?

Exit and act. Come back to the room. Do one ordinary thing.

# Appendix C: The extended de-hypnosis checklist

Use this checklist when a person, advertisement, ideology, spiritual teaching, romantic pull, or inner voice is pressing too hard.

“I notice influence is happening. I do not need to obey it or attack it. I can slow down. I can ask what is being presupposed. I can ask who benefits. I can notice what my body is signaling without assuming what it proves. I can ask what love, truth, humility, and ordinary life would require.

If this is true, it will survive questions. If this is love, it will survive boundaries. If this is guidance, it will survive sleep. If this is for me, it will not require me to abandon my adult self.”

Before acting, ask:

Is there urgency?

Is there secrecy?

Is there shame?

Is there a threat of exclusion?

Is there a promise of specialness?

Is there sexual charge?

Is there spiritual superiority?

Is there a demand for money, sex, loyalty, silence, or obedience?

Is there a narrowing of choices?

Is questioning treated as betrayal?

Is resistance pathologized?

Is ordinary life being downgraded?

Is the child part being seduced, scared, or rescued?

Is the adult self still allowed to say no?

Several yes answers mean slow down. Don’t deepen trance or make an irreversible decision. Return to the room, body, food, sleep, time, and counsel.

A good guide increases your adult self. A bad guide replaces it. A good guide can tolerate your no. A bad guide calls your no resistance, ego, fear, low vibration, trauma, sin, or proof that you need more of their guidance.

## Hypnosis-specific audit

Before accepting a recording or live suggestion, ask:

Was I plainly told that induction mechanics are being used?

Can at least one answer lead to pause, modification, full alertness, or stopping?

Does the speaker treat not noticing as secret proof that the process worked?

Are roles, ownership, obedience, age, identity, or belonging being used to lower adult judgment?

Are fractionation, cues, later behavior, or memory effects disclosed before they are used?

Are ordinary body sensations being turned into evidence for a hidden claim?

Does the exit restore ordinary identity, movement, orientation, and critical judgment—and plainly say that the session has ended?

When an answer is unclear, stay fully alert. Ask for the exact script, change the method, or leave.

# Appendix D: Anxiety — body first

Anxiety is often a Protector trying to prevent danger, shame, abandonment, failure, exposure, illness, rejection, loss of control, or a feeling it believes would be unbearable. Don’t begin by cross-examining it. Help the body first.

Body first. Inner child second. Inquiry third. Action fourth.

## Reduced breathing / Buteyko-style breathing

Use this gently. Buteyko-style reduced breathing can help some people who overbreathe or panic, but don’t force breath holds. Air hunger can briefly increase anxiety before it settles, so go slowly.

Breathe through the nose. Let the inhale be small. Let the exhale be quiet. Pause only if comfortable. Let the breath become less dramatic. Stop if dizzy, strained, panicky, or medically concerned.

## EFT tapping

EFT can be a simple bodily ritual: name the fear, tap gently, and stay in contact with what you feel. The evidence is still developing. A 2025 systematic review found seven randomized controlled trials involving EFT for anxiety disorders; EFT beat no intervention in several of them, although the evidence should still be treated cautiously beside first-line therapies.

## Running the whole NLP sequence

Here is a rough outline of the entire NLP process:

Induce/Contain the person (get their attention, or stabilize them first if they are already wide open).

Ask for their permission: is it useful to work with this image, feeling or pattern today?

Get a representation of their pattern (image, feeling, size, color, location, movement, etc.).

Find out what that part/pattern is protecting for them.

Gently change some aspect of their pattern (location, volume, space, posture, speed, etc.).

Put 3 good adults in with the person (Nurturer to warm, Protector to make it safe, and Guide to point a direction). In real life they’re all part of the one inner adult, so we can imagine them splitting apart if need be and coming together as well.

Anchor the person's good state (heart, fingers, breath, posture, etc.).

Future pace a scenario for them where their old pattern would have triggered, and have them do a small new behavior (If...then... statement).

Have them check in with their body for changes, ask whether the new behavior is kind and doable, and ask what supports their new interpretation of the situation beyond how it feels.

Get the person out of the altered state (bring them back to the room and have them do one ordinary thing).

# Appendix C: The extended de-hypnosis checklist

What to do when you feel like you’re being pushed too hard by something (person, ad, ideology, spiritual teaching, romantic attraction, internal voice, etc.): Say to yourself, “Okay, something is trying to influence me here. I don’t have to obey it. I don’t even have to attack it. I can just slow myself down and ask some questions like, 'What would this thing influence me to believe that isn’t true in order to get what it wants from me? What is my body telling me to do about this (how does my body feel)? What would love do here? What would truth do here? How would I approach this humbly? What would life have me do here?'”

If what you’re investigating is true, it can take your questioning (as the police like to say). If it’s love, it can accommodate your boundaries. If it’s real guidance, it’ll still be there tomorrow. If it’s really for you, it won’t want you to give up all the wisdom and sovereignty you’ve worked so hard to develop.

Before you do what it wants you to do, see if it does any of the following: create a sense of urgency, secrecy, shame, threat of exclusion if you don’t do what it says, specialness, sexual charge, spiritual superiority, ask you for money, ask you for sex, ask you for loyalty, ask you to be quiet, ask you to obey it, try to limit your choices, make it seem like it’s bad to question it, act like not obeying it is a problem that needs to be solved, make normal life seem boring/stupid/irrelevant in its presence, try to seduce/scare/rescue your inner child, not let your adult self say no.

If it does a bunch of these things, slow yourself down, don’t let it push you deeper into trance, don’t do anything you can’t undo, and then ground yourself for a while (take care of your body, eat, sleep, give it time, get counsel, etc).

A good guide will make your adult self stronger. A good guide can take no. A bad guide can’t take no. A bad guide wants to take your adult self’s job. A bad guide will try to make your no something that’s bad or needs to be fixed (resistance, ego, fear, low vibration, trauma response, sin, need for more of its guidance, etc).

## Hypnosis-specific audit

Before accepting a hypnotic recording or live suggestion, ask these important questions to avoid being manipulated:

What induction mechanics are being used to bring me under the spell?

Can my responses lead to pausing or modifying the script, or stopping & coming to full alertness?

Does the hypnotist treat my lack of awareness of what's going on as some kind of proof that the process worked?

Is the hypnotist using identity roles, obedience ladders, age regressions, or desires for/assumptions of belonging to lower my adult self's better judgment?

Keep in mind though, the answer to that last one requires either checking the script first, or explaining what your adult judgement would accept, as the hypnotist can't be expected to know that.

Continuing with the job interview:

Are fractionation, cues, or other methods of affecting my memory or modifying my post-session behavior disclosed before they are used?

Is the hypnotist implicitly turning my bodily sensations into evidence for a prior assumption?

When we're ready to exit hypnosis, will they give me a clear statement that the session has ended, and would I get back my normal waking (and walking) identity? Will I be able to use my critical judgement abilities as before?

When any answer to these questions is unclear, beware. Ask for the exact script. If they don't want to give it, change the method, or walk right out that door.

# Appendix D: Anxiety — body first

Remember that often the anxiety comes from a childhood guard part of you. It’s what protects you when your inner adult’s Protector isn’t functioning, the same as in childhood. It’s trying to help, making a guess about what’s coming: that you are in danger, or may be shamed, or abandoned, or fail, or be exposed, or be sick, or be rejected, or lose control, or feel something it believes would be unbearable. It can be right! It’s probably been wrong before too, and you know some of those times. Therefore, instead of interrogating that part, start with yourself:

Always work with yourself in a series – first your body, then your inner child, then some inquiry, then some action:

## Reduced breathing / Buteyko-style breathing

Work on your reduced breathing / Buteyko breathing. (NOTE: there are some good things to be said for slowing your breathing down, if you are breathing too much, or panicked. However, please don’t hold your breath until you are air hungry, which can make you more anxious in the short term.) Breathe in small amounts through your nose and let it out quietly, through your nose too. Only pause if that's comfortable. Can you make your breathing very quiet? If you feel bad, dizzy, or panicky, or have medical issues, STOP!

## EFT tapping

EFT tapping: One of the easiest ways to do EFT is to say something like, “Even though I have this anxiety, I am willing to be kind to the part of me that is afraid,” while you tap gently on your body and stay with what you feel. There isn’t a ton of evidence for EFT, but there are some studies on anxiety. A 2025 systematic review found 7 randomized controlled trials for anxiety disorders, and noted that EFT was more effective than no intervention in several of them. So use the evidence with caution, and know that there are first-line therapies for anxiety.
