# Affirmations Done Right

My dad always said: “Always, and in everything, expect the very best. But be prepared to take what you get. The best might come afterwards.” That’s a better affirmation than what most people think of as affirmation, because it’s not telling you something which you know to be false in the present. If you say “I am beautiful” but you know that’s not true, then you’re challenging your beliefs, and if you’re challenging your beliefs you should have some argument or reason why they are wrong, otherwise it’s bullying.

Another quote I use all the time from my dad… when someone asks me “How’s it going?” I say “Better & better!” This really confuses a lot of people. Some think it’s amazing that I’m always doing better. Some ask if I was sick or something. But it’s doing multiple jobs. On the one hand, it’s deflecting from the fake question because most people don’t actually want to hear how you are doing in detail anyway. On the other hand, more importantly, it’s reprogramming your mind to remove you from tunnel vision. You might be sad now, but if you say, “I am sad” then you’re subtly identifying with the sadness and it keeps you stuck. It’s best to say, “I’m feeling sad now, but I’m doing better and better, every day, in every way!” That’s more like a mission statement than a historical analysis. Although you can also re-evaluate your history as lessons to make that true.

Try it out right now and see how it feels!

# Reversing Accidental Affirmations

Many times, we say things that we wish we hadn’t said, or our minds wander into places that we actually don’t want to live in. A very common example is masturbation, where people might fantasize about someone they don’t really want to be with in real life. While it might be optimal to just avoid doing that, if it does happen, it’s good to apply a trick my sister taught me, which she learned from Positive Magic. Right after the wrong thought, say, “I take it out of the law of cause and effect.” It’s not exactly magic, and it’s not like you can actually remove anything from laws of nature, but it does sort of do that because without the rupture in your thought stream, you can end up just going directly where it leads you. With the intentional correction, you don’t have to be unconsciously led astray but your random wrong thoughts.

The Buddha also taught that the important thing is always to come back to mindfulness regardless of what disturbs the mind. My sister doesn’t realize how close she is to Buddhism!

# Hypnosis Evidence

Hypnosis has real evidence, although it doesn’t have magical evidence. A 2024 overview of 49 meta-analyses, covering 261 distinct primary studies, found the strongest support in pain, medical procedures, and work with children and adolescents. The quality still varied by outcome and study design.

Self-hypnosis has its own evidence. A systematic review of randomized controlled trials reported benefits in studies involving pain, childbirth, pediatric applications, stress, and anxiety. It appeared more useful when people learned it as a real self-directed skill and practiced it several times, rather than merely pressing play on an audio file.

That distinction matters here. I want you to learn an inner skill, not become a consumer of increasingly elaborate trance recordings.

Clinical caution still matters. Mayo Clinic warns that hypnosis used around stressful earlier-life events can trigger strong emotional reactions, and that other treatments may fit historical trauma work better.

Use self-hypnosis for regulation, dialogue, reparenting, clarity, habit change, present safety, emotional integration, and ordinary-life action.

Do not use it to force trauma memories, prove what happened, suppress medical symptoms, destabilize yourself, or replace appropriate clinical care.

# Forget the left-brain/right-brain cartoon

“Syncing the hemispheres” can be a useful description of how the state feels, but I wouldn’t turn it into literal neuroscience. A large resting-state fMRI study of 1,011 people didn’t support the pop-science picture of whole people being globally “left-brained” or “right-brained,” even though particular functions and networks can be lateralized.

You’re inviting the verbal, analytical, imaginal, emotional, somatic, intuitive, relational, and spiritual modes to cooperate. Nobody needs to win a hemisphere election.

The loving mind stays warm while the analytical mind stays bright. The intuitive mind offers signals without becoming dogma. The rational mind tests signals without killing them. The body gives yes, no, and not-yet. The imagination opens the field. The adult self chooses the next real action. Peace and precision become compatible.

Love can drift into fantasy without analysis. Analysis can become sterile without love. Intuition can become delusion when nothing tests it; testing can become dead when nothing intuitive is allowed to speak. Let the modes correct each other.

A powerful state matters only if something survives it: kinder behavior, more humility, better judgment, real service, or a more workable ordinary life.

# Why doing it myself worked better for me

Self-hypnosis became much more useful for me than working with a professional hypnotist. The session with the hypnotist did very little. Maybe part of that was state-dependent: I was sober and didn’t feel especially suggestible. The larger issue was trust and precision.

When I’m giving the suggestion, I can feel the exact moment a phrase is slightly wrong. I can stop, ask what the subconscious meant, and notice whether an image feels like guidance, fear, avoidance, wishful thinking, or something I don’t yet understand. I can catch the body’s no before the conscious mind writes a persuasive little essay called yes.

An outside hypnotist can’t easily conduct that rapid back-and-forth because they aren’t inside the mind receiving the suggestion. A skilled guide can still notice blind spots, help regulate the process, and hold a safer container. Self-hypnosis simply has one advantage nobody outside can duplicate: immediate dialogue with the system itself.

I found that especially amplified with loveyhuasca. My experiences included a much deeper understanding of my motivations, peaceful loving awareness operating alongside analytical clarity, the disappearance of unhelpful daydreaming, different priorities around relationships, stronger intuition, and repeated correction between intuition, subconscious response, and conscious analysis.
