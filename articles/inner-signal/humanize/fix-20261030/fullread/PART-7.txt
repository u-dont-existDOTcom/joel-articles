## Soft language can still take away choice

Permissive words can still conceal coercion. I watch for the following:

Indirect suggestion can slip past the skeptical guard because the conscious mind may never receive one clean proposition to accept or reject. The intended direction can still be quite specific even when the wording feels open, vague, conversational, or merely suggestive.

Induction hidden inside explanation: attention is being narrowed while the speaker pretends only to inform. Even an “anti-hypnosis” lesson can use induction mechanics.

Every answer means compliance: “yes,” “no,” noticing, not noticing, resistance, and silence are all redefined as proof that the suggestion is working.

“Whether you notice it or not” binds: uncertainty is converted into invisible success that cannot be checked.

Identity and ownership language: safety or goodness becomes tied to obedience, a role, the guide’s approval, or belonging to someone else.

Unannounced fractionation: moving in and out of absorption without telling you that repeated re-entry is part of the method.

Hidden post-session cues: words, gestures, blinks, sounds, or repeated listening are meant to produce later behavior that was not plainly disclosed.

Amnesia or truth inflation: dreamlike material, missing memory, imagery, or body sensations are treated as proof of what happened.

An exit that does not exit: the recording ends while preserving a role, reduced judgment, impaired movement, blankness, or an indefinite altered state.

A real choice includes at least one branch that doesn’t continue the induction: make it lighter, change the method, remain fully alert, pause, or stop. No remains no. Silence and uncertainty don’t become consent because the narrator has a soothing voice.

# Induction: gathering attention without disappearing

An induction gathers scattered attention until quieter material becomes easier to hear. You don’t have to surrender, play asleep, or obey anyone. When you’re sober, distracted, analytical, or guarded, an actual induction may help. When meditation, loveyhuasca, lucid dreaming, grief, music, prayer, fatigue, or another intense opening has already made you trancey, begin with orientation and containment instead of adding depth.

Aim for the amount of trance that suits the practice: focused, warm, receptive, embodied, and still able to stop. Depth is optional.

## What an induction is actually doing

Prepare the session, gather attention, deepen only when that helps, then let what you notice shape the words and the next response. Counting is one way in. Conversation, story, movement, repeated sound, and curiosity can also organize attention. Say so when you use them; elegance doesn’t excuse a covert induction.

For a simple, chosen target, you can test the suggestion before deepening. If the useful response is already happening, build from it. An induction helps you find a receptive way of listening; you don’t need to complete a ceremony before you’re allowed to respond.

## Please don’t put six metaphors in one trance

Choose one purpose, one starting anchor or image, and a way back. Decide whether warmth, a firm boundary, direction, or neutral witnessing would help. Floating, stairs, gardens, inner rooms, colored light, and child dialogue do not all need to report for duty at once. Switch consciously when the metaphor stops helping.

Imagery is optional. A signal may arrive through sensation, rhythm, movement, words, spatial sense, simple knowing, or no response at all. Keep the language in the world you chose: let a count mark something meaningful there, and reverse the journey when you finish. Borrow qualities such as warmth or steadiness without replacing your identity.

Pick one central verb, one sensory quality, one short refrain, and one transition phrase, then read the script aloud; long elegant sentences become soup during an induction.

## Before you begin

Read the induction and Come all the way back while fully awake. Choose the question or quality you want to explore, what you’re leaving alone today, and how much time you have. Love, play, confidence, creativity, and communion are worthwhile purposes; you don’t have to find a problem. Leave several minutes for returning and reflecting.

Choose a physically secure place without driving, water, machinery, or anyone needing your supervision. When intoxicated, acutely confused, or unable to keep yourself safe, deal with immediate support rather than exploratory hypnosis. The later Already open? practice is for orientation, not permission to intensify an unstable state.

Decide what kind of encouragement is welcome: spacious listening, warm company, or a firmer invitation to stay with a feeling. Preview eye closure, any repeated exit and re-entry, and the return. For a recording, read the script beforehand when possible. “Stop” ends the exercise; “less” reduces the demand; “quiet” stops the words. You can simply open your eyes and finish. No inner part has to approve your decision to stop.

When entry and return are unfamiliar, first rehearse them without a difficult subject. Once you know how to change direction, you don’t need to supervise every moment. Let a useful response become easier and less effortful. A no remains no; silence doesn’t authorize a narrator to add something you haven’t agreed to.

Let the next invitation grow from the response you got—not the response the script expected.Utilization means working with what actually happens: a thought, sound, movement, or objection may change where the practice goes. Notice what actually happens; no change doesn’t mean secret success. Keep safety and exit instructions plain. ## Soft language can still take away choice

Be aware that even when using permissive language, you can still take away the subject’s choice. Be aware of;

Indirect suggestions can by-pass the subject’s skeptical guard. They do this by not directly telling the subject what to do. This does not mean that indirect suggestions are not specific. It simply means that they are worded in a vague, conversational, suggestive manner.

1) Utilizing induction techniques while seemingly explaining something (even something like "anti hypnosis" can use the same mechanics of induction).

2) In any way making it so that whatever the subject says shows that a suggestion has worked, when this may not be the case (this includes answering yes, no, noticing something, not noticing something, trying not to do something, not answering, etc etc etc)

3) Including phrases such as whether or not you notice etc etc, so either way counts as success

4) Including any concepts that may make a subject think they must obey, or take on a role, or that it is good/safe to please the guide or belong to someone etc etc.

5) Fractionating a subject without telling them that you are going to take them in and out of absorption a few times.

6) Giving a subject cues (words, gestures, blinks, sounds, listening a certain number of times etc) that you intend to use to make them do something at a later date, without informing them exactly what it is that they will be doing.

7) Including suggestions for amnesia, or making a subject think that something (dream, amnesia, imagery, body experiences etc) has happened when this may not necessarily be the case.

8) Not taking a subject out at the end of a recording, or leaving them with a role to play, with their judgement down, with their physical abilities down, with their minds blank, or in any other way different for an indefinite length of time.

In order for a subject to be making a REAL choice whilst in a hypnosis, there MUST be at least one way for a subject NOT to carry on with the hypnosis if they wish. This could be by making the hypnosis lighter, using a different method of hypnosis, being in full awareness whilst in a hypnosis, or being able to pause or stop the hypnosis. Just so you know, if you say no, you are quite entitled to mean NO, even if someone with a nice voice is asking you to do something! Also, just because you don't say anything, or don't know what to say, DOES NOT mean yes!

# Induction: gathering attention without disappearing

Inductions serve to gather you up so you can access quiet stuff. They're not about surrendering or playing asleep or obeying someone. Sometimes it's nice to do an actual induction if you're sober but distracted or analytical or otherwise not very trancey. If on the other hand, you're already pretty trancey for whatever reason, skip straight to "Already open? Contain before you deepen" rather than going deeper.

Get trancey enough to do what you want to do (focused, warm, receptive, embodied, able to stop, and optionally deeper)

## What an induction is actually doing

Getting yourself ready to do something. Part of that is gathering your attention up. Part of that might be deepening. Part of that is letting what you notice inform the words you're hearing and what you do next. There are lots of different ways to do this. For this method, I'm going to be counting, but you could do all sorts of things. If you want to just talk, or tell a story, or move, or make sounds, or be curious, or whatever, by all means, just do that. The only thing is, say that you're doing it. Don't pretend you're doing something else, in the name of elegance or whatever.

Test: For a simple, chosen target, are you getting the response you want out of the suggestion already? If so, you might not need to deepen.

## Please don’t put six metaphors in one trance

"Keep it simple, stupid!" (KISS principle). It's really easy to take a whole bunch of techniques from all of this and make something so complicated that you "hypnotize" yourself to the point where you don't even know if it worked or not. So start off with setting one anchor/image. Decide on a hypnotic tone (warm, indirect, direct, neutral witnessing, etc.) for the session. If you're going to use metaphors, just start with one, and if you switch metaphors midway, understand the purpose of the switch. Then plan a simple stepwise walk out of the subconscious at session end.

Using metaphors and imagery is your call. You can use whatever form of inner signal that you notice. It may be a direct knowing, or an emotion, words, some kind of rhythm, movements or other kind of spatial perception. Keep the language tied to the subconscious world you've chosen, so if you're counting, and there are already raindrops falling, you can count the raindrops, for example. When you're done, come back the opposite way from how you went in. You can borrow qualities such as warmth or steadiness from someone who models them, but don't change who you are.

When you're writing the script, decide what the central verb you're using is to anchor it (like "letting go"), what the sensory quality is (unified, disjoint, fluffy, etc), what the short refrain is, and how you're going to slide from one idea to the next. Read it out loud as you're writing it. Long, elegant sentences don't read well when you're being induced. It's all just soup. Soft, but to the point, easy to remember, easy to say, easy to grok, that's what we're going for.

## Before you begin

1. Read this page, and the Come all the way back page, when you're awake.

2. Get clear on what it is you want to do during the induction. What do you want not to do today? How long do you want to spend on this? (You don't have to do this on a problem. You can do it on something you want to experience more of: love, play, confidence, creativity, communion, whatever.) Make sure you're leaving some time at the end to come back, and reflect on what you experienced.

3. Put yourself in a place where you can safely do this, which is not: driving a big machine, swimming with the fishies, or otherwise needing to be alert and functionally responsible for yourself or others. If you are intoxicated and/or more confused than usual, or for whatever reason not able to keep yourself safe, then get some immediate support instead of doing exploratory hypnosis. (The part below called "Already open? Contain before you deepen" can be used to help orient you if you're already open, but don't use it to make yourself deeper if you're not stable.)

4. Deciding to do it: how will you coax yourself to do this? Make it spacious? Make it warm and held? Make it firmer, so you stay with a feeling? Something else? Preview what you are going to do: will you close your eyes? Will you come out and go back in again? What will you do at the end? If you are going to use a recording, read the script beforehand if you can. Know that you can say "stop" at any time and the induction will end. If you want to change something, say "less" if you want it to be asking for less from you, or say "quiet" and the talking will stop. You can also just open your eyes and stop, and then come all the way back anyway. You don't need to ask any parts for permission to stop, even if a part tells you that you do.

5. If you are new to this or haven't done it for a while, work on the coming in and coming out part first, don't bring in the hard stuff till later. You don't have to watch yourself all the time, but for the first few times, make sure you can change state. Let yourself make it easier and easier to do this and notice that it takes less and less of you to do it. A no will stay a no. The narrator can't add stuff just because you don't say anything.

6. Let your response guide you as to what to do next regardless of what the script is expecting. Utilize: use what you have (a thought, sound, movement, or objection) to go to the next place, whatever it is. Learn how to notice what is there. Learn how to notice if there is no change, and that no change is not a secret success. For more on language you can use, see Appendix B, or keep reading below for information on using the tools after induction in Let the next response guide you. Either way, keep the instructions about how to stay safe and how to get out plain.
