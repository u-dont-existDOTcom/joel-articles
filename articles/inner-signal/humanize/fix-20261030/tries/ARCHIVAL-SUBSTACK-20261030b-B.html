When this needs more support than a guide can provide

You should get help now if you are in imminent danger, if you are very confused, if you cannot ensure your own safety, and/or if you are experiencing concerning physical symptoms (e.g., chest pain, fainting, etc). You should also seek professional help promptly if you are experiencing new or worsening suicidal thoughts, severe insomnia, new or worsening manic or psychotic symptoms, or if dissociative symptoms are impacting your ability to function.

You should seek suitable ongoing help if you are experiencing recurring panic attacks, compulsions, substance destabilization, major relationship instability, etc.

You probably don't need any more support than this if you keep the practice shorter, make it warm, pick a small and tractable subject, make sure you come back out, and then see how it affects a specific target (anxiety, daydreaming, helping a relationship, facing a task you've been avoiding, helping you sleep, being less reactive emotionally, improving your intuitive accuracy, etc) for a week or two. If you notice that your functioning is getting worse, or that you're getting more certain about unconfirmable memories, then reassess instead of pushing harder.

Methods to explore with a trained person present

If you want to deliberately do trauma-focused regression (going backwards in your memory or imagination to times when you were younger), or affect and somatic bridging (following associations from an emotional or somatic experience to find the origin of that experience, particularly if it was a traumatic experience you don't know about), or if you find that you're having substantial flashbacks or dissociative reactions, then you should do this with a trauma-informed clinician present. Make sure they're trained in the specific method you want to do. Ask any potential clinician what they would do and how they deal with uncertainty and unexpected reactions. Note that even if you work with a person, hypnosis is not a way of knowing what did or did not happen to you.

Note that having experienced trauma, crying, and even wanting to feel difficult feelings are not necessarily signs that you need more support than working on your own. You get to pick how wide or narrow a range you want to work in, but be aware that different people need different ranges. Pay attention to how you respond and what support you have. Feeling like you are handling it isn't a substitute for having support.

The main clinical practice guidelines for the treatment of major trauma recommend one or more of trauma-specific variants of cognitive behavioral therapy (often referred to by specific names, such as cognitive processing therapy, prolonged exposure, etc.) or eye movement desensitization and reprocessing, depending on the person and the guideline. These approaches in turn can be further supported by techniques such as self-hypnosis and inner child work. But high-risk trauma work needs a bigger container than that.

Your functioning in ordinary life is the feedback

Always keep in mind that your functioning in ordinary life is the feedback. If you find yourself more loving, more honest, more functional, more relational, more grounded, more precise, etc. than when you started this process, keep on keeping on gently. If you notice that your ordinary life is becoming less stable, don't try to go deeper. Go for more sleep, more eating, more grounding, more healthy boundaries with other people, more getting support from other people, etc.

Appendix A: How to ask the subconscious a useful question

Your subconscious mind is NOT an AI large language model. However, there is a bit of overlap in the way you can ask questions to both: how you ask shapes the answer. So, if you know how to do prompt engineering for AI, this can be very useful to help you be more present, honest, loving etc. On the other hand, you should NEVER use it to be more controlling.

To get as much precision as possible, you need to include the following in your prompt:

  1. Role: Who or what are you talking to?

  2. Context: Give some context so it knows what you’re talking about.

  3. Intention: What do you want to change or accomplish?

  4. Constraint: What do you need to do to make sure your asking is safe, true, kind etc.? That means no forcing, no false certainty, and making sure you can handle the answer before you ask for it.

  5. Output: How do you want to receive the answer? As an image, phrase, sensation in your body, memory, action, question, symbol, silence, rest etc.

  6. Evaluation: How are you going to evaluate the answer? How will you know if it’s true, kind, timely, stabilizing, specific, testable etc?

Iteration: Iterate by changing your prompt depending on what you get from reality as feedback.

Most importantly, you always need to make your prompt safe, otherwise the analytical part of your mind will make up an answer just so it fits with your prompt. I usually add the following to my prompt:

“If you need more time or the best and safest answer is silence, then I ask you to make my body go into a deeper state of comfortable rest. A peaceful silence would be a successful answer to my question.”

Once you do that, here are the core prompts you can use:

Core prompts

For getting clarity: “I ask you, my wise subconscious mind, to show me what is true, kind and timely. If what you want to show me is too intense for me to integrate, please transfer it to my conscious mind in a way I can integrate it, for instance as a symbol, a signal in my body, a next step I can take. Most importantly, show me first my capacity to be peaceful, and then, the content of my wounding.”

When you experience resistance: “What are you protecting me from? What would help you trust me 1% more?”

When you want to ask your inner child something: “What do you want me to know? What do you need me to do today so that you are not alone in dealing with it?”

When you want to bring in your Protector: “What would a minimally competent adult do here, in the external world, so that the system is safe?”

When you want to ask your leader or guru self: “What direction is the most in alignment with truth, love, life and all beings?”

When you want to listen to your intuition: “I ask my intuition to give me a signal, my analytical mind to decipher it, my body to give me feedback as to whether my deciphering is correct and reality to give me feedback as to whether my deciphering is correct.”

When you want to know if a particular intuition you have is coming from your universal heart or from your programming: “I ask my wise subconscious mind to help me see if the intuition or impulse I have about …is coming from my universal heart or from my family, my religion, marketing, erotic programming, my political tribe, my trauma response or an old deal I did as a child to belong. If it’s coming from my programming, I ask the parts responsible for it to step back and soften only as much as is safe, so that I can have true guidance from my universal heart that will help me make my life better in the most simple ways possible. Let my body give me feedback as to the difference.”

Once you get an answer, you can ask: “What can I do in my ordinary life to prove that I heard correctly?” If it tells you to do something, I'd only do it if it's loving and leaves you free. It would have to make sense too. And even with all that, I wouldn't do anything big or hard to undo until I'm calm and I've checked it against what I actually know.

You use the prompt to tell your subconscious what you want in a language it can easily understand. The NLP in Appendix B below can help smooth the prompt's entry and reduce resistance. Used together properly, the subconscious can feel safer in giving its response. That doesn't make the answers any more true, though.

Appendix B: NLP without turning yourself into a sales seminar

NLP stands for Neuro-Linguistic Programming.

You do not need to know this stuff to use the main path, but if you want to freshen up on your skills in the art of working with imagery, body states, behavioral rehearsal, talking to yourself in effective sentences etc then it might be useful, as long as it helps the system listen. Using the techniques can be an easy way to fall into the trap of thinking that NLP is a bag of tricks rather than something that needs to be done with love and truth and contact and consent.

The four NLP layers

The four levels of NLP are:

Sensory Level of NLP: working with the form of an inner experience (where do you see or hear it, how far away, how big, how bright, how loud, how do you hold your body, where is the pressure etc.)

State Level of NLP: making useful states (warmth, adult presence, courage, love etc.) easier to get to.

Strategy level of NLP: practising a better response.

Language Level of NLP: pacing what is true; opening up people’s awareness; presuming only that you can do something safe; linking cause and effect carefully; embedding invitations; asking de-hypnotic questions to break the effects of vague, absolute, manipulative or grandiose language.

The Milton Model, minus the covert sales seminar

We are going to use a model that’s called the Milton model. It’s all to do with sentence architecture. When we tell ourselves to ‘be better’, ‘relax now’, ‘stop feeling anxious’ or ‘trust me’ it doesn’t work, does it?

What we need to do is use indirect language to go around the part that wants to argue with us, so that we can actually get to where we want to get to.

So let’s start with something we know to be true, let’s take the pressure off, presume that we can only do a tiny bit and let’s invite one thing:

‘As my body begins to notice the sensation of the bed underneath it, it may be possible for it to begin to realize that it is not alone in carrying the burden of the day.’

We paced it; we opened up awareness; we validated the reason for the reaction; we presumed we could do it; we used a bridge word to make it easier and we invited one thing. We can then test this out with the body to see if it was possible.

Pattern 1: Pacing and leading — truth before suggestion

So how might we pace and lead in a way that works with anxiety?

It might go something like this: ‘It is completely true that the sensation of ________ (or the emotion of _____) is coming up for me. It makes complete sense that my system is trying to react and respond to that by wanting to do or think ________. Whilst that is completely true, there is another thing that is equally true, which is that my adult self is here, breathing in the room right now, and wanting to listen to what comes up for me next.’

Truth, Invite, Truth, Invite etc. is all you need to do.

Pattern 2: Awareness verbs

Open up awareness by using awareness verbs e.g. notice, realize, recognize, sense, feel, wonder, imagine, picture, listen to, allow, discover, remember, consider etc.

Example: ‘Notice how the answer comes to you… maybe as an image, or a word… maybe as a pressure, or some warmth… or a silence, or a sense of what to do next.’

Pattern 3: Presuppositions

Presumption is a powerful thing (a presupposition hides an assumption inside a sentence, and the person hearing it usually takes that assumption on board), so please don’t use it except to presume that we will have the choice of when it will happen, that we will be able to do it, that we are safe, that we are loved and that our adult can lead us. Don’t ever presume obedience, the way a hypnotist does when they say something like, ‘As you go deeper, it gets easier and easier to just do what I say.’

Example: ‘Before any part has to change, it can first become more understood.’

Pattern 4: Gentle cause-effect bridges

Bridge words are words like ‘as, whilst, because, when, after, before, which means’ etc. These are words that link events. However, even if you say them in a lovely gentle voice it doesn’t mean that it’s true if it isn’t! It’s not very likely that just by breathing in and out your trauma is going to go away! So an honest bridge might sound more like, ‘As I breathe, my adult self may become present enough to hear a little of what the wound needs.’

Pattern 5: Embedded invitations

An embedded invitation places a small direction inside a larger sentence. For our purposes in ethical communion-style hypnosis, we don't use these to hide commands, but to smooth them into nudges without breaking the communion dialog rhythm: ie. "And I can allow myself to experience..."

or, "As I sit here, I may begin to allow myself to become aware..."

Or, something similar that you might use when working with inner child type of parts: "My little one doesn't have to trust me all at once; they can simply notice I'm still here."

You can use this, of course, but don't use ten of them one after the other! People will know that you've just come out of some weekend certification course and they won't feel so safe with you.

Pattern 6: Ethical double binds

A Manipulative Double Bind Question is rigged so that you wind up doing what the speaker wants, whatever answer you give.

An ethical double bind gives you two ways to go, and both of them are actually safe. Ethical Double Bind Questions might be:

"Would you prefer to allow a feeling of warmth to come over you now, or would you prefer a feeling of space?"

Pattern 7: Conversational postulates

An example of a Conversational Postulate (a yes-or-no question that works as a request) is:

"Can I allow the system to show me the next thing to do, without showing me all the details of wounding that are involved?"

Pattern 8: Tag questions

Similarly, you can use tag questions, like "isn't it?" or "can't it?" or "doesn't it?" or others, but be aware that these can get pretty oily pretty quickly, so don't overdo it.

"Your body knows how to relax, doesn't it?"

Pattern 9: Nominalizations

Nominalizations are words that turn a process into a noun, like 'healing', 'safety', 'trust', 'integration', 'communion', 'clarity', 'rest', etc. These are a bit vague, and sometimes that can be useful, because it allows the subconscious to fill in the blank with something very specific for that person.

"I can allow myself some safety, in whatever way my body knows how."

Pattern 10: Meta Model questions — the de-hypnosis tool

To 'Milton Model' is to open things up. To 'Meta Model' is to say "What do you mean by that?" In order to be ethical with people, you need to do both.

"What do you mean specifically? According to who? Compared to what? Always? Never? What specifically would happen if you did? How do you know that? What evidence could you have that would change your mind?"

When people are starting to get stiff in their bodies, 'Milton' it. When you feel like you're losing touch with the truth of what's going on, 'Meta' it.

Sensory, state, and strategy tools

Submodalities — editing the form, not denying the truth

If your fear, craving, fantasy, belief, memory, whatever, has sensory properties in your experience (it has a place in your visual field, or sounds a certain way, or is heavy, or moving, or inside / outside your body, or something like that), you can work with those properties. If there are no sensory properties that you can notice, don't try to create any -- just don't use this tool!

How is this represented for you? Can you allow yourself to move that image back a couple of feet? Can you soften the edges of that image? Can you turn the volume down on that voice in your head? Etc. What changes?

"And I can allow myself to know that I am not deleting the message, just turning down the volume so that I am able to be present enough to hear what it is."

And, always ask the guard if it's ok to do this.

"Is it ok to move that image back a couple of feet and turn the volume down a bit, so that I can be present enough to hear what you need me to hear?"

Anchoring — giving the body a doorway back

There are many cues you can connect to a state. Put your hand on your heart, touch two fingers together, use a breath, a word, a particular body posture, smell, song, whatever. Feel a state that you have got to in reality that you want to be able to get to easily when you need to. Do your anchor thing and say to yourself that this is available to you. Do make sure you do it when you clearly feel that state. To get yourself back into a good hypnosis state easily, don’t go for a peak emotional state but for a usable, receptive one. See the main induction for a quick way to get back into hypnosis. Do make sure this is different from both your alert and calm cue and your synchronicity wake up cue.

Using synchronicity as your own wake-up bell

I call this the synchronicity wake up cue.

People often ask what the synchronicity means. Sometimes they get a great insight from it, but other times they get obsessed trying to work out patterns etc. I am using the noticing of the synchronicity to WAKE UP. It doesn't have to mean anything.

It is my cue to: Stop, orient, go wider, reality check, listen, do at least one thing from a loving place.

Whenever I notice it I use it as a cue for waking up inside whatever state I am in, feeling my body, feeling the room I'm in, remembering that what I'm noticing doesn't confirm what I'm reading into the coincidences, widening my state so that I can see beyond what my current state is showing me. I then look for the truth of what is going on (What is really going on, what is just my interpretation? What needs warmth? What needs protecting? What do I need to do?) and I go into the highest state I can get into, where I can see what is going on in that situation as objectively and clearly as I can, from the highest perspective I can get to, whilst remaining in my body, not leaving the facts of the situation and not leaving my regular human state. From that state I feel my feelings. If the state I started in is negative, I check if it is a message from my Inner Child or from a childhood guard part, or if I just need to grieve. I check if it is a message from my physical body (if there is a problem with my body that I need to take care of) or if I need to do something about the situation. From that place I choose what to do. I choose at least one thing to do that is from a loving place. I allow myself to be in whatever state is real for me, which may include objectivity, clarity, ease, peace, freedom, joy, gratitude, etc.

This way I don't go into identification but I still hear the message of the state if it's negative.

Installing the synchronicity cue

How to install Synchronicity cues:

Mentally rehearse the following: Imagine that you have noticed a synchronicity that is obviously harmless.

Feel the "whoa" of stimulation that you just got from noticing it.

  1. Use that stimulation to feel your feet, your body, your breathing, your room.

  2. Reiterate to yourself that whenever you notice a synchronicity, it is just a bell that rings to wake you up. It doesn’t confirm what you are reading into it.

  3. Ask yourself: "What does my Nurturer think about this?", "What does my Protector think about this?", "What does my Guide think about this?"

  4. Rehearse going back to a more grounded state and to a more wide view of the situation.

Rehearse yourself taking at least one action. Rehearse yourself waking up completely.

These cues do not authorize you to make a decision. They do not stop you from hearing the message of your intuition if it is warning you about something.

This is very similar to implementation intention (an if...then... plan). I'm not aware of research into implementation intention for synchronicity cues, but the general mechanisms appear to work; Gollwitzer’s paper explains how and why they work.

False positives, and turning the cue off

A downside of synchronicity cues is that they can lead to false positives. We create an association in our head on purpose when we rehearse what to do when we notice a synchronicity; however, when you are absorbed in something and/or when you are destabilized, you might think that you saw a synchronicity when in fact there wasn't any. Research has linked apophenia, the tendency to see patterns that aren't really there, with psychosis spectrum traits (see Blain and colleagues, and Grant and colleagues). That isn't saying that noticing patterns is bad. In fact, it's not a pathology.

If you feel that synchronicity cues are making these things worse (e.g. you feel like you have to look for them all the time, you can't sleep, you feel anxious, you are sure that random things happen in your life in order to tell you something, you can't tell the difference between noticing something and interpreting what it might mean, etc.), you should deactivate synchronicity cues.

You can do that by not rehearsing them anymore and by saying to yourself (when you are fully awake) that you don't want to use synchronicity as a cue anymore (e.g. "From now on, I am no longer going to use noticing synchronicity as a cue for waking up. I let go of this association, coincidences can go back to being just coincidences."). Saying this is just you deciding, it doesn't mean the association is gone straight away.

Reframing — changing the meaning without lying

Reframes are dishonest when they declare the problem was something that was good all along ("It's good nobody loves me, because I'm happier alone"). Instead ask yourself: What else might be true? or What is this part trying to do for me? or How might my Nurturer reframe this? or How might my Protector reframe this? or How might my Guide reframe this? etc., etc. So we might arrive at, "If it seems like nobody loves me, could it be a part of a vicious cycle in which I expect that, and my expectations are met?"

Perceptual positions — self, other, witness, universal heart

I go through four positions: 1) seeing from Self (what do I feel from inside myself?), 2) then seeing from Other (if I sit inside the other person, what might they be feeling or protecting?), 3) then seeing from Witness (if I watched this from across the room, what would I notice?), and 4) then seeing from Universal Heart (what serves truth, love, boundaries, and the good of everyone involved?). I do this to see the situation with more accuracy.

I will not use this to rationalize bad behavior, but only to break out of a prison of perception.

Future pacing — let the future test the insight

Once I have seen the situation more clearly, I will imagine future scenarios where my old behavior would have triggered, to test out if my new insight is working.

If these scenarios come up, I will choose new small behaviors using an Implementation Intention (If...then... statement)

The Swish, softened

I use an NLP technique called a "Swish Pattern" (it swaps a picture of an old reaction for a picture of a new one), but I will allow my old pattern to be there, while also holding my new pattern in my awareness. I feel what comes up somatically, and compare the differences between the future scenarios of my old and new patterns.

Parts integration, without pretending the conflict is solved

I use parts integration to ask my conflicting parts what they want, what they are afraid of, what their need is, and see if I can find a 3rd option that satisfies both parts' needs, without either part dominating my behavior.

What I will not use NLP for

Here is a list of things I do not do with NLP, to myself or to others:

  • I do not use NLP to talk myself out of a real signal.

  • I won’t tell anyone to override their fear without first asking what it’s protecting.

  • I do not use NLP for memory retrieval.

  • I do not use NLP to manipulate other people or convince myself an intuition is true.

  • I do not use NLP to remove pain, hunger, grief, anger, or medical symptoms.

  • I do not use NLP on people who are panicked, destabilized, manic, having a psychosis-like state, or are too high/dissociated to reality test.

  • I do not use NLP to make people feel like their no is resistance, or like they are spiritually inferior to me if they disagree with me.

  • I do not use covert commands in my language.

  • I do not use NLP to create urgency where time is needed.

I do not use NLP on people without their consent (the idea that "your subconscious will let me know if you want me to do this" is NOT consent).

I do not use NLP to get compliance from people in a suggestible state (in trance, tired, attracted to me, feeling shame or grief, or chemically altered).

Running the whole NLP sequence

Here is a rough outline of the entire NLP process:

  1. Induce or contain yourself (gather your attention, or stabilize yourself first if you are already wide open).

  2. Ask yourself for permission: is it useful to work with this image, feeling or pattern today?

  3. Get a representation of your pattern (image, feeling, size, color, location, movement, etc.).

  4. Find out what that part/pattern is protecting for you.

  5. Gently change some aspect of your pattern (location, volume, space, posture, speed, etc.).

  6. Bring in your 3 good adults (Nurturer to warm, Protector to make it safe, and Guide to point a direction). In real life they’re all part of the one inner adult, so we can imagine them splitting apart if need be and coming together as well.

  7. Anchor your good state (heart, fingers, breath, posture, etc.).

  8. Future pace a scenario where your old pattern would have triggered, and do a small new behavior (If...then... statement).

  9. Check in with your body for changes, ask whether the new behavior is kind and doable, and ask what supports your new interpretation of the situation beyond how it feels.

Get yourself out of the altered state (come back to the room and do something normal).

Appendix C: The extended de-hypnosis checklist

What to do when you feel like you’re being pushed too hard by something (person, ad, ideology, spiritual teaching, romantic attraction, internal voice, etc.): Say to yourself, “Okay, something is trying to influence me here. I don’t have to obey it. I don’t even have to attack it. I can just slow myself down and ask some questions like, 'What would this thing influence me to believe that isn’t true in order to get what it wants from me? What is my body telling me to do about this (how does my body feel)? What would love do here? What would truth do here? How would I approach this humbly? What would life have me do here?'”

If what you’re investigating is true, it can take your questioning (as the police like to say). If it’s love, it can accommodate your boundaries. If it’s real guidance, it’ll still be there tomorrow. If it’s really for you, it won’t want you to give up all the wisdom and sovereignty you’ve worked so hard to develop.

Before you do what it wants you to do, check how it’s trying to get you there. Is it rushing you, or telling you to keep things secret? Is it shaming you, or threatening to leave you out if you don’t do what it says? Maybe it makes you feel special, or there’s a sexual charge to it, or a sense that you’re spiritually above everyone else.

Then look at what it wants from you. Is it money, or sex? Your loyalty? Is it asking you to keep quiet, or to obey it?

Keep in mind though, people who mean well can ask for some of these too. A teacher charges for their time, and your partner probably wants your loyalty. If you can say no to them and they’re fine with it, that’s a good sign.

Back to the interrogation:

Is it trying to narrow down your choices? Does questioning it feel like you’re doing something wrong, or does it act like not obeying is a problem you need to fix? Does normal life start to seem boring, stupid or irrelevant when it’s around? Is it trying to seduce, scare or rescue your inner child? And does it try to stop your adult self from saying no?

If it does a bunch of these things, slow yourself down, don’t let it push you deeper into trance, don’t do anything you can’t undo, and then ground yourself for a while (take care of your body, eat, sleep, give it time, get counsel, etc).

A good guide will make your adult self stronger. A good guide can take no. A bad guide can’t take no. A bad guide wants to take your adult self’s job. A bad guide will try to make your no something that’s bad or needs to be fixed (resistance, ego, fear, low vibration, trauma response, sin, need for more of its guidance, etc).

Hypnosis-specific audit

Before accepting a hypnotic recording or live suggestion, ask these important questions to avoid being manipulated:

  • What induction mechanics are being used to bring me under the spell?

  • Can my responses lead to pausing or modifying the script, or stopping & coming to full alertness?

  • Does the hypnotist treat my lack of awareness of what's going on as some kind of proof that the process worked?

  • Is the hypnotist using identity roles, obedience ladders, age regressions, or desires for/assumptions of belonging to lower my adult self's better judgment?

  • Keep in mind though, the answer to that last one requires either checking the script first, or explaining what your adult judgement would accept, as the hypnotist can't be expected to know that.

  • Continuing with the job interview:

  • Are fractionation, cues, or other methods of affecting my memory or modifying my post-session behavior disclosed before they are used?

  • Is the hypnotist implicitly turning my bodily sensations into evidence for a prior assumption?

When we're ready to exit hypnosis, will they give me a clear statement that the session has ended, and would I get back my normal waking (and walking) identity? Will I be able to use my critical judgement abilities as before?

When any answer to these questions is unclear, beware. Ask for the exact script. If they don't want to give it, change the method, or walk right out that door.

Appendix D: Anxiety — body first


Remember that often the anxiety comes from a childhood guard part of you. It’s what protects you when your inner adult’s Protector isn’t functioning, the same as in childhood. It’s trying to help, making a guess about what’s coming: that you are in danger, or may be shamed, or abandoned, or fail, or be exposed, or be sick, or be rejected, or lose control, or feel something it believes would be unbearable. It can be right! It’s probably been wrong before too, and you know some of those times. Therefore, don’t start by interrogating that part. Help your body first.

Always work with yourself in a series – first your body, then your inner child, then some inquiry, then some action:

Reduced breathing / Buteyko-style breathing

Work on your reduced breathing / Buteyko breathing. (NOTE: there are some good things to be said for slowing your breathing down, if you are breathing too much, or panicked. However, please don’t hold your breath until you are air hungry, which can make you more anxious in the short term.) Breathe in small amounts through your nose and let it out quietly, through your nose too. Only pause if that's comfortable. Can you make your breathing very quiet? If you feel bad, dizzy, or panicky, or have medical issues, STOP!

EFT tapping

One of the easiest ways to do EFT is to say something like, “Even though I have this anxiety, I am willing to be kind to the part of me that is afraid,” while you tap gently on your body and stay with what you feel. There isn’t a ton of evidence for EFT, but there are some studies on anxiety. A 2025 systematic review found 7 randomized controlled trials for anxiety disorders, and noted that EFT was more effective than no intervention in several of them. So use the evidence with caution, and know that there are first-line therapies for anxiety.

Somatic Experiencing

If you would like more information about somatic experiencing, or even a free intro session, please see my somatic guide. In somatic experiencing (SE), you are not digging into the trauma. You learn to track. So you might start by tracking, by discovering where the activation is, where a neutral place is, and then whether you can move your attention from one to the other, very slowly, like a snail.

Gendlin Focusing

Focusing is another approach where you work with your body. You might say to yourself, “What is the felt sense of this whole anxiety?” Don’t answer with your head; let your body come up with a vague sense of the whole anxiety. Then ask, “What would this felt sense like me to know?” Try a word or a phrase and see how well it fits the felt sense. But also see if what the word or phrase says is true of the situation. The word may feel ok, but say something that doesn’t fit the situation, or vice versa. Use the words and the body to mutually correct one another.

Body supports

Work with the body. Sometimes the mind is too activated for meditation to help, so you may need to work directly with the body to calm it down. Maybe get a massage, or massage a foot, your scalp, a temple, or the back of the neck, the top of a shoulder, etc. (Avoid massaging the front of the neck. Also avoid massaging injured or numb body parts, or bruised, inflamed, or recently surgically operated on areas, or varicose veins. Also stop if the massage makes the anxiety symptoms worse, or makes you feel bad in any way, such as painful, dizzy, tingly, etc.) You’ve got lots of other options for the body too, like heat, pressure, a weighted blanket, darkness, your favorite calming music, or a massage gun if you’re careful with it, etc. Use whatever actually helps you, and don’t go surrounding the anxiety with a heap of wellness stuff.

But neither one of those products is necessary. Inner child love

Here is my basic inner child love practice technique. Put your hand over your heart and your other hand on your solar plexus, and say to yourself in a loving way, “Little one, you are not alone. You don’t have to fix this problem. I’m here now to listen to you.” Make sure you're actually talking to your inner child, not just repeating a script. If you can't genuinely say all that and feel it, then just rest your hands there and notice what you find.

Anxiety script

A script you could use for anxiety is:

Where are my body parts? What are my feet doing? How is my body positioned in the chair? How is my body breathing? How is my body situated in the room?

If you’re there, little one, I’m here. Thank you, guard, for letting me know you’re here. You didn’t have to scream. I got your message.

If there is something we need to do, let me know what the first thing is that we need to do at this moment. If there is nothing to do, help my body stand down. I’m not going to abandon you, but I’m not going to worship you either. I will listen to what you have to say and reality test it and then take some action.

Appendix E: Is the daydream helping or replacing your life?

Sometimes daydreaming is something that your subconscious mind is doing to help you. It is simulating an idea or event to help you work through grief or teach you something. The very act of daydreaming may be helping you to access your creativity or allowing your subconscious to show you a potential future. Sometimes your subconscious will show you things that you may be afraid of. Sometimes it will show you what you want.

Other times daydreaming may be taking you away from your life in a detrimental way. It may be that you are escaping reality and are addicted to fantasies rather than real life. You may be dissociating or obsessing about romantic partners or escaping from being an adult.

How do you know which it is? Simply ask yourself if the daydreaming is taking you closer to life, with more clarity, love and ability to take action or if it is taking you away from life, in a repetitive and craving way.

If it is helping you then simply ask yourself what the guidance is and how you can use it.

If it is taking you away from life then change your body position by sitting upright, open your eyes, feel your feet and ask your subconscious to help you. Simply say to yourself, “Dear subconscious thank you for showing me images. If what you are showing me is helpful then please also show me the guidance in it. If what you are showing me is a way of avoiding dealing with life then please show me in a gentle but structured way how to deal with life as it is right now.”

Then ask yourself what it is that you need to do in real life that you are avoiding by daydreaming.

You may need to send a text message or stop sending text messages. You may need to clean up your room, eat, sleep, deal with grief, create, apologize, leave, tell the truth, make a plan, get help, get back to a task that you were supposed to be doing, stop chasing love that is not available to you etc.

Appendix F: Self-trust, outside guidance, and oracle-like states

[Please see how to use synchronicity as a cue, not an oracle, above.]

One of the issues here is that people vary dramatically in how suggestible they are, both internally and externally, and different people vary in how suggestible they are in different states. Different types of suggestion (commands, imagery, music, metaphors, spiritual, etc) can be very different for different people. So there are definite advantages to both having someone work with you (pacing, containment, another set of eyes), and doing things yourself. Doing it yourself means being able to follow up immediately, to correct something in real time, to have a line of communication with guards, etc.

That said, self hypnosis may be the best way of talking to your subconscious on a day to day basis. If you’re dealing with particularly complicated trauma, blind spots, dissociation, destabilization, or relational wounds that need another nervous system, skilled outside support may be best.

Finding a hypnotist who can work with you

Ask them:

  • What they are trained in (what they can treat)

  • How they use hypnosis

  • What they will do if things don't fit

  • If they are treating trauma, whether they have specific training in trauma and dissociation (having hypnosis certifications matters, but doesn't mean they are trained in trauma care)

Tell them:

  • What has/hasn't worked for you in the past

  • How direct you want them to be

  • What kind of touch (if any) you are ok with

  • What kind of imagery (if any) you are ok with

  • What you want them to do if you want them to stop

Ask them:

  • What they will do if something unexpected happens during or after the session

  • What they do if you aren't sure if something is a memory or not

  • How they will do the full return (coming out of hypnosis) at the end of the session

You should be able to be firmly encouraged, but not forced to continue if you don't want to.

Then afterwards, check in about what was actually done, regardless of what you might call the method. Ask the person why they did what they did, and whether they told you in advance. Did you agree to it? How did the person respond to you? Keep in mind that just because something made you uncomfortable, it doesn't mean it was bad, but if they saw your discomfort as meaning that you needed more of it, that might be something to look into. You might even be able to use this app or ask around to see if someone can tell you what the things the person did meant. They probably won't be able to tell you whether the person was certified in those methods, though. Then you can decide if you want to try learning to do self-hypnosis, using both, or find someone else.

When the subconscious speaks like an oracle

Your subconscious is amazing at picking up on things your conscious mind isn't getting. Whether it's anticipations, reading patterns, or just picking up on uncanny things, it's good to respect what your subconscious is picking up on. But just because your subconscious gives you an image or tells you to do something, that doesn't mean you need to treat it like an order.

Especially if it's a big thing, or something that can't be easily undone, slow it down to the scale of its consequences. Look at it with your adult's eyes, and take it to council.

The council — internal first, then outward

Ask your child, your adult, your conscious mind, your subconscious mind, your intuition, your analytical mind. But if it's bigger than all of those, ask outside of yourself, too. The bigger and more un-undoable something is, the more you want to reach outside.

Ask people who love you, people who are like you, people who have done what you are thinking of doing. Sometimes it is right to go it alone, but I would suggest making it the last step, after you've tried to get counsel.

On the other hand, it is possible to give your power away. Seeking counsel can slide into abdicating to an authority figure without you noticing. Another person doesn’t get automatic trust, or automatic dismissal.

Sometimes you need the support of your peers. In Hearthwork, the concept is that you give each other equal time, warmth, confidentiality, and acceptance of “no” as an answer, and attention without interpretation, advice, or fixing. You lend each other the Nurturer and Protector for a while, but you don’t act as each other’s Guide. Then you reverse roles. Sometimes, with a question asked carefully and at the right time, your peers can help you hear your own Guide more clearly, but they don’t interpret what you experience.

Forgiveness without bypass

We cannot force forgiveness on ourselves. It has to come from a place of understanding, otherwise it can be bypassing. However, if we remain in the anger forever, we are allowing the injury to dictate how we live our lives in the present. So how do we hold all of this? We just remember that it can all be true at once. They really did hurt us and they still have to answer for it, and our boundary with them still matters, but one day we may find that we have forgiven them, or at least that we’re not being run by the resentment anymore. And we don’t have to forgive first to get safe, set boundaries, hold someone accountable, get justice or get better.

A helpful understanding to come to is that while their actions were harmful, in a sense, they could only make the moves they made, given their accumulated nature at the time (conditioning, history, pain, capacity, etc). This doesn’t negate that harm was caused, and it doesn’t stop you from holding someone accountable, allowing for consequences, working on making different moves now, etc. It simply helps us come to a place where we aren’t expecting something from someone that they can’t give. It doesn’t make their limitations matter any less.

“You don’t have to forgive before you’re ready. I won’t use spirituality to silence your pain. And I won’t build our whole life around this wound forever.”

Some questions that may be helpful to ask when coming to a place of forgiveness, while still holding all of the above:

What actually happened? What was the impact? What boundaries need to be set? What shaped the other person(s) into someone capable of that harm? What is theirs? What is mine? What does accountability require? How can I let this go, without denying any of the above?

Forgiving yourself

For self-forgiveness, name what you did, but don't take responsibility for what wasn't yours. Let yourself feel the remorse and take in the harm, or how it went against your values, and stop short of making yourself a permanent criminal.

Be specific about the action you took, and the impact it had, feel the remorse, do what you can to rectify or keep it from happening again. Then you're allowed to stop holding yourself hostage, and the responsibility stays. Every so often, come back to how you got to be someone who could do that, until you understand it well enough that it changes how you feel.

Appendix G: Altered states

This is NOT to say that you SHOULD use substances, but many people have used them when they first did self-hypnosis, contacted their inner child, or talked to their Higher Self. I’d learn the inner child work sober first, with enough Nurturer and Protector in the room before you go deep (see the inner child reparenting section). Borrowing them is fine, as long as it’s from someone steady who won’t try to run your trip for you.

Do not use altered states to try to FORCE yourself to get to a certain revelation about yourself, but rather to help yourself become more in tune with love and to better your life.

A lot of people have had dramatic openings to love at some point in their life. If you've had a similar experience, you know that there is a difference between having experienced it at one point, or only being able to access it in a certain state, and being able to get to it at any point in your life. While you're building a way to reach love on a normal day, let that experience you had serve as a reference point for what can happen. (If you can't give that love to the child, try to deal with why you can't, instead of trying to ramp up the experience. Often there's a child guard part that’s seen love come with strings or get revoked before in a painful way. Love starts with hearing out the resistance to it first.)

Use your altered state of consciousness, either while you're in it or after, to help yourself by asking things like:

Help me to become more grounded. Help me to only take what I can integrate. Help me to remember love. Help me to come back when it's time. Help me to figure out how to change one thing in my life to make it better.

DO NOT use self-hypnosis to try to keep the altered state of consciousness going indefinitely, to try to recover memories, to make yourself MORE afraid, to help you not need to sleep, to help you not need to take care of medical problems, to help you become LESS communicative, etc.

In other words, if using self-hypnosis while you're in an altered state of consciousness is NOT helping you become MORE loving, MORE grounded, MORE honest with yourself, helping you SLEEP, become MORE communicative, MORE able to do your everyday responsibilities, etc, then stop trying to go DEEPER into your altered state of consciousness, and start working on integrating what you've already gotten from it.

Stabilize before you interpret

Self-hypnosis can be POWERFUL when you are in an altered state of consciousness (e.g. when you're on loveyhuasca, meditating, have a lucid dream, are dealing with grief, are dealing with sexual energy, are praying, haven't slept, are around plant medicine, etc, etc, etc). Sometimes you may OPEN yourself up to things FASTER than you are able to put them into context with your adult self.

So what feels like a meaningful altered state of consciousness may or may not actually be meaningful - it could be your inner child, it could be a childhood guard part, it could be all of the things you're ashamed of / sad about / angry about / etc, coming out to be dealt with. Or it could be that your body is overloaded, your environment is chaotic, you haven't slept enough, etc, etc, etc. Regardless of how scary an image is, regardless of how amazing the lighting was on the "download", regardless of how bad you feel, it doesn't necessarily mean what you think it does!

Before you even THINK about interpreting what your altered state of consciousness is, ask yourself something simpler:

“What kind of problem is happening?”

Appendix H: Demons, jinn, hostile presences, and “Unattached Burdens”

I speak here from experience.

When you’re doing hypnosis, meditation, lucid dreaming, loveyhuasca-ing, going through a period of intense grief, praying, or whatever else may alter your consciousness, it’s possible you may run into something that you perceive to be a demon, a jinn, a parasitic energy, or whatever other dark entity or terrifying archetype you may encounter – it may be a voice or an image, and it doesn’t just feel like an emotion or a younger part of you, but something that feels intrusive, hateful, seductive, mocking, etc.

First of all, it doesn’t matter if they’re demons or not. They may be literal demons, for all you know. Or they may be manifestations of your mind, burdens you’re carrying from previous relationships, whatever. What matters is what is the best way to deal with them, so you end up safer, clearer, more loving, more grounded and less hooked.

Start with the room, the body, and basic safety

So what do you do when you encounter a demon, or a jinn, or something else that’s bad and intrusive?

First of all, do the usual things – make sure you’re not in danger from a medical standpoint, check your environment, check your body, make sure you’re not overstimulating yourself, make sure you’re not opening your mind too much. If you need to, open your eyes, ground yourself in your body and your environment, remove yourself from any bad influence you’re surrounding yourself with, stop stimulating your mind. Don’t go further into whatever it is you’re doing – don’t be curious, don’t try to figure out what it is.

Tell yourself, “If this can be integrated in this moment, it’s welcome. If not, it can wait until later. The adult part of me is still here, and my body can return to safety.”

If you feel like you’re in any danger of doing anything harmful to yourself or others, if you feel like you’re going psychotic and you’re afraid of it, if you feel like you won’t be able to sleep when the self-hypnosis session is over, if you feel overly paranoid, if you feel physical pain in your chest, if you feel like you’re going to have a seizure, if you feel like you’re too hot in a bad way, if you feel like you’ve taken too much of something, if you feel like you won’t be able to function in the world anymore – then the self-hypnosis session is over, and you should get help in the real world.

A response check, not a verdict about what it is

If, however, you feel like you’re stable enough to stay present, check in with yourself and see what’s best to do. Put your hand on your heart if that feels okay, breathe comfortably, and stay aware of the room.

Internalize the fact that you don’t have to know what it is. Find out if it helps to be warm towards it or to keep it at arm’s length, and keep your boundary either way. Keep in mind that you don’t have to do what it tells you to do.

If you feel vulnerable, if you feel tightness in your throat, if you feel sadness or fear or like you’re trying to protect something, call upon your Nurturer. If you don’t feel better, or if it continues being hostile towards you, stop trying to deal with it and return to your body and the room.

You don’t have to find out if it’s a guard immediately, and you may not be able to comfort it quickly. Just keep an eye on whether the way you’re responding to it helps you get clearer and steadier and less hooked.

Do not fight from fear

Your first instinct may be to fight it, banish it, hate it, show it that you’re more powerful than it is, argue with it, panic, become your own threatened self in fear of it, etc. Some people may want to use traditional prayer or mantras or protection or banishing or exorcism techniques, and those may well work for them. But if you’re fighting it out of fear, it can backfire:

It becomes a center of attention if you fear it, and if you hate it you give it a hook in you. (If you’re proud you give it an opponent, and if you panic you give it your nervous system.)

So the next time it comes up you can just say, “I see that there is no reason to hate and revile what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to merge with or become what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to obey what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to try to defeat what I am experiencing out of fear. I choose to return my attention to loving awareness.”

A bodily boundary: compassion present, door closed

You can also tell the presence that while you have compassion for it, the door is closed.

This is especially a good idea if the presence you’re experiencing seems even the slightest bit hostile, cold, alien, parasitic, etc. It’s not really helpful to approach it with the kind of soft, sentimental love that you’d use if you were inviting it to stay for tea, but it can be useful to approach it with love while also making it clear that it needs to respect your boundaries.

Sit up, make sure your feet are grounded on the floor, straighten your spine, pull your shoulder blades back a little, open your chest a little, feel the boundary of your torso’s skin, feel the perimeter of your body, and tell the presence, “While I have compassion for you and any suffering you may be experiencing, this suffering needs to go back where it came from. The boundary of my skin is the boundary of an adult now, and you’re not welcome to come inside the house anymore.”

If it might be psychosis or destabilization

This is assuming that you are having a symbolic experience of “hostile presences” which responds to grounding and love. It may be that what you are experiencing is actually psychotic or a form of destabilization.

If you feel like you are under attack for multiple days at a time, if you are not able to sleep, if you are not able to function normally, if you are hearing voices telling you to do things, if you feel you need to hurt yourself or somebody else, if you feel you are going manic, if you feel you can’t communicate normally, if you feel you can’t distinguish between what you are experiencing symbolically and normal reality, get help immediately. It’s not something to deal with in hypnosis.

The General Rule

The General Rule is: if you find that what you're doing is making you more loving, more grounded, more relational, more honest, more functional, then it's probably OK to keep doing it gently. But if what you're doing is making you more paranoid, sleepless, grandiose, isolated, scared, or more and more unreal, then DON'T GO DEEPER! GET HELP!

The cosmic-download humility test

Presences, shadow forces, or grandiose parts don’t always attack through fear, either. They may also try to give you a sense that you’re special, try to “download cosmic info” to you, try to give you a special mission, make you feel doing the dishes is beneath you, etc. You can just say, “Thank you for the imagery, but I don’t need your grand performance. Unless the info you’re trying to download to me can help me do the dishes, clean my room, be more honest, be more helpful, be more loving, keep my promises, take care of my body by resting and eating etc, I don’t need it.”

Guidance worth keeping should make ordinary life more beautiful, honest, relational, humble, reliable, and useful to other people.

Don’t become an entity hobbyist

Finally, some of you may be tempted to make this an area of hobbyism – to become interested in demons or jinn or hostile presences or Unattached Burdens or whatever. I just don’t want you to do that. They all lead to the same thing, which is how to maintain a presence of love without giving up your boundaries, while also making your mind harder to hook.

Appendix J: A practical induction library

Preview the sequence. Work out in advance which one you want to do and what you want to use it for (pick just one) and whether or not you want to do it at all! Any of these families of induction can be done as light inductions, with eyes open, or you can just do them as a no-trance inner contact.

Once you start an induction, you will want to notice what’s going on (even if nothing changes) and work out what to do next: move, imagine something else, change the sensory channel you’re using, do nothing, or stop the induction sequence and do something else… Whatever you do, make sure you bring yourself fully back to waking at the end of an induction.

NB: if you can’t ‘see’ the images you’re trying to visualize, that’s fine, change them into words, rhythm, spatial relations, something to touch or move or just plain knowing…

1. Stretch and release

Work out what will be supporting you, and focus on the feeling of support. You can also focus on where one of your hands or feet is. Either actually, or in your imagination, stretch a little bit, either stretching that part of your body slightly or pressing gently down into the support, and then let go of the effort, just relax. See if there is any difference (it’s fine if there isn’t). Do this for no more than 3 places on your body. Let releasing guide you as you go through the sequence. Which bit of your body, or inner voice, needs this? What do you need to support it? Bring your body back to normal movement and return.

2. Supported floating

Work out what is supporting you and where your weight is. Imagine that the support is holding a tiny bit (1%) more of your effort. It’s like floating but you don’t have to lose contact, or you can just keep it at the same level, with just enough weight/pressure. See what happens. You can fractionate this if you want, perhaps noticing your normal awareness when you breathe in and the support when you breathe out. Tell yourself you are going to fractionate before you do it. What can you hear more easily now? Keep the support, come back to your normal weight and firm contact, and return.

3. Rhythmic breath or pulse

Use your breathing or a slight hum in your throat for a natural rhythm. You could also tap your finger, or follow a sound outside. You can even do it by counting to yourself (silently) or with no rhythm at all. Say “here, and here again” over and over, and notice what you pay attention to in between the phrases. Then stop saying it. Notice what you become aware of in the first beat – you don’t need to make your mind blank, just notice what comes up. Does it make any words, feelings, needs or actions more clear? Bring back your normal rhythm and return.

4. Unwinding object

Unwinding an object is good for when you have a few different strands to one thing you want to work on. Imagine an object unwinding – maybe some yarn, some bubbles, or some knots that you place beside you. Once you’ve placed them all, give yourself a minute to look at them all. You don’t have to change anything, but you can label them if you need to (just a word or two). How many did you put down? Which one do you need to work on, and what does it need – warmth, protection, action? Keep one strand in your attention and put the others somewhere you can see them and return.

5. Sensory journey

Pay attention to the sounds in the room you’re in and how your body feels, then imagine yourself walking somewhere. Where do you want to go? Listen to the sounds of your footsteps and take in your surroundings as you walk. What do objects feel like if you touch them? What landmarks do you see? When you feel you have gathered enough of your attention, imagine the scenery changing until you are in a place where you can talk to whoever you need to (or you can just cut to the chase, if you prefer). Pay attention to something without worrying about what it means. Allow yourself to be curious about what’s going to happen next, but don’t make it a “what are they going to do to you?” kind of thing. When you are ready to finish, imagine yourself walking back the way you came and close the space.

6. Gradual wave

Start with a very small neutral area, feel into it a little and then imagine a wave of warmth, steadiness, or attention spreading out to a new area that is still ok. Sense into the new area (or continue using the old). See if there is anything different at each step. Does the body want any contact, space, words, other care? Let the mental image go, sense your body, sense your contact points, come back.

7. Care workshop

Prepare the workshop (time, place, tools, boundaries, etc). Invite the signal/feeling/young part to come only as close as is welcome. Try to avoid any language that makes you feel you're not one person. Care for it as best you can, by asking what it wants and giving that if you can, or sensing into it and responding, or just being with it. Take breaks, get back in contact if that's ok, take more breaks, etc. Keep bringing in adult functions (or borrow someone else's function). Have the adult part of you run the show. The goal is to make it "good as you" rather than "good as new", not to erase history. Tell your little one, "That's enough for today. We're going back to the life we're building." Close the workspace, reassure yourself that you are one whole person, that you're an adult now, and come back.

8. Resource embodiment

Pick a resource quality you understand (warmth, boundary, steadiness, curiosity, or dignity). Imagine yourself accessing 1% more of that quality (or, if you prefer, just observe it rather than embody it). What is different? How is your body, voice, etc? Do this a few times to strengthen it. Come back, keeping the quality and a positive action that you would do with it, but leaving the imagined you behind, saying "I am me".

9. No-trance inner contact

For the no trance inner contact, I would follow something along the lines of these steps:

For pacing (warm-up), notice reality as it is now: what you can see, what day of the week it is, that you can read or that you still have your feet, etc. Hopefully your name didn't suddenly change to Larry.

For the suggestion: in your mind, ask a clear question and then listen gently.

Now keep your sense of agency during the answer phase. You might notice an answer popped up, or you might decide you don't know. You can always take a break or rephrase the question. Sometimes you might ask advice from someone else.

Keep aware of the subtle inner signal: write whatever you get. It might be a word or sensation. An impulse or objection. Sometimes there's no signal, just silence, itself a kind of signal. Don't jump to interpreting it as validation of some theory.

Hand off the role: have your adult self answer that same question in whatever way is appropriate, preferably by writing or doing.

Integration/exit: reality test the signal you noticed. As part of testing, if it seems appropriate, you can do something simple based on it.

There's no elaborate exit protocol here since you didn't go into trance. Just close the exercise and carry on with your day.

Inner-child care and adult capacities

For information about borrowed adulthood, love that doesn't require trust, practical and relational repair, spiritual struggle, differentiation and mutual peer support, see my Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide. The practices in this post are designed to match with those in the guide, but include some elements of learning to get into self hypnosis, as well as other helpful language, and making sure you come back at the end, etc.

Some of the information in this post comes from Healing the Divided Self, Phillips and Frederick, 1995, pp 148-153, 171-174, about renurturing work, and how it can be combined with somatic work, and how to develop capacities for nurturing, etc. For information about attunement, and how every child is different and needs to be responded to differently, see Trauma Through a Child's Eyes, Levine and Maggie Kline, especially ch 4, 5, 10, etc, as it talks about actual kids (and this guide is applying this to how to respond to younger feeling states within an adult). The sequence for doing this with borrowed adulthood and the arrangement of 3 functions of adulthood, etc, is not from these authors, however.

Inner speech and putting experience into words

The study Not everybody has an inner voice (Nedergaard and Lupyan 2024) is not about self hypnosis adaptations, but about people with different amounts of inner speech. The questions in the quick start are a practical way of seeing for yourself how much inner speech you have.

Memory, support, and choosing a practitioner

Chapter 7 of Trauma and Memory, by Peter Levine, 2015, is useful on the difference between compelling experience and historical verification. This US Department of Veterans Affairs website contains useful general information about hypnosis, such as the fact that it can be very strong and should be carefully used, and that it may result in false memories. This ASCH website contains information about the usefulness of getting certified in hypnosis, and why it doesn't guarantee competence. Links are available for these and other useful groups in the evidence and specialist sections, but they point to their particular claims rather than validating every use of hypnosis.
