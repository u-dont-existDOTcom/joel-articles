## Let the response change the invitation

“My shoulders tightened” and “that wording feels wrong” are different observations. Tightening alone doesn’t explain itself. Ask what happened and whether the invitation fits. An explicit no needs no further justification; change the words, the pace, or the approach. Don’t repeat an unwelcome suggestion more persuasively.

I notice the tightness. I can leave it as it is for a moment. Does this invitation fit, or would I prefer something different?

Sometimes the sentence was simply wrong. Sometimes an unfamiliar possibility brings a feeling worth meeting. You don’t have to decide which before making space to find out. Skepticism is not automatically a protector, and silence is not proof that a hidden process is working.

## Look at the situation too

When another person is involved, notice what they actually do before making the whole problem an inner-child reaction. Can they hear disagreement, take responsibility, and make room for your experience? Keep the judgment specific and revisable; disagreement or a boundary alone doesn’t establish emotional immaturity. You don’t need repeated exposure to danger before protecting yourself.

Notice your side too. Guilt doesn’t prove wrongdoing or manipulation. Did you cause harm that needs repair, abandon your position to make their disappointment go away, or both? Choose a realistic purpose for the next interaction. Sometimes it is mutual understanding; sometimes it is conveying information, saying no, or ending the exchange. You don’t have to make someone understand before you can act.

## Change the amount of contact

When you need less, widen attention to the room, the chair, a sound, or somewhere in the body that is easier to meet. Let that support become noticeable alongside the difficulty. Moving between difficult and more manageable experience is often called pendulation; approaching in smaller amounts is titration. Use the actual response to set the pace, rather than counting compulsory cycles.

I can let this much be here and notice the support beside it.

Return to the difficult material only when you choose. If you’re losing track of the present, becoming increasingly unreal, or unable to shift or stop, end inward exploration and use the full return. Persistent disorientation needs human help rather than a stronger induction.

An impulse to turn, reach, push away, or change posture can also be explored through a small, voluntary, physically comfortable movement—or imagined without moving. Notice what follows. You’re not obliged to enact an impulse, shake, or produce a release. Stop movement that causes pain or concerning symptoms.

## Offer the care that is actually wanted

When a younger feeling appears, offer company without making it become literally younger or explain its history. When a protector is recognizable, listen to its concern without demanding that it step aside. No part is exiled from the meeting, and none has to disappear or trust you immediately.

I’m here. Would company help, or would you prefer some space?

“Don’t come closer” is information you can act on. Stay at the chosen distance and notice what receiving that response is like. The Nurturer can change the tone; the Protector can carry out a boundary; the Leader can point toward the life you want. They don’t each need a speech. When a capacity isn’t available, borrow that one function. Use the Witness only if you need it to begin; don’t restart a step you can already do.

You can also deliberately try support: a welcome hand placement, an imagined presence, words you’d like to hear, a different posture, or more distance. Introduce one change and discover its effect. A comforting-sounding resource earns its place by helping this person now.

Care can bring grief for what was missing. That needn’t mean the love failed. Stay with the response when you want to and can remain present; more tears don’t prove more healing. If this particular voice, closeness, touch, or image feels intrusive, change it before concluding that you can’t receive love.

A metaphor can develop before you decide what it means. “If this problem had its own image or story, what would appear—and what wants to happen next?” Try only the missing resource, boundary, distance, or action that seems useful. The result is symbolic information or a hypothesis, never proof of history.

Or spend the session developing love, play, courage, or curiosity. Let a pleasant experience have time to grow. Rehearse a chosen action and notice what it would take to carry it out. Hypnosis can invite something new as well as listen to what is already present.

## Finish without forcing a revelation

When you’ve received enough, stop opening new questions. Ask what would help you leave this here for now. Thank the parts or capacities you worked with, in whatever words you can mean. A meal, a boundary, rest, a repaired relationship, or a promise kept may matter more than another insight. There needn’t be an action when none is called for.

Keep the analytical mind available throughout: you can question, disagree, and stop. Save extended factual checking and consequential commitments for after the return. An image can be meaningful without being a historical memory; a bodily shift or relief doesn’t make an interpretation true.

Use Come all the way back, even when you ended early. Afterwards, write a few lines: what was there before; what I tried; what happened; what remains uncertain. Include a phrase, bodily signal, child’s need, protector’s concern, boundary, repair, or what not to force only when it actually appeared. You don’t owe the notebook a complete cast of characters.

# The 15-minute daily practice

Use the same practice rather than learning a second script: gather attention, listen, respond, wait, return. Fifteen minutes is a convenient appointment with yourself, not a therapeutic dose or a timetable your feelings must follow. Use a few minutes to enter, leave several minutes to return, and let the middle unfold at its own pace. Five minutes of silent listening is available when useful; you can interrupt it to change direction.

Another simple entry is to notice three things you see, three you hear, and three bodily sensations; then two of each, then one. Count from five to one and begin listening. Use the entry that suits you, rather than stacking inductions.

After familiar runs, shorten a recording to the cues you still need, then try practicing without playback. Keep recordings when you enjoy them; independence means being able to guide yourself, not having to refuse help. The optional re-entry cue can shorten an already familiar induction.

Afterward, ask what needed warmth, what needed protection, and what direction serves truth, love, and life. Was there something to explore, or capacity to build? What can you carry yourself now? When action is needed, make it concrete: “If [cue] happens, then I will [small action].” Check how you sleep, relate, and function later, not only how impressive the session felt.

# The weekly deeper practice

Once or twice a week, give the same practice more time when that is useful. More time needn’t mean greater intensity. Start from Higher Self or neutral witnessing, follow what appears, borrow an adult function when needed, and ask whether anything important is being bypassed. Leave room for the full exit and any ordinary action needed afterward.

Do the ordinary action immediately when possible. That teaches the subconscious that the adult heard and acted, instead of staging another beautiful internal meeting and then leaving the dishes exactly where they were.

# Capacity before content

Altered-state rule: do not ask for hidden truth while medically unsafe, panicking, sleep-deprived, nauseated, destabilized, manic, psychosis-like, or chemically overwhelmed. First stabilize. Truth that requires destabilization is not ready to be known directly. Ask for capacity, not content.

Sometimes it feels as though the subconscious knows something the conscious mind can’t yet tolerate. The subject may be sexuality, grief, trauma, family truth, betrayal, shame, vocation, relationship reality, illness fears, spiritual calling, addiction, identity, or moral conflict.

Interrogation is the dangerous response:

“Tell me now.” “Reveal everything.” “Stop hiding.” “I demand the truth.”

That pressure can produce flooding, panic, false certainty, confabulation, dissociation, or a generally worse Tuesday. Flooding can make the adult capacities needed for pausing, comparing options, reality-testing, planning, and choosing much harder to reach. In that state, regulation and support come before deeper dialogue. Silence may be protection when the adult lacks enough regulation, support, humility, or practical capacity to receive the material.

Ask for the next tolerable piece:

“Do I need to know the content now, or do I need to build capacity first?”

“What would make the conscious mind 5% more able to receive this?”

“Can you show me the truth as a symbol, not a full revelation?”

“Can you give me the next protective action without revealing the whole story?”

“Who, if anyone, should help me hold this?”

A safer script:

“Wise subconscious, if there is something I cannot yet handle, please do not break it open violently. Help me become someone who can know it safely. Give me preparation before revelation. Give me grounding before memory. Give me support before intensity. Give me one next action.”

Hypnotic imagery can be meaningful without being literal. The mind symbolizes, compresses, distorts, dramatizes, and borrows from culture and fear. Around trauma and memory especially, treat an image as an image and involve outside support when needed.

# Troubleshooting

## Nothing happens

Start smaller:

“Show me one body sensation.” “Show me one word.” “Show me whether today is for listening or resting.”

## It feels fake

First ask what feels fake: the wording, the image, the expectation, or the role you’re trying to perform. Not experiencing an inner voice or image isn’t, by itself, resistance or a missing adult capacity. Correct an explicitly unwanted invitation. If you recognize a protective part, you can talk to it:

“What are you protecting me from?” “Are you afraid I will lie to myself again?” “Are you afraid this is performance?”

The voice may be the inherited parent enforcing an old belonging rule. Don’t argue with it. Ask which rule it is enforcing and what ordinary evidence would make the new adult voice more believable.

The fake feeling can also mean that a needed adult capacity isn’t available yet. Stop demanding a convincing voice. If you can already observe what is happening, borrow the particular warmth, boundary, or direction you lack. A real or imagined caring figure can help. Try one believable sentence or action and see how it is received.

## Let the response change the invitation

Your shoulders tightening, or 'hmm, that feels kind of wrong', is a signal to investigate further, but 'No! That is Not Right!' is a signal to stop what you're doing and change it. If you don't feel like it fits, don't keep doing it. Just change it. You don't need any other reason.

"I notice the tightness, and I can just leave it like that. I wonder if that fits, or if I'd rather do something different?"

Sometimes it just isn't right - sometimes it's something you hadn't thought of. Neither is automatically a guard, and just being silent doesn't mean there's a process.

## Look at the situation too

If there is another person involved in the situation, it may be a good idea to look at what they are doing as well. Are they able to communicate in a mature fashion? Try and be specific in your description, and be willing to alter it if you find out that it was incorrect. Just because someone is disagreeing with you or trying to set boundaries around you does not mean they are in their emotionally immature parts. You don’t need to subject yourself to something that may be dangerous to determine if this is the case.

On the other hand, you may find that you have acted inappropriately and you feel guilty. Having feelings of guilt does not mean that you have done something wrong or that you have been manipulated. Have you done something that was wrong or that caused harm? If so, what can you do to fix it? On the other hand, did you give in to someone else just to keep them from being disappointed in you? What do you want to do or achieve the next time you interact with this person? Do you need them to understand what you are feeling/thinking? Do you just need to communicate information to them? Do you need to set boundaries with them? Do you need to end the interaction prematurely? You don’t have to make them understand before you can act.

## Change the amount of contact

When you start to contact the difficult thing, if it feels too much, immediately start to widen your awareness to something else in the room, in your body, etc. Keep moving back and forth between the difficult thing and the easier thing. This is called pendulation and titration. Do as much or as little as you feel you can.

Ok, I think that is enough for now.

Feel free to go back and explore more if you wish to do so. If, at any point, you start to lose contact with the here and now, feel that things are becoming more and more unreal, or you feel that you are not able to switch to something else or stop, stop trying to explore inside of yourself and just do the entire "coming back" part. If you have trouble orienting yourself and it lasts for a prolonged period of time, please get some human help rather than trying a stronger induction back to the here and now.

### If you feel an urge to move

If you feel an urge to move, turn, reach, push, or change your body’s position, feel free to either actually do something that will not cause your body pain or discomfort, or just imagine doing what you feel you need to do, and see what happens next. You do not need to act out the urge, feel that you need to move, shake, release, etc. If you do move and you feel that you are causing yourself pain or other symptoms that you are concerned about, stop moving.

## Offer the care that is actually wanted

If any younger parts come up, offer them the opportunity to spend time with you, or some space if they would rather. If one of them says “don’t come closer”, stay at the distance they choose. You don’t have to consider them literally younger, nor do you have to ask them why they are there or what happened to them. If you can identify any guards (childhood protector parts), talk to them in the same Nurturer voice you meant for your little one, and find out what they are concerned about. All of them are welcome to the meeting. You don’t have to get rid of any of them.

If you feel that it would be useful to bring in your Nurturer, Protector or Guide to help you either change the tone of the interaction, set boundaries, or find your way toward the life you want, feel free to do so. You don’t have to talk to them, just use the one whose job you need, and if you can’t do it yet, borrow it. You only need to use the Witness if you need help getting started with a step. If you are already able to do a certain step, you don’t have to do it again.

Try some different ideas as to what might be helpful for this inner part to feel better (being welcomed by you, visualizing someone else, giving them the sort of words they would like to hear, you placing yourself in a different body position, you moving further away etc). Only try one idea at a time to see if it works.

It is possible that when you try and give the part what it wants, you might feel very upset. It may be that it never got what it needed at the time and now that you are giving it what it wants, it is processing the upset of not getting what it wanted before. If this happens, allow yourself to respond for as long as you want to do so, and for as long as you can stay there (ie remain in the process). If you feel that the voice, distance, touch, image etc is too much, then change it to something that is less. Don’t think that you can’t take the love.

Allow yourself to see where the metaphor takes you. Ask questions like ‘if the problem had an image or story, what would it be?’ or ‘what would need to happen to make things right?’ If it feels like you need to do something to give yourself the resource that you feel you need (or to put a boundary in place, or to move away or to take some other action) then try doing that for as far as it takes to be of use to you. Remember that whatever you find out is likely to be metaphorical. It may be a good hypothesis for what you need to do but it is not a proven fact.

Alternatively, you may want to use the session to develop a capacity that you want to develop (ie love, play, courage, curiosity etc). You can also use it to rehearse something that you have decided to do.

## Finish without forcing a revelation

Ending the session. Don’t feel that you have to end the session with a great revelation! When you have done as much as you want to do, it is a good idea to find out if there is anything that you need to do to make sure that you can leave things where they are for the time being. This might include thanking parts for their help, or making sure that you do something in real life (eat a good meal, put a boundary in place, get some rest, repair a relationship, keep a promise etc). Whatever it is, tell your little one, "That’s enough for today. We’re going back to the life we’re building."

When you have finished, even if you stopped part way through, use the ‘Come all the way back’ procedure to bring yourself back. When you are back, it is a good idea to write a few lines in a notebook about what you have done. Just jot down the essentials (what was there before you started, what you have done to change things, what you are unsure about, any phrases that were said, any body signals, any information that the inner child said that it needed, anything that a guard wanted you to know, any boundaries that you put in place, any repairs that you did, anything that you learnt about what it is a mistake to try and force etc). You don’t need to write down the whole cast of characters, just what you actually noticed.

### Throughout the exercise

Note: throughout the exercise, allow your analytical part to make comments and/or tell you that it doesn’t like what you are doing. Just tell it that you will check things out when you come back and make any commitments etc when you are back. If it says no to going deeper, or tells you to stop doing the exercise, I’d stop there and not test it, even when it sounds like a prediction. You can stop whenever you want to. Remember that the images that you see can be meaningful without being actual memories of things that happened and just because you see an image, or feel a body release, that doesn’t mean that you are right about what is going on!

# The 15-minute daily practice

Notice there is no need to learn a second script! The process is the same. You are simply practicing for fifteen minutes or so. That is long enough to induce yourself (it only takes a few minutes), practice and let it unfold at its own pace, and come out again (that takes a few minutes too). Five minutes is plenty to see what happens when you simply sit in silence, listening, and you can interrupt yourself at any time to change direction if that feels better.

You can also try other ways of inducing yourself such as becoming aware of 3 things you can see, 3 things you can hear, 3 things you can feel in your body; then 2 of each, 1 of each, counting from 5 to 1 and beginning to listen. Choose one way to induce yourself, you don't need to try all of them! (In fact, it might be counter-productive!)

Once you have practiced listening a few times with the recordings, you may want to shorten them to speed up your induction, or even practice without playing them back at all. On the other hand, if you like using the recordings, there is no reason to stop! (NB. You may wish to use the re-entry cue to help you re-enter your process, this will also shorten your induction if you already know what to do.)

As you come out of the process ask yourself what you learned needed warmth, what needed to be protected, what you need to do differently to be more in accord with truth and love and life. Is there something you want to explore more fully or a capacity you want to build? And most importantly, what can you carry with you as you move forward from this? If you identified something you needed to do differently, make a plan with yourself to do that thing. The more specific you can be, the more likely you are to change: “If _________ (fill in the cue for the old pattern), then I will do ________ (fill in one small action that will begin to change the pattern).” Then check back later and see how you are doing – sleeping, relating, functioning, etc., not only how impressive the session felt.

# The weekly deeper practice

It’s good to give this more time once or twice a week, when that’s useful. It doesn’t have to be very intense but it is good to start from Higher Self or at least a neutral witnessing place and follow what appears. It’s also good to bring in some adult function to make sure nothing is being bypassed and so that at the end of the exercise, when the experience is over, there is the possibility of coming out completely and doing whatever normal thing needs doing.

If possible, it’s good to do the normal thing immediately after the exercise, so that the subconscious sees that the adult heard and acted. Otherwise, the dishes won’t get done.

# Capacity before content

NOTE: If it is medically unsafe or you are in a place of panic, sleep deprivation, nausea, deep destabilization, mania, psychosis-like symptoms, feeling overwhelmed by chemicals etc. then first focus on stabilizing and then look for what your subconscious needs you to know! If it takes you destabilizing to know what your subconscious knows, it is not meant for you to know directly at this time! Focus on asking for capacity over content!

Sometimes, it feels like your subconscious knows something that your conscious self is not prepared to know. It may be something related to your sexuality, grief, trauma, family issues, betrayal, shame, vocation, a relationship, illness fears, spirituality, addiction, identity, moral issues etc.

What you DON’T want to do is bully your subconscious into telling you what it knows by saying things to yourself like:

“What is it you won’t tell me? What are you hiding? Tell me NOW! I am telling you to tell me!”

This can lead to flooding, panic, false certainty, confabulation, dissociation etc. and can make your day SUCK! When you are flooded, it is much harder to access your adult capacities so focus on being able to regulate and getting support! Just ask for what you need to know in a way that you CAN tolerate:

“What can you tell me, that will make my conscious self 5% better at knowing the thing you want to tell me?” “Can you tell me in symbolic form so I am not completely overwhelmed by the revelation?” “Can you tell me what I need to do to be safe, without actually telling me what it is?” “Do I need to know WHAT it is, or do I need to know that I need to get some support to have the capacity to know what it is, and who would be a good person to help me?” etc.

Instead, say to yourself something like this:

“Dear Wise Subconscious Mind, if there is something you know that my conscious self is not ready to know, please don’t break it open violently! Do what you can to make me able to know it in a way that is safe! Prepare me BEFORE you break it open! Ground me BEFORE you show me the memory! Support me BEFORE you make it intense! Give me one next action!” etc.

REMEMBER: Not everything in hypnotic imagery is literal, especially if it relates to trauma and memory. The mind makes it symbolic, squashes things together, distorts, dramatizes, pulls from cultural influences, fears etc. So, treat it like an image and get some help outside of yourself!

# Troubleshooting

## Nothing happens

Is there a way you could try this on a smaller scale?

Ask: “Show me one body sensation.” “Show me one word.” “Show me whether today is for listening or resting.”

## It feels fake

Do you have a sense of what it is that feels fake? The words, an image, what you are expecting to get from this, a role you feel you have to play etc. It doesn't matter that you don't have an inner voice/image to do this, it isn't necessarily about not having a 'grown up' or resisting doing the work. If it feels like you are being asked to do something you don't want to do then correct this. It could be that there is a part that is protecting you, so talk to it.

You could say something like 'What are you protecting me from?' or 'Are you afraid I will lie to myself again?' or 'Are you afraid this will just be a performance?' If it says yes, that one can sting, because there could be something to it. Maybe you've fooled yourself before, and that part remembers. "Tell me what you remember, I'm listening." works well here.

It could also be your inherited parent script that you are picking up, and it is trying to enforce a rule about how you should be to belong to them. Find out what rule it is enforcing. Comfort your little one before you answer any of it. Your answers can be firm or funny, as long as they aren’t mean. Then work on finding some ordinary evidence to build a more believable 'grown up' voice of your own.

If the fake feeling is telling you that you don't have the grown up ability to do this yet, then don't try and convince yourself by using an inner voice. As long as you can still observe yourself well enough to do this, try and borrow what you need from someone who you can imagine is good enough to lend it to you. This could be a family member, friend, famous person etc. What you need might be warmth, boundary, direction etc. For warmth, just make sure it's someone who liked you the way you were. Try out something which rings true to you, whether it is a sentence, an action etc, and see how you get on.
