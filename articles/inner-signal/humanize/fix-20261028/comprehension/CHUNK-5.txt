## Stay awake when your mind likes to escape

Sit upright. Open the eyes. Feel the feet. Let the breath be natural.

Say: “I am not going away. I am coming more fully here.”

Look at one object. Feel the spine.

“Is this daydream guidance, emotional digestion, or avoidance?”

“If it is guidance, show me the action inside it. If it is avoidance, bring me gently into the present and show me what I am avoiding.”

If stillness makes you sleepy, diffuse, or escape-prone, hypnosis can become more alert rather than more relaxed. Keep the eyes open and pair a small repeated hand movement with increasing clarity; stop the movement and notice what remains without trying to disappear inward.

“Eyes open. Press thumb and forefinger together gently. Release. Again — clearer, not sleepier. One more time. Now stop the deliberate movement. Notice the first second after the rhythm ends. Is there an echo in the fingers, the breath, the attention, or nowhere at all? Stay upright and work from exactly that level of alertness.”

## The two-hand induction

Place one hand on the heart. Place the other hand on the solar plexus.

Let the heart hand represent the Nurturer. Let the solar plexus hand represent the vulnerable child.

Breathe gently.

“Love above. Child below. Adult present.”

“I send love to the child. I receive the child’s signal. I send love again. I listen again.”

## Sleep induction

Sleep induction has a different job. In inner work, the adult returns, writes, and acts. At bedtime, analysis can remain off duty. Revelation can wait; the task is rest.

For sleep hypnosis, see Nimja’s sleep playlist.

## Optional fractionation: practice coming back

Fractionation means returning toward ordinary awareness and then deciding whether to enter absorption again. Used openly, it teaches that trance has a door in both directions. Name the technique before using it; every answer cannot secretly mean “deeper.”

“Open the eyes and return toward ordinary awareness. Feel the room and decide whether to stop here. If you choose to continue, close or soften the eyes and notice what changes as attention gathers again. You may stop at either point.”

## Deepening, if useful

“If it is useful, attention may become more gathered. If it is not useful, I remain exactly as awake as I need to be.”

Choose one simple scene: a room in the heart, a garden, a temple, a fire with the Protector nearby, a clear lake, or a chair where the adult waits. One scene is plenty.

“Who or what needs to speak first?”

## Optional trance depth check

“As I wait, I give up the responsibility of making anything happen.

If it is useful, the wise subconscious may let one finger twitch, pulse, lift, warm, tingle, or become subtly noticeable when the inner meeting space is ready.

I will not force it. I will not fake it. I will simply wait with love and notice.”

A finger may move, a hand may warm, the breath may change, or nothing may happen. None proves truth; at most, something may be listening.

## A shortcut back into hypnosis

Learn a useful induction and the full return below before developing a shortcut. My dad brought two fingers together in a mudra like the OK sign to help himself relax, including while driving. That’s his experience, not a recommendation to enter trance at the wheel. Keep alert calming and inward hypnosis on different cues.

Choose a distinctive word, phrase, or optional gesture. During a familiar, welcome hypnotic state, pair it with that receptive way of listening—not peak emotional intensity, a child-state, or a traumatic scene. Give it the meaning you choose:

When I’ve set aside time, settled somewhere appropriate, and deliberately use my cue to begin, I can return to this familiar way of listening. I can change direction or finish.

Return fully. On a later suitable occasion, deliberately begin practice and try the cue. Give it time; gradually shorten the longer induction when the shortcut helps, and use the familiar route when it doesn’t. No fixed repetition count, instant depth, or particular sensation is required.

Check usefulness and selectivity: does it help when chosen, remain absent when unchosen, and leave changing direction and returning workable? It mustn’t become obedience to a narrator, notification, or overheard word. Faster entry doesn’t determine what material you can handle.

Never train, test, or use hypnotic re-entry while driving or doing anything requiring active attention. Open eyes and conditional wording are not safeguards.

To retire the cue, stop rehearsing it, remove it from recordings or active app instructions, and return to the familiar induction. State your decision while awake; a cancellation sentence does not prove the association vanished instantly. Persistent unwanted responses or disorientation need qualified help. Any retained cue is for a future, separately chosen session; today’s altered state ends with the return.

# Come all the way back

Reverse whatever you used to enter. If you floated, regain chosen weight. If you became small, return to ordinary scale. If you met a younger state or several parts, confirm present wholeness, body ownership, and adult judgment. If you traveled, come back by the same route. Keep only the qualities you deliberately chose.

“I bring back only what is useful now. The rest can remain safely held until there is more capacity. I return to the room, the body, the day, and one honest action. All instructions to deepen or remain in this session are finished. I keep only the ordinary-life intention or future-practice cue I consciously chose; neither keeps today’s altered state running. I am myself, in my body, with adult judgment fully available.”

One — feel the body’s contact and chosen weight. Two — let the breath return to its ordinary rhythm. Three — hear the room and name where you are. Four — move fingers, toes, shoulders, and eyes. Five — eyes open, present, ordinary, clear. The session has ended.

Name the day or date. Look left, right, near, and far. Move before standing, and stand only when steady. Check that you are actually oriented and alert; repeating the count is not proof of a complete return. Note only what actually appeared, and any action it calls for. Then do something normal.

If a recording stops unexpectedly: say, “The recording stopped and the session is over.” Open your eyes, name the location and day, press your feet or hands into a surface, move, look around, and use the five-step count. Don’t wait for the missing narrator to release you.

Sleep is separate. A sleep recording may end in sleep or quiet rest. A waking recording shouldn’t end by preserving a role, reduced judgment, impaired movement, blankness, an undefined reset word, or an invitation to remain altered indefinitely.

# The actual inner meeting

Sit rather than lie down when you tend to daydream, dissociate, or fall asleep. Let the spine rise without becoming military, soften the jaw, and keep some awareness of the room. Save lying down for sleep, deep rest, or dream incubation.

“I am not leaving life. I am listening more deeply to life.”

Begin by invoking the larger field:

“Higher Self, universal heart-mind, wise subconscious, body intelligence, inner child, inner adults, protective parts, and all that serves truth and love: please help me understand what I need to know, only in the form and timing that is useful, kind, stabilizing, and real.”

You’re asking the conscious mind to listen without forcing an answer.

Thoughts do not have to stop before deeper listening begins. Let thoughts, sensations, images, doubts, and emotions pass through awareness without fighting or following them; keep the intention lightly in the background and notice what becomes salient on its own.

“Please tell me what I need to know, or ask what you need to know. I will wait silently for five minutes.”

The response may be a phrase, sensation, memory, color, child, protector, pain, warmth, silence, task, gratitude, an urge to stop, or the realization that today’s practice is over.

Listen to all of it. Believe none of it automatically.

# Let the next response guide you

The induction and inner meeting bring you to a place where you can listen. From here, learn to answer what happens instead of reciting a speech that has already decided what every part will say. Read these examples before practicing, then put the screen aside.

Notice one thing accurately. Offer a small, believable invitation, an act of care, or more time. Acknowledge only what actually happens, then wait. A sensation can lead to an image, a gesture, a feeling, or words; you don’t need to fill every category.

## Finding your own words

Suppose you notice, “My chest tightened when I imagined asking for help.” Start with that, rather than deciding you’ve found an abandoned child or a protective mechanism.

And as I notice that tightening, I can give myself a little time before deciding what it means … and notice whether there’s something I’d like to say, change, or simply stay with.

Then listen. That is enough language for one invitation. A plain “What happens next?” may be better than an elegant sentence. When a feeling is developing, pause the words if that gives it room; keep using them when they help you understand.

You can be firmer when that is the help you chose: “Let the explanation wait a moment. What do I feel as I say that?” Directness can focus attention without deciding the answer. A difficult feeling needn’t become pleasant before you can stay with it. When you remain present, can change direction, and want to continue, sadness or tension alone doesn’t require an exit.

Keep apart what you noticed, what it seemed to mean, and what you think about that meaning now. These can overlap; you don’t need to prove which came first. When useful, ask: “What did I notice? What did it seem to mean? What supports or challenges that interpretation?”

“My stomach tightened” reports a sensation. “That person is evil” is a conclusion—even if it arrived as a wordless certainty. Ask what they actually did, what cues you noticed, and what else could explain the alarm. A sentence you find now can help examine the meaning without being a sentence you thought then. Nonverbal doesn’t mean deeper or truer; earlier doesn’t mean more reliable. Feelings deserve attention, and their meanings remain open to analysis.

## A shortcut back into hypnosis

Once you have learned the above induction and how to come back down at the end, you can do the following….

Have an unusual word, phrase, and/ or motion (optional) that you will use to go into hypnosis again. Say the word and/ or do the phrase while doing the motion (if applicable). Be sure to do this when you are in hypnosis and in a good place. Don't do this when you are in a very emotional state, child state, or thinking about something that could be considered trauma. The idea is for you to be able to pick a good state to go into, and you can decide what that is.

Ex: My dad used to use a mudra (like putting two fingers together like an "OK" sign) when he needed to relax. He even did it when he was driving. Not that I recommend driving in a trance, don't do that! Make sure that your relaxation/ stay alert cue is not the same as your cue to go inward to hypnosis.

When you pick the time and place (it needs to be an appropriate time and place), say the word and/ or do the phrase to begin going into this state that you picked, and are familiar with. It's fine to change the direction you are going in, or stop this at any point.

Now come all the way back up again, and at a later time, when it is appropriate, start using this. Over time, you can shorten the length of the longer induction, or use the longer induction if this doesn't seem to be enough for your needs. There is no need to have to do this a certain number of times, go to a certain depth, or even have a certain sensation when you do this.

Also, be sure that this is working for you. Be able to turn it on and off at will. Make sure it isn't there when you don't want it to be. Be able to change directions or come back up when you are in it. Don't use it in a way that makes you follow instructions that you are hearing from a narrator, notifications, or something that you overhear. Don't use this to go into hypnosis faster for things that you shouldn't be working on yet.

There is something you should never do while using this induction. And that's use it, practice it or test it while you're driving or doing something where you need to be actively paying attention. It's just not safe to be going into a trance where you are actively paying attention to what's going on inside of you when you need to be paying attention to what's going on outside of you. Even though your eyes can be open, even though you're using "if it would be useful for you to do so" type language, it's just not safe.

### Turning the shortcut off, or keeping it

If you want to turn this one off, simply don't practice it anymore. Take it out of your recordings, take it out of your apps if you're using it in an app, and start using your regular induction. Tell yourself, when you're awake, that you won't be using it anymore. Just because you say that it'll be gone doesn't mean that it'll be gone right away. If it's causing you a problem, if you're still responding to it when you don't want to respond to it, if you're having issues with disorientation, etc, get some help from someone who's qualified to help you with this. If, on the other hand, you want to keep it so that you can use it to go back into trance at a later date when you do want to go into a trance, that's fine too. For now, though, once you come out of this trance, it'll be over for today.

# Come all the way back

If you have done anything to alter your state when entering the trance, make sure to reverse those things. If you have floated, make sure to gain your weight again (whatever weight you want of course). If you have shrunk, make sure to grow back to your regular size. If you have met a younger state or are working with parts, make sure to come back to feeling complete in your body in the here and now, that your body belongs to you, and that it is under the control of your adult mind. If you have gone somewhere, make sure to go back the same way you went there. Etcetera.

Here is a generic bring-back that you can use: "When I come out of this session, I will only bring with me that which is useful to me in my everyday life right now. Anything else I will bring with me at a later date, when I am better able to integrate it. When I come out of this session, I will be coming back to the room I am in now, and to the day in which I am participating in this session now. All the instructions you have given me to carry me deeper into this session or keep me in this state are now finished. The only intention I will take with me into my everyday life, and the only cue I will use to take me into this state at a later date, will be the ones I consciously choose, and neither of them will keep me in this state. When I come out of this session I will be me, in my body and with my adult judgment."

Then do a count up: On 1 start to feel your body again and what it's resting on and feel your weight (of your choice). On 2 start to feel your breathing return to normal. On 3 start to hear the room around you and name where you are. On 4 start to wiggle your fingers and toes, move your shoulders and your eyes. On 5 open your eyes, back in the here and now, feeling normal and clear and the session is now over.

When you come out make sure you can name what day (or date) it is, look to your left and right, look at something near to you and something further away. Move around a bit. Don't get up until you feel like you can do so without falling over. Make sure that you are oriented and alert (counting does this, but not always). Take mental note of only what actually came up for you and any actions you need to take regarding it. Then go on and do something normal.

If your recording cuts out for some reason, you can say to yourself, "The recording has stopped and the session is over," and then do the above on your own. Once your eyes are open, press your feet or hands into a surface as well.

Note: If you are going to sleep to a recording then it is fine to go to sleep or just remain resting quietly at the end of it. However, if you are coming out of a recording then you should not leave yourself in an altered state. This includes leaving yourself in a role, with lowered judgment, with lowered ability to move, blank or with a non-specific reset word or for an indefinite length of time.

# The actual inner meeting

Note: If you have a tendency to daydream, dissociate or fall asleep, it is better to do this exercise sitting up and save lying down for when you want to go to sleep, get a very deep rest or incubate dreams. If you are sitting, keep your spine straight but not rigid like a soldier. Relax your jaw and stay somewhat connected to the room you are in.

“I am not leaving life; I am listening more deeply to life.

I invoke my Higher Self, universal heart-mind, wise subconscious mind, intelligence in my body, inner child(ren), inner adult(s), guard(s) and all that is of truth and love to help me know what I need to know, only in a form and at a time that will be useful, kind, stabilizing and real for me.”

Ask your conscious mind to listen.

This does not mean you have to stop thinking to listen. Just notice your thoughts, body sensations, pictures, doubts, emotions, etc. Don’t fight what comes up, but don’t go with it either. Just notice what stands out on its own as you hold the idea of what you are doing in the back of your mind.

Internally say, “Tell me what I need to know or ask me what you need to know and I’ll sit in silence for 5 minutes and listen.”

You may hear a phrase, feel a body sensation, notice a memory, see a color, be contacted by a child or guard, feel pain, warmth, complete silence, know that you need to do something, be grateful for something, feel you need to stop the listening or any number of things.

Listen to everything and believe nothing automatically.

# Let the next response guide you

Read through these sections, and then try doing the inner meeting without looking at the screen.

Respond to each thing that comes up with an accurate notice and a small reasonable invitation (or action or taking more time etc). The thing might be a sensation, image, gesture, feeling, words, or anything. The notice and invitation don't need to take all those forms, just notice and invite what happens.

## Finding your own words

An example notice might be, "I notice my chest tightening when I imagine asking for help". Don't add interpretation like "this must be the abandoned child part of me, or a protective mechanism" - just notice what you notice. An example invitation might be, "And as I notice that, I can take some time before deciding what it means, and notice if I want to say anything, or change it, or just stay like that".

Then just listen. You don't need fancy language - often it will be better to just ask what happens next, or whatever. If you notice a feeling developing, it might be best not to say anything while it's developing (if you can help it) but some days you need to talk to understand, so just do that.

You can be pretty straightforward when that's the help you chose - "Let the explanation wait a moment. What do I feel as I say that?" can be a perfectly valid thing to say to yourself, and help you focus. As long as you're still present, and able to change if you want to, and happy to carry on, a sad feeling or tension or whatever doesn't mean it's not ok.

### Notice, meaning, and your thoughts on the meaning

It's worth trying to separate the notice, the meaning, and your thoughts on the meaning, but in practice it will often be pretty fuzzy. When you can, it can be helpful to separate it out like that though - "I noticed... this seemed to mean... here's why that might be true / false" or something like that.

Is this a notice or meaning? "My stomach went tight" is a notice, "He's a complete bastard" is a meaning, even if you didn't think it in words. You can then go on to find out what he did, notice what it was that gave you the idea, and consider if there might be other explanations. It's also ok to use phrases that you didn't think in your head - if something comes up that you're analyzing, it's still good to analyze, even if it's not in your head already. Nonverbal thoughts and early thoughts aren't necessarily any better or worse than other thoughts. Feelings are good to pay attention to, but it's still good to consider what they mean.
