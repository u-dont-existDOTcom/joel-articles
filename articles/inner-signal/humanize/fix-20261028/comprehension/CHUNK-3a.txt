## The Protector

The Protector turns warmth into something the child can believe.

“I will set the boundary.” “I will leave the unsafe conversation.” “I will stop texting the person who destabilizes us.” “I will eat.” “I will sleep.” “I will keep a small promise.” “I will not leave the child alone with adult consequences.”

This is where inner-child practice often turns decorative. The voice says loving things while the adult sends the destabilizing message, skips the meal, avoids the task, and breaks the promise. Trust begins when you handle the consequence.

If warmth is unavailable, a protective act can begin the relationship. Make it small enough to carry out and review what actually happened. A missed action might reflect fear, exhaustion, missing skills, a practical barrier, or a poor plan. Completion without immediate relief isn’t automatically failure, and a tick on a list doesn’t prove the action helped.

## Protection that knows when to stop scanning

The Protector is more than an inner voice saying no. You can practice it as a bodily sequence:

Orient: look around and feel the support beneath you.

Assess: ask what is known, what is uncertain, and what needs checking.

Distinguish: separate present danger from uncertainty, old alarm, or imagined danger.

Set one proportionate boundary: move, stop, delay, ask, leave, or get support.

Verify enough safety: not perfect certainty, just enough reality-based evidence for the next step.

Stand down and rest: protection includes ending the scan when continued vigilance is no longer helping.

A mature Protector can say, “I don’t know.” It can ask another human, delay interpretation, or choose a no-trance practice. I’m aiming for discernment that acts when needed and then quits scanning the room after the danger has passed.

## Inner child reparenting

I connect my reparenting therapy to self-hypnosis because I find they are a perfect match. The child may arrive as an age, image, posture, voice, memory, emotion, or plain vulnerability. Sometimes no child appears. Stay with a recognizable protector, or with the sensation, feeling, or preference that is actually present.

When the child does appear, resist the urge to begin a healing project immediately. Be with them first.

“Little one, I am here. You do not have to explain everything. You do not have to be healed today. You do not have to become impressive. I only want to know what would help you feel less alone.”

The need may be warmth, play, protection, grief, anger, food, rest, a boundary, a safer relationship, less fantasy, less overwork, or one adult promise kept today.

## Don’t rush the repair

When a younger state, protector, feeling, or bodily signal needs care, move patiently:

Prepare: create enough time, privacy, support, and adult capacity.

Inspect: notice what is present before deciding what it means.

Ask permission: “May I come closer, or would you prefer warmth, space, words, silence, or no contact?”

Respond to one need: do the smallest useful thing rather than attempting a total repair.

Wait: let the response settle. Some changes need quiet, sleep, or ordinary time.

Reconnect: invite contact only where it is welcome. No part has to merge, disappear, forgive, or become cheerful.

Rest: stop before care becomes another performance.

Reassess: return later and ask what actually helped.

The goal isn’t “good as new,” as though history should disappear. Aim for good as you: more supported and connected while still recognizably yourself. If parts language makes you feel more divided, use signals, feelings, younger states, or another metaphor that helps.

## The Guide

You can also work with your Guide. Call them whatever you want, Leader, Guru, Wise One, Sage, Inner Teacher, Director, Higher Adult, whatever.

Ask them for help with directing your life. The Guide holds standards, the way a good parent would, and asks hard things of your inner child so it can grow, at its own pace. Your parent’s harsh tone doesn’t come with the job, though, so be careful not to ask for the kind of “tough it out, you don’t have needs, you just need discipline!” kind of direction. Without the Guide, you can also slide from looking after your inner child into spoiling it: sweets and screens can pass for loving yourself, and so can getting it out of anything hard. Ask them for help in directing you to do adult things in a way that takes your needs into account: asking for the help you need, expressing your preferences, letting a safe person see you in an imperfect way, finding ways to get your need for closeness met without punishing someone for their need for space, etc. etc.

Affirmations might be helpful here, too: “We’re not just here to feel good. We’re here to become whole. We’re here to belong to life, not only to people who approve of us. We’re here to serve truth/beauty/love/God/source/nature/the universe/dharma/the Tao/the benefit of all beings,” etc. etc.

Start with whatever adult function you can connect to, but most of us need enough Nurturer and Protector first, before the Guide. Otherwise the Guide just overrules your inner child in the name of something higher (spiritual bypassing, dressed up as direction). Even if you start with protection and can’t do the warmth stuff yet, make sure the direction you’re being led in is appropriate for your inner child’s growth (i.e. not depriving them of things they need, like play or support, etc.)

## Working with your guards

Feel free to use parts language here, too, if it’s helpful. Often, we see a sensation or a distraction or an objection as something that needs to be explored, when in fact it might just be hunger, or reasonable skepticism about something, or a sentence that doesn’t fit. It’s good to notice it, though, and then to see what might be going on.

If you're not careful, you might find yourself expecting that when you go within to meet your inner child, you'll find your inner child.

Instead, you might feel numb, cynical, angry, hungry, like you need to look at your phone, sexually aroused, like you need to make plans, sleepy, dissociated, or you might even think that what you're doing is stupid. Those are not failures. Often they are you experiencing a very charming guard. It's what protects you when your consciously installed parental Protector isn't functioning, the same as when you were a child. But before you decide where it's coming from, ask it what it's trying to stop, or what it wants. It could also be your inner child, mad that you took so long to come back, or your inherited parent script, or something from earlier today, or a bit of each.

(To the guard, in the same Nurturer voice you meant for your inner child: “I see you. I'm not going to force my way past you. What's going on?” Then wait and listen patiently. If nothing comes, that's fine for now. At some point you can ask, “What are you afraid would happen if you stopped protecting me by…..?”)

If necessary take time and listen. (Perhaps they are afraid that you will lie to yourself again or pretend again or that it will be too painful or you will get spiritual bypass again or you will forgive someone who shouldn’t be forgiven yet or you will see what you want to see or that you will leave them hanging after the session or…..)

(Now talk to the guard. Tell them that it makes sense that they want to protect you and that they don’t have to stop protecting you, but that you want to know what could make them trust you 1% more that you can do that. If it says no, I’d listen, and if it says no to going deeper in this work or to an altered state, I’d stop there and not test it, even when it sounds like a prediction.)

Most of the time the guard wants you to do certain things, set boundaries, do whatever you are supposed to do, eat, not text someone, keep a promise, go to sleep, talk to the therapist, admit that you owe someone money, write something down, etc.

## Your inherited parent script

One important thing to become aware of when approaching your inner child is your inherited parent script. This is the set of rules and ways of treating yourself that you internalized as a child, mostly based on how your parent(s) treated you, and it doesn’t always sound like a voice. This is not the same as your inner adult (Nurturer, Protector, Guide) that you build up on purpose. Often this script acts as a critic or warden, telling your inner child not to cry, not to be “needy”, not to embarrass them, not to be too independent, etc. So when you begin talking to your child in a new, loving way, the script may jump in to say that what you’re doing is “fake”.

## Protection that knows when to stop scanning

Do you want to embody your Protector? Then you could practice this:

orient self (look around, feel the support around you, etc.)

assess the situation (know what you know, what you don’t know and check out what you need to)

differentiate between actual/real danger, unknown, past or imagined dangers

set boundaries (start with one and make it relative to the danger; move, stop, delay, ask, leave, get support, etc.)

verify if it is safe enough

stand down and rest

This seems to me like a mature Protector. One that is not afraid to say that he doesn’t know, or one that can ask another human being, or one that can delay interpreting a situation or one that can use a no-trance method. I am learning to do discernment when I need to and stop scanning the room when it isn’t needed anymore.

## Inner child reparenting

I see a lot of connection between reparenting therapy and self hypnosis. They fit well together, though I’d learn to do inner-child therapy sober first, in your ordinary mindstate, since that’s the one you’ll be living in, and get enough Nurturer and Protector in the room before you go deep. When you go into hypnosis the child might come to you. You might see that child in any form. It can be an image, a posture, a voice, a memory, an emotion, etc. It can be any age. It can be any vulnerable part of you. It doesn’t even have to be a child, if there is no child there, just work with a guard or whatever you find.

## Don’t rush the repair

Just be with the child.

If you can, tell the child that you are there for them and that they don’t have to explain themselves, or be healed or impress you. Just tell you what they need to not be alone anymore.

It can be anything. What does the child need? warmth, to play, to be protected, to mourn, to be angry, food, rest, boundaries, better relationships, to stop fantasizing, to stop working their butt off, that you keep a promise to them for one day, etc. etc.

Find a younger you/guard/feeling/signal that comes up in your body, and

A. Prepare yourself to tend to them (get yourself into a place where you can spend time with them, have privacy, feel you can access support if you need it, and be as adult as you can be). How much of the grown-up you is actually here right now? Try looking around the room, pausing before you act, making a choice and following through on it. If you can't do that because you're basically the frightened kid, leave the deeper conversation until later.

B. Look at what's there before judging or deciding what it is.

C. Ask them what you can do or if you can move closer to them. If you feel like you already know what they need, ask if they'd prefer warmth, space, verbal reassurance, silence, distance, whatever.

D. Give them what you can- don't feel like you have to fix it all.

E. Give yourself some time to let what you did sink in- it may take some time, it may take you to go to sleep, etc.

F. Reconnect with them- don't expect them to merge, go away, forgive you, become happy, etc. Just reconnect with them if they're willing.

G. Rest, and reconnect with them later to see what was helpful.

Remember the goal isn't to make yourself good as new but to make yourself good as you- more supported and connected to yourself. If you don't like the idea of part work or it makes you feel more divided, you can substitute whatever part you're working with for a signal or a feeling, or an imaginary younger you that you feel embodies the issue you're having.
