# A useful map of the mind

In this guide, subconscious means the deeper processes shaping perception, emotion, desire, avoidance, imagery, memory, bodily signals, habits, intuition, and automatic behavior. Sometimes it helps to address all of that as one mind; sometimes an ecology of parts gives more precision. Both are maps. Neither needs its own priesthood.

Parts give precision. Higher Self gives coherence. Communion is the movement between them.

When you need coherence, speak to the deeper mind as a whole:

“Wise subconscious, please help me understand.”

When you need precision, notice the different tendencies inside it: the child wanting love, the protector wanting safety, the critic wanting standards, the fantasy-maker wanting possibility, the body memory wanting recognition, the social self wanting belonging, the spiritual self wanting God or source or the benefit of all beings, and the wiser part wanting what is true.

Begin with unity when the system feels scattered:

“Higher Self, universal heart-mind, wise subconscious, please guide me.”

Differentiate only when the question needs it:

“Which part of me is afraid?” “Which part is protecting?” “Which part is avoiding?” “Which part knows the next step?” “Which part is too young to carry this?”

Then bring the parts back into one life:

“Let all of this belong to one life. Let the adult self lead with love.”

## Sometimes start with the whole

Some people feel more fragmented when they begin by naming twelve parts and assigning everybody office space. Start with the sacred whole when that happens.

“I begin from the universal heart. I begin from Higher Self. I begin from the part of me that belongs to life before it belongs to any wound, role, family, group, identity, ideology, or story. Please help me understand what is true, kind, timely, stabilizing, and useful.”

If Higher Self, the universal heart, or any sacred language feels unavailable or borrowed from somebody else, begin with plain witnessing instead. Notice the room, the body, and the younger state. The fact that something can notice is enough adult presence for the first step.

If you can already notice and distinguish what is happening inside, you don’t need to keep rehearsing the Witness. Work with the warmth, protection, or direction that is actually missing. Higher Self language can help you gather around a meaningful source of care; it doesn’t establish that a perfectly capable inner parent is already available. Those adult functions still grow through practice.

Differentiate only when something specific appears:

“Is this the inner child?” “Is this a protector?” “Is this the body?” “Is this fear?” “Is this intuition?” “Is this social conditioning?” “Is this divine guidance, or my interpretation of guidance?”

Start from the heart. Differentiate only as needed. Return to the heart.

## When the adult role is not available yet

Some people can summon one of those adult functions. Others go blank, hear only the inherited critic, or feel like a child pretending to be grown. Telling them to “be the adult” assumes the capacity they are trying to build.

For that starting point, I use a provisional, author-developed bridge I call borrowed adulthood. It combines familiar practices—witnessing, modeling, supported action, and gradual transfer of responsibility—without claiming that the complete sequence has been validated as one treatment.

Witness, when needed. Notice what is present without demanding warmth, wisdom, or certainty. If you already distinguish the internal positions, move to the adult function you need.

Borrow one function. Choose a safe adult, mentor, older sibling, future self, spiritual figure, value, or written plan. Borrow only the warmth, boundary, or direction needed now.

Become the adult apprentice. Choose the exact words or action yourself and carry at least one small part.

Hand it back. Name what the borrowed figure modeled and what five percent you can now do without them.

The borrowed figure stays beside your judgment rather than replacing it. The fuller sequence is in my Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide.

You don’t need a history full of trustworthy people to borrow care. Use a remembered person when that helps, or an imagined adult whose intention is to take good care of you. Ask, “What would that person say or do with my actual history, limitations, and options?” Begin with the least cruel response you can believe if warmth is unavailable. Once you find one usable sentence or action, try it rather than repeating the exercise in different words.

Borrowing can also help the position trying to be the adult: how would a decent adult hear distrust without retaliating, defending their goodness, collapsing, or leaving? Borrow that one response. You may still feel young while doing it; adult identity can develop after the behavior.

## Let the child speak without handing over the steering wheel

Letting a younger feeling-state speak doesn’t mean handing adult judgment to an outside caretaker or trying to become literally younger. Wonder, play, vivid sensation, vulnerability, honest need, or the wish to carry fewer decisions can all come forward while the internal adult remains present.

“A younger part of me may speak. The adult in me remains here to listen, evaluate, protect, and stop. No child-state, guide, recording, or outside person replaces adult judgment.”

That distinction lets you recover curiosity, spontaneity, play, sensory freshness, honest need, and quick emotional movement without pairing those gifts with obedience, helplessness, scolding, or weakened judgment.

## Love doesn’t have to wait for trust

When love is available, the younger state doesn’t have to believe in it before you can offer it. Trust can question your promises, remember failures of protection, and take its time. Love can remain present while that happens.

“Okay. I believe that you don’t believe me. I still love you. You don’t have to love me back. Tell me what feels unsafe.”

That isn’t a way to end the complaint. Listen for what is true in it and answer seriously. Hearing anger without retaliation, admitting a real failure, and repairing after an internal attack are evidence alongside meals, boundaries, appointments, and kept promises. Non-retaliation expresses love; it shouldn’t replace love with a colder performance of good behavior.

Notice what each position teaches the others. A vow followed by blame when it is doubted teaches that love depends on receiving it correctly. The blaming voice and the voice making the vow may be different positions, the same position, or a blend. Don’t decide from the person’s chronological age.

When a younger version of you becomes the defendant, ask what they understood then, what options they could see and tolerate, and what happened because of the choice. Harm can require responsibility and repair. Punishment cannot retroactively create the capacity that was missing.

## The two-hand practice

Place one hand at the center of the chest as a physical connection to love. Put the other at the solar plexus, just beneath the chest, where you can relate to the vulnerable child.

Send warmth from the heart toward the child. Notice whatever comes back—a sensation, image, feeling, resistance, or nothing—and answer with warmth again. Let it become a conversation rather than a performance.

Love above. Child below. Adult present.

## The Nurturer

The Nurturer brings warmth without trying to rush the feeling away, and takes pleasure in the child’s company: “I’m glad you’re here.” The child needs to be enjoyed, not only comforted when hurt.

“It’s okay to feel this.” “I’m here.” “You don’t have to do anything right now.” “You are not too much.” “You can be small here.” “You can be angry here.” “You can be sad here.” “I will stay.”

## The Protector

The Protector turns warmth into something the child can believe.

“I will set the boundary.” “I will leave the unsafe conversation.” “I will stop texting the person who destabilizes us.” “I will eat.” “I will sleep.” “I will keep a small promise.” “I will not leave the child alone with adult consequences.”

This is where inner-child practice often turns decorative. The voice says loving things while the adult sends the destabilizing message, skips the meal, avoids the task, and breaks the promise. Trust begins when you handle the consequence.

If warmth is unavailable, a protective act can begin the relationship. Make it small enough to carry out and review what actually happened. A missed action might reflect fear, exhaustion, missing skills, a practical barrier, or a poor plan. Completion without immediate relief isn’t automatically failure, and a tick on a list doesn’t prove the action helped.

# A useful map of the mind

Note: whenever I refer to the subconscious I’m referring to all of the processes under the surface of your conscious awareness: perceptions, emotions, desires, avoidance tendencies, imagery, memories, body signals, habits, intuitions, automatic behaviors and so forth. You can work with all of these together as if it’s all one mind, or you can work with it as a set of parts, an ecology if you like. There isn’t a right or wrong way to approach it. They’re all maps. Don’t form a priesthood around either one!

Working with parts: Precision. Working with Higher Self: Coherence. Doing both: Communion.

If you need to work with the whole, the subconscious as one:

a) Invoke the subconscious in some way. Something simple like ‘Hello, wise subconscious, please help me understand…’ or whatever works for you.

If you need to work with the ecology in the subconscious (child parts, guards, critic, fantasy making parts, body memories, social part, spiritual part (God/source/best benefit of all beings part) wiser part etc etc) then:

a) It’s usually a good idea to start with unity. So invoke your Higher Self: Hello my Higher Self/universal heart-mind/wise subconscious, please guide me…

b) Move into differentiation. “Which part is afraid? Which part wants to protect you? Which part wants to avoid it? Which part knows what to do? Which part is too young for this?” Etc.

c) Move back into unity again. “And all of these parts are part of the one life, and as the adult in charge of this life you can lead with love…” Etc.

## Sometimes start with the whole

Some people make themselves too fragmented by starting to work with parts. ‘Here’s a part! Here’s another one! I’ll get twelve of them in a room and give them all an office to hang around in!’ So it’s better to start from the Sacred.

Start like this: ‘I’m approaching this from the universal heart. I’m approaching this from my Higher Self. I’m approaching this from the part of me that knows it belongs to life before it belongs to any wound, or role, or family, or group, or identity, or ideology, or story. So can you please tell me what’s going to be true and kind and timely and stabilizing and useful here?’ Etc.

It can be that you can’t even access your Higher Self, or the universal heart, or whatever works for you. (Btw, if it doesn’t work for you don’t use it! It’s no good adopting somebody else’s map.) In that case, start by just witnessing whatever is coming up. Notice the room, your body, the fact that you’re in a younger state etc. Being able to notice is enough adult presence for the first step.

Once you have the ability to notice, and to differentiate, you don’t need to keep rehearsing it. Just work on whatever it is you need to work on (often being warm to yourself, protecting yourself and knowing what to do etc). So even though it can be useful to talk about your Higher Self, it doesn’t mean you already have all the good inner parenting functions etc that you need. You usually need to work on developing them. And I don’t think the Higher Self has any specific wisdom. It’s a vantage point, where more wisdom and compassion can come up, but problems can also look like they were never problems at all from up there, and that can ironically lead instead to more foolish indifference.

Differentiate only as you need to. “Is this my inner child? Or is this a guard? Is this my body? Is this fear? Is this intuition? Is this my social part? Is this divine guidance, or is this my interpretation of divine guidance?”

# The inner child, the guards, and the inner adult’s three jobs

Your inner child won’t trust you right away. It’s not going to be enough for you to meditate, say three nice sentences to the inner child and expect to have its trust. What’s going to help your inner child to trust you is you, the person you are right now, becoming reliable.

Here I’m going to talk about what you develop in yourself as you reparent your inner child. I’m going to talk about it in terms of three jobs: your loving Nurturer, your safe Adult Protector (hereafter just the Protector) and your wise Guide. That’s one grown-up doing three jobs, and each job keeps the others in balance. It’s you that needs to develop these qualities.

Also, your inner child’s worth is not dependent on these qualities… It has its own worth, and its own qualities, like playing, being tender, not taking life too seriously, being creative and wondering at the world. Those belong in your relationship with it too, not just the wounds.

## When the adult role is not available yet

The problem is that some people do not have access to their adult functions at will. Instead, they may go blank, access an inherited inner critic, or feel like a child part who may be playing at being an adult (what I call forced adulthood). Telling them to just access their adult is like telling a baby bird to just fly.

For that starting point, I have developed a kind of provisional bridge that I call “borrowed adulthood.” It’s built from familiar practices like modeling and supported action, but I’m not suggesting that the whole sequence has been formally tested and proven as one treatment.

Access your witnessing position if necessary, just noticing what’s there, without needing it to be warm or wise or certain. If you already know how to access different internal positions, move immediately to accessing the adult position you want to borrow.

If you have a safe adult in your life you can borrow from them, if not borrow from a mentor, older brother/sister, imagine your future self, your god/goddess or higher power, a value you have, a plan you have written, etc. It’s ok to just borrow what you need right now, warmth or a boundary or direction, just like you would borrow a cup of sugar from a neighbor.

Next, put on your adult hat as if you are an apprentice adult and do or say what you think you should do or say. You should do at least part of what you think you should do or say.

Return the adult hat, noticing what you borrowed and what you were able to do on your own (even if it’s only 5%).

This is a borrowed figure, so you’re still making the choice. The longer version of this exercise can be found in my Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide.

You don’t have to have trustworthy people in your history. Use someone you remember when that helps, or just imagine a good adult whose intention is to take good care of you. See if you can imagine what they would say/do in your situation, with your history, limitations, options, etc. If you can’t imagine good, or just imagining a caring grown-up makes you feel more threatened, or lonelier, start smaller, with the least cruel response you can believe. Once you’ve found something good, try it, don’t keep doing the exercise.

It can be helpful to use borrowing when you’re in the adult position and not knowing what a decent adult would do, especially when the child doesn’t trust you and every response you can think of is retaliating, defending your goodness, collapsing or leaving. How would a decent adult hear that distrust? Borrow that one response. You may still feel young when you do it, it’s okay, feeling like an adult can come after you act like one.

## Let the child speak without handing over the steering wheel

You can choose to let the parts of you that feel younger express themselves without handing over the adult part of you to the caretaker in another person or without actually going back in age. In this way, you can access all of the positive aspects of what it can be like to feel younger such as feeling wonder, silliness, approaching things through the senses more, feeling it’s ok to be vulnerable, to let someone take care of you, to feel need, and to want to offload some decision making.

"It is okay for a younger part of me to talk. But I understand that the adult in me is always going to be here, listening, evaluating, protecting me and stopping me if needed. I am not going to replace my adult judgement with any child state, guide, recording or person."

This way you get the benefits of being young: curiosity, spontaneity, play, sensation, the ability to state your needs, to feel easily etc without any of the negatives of being a child, like obeying, being helpless, being scolded, the lack of good judgement etc.

## Love doesn’t have to wait for trust

What if your little one doesn't believe you love them? It is possible to love them even if they don't believe you love them. It is possible for trust to question your promises, remember when you failed to protect them, and take its time.

You could say something like, "Okay. I believe that you don't believe me. I still love you. You don't have to love me back. Tell me what feels unsafe." Try not to turn "I still love you" into a line you say on cue, though, because then it can brush the anger off, and what it's really saying is "so stop being angry."

Then respond to the complaints that are sure to come. You don't have to accept every conclusion they reach about you, but do listen for the concrete complaint without fighting the yelling and swearing around it, because even if they are coming from a place that is not completely "right," there will likely be something true in what they are saying. If it's something serious, answer it seriously. Do what you can to show your love for them. This might include some or all of the following: Learning to listen to them when they are angry without hitting back. Owning what was not okay. Working on changing yourself so that you are less likely to turn on them. Finding other ways to make up to them for what you did. Finding other ways to show your love for them (this might include taking care of them, such as by buying and cooking food for them, setting limits for them, taking them to the doctor when they need it, and doing what you said you would do). Just not hitting back is enough to start being the adult, even if it isn't enough yet for them to trust you. That takes warmth, and if there isn't any yet, borrow some.

## Instead of blame

Be aware of what you are teaching your other positions. For example, are you teaching your little one that you are going to love them no matter what? Or are you teaching them that it is their responsibility to believe your love for them, and that if they don't, it is their fault? Is it two separate positions, one making the vow to love your little one no matter what, and the other blaming your little one for not taking in your love? Or is it one position doing both of these things? Or are you blending them? Their age might not be your chronological age. Both talk in your grown-up voice, so to your little one they're the same. Be careful not to take the blaming as proof your love really is conditional, when it could just be another part talking.

When you're placing your younger self on trial, instead of asking which age of you deserves the blame, ask: What was that age of you trying to get, or get away from? What did it understand back then? What options could it actually see? Could it stand to take any of them? What happened because of the choice it made? What was missing? Some ability, or help/protection from someone else? What can you learn or repair now, and how would you act differently in such situations? Even if what it did was harmful to you and others, punishing yourself now is not going to give it the capacity it needed at the time.

# Working with your inner child

## The two-hand practice

There is a simple and easy body technique to connect with your inner child. Just place one hand in the center of your chest which is physically connecting you to your love and then place your other hand just below that which is your solar plexus and where your child can be.

Begin to send love from your heart down to your child and just notice what comes up for you. If some comes back up, let it, and send it on down again, so it goes around like a loop. That part's a gift from them, and they don't owe it to you, so if nothing comes back, just keep sending yours. If you feel resistance, just meet it with love. If you feel or see something, just meet that with love as well. No warmth today? Then rest your hands there and notice what you find, no faking required. This is a conversation with your child from your heart.

## The Nurturer

The Nurturer: Some of the work you can do as the Nurturer is to enjoy your inner child. You don't have to move their feelings, just sit back and enjoy them. Be happy that they are there. Children need to be enjoyed! Say things to your inner child like:

It's okay to feel the way you're feeling.

I want you to know that I'm here for you.

You don't need to do anything right now.

It's okay. You're not too much. You can be small here.

You can be angry here.

You can be sad here.

## The Protector

As the Protector, work with setting boundaries together. They can be simple like, "I'm going to leave this conversation because it's not safe" or "I'm going to stop texting the person who destabilizes me." It can be self-care boundaries like, "I'm going to eat" or "I'm going to get some sleep." It can be trust-related boundaries like, "I'm going to keep the promise to myself" or "I'm not going to leave you to do adult things by yourself." You can protect your inner child from yourself too, like by repairing after an internal attack: calling yourself an idiot for losing your keys again, for one. Your inner child was listening, so you go back and apologize to them.

A lot of times, inner child work doesn't work because people are doing all the fluffy stuff but then they go and text the person who is destabilizing them, or they don't eat, or they don't do whatever it is they're supposed to be doing.

If it's hard to bring up the warm stuff, you can always do something to protect your inner child. Make sure they're small enough that you can actually do them. If you're not able to, figure out what's getting in the way of you doing them. Are you scared? Tired? Do you not know how to? Is there a practical thing that's getting in your way? Have you set yourself up to do it in a way that's likely to be successful? Don't make it so that doing it successfully means you have to feel good about it, or be able to check it off a list.
