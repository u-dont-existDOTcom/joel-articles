# Appendix A: How to ask the subconscious a useful question

The subconscious isn’t an AI model, but prompt engineering offers one useful lesson: the shape of a question changes what kind of answer can appear. A prompt should make you more present, honest, loving, and able to hear the next signal—never more controlling.

I use seven elements when I want a prompt to be precise:

Role: who or what you are addressing.

Context: what situation you are bringing.

Intention: what kind of change is desired.

Constraint: safety, truth, kindness, no forcing, no false certainty, and capacity before content.

Output: image, phrase, body signal, memory, next action, question, symbol, silence, or rest.

Evaluation: true, kind, timely, stabilizing, specific, testable.

Iteration: revise after life gives feedback.

Always include a safe fallback so the analytical mind doesn’t treat silence as failure and manufacture an answer.

“And if the system needs more time to process this, or if silence is the safest answer today, let my body settle into deeper comfortable rest instead. A peaceful silence is a fully successful answer.”

Sometimes nothing answers because nothing is happening. Sometimes the answer is not yet, rest first, build capacity, or do the ordinary thing you already know to do.

## Core prompts

For clarity:

“Wise subconscious, please show me what is true, kind, and timely. If the full truth of this situation is too intense for my body to integrate today, pass it to my conscious mind only as a gentle symbol, a body signal, or a single next step. Show me my capacity to hold peace before you show me the content of the wound.”

For resistance:

“What are you protecting me from, and what would help you trust me 1% more?”

For the inner child:

“Little one, what do you need me to understand, and what can I do today so you are not alone with it?”

For the Protector:

“What real-world action would make this system safer?”

For the Leader/Guru:

“What direction serves truth, love, life, and the benefit of all beings?”

For intuition:

“Let intuition offer a signal, let analysis examine its meaning, let the body give feedback, and let reality test the interpretation.”

For separating intuition from conditioning:

“Wise subconscious, please help me look at this impulse clearly. If part of this signal belongs to family programming, religious fear, marketing, erotic imprinting, political tribe, trauma response, or a childhood bargain for belonging, let that part become visible as conditioning. Let it soften or step back only as much as is safe. Leave behind whatever belongs to true guidance, the universal heart, and a better ordinary life. Let my body feel the difference.”

For daydreaming:

“If this daydream is useful, reveal the guidance inside it. If it is avoidance, bring me gently into the present and help me deal with what I need to deal with.”

For anxiety:

“What is anxiety trying to protect, and what would help it trust the adult self?”

For hidden truth:

“If the full truth would overwhelm me, please do not reveal it all. Show me the capacity before the content.”

For integration:

“What ordinary-life action would prove that I heard correctly?”

The prompt defines the search. NLP language affects how gently the question enters. Used together, they can create a safer field for an answer without pretending the answer is automatically true.

# Appendix B: NLP without turning yourself into a sales seminar

You can practice the core method without learning one term of Neuro-Linguistic Programming. Read this appendix when you want more precision about imagery, bodily states, rehearsal, and sentence design. NLP is useful when it helps the system listen; it becomes a bag of tricks when technique replaces love, truth, contact, or consent.

## The four NLP layers

Sensory NLP changes the form of an inner experience: image, sound, distance, size, brightness, volume, posture, pressure.

State NLP makes useful states easier to access: warmth, adult presence, courage, steadiness, love, clarity.

Strategy NLP rehearses a better response: when this cue appears, the adult does this.

Language NLP shapes the sentence: pace what is already true, open awareness, presume only safe capacity, link cause and effect carefully, embed invitations, and use de-hypnotic questions when language becomes vague, absolute, manipulative, or grandiose.

## The Milton Model, minus the covert sales seminar

The Milton Model concerns the architecture of the sentence. Commands such as “Relax now,” “Stop being anxious,” or “Trust me” often make a part brace.

Part of the point of indirect language is to get past that reflexive skeptical argument without having to overpower it. The destination can still be deliberate even when the route is subtle.

Begin with what is already true, lower the pressure, presume only a small safe capacity, and invite one movement:

“As the body notices the bed beneath it, it may become a little easier to stop holding the whole day alone.”

Pace reality. Open awareness. Validate function. Presuppose capacity. Bridge gently. Invite one small movement. Test with the body.

### Pattern 1: Pacing and leading — truth before suggestion

Pacing and leading means stating what is already true before inviting anything new.

“Anxiety is here. The body is activated. A protector is trying to help. And one breath at a time, the adult can start to listen.”

The simplest form is a Truth + Invite loop:

“It is completely true that [current sensation or emotion] is here. It makes sense that my system is reacting by [current protective behavior or thought]. And right alongside that truth, my adult self is here, breathing in this room, ready to listen to whatever comes up next.”

### Pattern 2: Awareness verbs

Awareness verbs point attention without yelling at it: notice, realize, recognize, sense, feel, wonder, imagine, picture, listen, allow, discover, remember, consider.

“Notice whether the answer comes as an image, a word, a pressure, a warmth, a silence, or a next action.”

### Pattern 3: Presuppositions

A presupposition hides an assumption inside a sentence. Use that power only to presume choice, timing, capacity, safety, love, and adult leadership. Don’t smuggle in obedience.

“Before any part has to change, it can first become more understood.”

### Pattern 4: Gentle cause-effect bridges

Words such as as, while, because, when, after, before, and which means connect one event to another. Use them carefully. A gentle voice doesn’t make a false causal claim true.

Too strong: “Because I breathe, the trauma leaves.” Better: “As I breathe, the adult self may become present enough to hear one small piece of what the wound needs.”

### Pattern 5: Embedded invitations

An embedded invitation places a small direction inside a larger sentence. It should invite a modest inner movement, not hide a demand.

“And somewhere inside this breath, I can let the adult self listen.”

“As I sit here, I may allow one small truth to become clear.”

“The child does not have to trust me all at once; it can simply notice I am still here.”

Use one when it helps. Ten embedded invitations in a row feel like somebody took a weekend certification.

### Pattern 6: Ethical double binds

A manipulative double bind traps every answer inside the speaker’s agenda. An ethical one offers two genuinely safe routes.

“Would this part prefer warmth first, or space first?”

### Pattern 7: Conversational postulates

These are questions whose ordinary conversational form also acts as an invitation.

“Can the system show me the next step without showing me the whole wound?”

### Pattern 8: Tag questions

Tag questions—isn’t it, can’t it, doesn’t it—can soften a sentence. They become oily very quickly, don’t they? Use them lightly.

“The body already knows something about resting, doesn’t it?”

### Pattern 9: Nominalizations

Nominalizations turn processes into nouns: healing, safety, trust, integration, communion, clarity, rest. Their vagueness can sometimes help because the subconscious supplies its own concrete meaning.

“A little more safety can arrive in whatever way the body understands.”

### Pattern 10: Meta Model questions — the de-hypnosis tool

The Milton Model opens possibility. The Meta Model asks exactly what somebody means. Ethical practice needs both.

“How specifically?” “According to whom?” “Compared to what?” “Always?” “Never?” “What exactly would happen?” “How do I know?” “What evidence would change my mind?”

Use Milton Model to soften into the body. Use Meta Model to return to truth.

# Appendix A: How to ask the subconscious a useful question

Your subconscious mind is NOT an AI large language model. However, there is a bit of overlap in the way you can ask questions to both. So, if you know how to do prompt engineering for AI, this can be very useful to help you be more present, honest, loving etc. On the other hand, you should NEVER use it to be more controlling.

To get as much precision as possible, you need to include the following in your prompt:

Role: Who or what are you talking to?

Context: Give some context so it knows what you’re talking about.

Intention: What do you want to change or accomplish?

Constraint: What do you need to do to make sure your asking is safe, true, kind etc.

Output: How do you want to receive the answer? As an image, phrase, sensation in your body, memory, action, question, symbol, silence, rest etc.

Evaluation: How are you going to evaluate the answer? How will you know if it’s true, kind, timely, stabilizing, specific, testable etc?

Iteration: Iterate by changing your prompt depending on what you get from reality as feedback.

Most importantly, you always need to make your prompt safe, otherwise the analytical part of your mind will make up an answer just so it fits with your prompt. I usually add the following to my prompt:

“If you need more time or the best and safest answer is silence, then I ask you to make my body go into a deeper state of comfortable rest. A peaceful silence would be a successful answer to my question.”

Once you do that, here are the core prompts you can use:

## Core prompts

For getting clarity: “I ask you, my wise subconscious mind, to show me what is true, kind and timely. If what you want to show me is too intense for me to integrate, please transfer it to my conscious mind in a way I can integrate it, for instance as a symbol, a signal in my body, a next step I can take. Most importantly, show me first my capacity to be peaceful, and then, the content of my wounding.”

When you experience resistance: “What are you protecting me from? What would help you trust me 1% more?”

When you want to ask your inner child something: “What do you want me to know? What do you need me to do today so that you are not alone in dealing with it?”

When you want to bring in your Protector: “What would a minimally competent adult do here, in the external world, so that the system is safe?”

When you want to ask your leader or guru self: “What direction is the most in alignment with truth, love, life and all beings?”

When you want to listen to your intuition: “I ask my intuition to give me a signal, my analytical mind to decipher it, my body to give me feedback as to whether my deciphering is correct and reality to give me feedback as to whether my deciphering is correct.”

When you want to know if a particular intuition you have is coming from your universal heart or from your programming: “I ask my wise subconscious mind to help me see if the intuition or impulse I have about …is coming from my universal heart or from my family, my religion, marketing, erotic programming, my political tribe, my trauma response or an old deal I did as a child to belong. If it’s coming from my programming, I ask the parts responsible for it to step back and soften only as much as is safe, so that I can have true guidance from my universal heart that will help me make my life better in the most simple ways possible. Let my body give me feedback as to the difference.”

Once you get an answer, you can ask: “What can I do in my ordinary life to prove that I heard correctly?” If it tells you to do something, I'd only do it if it's loving and leaves you free. It should make sense, too. Even then, anything big or hard to undo can wait until you're calm and you've checked it against what you actually know.

The prompt will tell your subconscious what you want. The NLP in Appendix B can help you get it in a safer way, but that doesn’t make the answer automatically true.

# Appendix B: NLP without turning yourself into a sales seminar

Appendix B - NLP (Neuro-Linguistic Programming)

You do not need to know this stuff to do it but if you want to freshen up on your skills in the art of working with imagery, body states, behavioral rehearsal, talking to yourself in effective sentences etc then it might be useful, when it helps the system listen. Using the techniques can be an easy way to fall into the trap of thinking that NLP is a bag of tricks rather than something that needs to be done with love and truth and contact and consent.

## The four NLP layers

The four levels of NLP are:

Sensory Level of NLP: working with the form of an inner experience (where do you see or hear it, how far away, how big, how bright, how loud, how do you hold your body, where is the pressure etc.)

State Level of NLP: making useful states (warmth, adult presence, courage, love etc.) easier to get to.

Strategy level of NLP: practising a better response.

Language Level of NLP: pacing what is true; opening up people’s awareness; presuming you can do something safe; linking cause and effect; embedding invitations; asking de-hypnotic questions to break the effects of vague, absolute, manipulative or grandiose language.

## The Milton Model, minus the covert sales seminar

To do this we are going to use a model that’s called the Milton model. It’s all to do with sentence architecture. When we tell ourselves to ‘be better’, ‘relax now’, ‘stop feeling anxious’ or ‘trust me’ it doesn’t work, does it?

What we need to do is use indirect language to go around the part that wants to argue with us, so that we can actually get to where we want to get to.

So let’s start with something we know to be true, let’s take the pressure off, presume that we can only do a tiny bit and let’s invite one thing:

‘As my body begins to notice the sensation of the bed underneath it, it may be possible for it to begin to realize that it is not alone in carrying the burden of the day.’

We paced it; we opened up awareness; we validated the reason for the reaction; we presumed we could do it; we used a bridge word to make it easier and we invited one thing. We can then test this out with the body to see if it was possible.

### Pattern 1: Pacing and leading — truth before suggestion

So how might we pace and lead in a way that works with anxiety?

It might go something like this: ‘It is completely true that the sensation of ________ (or the emotion of _____) is coming up for me. It makes complete sense that my system is trying to react and respond to that by wanting to do or think ________. Whilst that is completely true, there is another thing that is equally true, which is that my adult self is here, breathing in the room right now, and wanting to listen to what comes up for me next.’

Truth, Invite, Truth, Invite etc. is all you need to do.

### Pattern 2: Awareness verbs

Open up awareness by using awareness verbs e.g. notice, realize, recognize, sense, feel, wonder, imagine, picture, listen to, allow, discover, remember, consider etc.

Example: ‘Notice how you find out the answer.’

### Pattern 3: Presuppositions

Presumption is a powerful thing, so please don’t use it except to presume that we will have the choice of when it will happen, that we will be able to do it, that we are safe, that we are loved and that our adult can lead us.

### Pattern 4: Gentle cause-effect bridges

Bridge words are words like ‘as, whilst, because, when, after, before, which means’ etc. These are words that link events. However, even if you say them in a lovely gentle voice it doesn’t mean that it’s true if it isn’t! It’s not very likely that just by breathing in and out that your trauma is going to go away!

### Pattern 5: Embedded invitations

ie. "And I can allow myself to experience..."

or, "As I sit here, I may begin to allow myself to become aware..."

Or, something similar that you might use when working with inner child type of parts.

You can use this, of course, but don't use ten of them one after the other! People will know that you've just come out of some weekend certification course and they won't feel so safe with you.

### Pattern 6: Ethical double binds

Manipulative Double Bind Questions are set up so that whatever you answer, you end up doing what the speaker wants.

An ethical one offers two genuinely safe routes. Ethical Double Bind Questions might be:

"Would you prefer to allow a feeling of warmth to come over you now, or would you prefer a feeling of space?"

### Pattern 7: Conversational postulates

An example of a Conversational Postulate (a yes-or-no question that works as a request) is:

"Can I allow the system to show me the next thing to do, without showing me all the details of wounding that are involved?"

### Pattern 8: Tag questions

Similarly, you can use tag questions, like "isn't it?" or "can't it?" or "doesn't it?" or others, but be aware that these can get pretty oily pretty quickly, so don't overdo it.

"Your body knows how to relax, doesn't it?"

### Pattern 9: Nominalizations

Nominalizations are words that turn a process into a noun, like 'healing', 'safety', 'trust', 'integration', 'communion', 'clarity', 'rest', etc. These are a bit vague, and sometimes that can be useful, because it allows the subconscious to fill in the blank with something very specific for that person.

"I can allow myself some safety, in whatever way my body knows how."

### Pattern 10: Meta Model questions — the de-hypnosis tool

To 'Milton Model' is to open things up. To 'Meta Model' is to say "What do you mean by that?" In order to be ethical with people, you need to do both.

"What do you mean specifically? According to who? Compared to what? Always? Never? What specifically would happen if you did? How do you know that? What evidence could you have that would change your mind?"

When people are starting to get stiff in their bodies, 'Milton' it. When you feel like you're losing touch with the truth of what's going on, 'Meta' it.
