# When “your voice” is everybody else’s voice

Sometimes the inner-child question lands nowhere because a private self had very little room to develop. In controlling families, rigid religions, enmeshed communities, or high-pressure achievement systems, performance can begin so early that a person doesn’t know what they feel, want, believe, or prefer until somebody else tells them first. Then “love yourself” produces a reasonable question: love whom? Reparenting and identity-building can proceed together. For some people the child-self is already easy to feel. For others, creating enough safety for preference, curiosity, and experimentation is part of how that self becomes easier to hear.

This may apply when:

You do not know what you want unless someone else says what they want first.

Your opinions shift dramatically depending on who is in the room.

You agree in the moment and later realize you did not agree.

You feel responsible for other people’s emotions to the point of self-erasure.

Self-love feels performative.

The question “What does my inner child feel?” produces guesses, not felt answers.

You can rebel, but you cannot yet choose.

A useful developmental explanation is that when childhood is organized around avoiding pain and preserving attachment, attention goes into reading danger, meeting expectations, and keeping the bond. That can leave less room for the ordinary experiments that answer, “What do I like? What do I want to try? Who am I when nobody is grading me?” This is a practical model, not a claim about one specific brain system.

Sometimes a temporary, intentional period of distance from the people or group supplying the answers helps quieter preferences surface. Use that distance only long enough to tell your own signal from the social echo; prolonged isolation would undermine the purpose.

The difference between a human doer and a human being helps here. When worth isn’t felt internally, it gets earned through beauty, performance, intellect, status, spirituality, or becoming indispensable to everybody else. Those can all be real gifts. They become belonging bargains when failure in the arena feels like losing yourself.

Useful questions:

“What do I like when no one is watching?” “What do I believe before I check whether others approve?” “What do I want that is neither rebellion nor compliance?” “Where am I buying belonging with self-betrayal?” “Which arena am I using to earn the right to belong?” “What would I try if nobody needed to be impressed?” “Which relationships let me remain myself?” “What am I noticing in my body, and what meaning am I giving it?”

Give the inner child a small experiment with no résumé value: painting, dancing, gardening, a language, a sport, volunteering, music, or anything else you’re allowed to be bad at. The inherited parent may demand immediate talent. The adult can answer, “We’re finding out whether we enjoy this, not proving our worth.” Give it enough repetitions to get past the humiliation of being a beginner.

For a child, belonging can feel like survival, so families, religions, romances, political tribes, spiritual groups, institutions, and online communities can reach astonishingly far into identity. Adulting means noticing where you buy belonging with self-betrayal, choosing your own ground, and learning that you can disappoint someone without disappearing. The subconscious contains a crowd of borrowed voices, which is why this belongs in a self-hypnosis guide.

“Where do I become less true in order to remain included?”

“What would belonging look like if I did not have to disappear?”

“Is this guidance, or is this a belonging program?” “Is this love, or is this fear of exclusion?” “Is this conscience, or is this conditioning?” “Is this devotion, or is this compliance?” “Is this rebellion, or is this freedom?”

Then wait for the body. It isn’t infallible, but it deserves a seat at the hearing.

# De-hypnosis: wake up inside the influence

Self-hypnosis also teaches influence literacy: noticing when somebody else is already shaping your attention. Screens, advertising, politics, religious fear, erotic imprinting, charismatic authority, belonging pressure, shame, urgency, and repetition all do it. Influence is everywhere—a lullaby, a prayer, a good teacher, a beautiful song, love. Ask what it does to your ability to see and choose.

Manipulation often arrives with urgency, specialness, fear of missing out, threatened exclusion, sexual charge, shame, confusion, rescue fantasy, destiny, obedience, secret knowledge, or far too much certainty arriving far too quickly. You may also feel that one honest question would break the spell.

Pause there. Don’t obey immediately, and don’t perform automatic rebellion either. Wake up inside the influence.

Hypnotic language turns manipulative when it smuggles in your consent, makes one choice seem inevitable, or hides a disputed claim inside the sentence.

“When you finally admit…” “As you realize I’m right…” “The only reason you resist is…” “Part of you already knows…” “Your higher self wants…” “Everyone who is truly awake understands…” “If you doubt this, that proves your ego is afraid.”

The grammar assumes the conclusion, turns resistance into proof, and makes obedience feel like awakening.

“What exactly is being claimed?” “What choice is being framed as inevitable?” “What is being presupposed?” “Who benefits if I accept this frame?” “What emotion is being amplified?” “What information is missing?” “Can I say no without being punished, shamed, mocked, abandoned, or spiritually downgraded?”

A useful guide leaves the adult self more present. Powerful states need stronger boundaries.

## Soft language can still take away choice

Permissive words can still conceal coercion. I watch for the following:

Indirect suggestion can slip past the skeptical guard because the conscious mind may never receive one clean proposition to accept or reject. The intended direction can still be quite specific even when the wording feels open, vague, conversational, or merely suggestive.

Induction hidden inside explanation: attention is being narrowed while the speaker pretends only to inform. Even an “anti-hypnosis” lesson can use induction mechanics.

Every answer means compliance: “yes,” “no,” noticing, not noticing, resistance, and silence are all redefined as proof that the suggestion is working.

“Whether you notice it or not” binds: uncertainty is converted into invisible success that cannot be checked.

Identity and ownership language: safety or goodness becomes tied to obedience, a role, the guide’s approval, or belonging to someone else.

Unannounced fractionation: moving in and out of absorption without telling you that repeated re-entry is part of the method.

Hidden post-session cues: words, gestures, blinks, sounds, or repeated listening are meant to produce later behavior that was not plainly disclosed.

Amnesia or truth inflation: dreamlike material, missing memory, imagery, or body sensations are treated as proof of what happened.

An exit that does not exit: the recording ends while preserving a role, reduced judgment, impaired movement, blankness, or an indefinite altered state.

A real choice includes at least one branch that doesn’t continue the induction: make it lighter, change the method, remain fully alert, pause, or stop. No remains no. Silence and uncertainty don’t become consent because the narrator has a soothing voice.

# Induction: gathering attention without disappearing

An induction gathers scattered attention until quieter material becomes easier to hear. You don’t have to surrender, play asleep, or obey anyone. When you’re sober, distracted, analytical, or guarded, an actual induction may help. When meditation, loveyhuasca, lucid dreaming, grief, music, prayer, fatigue, or another intense opening has already made you trancey, begin with orientation and containment instead of adding depth.

Aim for the amount of trance that suits the practice: focused, warm, receptive, embodied, and still able to stop. Depth is optional.

## What an induction is actually doing

Prepare the session, gather attention, deepen only when that helps, then let what you notice shape the words and the next response. Counting is one way in. Conversation, story, movement, repeated sound, and curiosity can also organize attention. Say so when you use them; elegance doesn’t excuse a covert induction.

For a simple, chosen target, you can test the suggestion before deepening. If the useful response is already happening, build from it. An induction helps you find a receptive way of listening; you don’t need to complete a ceremony before you’re allowed to respond.

## Please don’t put six metaphors in one trance

Choose one purpose, one starting anchor or image, and a way back. Decide whether warmth, a firm boundary, direction, or neutral witnessing would help. Floating, stairs, gardens, inner rooms, colored light, and child dialogue do not all need to report for duty at once. Switch consciously when the metaphor stops helping.

Imagery is optional. A signal may arrive through sensation, rhythm, movement, words, spatial sense, simple knowing, or no response at all. Keep the language in the world you chose: let a count mark something meaningful there, and reverse the journey when you finish. Borrow qualities such as warmth or steadiness without replacing your identity.

Pick one central verb, one sensory quality, one short refrain, and one transition phrase, then read the script aloud; long elegant sentences become soup during an induction.

## Before you begin

Read the induction and Come all the way back while fully awake. Choose the question or quality you want to explore, what you’re leaving alone today, and how much time you have. Love, play, confidence, creativity, and communion are worthwhile purposes; you don’t have to find a problem. Leave several minutes for returning and reflecting.

Choose a physically secure place without driving, water, machinery, or anyone needing your supervision. When intoxicated, acutely confused, or unable to keep yourself safe, deal with immediate support rather than exploratory hypnosis. The later Already open? practice is for orientation, not permission to intensify an unstable state.

Decide what kind of encouragement is welcome: spacious listening, warm company, or a firmer invitation to stay with a feeling. Preview eye closure, any repeated exit and re-entry, and the return. For a recording, read the script beforehand when possible. “Stop” ends the exercise; “less” reduces the demand; “quiet” stops the words. You can simply open your eyes and finish. No inner part has to approve your decision to stop.

When entry and return are unfamiliar, first rehearse them without a difficult subject. Once you know how to change direction, you don’t need to supervise every moment. Let a useful response become easier and less effortful. A no remains no; silence doesn’t authorize a narrator to add something you haven’t agreed to.

Let the next invitation grow from the response you got—not the response the script expected.Utilization means working with what actually happens: a thought, sound, movement, or objection may change where the practice goes. Notice what actually happens; no change doesn’t mean secret success. Keep safety and exit instructions plain. Appendix B teaches the richer language tools, and Let the next response guide you shows how to use them after the induction.

## Sober induction: 3–5 minutes

Read this slowly into a recorder, speak it aloud, or learn its movement and use silent words if that comes easily. Choose one anchor—a sound, bodily contact, or your two-hand practice—and an opening phrase. Keep using the same anchor where the words refer to it. Pause between paragraphs; don’t read the delivery directions aloud.

Let your eyes settle somewhere in the room, somewhere they can rest for a little while, and notice the difference between looking for something and letting what is already there come into view.

There is a surface beneath you. Notice where you meet it … the contact, the pressure, whatever you can actually feel. And while you notice that, a sound may reach you, or a breath may draw your attention, without needing you to arrange either one.

You can give this a little time.

A thought about the day may still be here. A thought about whether you’re doing this properly may be here too. There is room to notice a thought and return to the anchor you chose, without finishing the thought first.

And as you return, you may find one detail that holds your interest … something about the sound, or the contact, or that particular way of listening … and let attention stay with it long enough to become curious.

Perhaps you can give yourself the time you would give someone you care about who has begun to speak and hasn’t yet found the words. You can remain nearby, listening, without finishing their sentence for them.

Let your chosen phrase come to mind now. Say it as an invitation, and notice how it is received.

As you continue, your eyes can close, soften, or stay open. Let that be a choice you make. Listening may deepen while a sound from the room remains available in the background.

Count slowly from five to one. Give each number its own moment.

Five. Notice your chosen anchor again. Four. Let attention gather around what you find. Three. Leave a little space after the words. Two. Listen into that space, without preparing an answer. One. Stay here for a moment.

Bring your chosen concern lightly to mind, or remain with your wish for contact. Let it be present without rehearsing its whole story.

What am I noticing now?

Give the response time to take its own form. It might be a sensation, a few words, an image, a feeling, or an impulse to move. There may be nothing clear yet.

Leave room to notice, with words or without them.

## Already open? Contain before you deepen

Use this when loveyhuasca, meditation, lucid dreaming, grief, music, prayer, erotic energy, exhaustion, or intense beauty has already opened the state. Stabilize before making it stronger.

“The state is already open. I do not need to intensify it. I need to aim it, stabilize it, and keep it kind.”

Look around the room. Name where you are. Name the date, or at least the day. Feel your feet, hands, and spine. Press your thumbs gently but firmly against your index fingers. Focus on the physical friction of skin meeting skin.

“Only what can be integrated is welcome now. Anything too much can wait. The body may stay safe. The adult self remains present. The inner child does not have to carry this alone.”

Once you’re oriented, choose one intention:

“Please show me the next useful truth, symbol, feeling, question, or action — only in the amount I can live well with.”

## Belonging without betraying yourself

When you are a child you feel that you need to belong to the family in order to survive. This is why people do things for their family, religion, romantic partner, political tribe, spiritual group, institution, online community etc., that they wouldn’t otherwise do. Part of adulting is working out where you are selling yourself in order to belong and choosing your own ground, and finding out you can disappoint someone and not disappear. A large part of your subconscious mind is made out of other people. That is why you are reading a self hypnosis guide!

Where am I betraying myself in order to belong?

What does it feel like to belong without betraying myself?

Where do I think I am following my own guidance, but I am in fact following my belonging program?

Where do I think I am doing things out of love, but I am actually doing it out of fear of not belonging?

Where do I think I am listening to my conscience, but I am actually listening to my conditioning?

Where do I think I am doing something out of devotion, but I am actually doing it out of compliance?

Where do I think I am free, but I am actually rebelling?

And then look what your body tells you. It is not always correct, but it is a good place to start.

# De-hypnosis: wake up inside the influence

One of the best side-effects of learning self-hypnosis is that sudden "waking up" to all the ways your attention is being shaped in your day-to-day life. There are actually multiple patents on using screens to send subliminal messages, but even without that kind of potentially illegal tech, the screen is one connection to the omnipotent Algorithm. Which shows us ads and guides our preferences. Then we wonder why we vote for this or that politician. But there are also the older, more mundane influences: erotic imprinting (as if breast implants are actually sexy), religious fear, which tags along with shame, charismatic authority and pressure to belong. And all of that drilled repeatedly into us, because one exposure won't do the trick. Sometimes it’s benign – like lullabies or prayers or being taught by a good teacher or listening to a beautiful song or being in love. But you may want to ask yourself what effect it’s having on your ability to see clearly and make choices.

Manipulation often comes with any of the following: urgency, you are special, FOMO (fear of missing out), not being included, sexual charge, shame, confused, someone will rescue you, destiny, you should obey, secret knowledge, an excessive sense of certainty about something way too quickly, and a sense that one good question will break the spell.

## If you sense you are being influenced

First of all, if you sense you are being hypnotically influenced, stop. Don’t obey immediately, but don’t rebel automatically, just realize that you are being influenced.

Secondly, some of the ways that hypnotic language is used to manipulate are where you have consented to something without realizing it, where you are given only one choice, or where something not yet agreed upon is embedded in a sentence. For example;

You will soon realize that I am right …. Or you may, in time, decide to admit that …. Or the only reason you do not agree is …. Or part of you already knows …. Or your higher self wants …. Or everyone who is awake knows …. Or the reason you do not believe that is true is because your ego does not want you to …

If you are unsure, you may like to ask yourself the following questions;

What am I being asked to agree to? What choice am I being given? What is being assumed that I may not agree with? Who benefits if I comply? What is the emotionally charged part of what is being said? What is not being said? If I say no, what will the consequences be? (Will I be punished, shamed, laughed at, not be included, be told I am not ‘awake’ etc?)

This is a good guide to help engage your adult in the conversation. The more powerful the state the more you will need to make use of these.

## Soft language can still take away choice

Be aware that even when using permissive language, you can still take away choice. Be aware of;

Indirect suggestions can by-pass the subject’s skeptical guard. They do this by not directly telling the subject what to do. This does not mean that indirect suggestions are not specific. It simply means that they are worded in a vague, conversational, suggestive manner.

1) Utilizing induction techniques while seemingly explaining something (even something like "anti hypnosis" can use the same mechanics of induction).

2) In any way making it so that whatever the subject says shows that a suggestion has worked, when this may not be the case (this includes answering yes, no, noticing something, not noticing something, trying not to do something, not answering, etc etc etc)

3) Including phrases such as whether or not you notice etc etc

4) Including any concepts that may make a subject think they must obey, or take on a role, or that it is good/safe to please the guide or belong to someone etc etc.

5) Fractionating a subject without telling them that you are going to take them in and out of absorption a few times.

6) Giving a subject cues (words, gestures, blinks, sounds, listening a certain number of times etc) that you intend to use to make them do something at a later date, without informing them exactly what it is that they will be doing.

7) Including suggestions for amnesia, or making a subject think that something (dream, amnesia, imagery, body experiences etc) has happened when this may not necessarily be the case.

8) Not taking a subject out at the end of a recording, or leaving them with a role to play, with their judgement down, with their physical abilities down, with their minds blank, or in any other way different for an indefinite length of time.

In order for a subject to be making a REAL choice whilst in a hypnosis, there MUST be at least one way for a subject NOT to carry on with the hypnosis if they wish. This could be by making the hypnosis lighter, using a different method of hypnosis, being in full awareness whilst in a hypnosis, or being able to pause or stop the hypnosis. Just so you know, if you say no, you are quite entitled to mean NO, even if someone with a nice voice is asking you to do something! Also, just because you don't say anything, or don't know what to say, DOES NOT mean yes!

# Induction: gathering attention without disappearing

Inductions serve to gather you up so you can access quiet stuff. They're not about surrendering or playing asleep or obeying someone. Sometimes it's nice to do an actual induction if you're sober but distracted or analytical or otherwise not very trancey. If on the other hand, you're already pretty trancey for whatever reason, skip straight to "Already open? Contain before you deepen" rather than going deeper.

Get trancey enough to do what you want to do (focused, warm, receptive, embodied, able to stop, and optionally deeper)

## What an induction is actually doing

Getting yourself ready to do something. Part of that is gathering your attention up. Part of that might be deepening. Part of that is letting what you notice inform the words you're hearing and what you do next. There are lots of different ways to do this. For this method, I'm going to be counting, but you could do all sorts of things. If you want to just talk, or tell a story, or move, or make sounds, or be curious, or whatever, by all means, just do that. The only thing is, say that you're doing it. Don't pretend you're doing something else, in the name of elegance or whatever.

Test: Are you getting the response you want out of this already? If so, you might not need to deepen.

## Please don’t put six metaphors in one trance

Keep it simple. One thing you're doing, one anchor/image you're going with at first, one way of coming back, and feel free to set a tone (warm, bounded, directed, neutral witnessing, whatever) for yourself. If you're going to use metaphors just do one, and if you switch metaphors, do it in a way that's conscious.

Using metaphors and imagery is entirely optional. You can use whatever form of signal makes sense to you (feelings, rhythms, movements, words, spatial, simple knowing, lack of signal, whatever) but if you're using words, try to keep them related to whatever world you're creating. If you're going to count, do it in a way that makes sense in your world. And when you're done, come back the other way. (You can borrow qualities, but don't change who you are.)

When you're writing this, think about what the verb you're using is, what the sensory quality is, what the short refrain is, and how you're going to transition between ideas. Read it out loud as you're writing it. Long, elegant sentences don't read well when you're being induced. It's all just soup.

## Before you begin

Read this page, and the Come all the way back page, when you're awake.

Get clear on what it is you want to do during the induction. What do you want not to do today? How long do you want to spend on this? (You don't have to do this on a problem. You can do it on something you want to experience more of: love, play, confidence, creativity, communion, whatever.) Make sure you're leaving some time at the end to come back, and reflect on what you experienced.

Put yourself in a place where you can safely do this. Don't do this if you're driving, in the water, operating machinery, or otherwise need to be attending to yourself or other people. If you are intoxicated, acutely confused, or not able to keep yourself safe, then get some immediate support instead of doing exploratory hypnosis. (The part below called "Already open? Contain before you deepen" can be used to help orient you if you're already open, but don't use it to make yourself deeper if you're not stable.)

Deciding to do it. How will you coax yourself to do this? Make it spacious? Make it warm and held? Something else? Preview what you are going to do: will you close your eyes? Will you come out and go back in again? What will you do at the end? If you are going to use a recording, read the script if you can. Know that you can say "stop" at any time and the induction will end. If you want to change something, say "less" if you want it to be asking for less from you, or say "quiet" and the talking will stop. You can also just open your eyes and stop, and then come all the way back anyway. You don't need to ask any parts for permission to stop, even if a part tells you that you do.

If you are new to this or haven't done it for a while, work on the coming in and coming out part first, don't bring in the hard stuff till later. You don't have to watch yourself all the time, but for the first time, make sure you can change state. Let yourself make it easier and easier to do this and notice that it takes less and less of you to do it. A no will stay a no. The narrator can't add stuff just because you don't say anything.

Let your response guide you as to what to do next regardless of what the script is expecting. Utilize. Use what you have to go to the next place, whatever it is. Learn how to notice what is there. Learn how to notice if there is no change, and that no change is not a positive change. For more on language you can use, see Appendix B, or keep reading below for information on using the tools after induction in Let the next response guide you. Either way, keep the instructions about how to stay safe and how to get out plain.

## Sober induction: 3–5 minutes

You can read this slowly to yourself on your recorder, you can read it out loud or say it to yourself if you are good at that. Choose a sound, or a contact, or the two hands thing (below) or something to anchor yourself, and choose the thing you will say to open it up. Use the same anchor for everywhere that anchor is mentioned. Don't read the directions, just pause between paragraphs.

Script:

You can allow your eyes to rest on something in the room for a bit, and let what's already there come into view.

You can allow yourself to notice something you can feel, like the sensation of something under you, and notice the sensation of the contact, the pressure. Or maybe there is a sound, or something else to notice.

Allow yourself some time to do this.

You might notice yourself thinking, and whenever you notice yourself thinking, you can allow yourself to re-engage with your anchor and leave the thought unfinished.

You might notice something that you are doing that holds your interest, something about the sound, or sensation, or listening or something else.

Allow yourself some time to do this, as much time as you would for someone you care about who has started to talk and hasn't gotten to what she wants to say yet, so you are going to stick around till she gets it all out.

Repeat the phrase that you chose, if you want to. Say it as an invitation and notice how it's received.

You can close your eyes now if you want to. If not, it's fine to keep them open, or soften your gaze. The listening might go deeper while there's still a background sound you can hear.

Count 5 to 1, taking your time if you like.

Let your attention come back to your chosen anchor. 4. Let more of your attention come to what you've found. 3. Leave a little space. 2. See if you can listen to a space after the words without preparing an answer. 1. Take your time.

Bring to mind, if you want, the thing that you want to talk about, or just the wish to have inner contact. Just let it be there for now. You don't need to tell the whole story yet.

What do you notice now?

Let yourself notice what comes to you, if anything. It may not be clear yet what it is. It may be sensations, words, pictures, feelings, movements, or anything else.

Let yourself take your time to notice, with or without words.

## Already open? Contain before you deepen

This is for when you're already open for some reason (loveyhuasca, meditation, lucid dreaming, going through grief, listening to beautiful music, prayer, erotic energy, exhaustion, seeing something you love, etc) and want to aim and stabilize it.

Know that you're already open, you just need to aim and stabilize it, and keep it kind.

Orient yourself by looking around. Where are you? What's the date or day? How does your body feel? Where are you grounded? Can you feel your feet/hands/spine/etc? Make some contact with yourself, eg press your thumbs against the tips of your index fingers gently but firmly so that you can feel friction. Focus on that.

Say something like, "Only what I can integrate, please. If there's too much it can wait. My body may stay safe. I'll be here as an adult, this won't just be my inner child."

When you feel oriented, make an intention.

Something like, "Please show me the next truth/symbol/feeling/question/action that is useful for me, that I can integrate."
