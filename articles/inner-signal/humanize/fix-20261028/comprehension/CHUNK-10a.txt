## When the subconscious speaks like an oracle

The subconscious can notice things the conscious mind misses: anticipations, pattern reads, and sometimes something uncanny. Respect the signal without turning a vivid image or directive into an order.

When the message has large or irreversible consequences, slow the action to the scale of those consequences, ask the adult questions, and bring the message to a council.

## The council — internal first, then outward

The first council is internal: child and adult, conscious and subconscious, intuition and analysis. Reach outside when the message is bigger than those parts can hold alone. The larger and less reversible the move, the more seriously you seek other minds.

Ask people who love you, like-minded people, or somebody who has walked the path. Going alone can be legitimate, but I’d make it the last step after counsel was genuinely sought.

The opposite danger is giving away your power. “Seek counsel” can quietly become “find an authority and obey.” Another person deserves neither automatic trust nor automatic dismissal.

Peer support can lend a different kind of help. In Hearthwork, equal time, warmth, confidentiality, and respect for “no” let each person lend Nurturer and Protector without taking over the other’s Guide. Then you switch roles. A carefully timed question can help your own direction become audible; the peer doesn’t decide what the experience means.

## Forgiveness without bypass

Forgiveness can’t be forced. It grows from understanding, and pushing it too early creates bypass. Staying angry forever can also let the injury keep governing the present. All of these can remain true together: the harm happened, the boundary still matters, the person remains accountable, and forgiveness may come later when your relationship to the event changes on its own.

The understanding that helps me is seeing how conditioning, history, pain, and capacity shaped the harmful move. Given the total person they had become by that moment, the harmful move was the only one their accumulated nature had available. The harm remains real, and so do accountability, consequences, and the work of making a different response possible now. Understanding someone’s limitations should make expectations more realistic, not make the limitations less consequential.

“You do not have to forgive before you are ready. I will not use spirituality to silence your pain. I will also not build our whole life around this wound forever.”

Questions for forgiveness without erasing anything:

“What happened?” “What was the impact?” “What boundaries are still needed?” “What shaped this person into someone capable of that harm?” “What belongs to them?” “What belongs to me?” “What does accountability require?” “What would release look like without denial?”

For self-forgiveness, name what you did without taking responsibility for what wasn’t yours. Let remorse register the harm or violation of your values without making the person who acted a permanent criminal. Then move through responsibility, remorse, rectify, release: name the action and impact; allow the remorse; repair or prevent repetition where possible; and stop holding yourself hostage after responsibility has been faced.

# Appendix G: Altered states

This guide doesn’t tell anyone to use substances. It does acknowledge that many people first encounter self-hypnosis, inner-child contact, or Higher Self dialogue while altered.

Do not use altered states to force revelation. Use them to deepen listening, strengthen love, and improve ordinary life.

A past opening can be a resource without becoming a state you must reproduce. Having experienced love once, being able to access it only in a particular state, and contacting it reliably in daily life are different things. Let the experience widen what seems possible while you build usable access. If the child cannot receive it, meet the difficulty rather than escalating the intensity.

During or after an altered state, these prompts are usually more useful than demanding a revelation:

“Help me stay grounded.” “Help me receive only what I can integrate.” “Help me remember love.” “Help me return when it is time.” “Help me translate this into one ordinary-life change.”

Don’t use self-hypnosis to prolong an altered state indefinitely, recover memories, intensify fear, bypass sleep, override medical needs, or make yourself less able to communicate.

If the state makes you less loving, less grounded, less honest, less able to sleep, less able to communicate, or less able to meet ordinary responsibilities, stop deepening and start integrating.

## Stabilize before you interpret

Self-hypnosis can become very powerful during loveyhuasca, meditation, lucid dreaming, grief, sexual energy, prayer, sleep loss, or other plant-medicine-adjacent states. Sometimes the mind opens faster than the adult self can organize what is arriving.

Intensity then gets mistaken for meaning. Sometimes the inner child is finally speaking. Sometimes a Protector is showing fear, or shame, grief, anger, longing, and other buried material are asking for care. But an intense state can also be bodily overload, environmental chaos, sleep deprivation, or plain confusion. A frightening image isn’t automatically truth, a bad feeling isn’t automatically a lesson, and a “download” doesn’t deserve belief merely because it came with excellent lighting.

Before trying to interpret the state, ask a simpler question:

“What kind of problem is happening?”

## When the subconscious speaks like an oracle

Your subconscious is amazing at picking up on things your conscious mind isn't getting. Whether it's anticipations, reading patterns, or just picking up on uncanny things, it's good to respect what your subconscious is picking up on. But just because your subconscious gives you an image or tells you to do something, that doesn't mean you need to treat it like an order.

Especially if it's a big thing, or something that can't be easily undone, slow it down to the scale of the thing. Look at it with your adult's eyes, and take it to council.

## The council — internal first, then outward

Ask your child, your adult, your conscious mind, your subconscious mind, your intuition, your analytical mind. But if it's bigger than all of those, ask outside of yourself, too. The bigger and more un-undoable something is, the more you want to reach outside.

Ask people who love you, people who are like you, people who have done what you are thinking of doing. Sometimes it is right to go it alone, but I would suggest making it the last step, after you've tried to get counsel.

On the other hand, it is possible to give your power away. Seeking counsel can quietly turn into abdicating to an authority figure. Another person doesn’t get automatic trust, or automatic dismissal.

Sometimes you need the support of your peers. In Hearthwork, the concept is that you give each other equal time, warmth, confidentiality, and acceptance of “no” as an answer, and attention without interpretation, advice, or fixing. You lend each other the Nurturer and Protector for a while, but you don’t act as each other’s Guide. Then you reverse roles. Sometimes, with a question asked carefully and at the right time, your peers can help you hear your own Guide more clearly, but they don’t interpret what you experience.

## Forgiveness without bypass

We can not force forgiveness on ourselves, it has to come from a place of understanding otherwise it can be bypassing. However, if we remain in the anger forever, we are allowing the injury to dictate how we live our lives in the present. So how do we hold all of this? We just remember that it can all be true at once. They really did hurt us and they still have to answer for it, and our boundary with them still matters, but one day we may find that we have forgiven them, or at least that we’re not being run by the resentment anymore. And we don’t have to forgive first to get safe, set boundaries, hold someone accountable, get justice or get better.

A helpful understanding to come to is that while their actions were harmful, in a sense, they could only make the moves they made, given their accumulated nature at the time (conditioning, history, pain, capacity, etc). This doesn’t negate that harm was caused, and it doesn’t stop you from holding someone accountable, allowing for consequences, working on making different moves now, etc. It simply helps us come to a place where we aren’t expecting something from someone that they can’t give. It doesn’t make their limitations matter any less.

“You don’t have to forgive before you’re ready. I won’t use spirituality to silence your pain. And I won’t build our whole life around this wound forever.”

Some questions that may be helpful to ask when coming to a place of forgiveness, while still holding all of the above:

What actually happened? What was the impact? What boundaries need to be set? What shaped the other person(s) into someone capable of that harm? What is theirs? What is mine? What does accountability require? How can I let this go, without denying any of the above?

## Forgiving yourself

For self-forgiveness, name what you did, but don't take responsibility for what wasn't yours. Let yourself feel the remorse and take in the harm, or how it went against your values, and stop short of making yourself a permanent criminal.

Be specific about the action you took, and the impact it had, feel the remorse, do what you can to rectify or keep it from happening again, and then stop holding yourself hostage, and the responsibility stays. Every so often, come back to how whoever did it got to be someone who could do that, until you understand it well enough that it changes how you feel.

# Appendix G: Altered states

This is NOT to say that you SHOULD use substances, but many people have used them when they first did self-hypnosis, contacted their inner child, or talked to their Higher Self. I’d learn the inner child work sober first, with enough Nurturer and Protector in the room before you go deep (see the inner child reparenting section). Borrowed is fine, as long as it’s from someone steady who won’t try to run your trip for you.

Do not do it to try to FORCE yourself to get to a certain revelation about yourself, but rather to help yourself become more in tune with love and to better your life.

A lot of people have OPENED themselves up to something at one point in their life. If you've had a similar experience, know that there is a difference between having experienced it at one point, or only being able to access it in a certain state, and being able to get to it at any point in your life. Let that experience inform what you OPEN yourself up to. (If you can't give it to the child, try to deal with why you can't, instead of trying to ramp up the experience. Often it’s a protective part that’s seen love come with strings before. Hear it out first.)

Use your altered state of consciousness, either while you're in it or after, to help yourself by asking things like:

Help me to become more grounded. Help me to only take what I can integrate. Help me to remember love. Help me to come back when it's time. Help me to figure out how to change one thing in my life to make it better.

DO NOT use self-hypnosis to try to keep the altered state of consciousness going indefinitely, to try to recover memories, to make yourself MORE afraid, to help you not need to sleep, to help you not need to take care of medical problems, to help you become LESS communicative, etc.

In other words, if using self-hypnosis while you're in an altered state of consciousness is NOT helping you become MORE loving, MORE grounded, MORE honest with yourself, helping you SLEEP, become MORE communicative, MORE able to do your everyday responsibilities, etc, then stop trying to go DEEPER into your altered state of consciousness, and start working on integrating what you've already gotten from it.

## Stabilize before you interpret

Self-hypnosis can be POWERFUL when you are in an altered state of consciousness (i.e. when you're on loveyhuasca, meditating, have a lucid dream, are dealing with grief, are dealing with sexual energy, are praying, haven't slept, are around plant medicine, etc, etc, etc). Sometimes you may OPEN yourself up to things FASTER than you are able to put them into context with your adult self.

So what feels like a meaningful altered state of consciousness may or may not actually be meaningful - it could be your inner child, it could be a childhood guard part, it could be all of the things you're ashamed of / sad about / angry about / etc, coming out to be dealt with. Or it could be that your body is overloaded, your environment is chaotic, you haven't slept enough, etc, etc, etc. Regardless of how scary an image is, regardless of the lighting that came with the "download", regardless of how bad you feel, it doesn't necessarily mean what you think it does!

Before you even THINK about interpreting what your altered state of consciousness is, ask yourself something simpler:

“What kind of problem is happening?”
