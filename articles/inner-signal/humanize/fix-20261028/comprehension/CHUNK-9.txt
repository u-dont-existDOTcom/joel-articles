## What I will not use NLP for

To talk myself out of a real signal.

To override fear before asking what the fear protects.

To recover memories.

To manipulate anyone else.

To convince myself an intuition is true.

To erase pain, hunger, grief, anger, or a medical symptom.

While panicking, destabilized, manic, psychosis-like, chemically overwhelmed, or too dissociated to reality-test.

To make another person’s no feel like resistance.

To make someone feel spiritually inferior for disagreeing.

To hide a command inside soft language.

To create urgency where time is needed.

To bypass consent by calling something “subconscious agreement.”

To use trance, fatigue, attraction, shame, grief, or altered states to get compliance.

## Running the whole NLP sequence

Induce or contain. Gather attention, or stabilize first if you are already wide open.

Ask permission. Is it useful to work with this image, feeling, or pattern today?

Represent the pattern. How does this show up — image, voice, sensation, posture, distance, size, color, movement?

Ask what it protects. What is this trying to do for me?

Adjust the form, gently. Move it back, lower the volume, add space, change posture, slow the scene.

Bring in the three adults. Nurturer warms, Protector makes it safe, Leader points a direction.

Anchor the useful state. Heart, fingers, breath, posture.

Future pace. If the old cue shows up, what is the smallest new action?

Check the fit and the evidence. What changes in the body? Is the response kind and doable? What supports the interpretation beyond how it feels?

Exit and act. Come back to the room. Do one ordinary thing.

# Appendix C: The extended de-hypnosis checklist

Use this checklist when a person, advertisement, ideology, spiritual teaching, romantic pull, or inner voice is pressing too hard.

“I notice influence is happening. I do not need to obey it or attack it. I can slow down. I can ask what is being presupposed. I can ask who benefits. I can notice what my body is signaling without assuming what it proves. I can ask what love, truth, humility, and ordinary life would require.

If this is true, it will survive questions. If this is love, it will survive boundaries. If this is guidance, it will survive sleep. If this is for me, it will not require me to abandon my adult self.”

Before acting, ask:

Is there urgency?

Is there secrecy?

Is there shame?

Is there a threat of exclusion?

Is there a promise of specialness?

Is there sexual charge?

Is there spiritual superiority?

Is there a demand for money, sex, loyalty, silence, or obedience?

Is there a narrowing of choices?

Is questioning treated as betrayal?

Is resistance pathologized?

Is ordinary life being downgraded?

Is the child part being seduced, scared, or rescued?

Is the adult self still allowed to say no?

Several yes answers mean slow down. Don’t deepen trance or make an irreversible decision. Return to the room, body, food, sleep, time, and counsel.

A good guide increases your adult self. A bad guide replaces it. A good guide can tolerate your no. A bad guide calls your no resistance, ego, fear, low vibration, trauma, sin, or proof that you need more of their guidance.

## Hypnosis-specific audit

Before accepting a recording or live suggestion, ask:

Was I plainly told that induction mechanics are being used?

Can at least one answer lead to pause, modification, full alertness, or stopping?

Does the speaker treat not noticing as secret proof that the process worked?

Are roles, ownership, obedience, age, identity, or belonging being used to lower adult judgment?

Are fractionation, cues, later behavior, or memory effects disclosed before they are used?

Are ordinary body sensations being turned into evidence for a hidden claim?

Does the exit restore ordinary identity, movement, orientation, and critical judgment—and plainly say that the session has ended?

When an answer is unclear, stay fully alert. Ask for the exact script, change the method, or leave.

# Appendix D: Anxiety — body first

Anxiety is often a Protector trying to prevent danger, shame, abandonment, failure, exposure, illness, rejection, loss of control, or a feeling it believes would be unbearable. Don’t begin by cross-examining it. Help the body first.

Body first. Inner child second. Inquiry third. Action fourth.

## Reduced breathing / Buteyko-style breathing

Use this gently. Buteyko-style reduced breathing can help some people who overbreathe or panic, but don’t force breath holds. Air hunger can briefly increase anxiety before it settles, so go slowly.

Breathe through the nose. Let the inhale be small. Let the exhale be quiet. Pause only if comfortable. Let the breath become less dramatic. Stop if dizzy, strained, panicky, or medically concerned.

## EFT tapping

EFT can be a simple bodily ritual: name the fear, tap gently, and stay in contact with what you feel. The evidence is still developing. A 2025 systematic review found seven randomized controlled trials involving EFT for anxiety disorders; EFT beat no intervention in several of them, although the evidence should still be treated cautiously beside first-line therapies.

“Even though this anxiety is here, I am willing to be kind to the part of me that is afraid.”

## Somatic Experiencing

More info, and a free intro session on my Somatic Guide. With SE, we track gently. Don’t turn the exercise into trauma excavation.

“Where is the activation?” “Where is the neutral place?” “Can attention move slowly between them?”

## Gendlin Focusing

“What is the felt sense of this whole anxiety?”

Don’t answer from the head immediately. Give the body time to form one vague sense of the whole situation, then ask:

“What would this felt sense like me to know?”

Try a word or phrase and check how well it fits the felt sense. Then examine what it suggests about the situation; feeling right doesn’t settle that. Words and bodily attention can help correct each other.

## Body supports

Sometimes the mind is too activated to meditate usefully. Then help through the body: foot, scalp, temple, neck, or shoulder massage; warmth; pressure; a weighted blanket; darkness; familiar calming music; or a massage gun used carefully. Avoid the front of the neck, injuries, numb areas, bruises, inflamed tissue, recent surgery sites, varicose veins, and anything that produces pain, dizziness, tingling, or a symptom that feels wrong.

Other people settle with warmth, darkness, music, massage, weighted blankets, breathwork, familiar objects, or personal relaxation technology. Use the support that actually helps rather than assembling a tiny wellness trade show around the anxiety.

# Appendix E: Is the daydream helping or replacing your life?

Daydreaming can be intuition, simulation, grief digestion, creative rehearsal, symbolic teaching, or the subconscious showing a possible future, fear, or desire. It can also become avoidance, fantasy addiction, dissociation, romantic obsession, or an escape hatch from adult life.

Does the daydream return me to life with more clarity, love, and action? Or does it pull me away from life into repetition, craving, and avoidance?

When it seems useful, ask:

“What is the guidance inside this?”

When it seems to be pulling you away, change the body before debating the story. Sit upright. Open the eyes a little. Feel the feet.

“Subconscious, thank you for showing me images. If this daydream is useful, please reveal the guidance inside it. If it is avoidance, please bring me gently into the present and help me deal with what I need to deal with in a gentle but structured way.”

Then get embarrassingly practical:

“What real-life action is this daydream replacing?”

The answer may be: send the message, stop sending the message, clean the room, eat, sleep, grieve, create, apologize, leave, tell the truth, make the plan, ask for help, return to the task, or stop chasing unavailable love.

# Appendix F: Self-trust, outside guidance, and oracle-like states

The synchronicity cue above turns noticing into orientation and reality-testing rather than an oracle.

Suggestibility varies wildly. Some people open more with an external hypnotist, some with themselves, some only in particular states, and some barely respond to commands while responding deeply to imagery, music, metaphor, or spiritual feeling. A professional can bring pacing, containment, and another set of eyes. Self-hypnosis gives you instant follow-up, real-time correction, and a private line to your own protectors.

Self-hypnosis may be best for daily intimacy with the subconscious. Skilled outside support may be best for trauma complexity, blind spots, dissociation, destabilization, and relational wounds that need another nervous system.

## Finding a hypnotist who can work with you

Ask what they are trained to treat, how they use hypnosis, and how they respond when a suggestion doesn’t fit. For trauma work, ask about actual trauma and dissociation training, not only a hypnosis certificate. Credentials matter without guaranteeing competent care.

Describe what has helped, what hasn’t, your preferred directness, acceptable touch and imagery, and how you’ll stop. Ask about unexpected reactions, uncertain memories, and the full return. Firm encouragement can fit; refusal still ends it.

Evaluate what actually happened, not the method’s name. Was its purpose explained? Did you agree? How did the practitioner respond to you? Discomfort alone doesn’t settle the question; treating every objection as proof you need more of the same deserves scrutiny. Ask the app or another informed person for explanation, without expecting a practitioner to be certified from a brief account. Self-hypnosis, combined work, or another practitioner remain your choices.

## Running the whole NLP sequence

Here is a rough outline of the entire NLP process:

Induce/Contain the person (get their attention, or stabilize them first if they are already wide open).

Ask for their permission.

Get a representation of their pattern (image, feeling, size, color, location, movement, etc.).

Find out what that part/pattern is protecting for them.

Gently change some aspect of their pattern (location, volume, space, posture, speed, etc.).

Put 3 good adults in with the person (Nurturer to warm, Protector to make it safe, and Guide to point a direction). In real life they’re all part of the one inner adult, so we can imagine them splitting apart if need be and coming together as well.

Anchor the person's good state (heart, fingers, breath, posture, etc.).

Future pace a scenario for them where their old pattern would have triggered, and have them do a small new behavior (If...then... statement).

Have them check in with their body for changes and look for evidence to support their new interpretation of the situation.

Get the person out of the altered state (bring them back to the room and have them do something).

Remember that often the anxiety comes from a childhood guard part of you. It’s what protects you when your consciously installed parental Protector isn’t functioning, the same as in childhood. ## Reduced breathing / Buteyko-style breathing

Work on your reduced breathing / Buteyko breathing. (NOTE: there are some good things to be said for slowing your breathing down, if you are breathing too much, or panicked. However, please don’t hold your breath until you are air hungry, which can make you more anxious in the short term.) Breathe in small amounts through your nose and let it out quietly, through your nose too. Only pause if that's comfortable. Can you make your breathing very quiet? If you feel bad, dizzy, or panicky, or have medical issues, STOP!

EFT tapping: One of the easiest ways to do EFT is to say something like, “Even though I have this anxiety, I am willing to be kind to the part of me that is afraid,” while you tap gently on your body and stay with what you feel. There isn’t a ton of evidence for EFT, but there are some studies on anxiety. A 2025 systematic review found 7 randomized controlled trials for anxiety disorders, and noted that EFT was more effective than no intervention in several of them. So use the evidence with caution, and know that there are first-line therapies for anxiety.

# Appendix E: Is the daydream helping or replacing your life?

Sometimes daydreaming is something that your subconscious mind is doing to help you. It is simulating an idea or event to help you digest grief or teach you something. The very act of daydreaming may be helping you to access your creativity or allowing your subconscious to show you a potential future. Sometimes your subconscious will show you things that you may be afraid of. Sometimes it will show you what you want.

Other times daydreaming may be taking you away from your life in a detrimental way. It may be that you are escaping reality and are addicted to fantasies rather than the real life. You may be dissociating or obsessing about romantic partners or escaping from being an adult.

How do you know which it is? Simply ask yourself if the daydreaming is taking you closer to life, with more clarity, love and ability to take action or if it is taking you away from life, in a repetitive and craving way.

If it is helping you then simply ask yourself what the guidance is and how you can use it.

If it is taking you away from life then change your body position by sitting upright, open your eyes, feel your feet and ask your subconscious to help you. Simply say to yourself, “Dear subconscious thank you for showing me images. If what you are showing me is helpful then please also show me the helpfulness in it. If what you are showing me is a way of avoiding dealing with life then please show me in a gentle but structured way how to deal with life as it is right now.”

Then ask yourself what it is that you need to do in real life that you are avoiding by daydreaming.

You may need to send a text message or stop sending text messages. You may need to clean up your room, eat, sleep, deal with grief, create, apologize, leave, tell the truth, make a plan, get help, get back to a task that you were supposed to be doing, stop chasing love that is not available to you etc.

# Appendix F: Self-trust, outside guidance, and oracle-like states

[Please see how to use synchronicity as a cue, not an oracle, above.]

One of the issues here is that people vary dramatically in how suggestible they are, both internally and externally, and different people vary in how suggestible they are in different states. Different types of suggestion (commands, imagery, music, metaphors, spiritual, etc) can be very different for different people. So there are definite advantages to both having someone work with you (pacing, containment, another set of eyes), and doing things yourself. Being able to follow up immediately, to correct something in real time, to have a line of communication with guards, etc.

That said, self hypnosis may be the best way of talking to your subconscious on a day to day basis. If you’re dealing with particularly complicated trauma, blind spots, dissociation, destabilization, or relational wounds that need another nervous system, skilled outside support may be best.
