Introduction

Quick start: do this first

To work on difficulties or cultivate positive qualities (love, curiosity, play, confidence, etc.), simply do these steps, in order: 1) Gather attention; 2) Notice what is here; 3) Offer a fitting response; 4) Wait; 5) Come all the way back. Not much needs to happen in each session!

Prior to your first session, read through the induction and the full return to waking life, so you’re more familiar with them. Then put the screen away. You’re learning to run the practice yourself, not reading from a teleprompter like a news anchor person.

Follow whatever shows up, even if it’s an objection or a long silence, and let it shape what you invite next. Vary the invites as needed to be responsive & warm, to create a boundary, to give direction or more time, etc. Complete the return to normal waking consciousness. After, note what happened & any real-world actions it calls for.

If at any point you feel panicky, disoriented, more unreal, or less functional, this is not the time for bravely sticking with difficult feelings (that's another choice, made freely & while staying present). Stop deepening, return to the room & get appropriate support.

What I mean by hypnosis

APA Division 30 commonly defines hypnosis as focused attention, reduced peripheral awareness, and increased responsiveness to suggestion. That’s basically true, but there’s a lot more than meets the eye. In fact, hypnosis is such a broad and common type of altered state that it’s hard to confine it to one use. But I’m using hypnosis here as a therapy.

And hypnosis as a therapy is as old as time itself. While the modern technique of it was discovered by Franz Anton Mesmer in the 1770s, shamans have been using dancing, music, and drugs to induce hypnosis for therapeutic purposes forever. We enter into trance states every night which resemble hypnosis as we go to sleep and as we wake up as well. Those states can also be used for deep therapy.

Hypnosis Evidence

Is there real (not magical) evidence for hypnosis? Yes. In 2024, there was an overview of 49 meta-analyses containing a total of 261 distinct primary studies. Evidence was strongest for pain, medical procedures, and work with children and adolescents, but varied with different outcomes and study design quality.

Is there evidence for self-hypnosis? Yes. There has been a systematic review of Randomized Controlled Trials of self-hypnosis, finding benefit in studies of pain, childbirth, children, stress, anxiety etc. Self-hypnosis seemed more useful when people learned it as a real skill and practiced it several times than when they just listened to an audio file.

Here I will be teaching you a real skill, so you don’t end up a consumer of more and more elaborate trance recordings.

What to Use Self-Hypnosis For, and What Not To

The Mayo Clinic warns that using hypnosis to deal with stressful events from earlier in life may bring up strong emotions and that there are other types of treatments that may be more helpful with historical trauma.

Use self-hypnosis to help you: regulate yourself, talk to yourself, reparent yourself, gain clarity, change habits, make your life safer in the now, integrate emotions, and take action in ordinary life.

Don’t use self-hypnosis to: force yourself to remember trauma, force yourself to prove trauma through memories, push symptoms down instead of getting medical attention, destabilize yourself, or replace appropriate clinical care.

A useful map of the mind

Note: whenever I refer to the subconscious, I’m referring to all of the processes under the surface of your conscious awareness: perceptions, emotions, desires, avoidance tendencies, imagery, memories, body signals, habits, intuitions, automatic behaviors and so forth. You can work with all of these together as if it’s all one mind, or you can work with it as a set of parts, an ecology if you like. There isn’t a right or wrong way to approach it. They’re both maps. Don’t form a priesthood around either one!

Working with parts gives you precision, and working with the Higher Self gives you coherence. Communion is going back and forth between the two.

If you need to work with the whole, the subconscious as one:

a) Invoke the subconscious in some way. Something simple like ‘Hello, wise subconscious, please help me understand…’ or whatever works for you.

If you need to work with the ecology in the subconscious, notice what each part wants: the child parts usually want love, the guards want you safe, the critic wants you to meet some standard, the fantasy making parts are after possibility, body memories want to be recognized, the social part wants to belong, the spiritual part wants God or source or what’s best for all beings, and the wiser part just wants what’s true. Then:

a) It’s usually a good idea to start with unity. So invoke your Higher Self: “Hello, my Higher Self/universal heart-mind/wise subconscious, please guide me…”

b) Move into differentiation. “Which part is afraid? Which part wants to protect you? Which part wants to avoid the whole thing? Which part knows what to do? Which part is too young for this?” Etc.

c) Move back into unity again. “And all of these parts belong to the one life, and as the adult in charge of this life, you can lead with love…” Etc.

Sometimes start with the whole

Some people make themselves too fragmented by starting to work with parts. ‘Here’s a part! Here’s another one! I’ll get twelve of them in a room and give them all an office to hang around in!’ If that happens to you, it’s better to start from the sacred whole.

Start like this: ‘I’m approaching this from the universal heart. I’m approaching this from my Higher Self. I’m approaching this from the part of me that knows it belongs to life before it belongs to any wound, or role, or family, or group, or identity, or ideology, or story. So please help me see what’s true and kind and timely and stabilizing and useful here.’ Etc.

It can be that you can’t even access your Higher Self, or the universal heart, or whatever you call it. (By the way, if it doesn’t work for you, don’t use it!) In that case, start by just witnessing whatever is coming up. Notice the room, your body, the fact that you’re in a younger state etc. Being able to notice is enough adult presence for the first step.

Once you have the ability to notice, and to differentiate, you don’t need to keep rehearsing it. Just work on whatever it is you need to work on (often being warm to yourself, protecting yourself and knowing what to do etc). So even though it can be useful to talk about your Higher Self, it doesn’t mean you already have all the good inner parenting functions etc that you need. And I don’t think the Higher Self has any specific wisdom. It’s a vantage point, where more wisdom and compassion can come up, but problems can also look like they were never problems at all from up there, and that can ironically lead instead to more foolish indifference.

When something specific comes up, that’s the time to sort out what it is. “Is this my inner child? Or is this a guard? Is this my body? Is this fear? Is this intuition? Is this my social part? Is this divine guidance, or is this my interpretation of divine guidance?”

The inner child, the guards, and the inner adult’s three jobs

Your inner child won’t trust you right away. It’s not going to be enough for you to meditate, say three nice sentences to the inner child and expect to have its trust. What’s going to help your inner child to trust you is you, the person you are right now, becoming reliable.

Here I’m going to talk about what you develop in yourself as you reparent your inner child. When the adult role is not available yet

The problem is that some people do not have access to their adult functions at will. Instead, they may go blank, access an inherited inner critic, or feel like a child part who may be playing at being an adult (what I call forced adulthood). Telling them to just access their adult is like telling a baby bird to just fly.

For that baby bird, I use a kind of provisional bridge (also found in Schema Therapy) that I call “borrowed adulthood,” which I'll detail here.

  1. First, access your witnessing position if necessary, just noticing what’s there, without needing it to be warm or wise or certain. If you already know how to access different internal positions, move straight to the adult function you need to borrow.

  2. If you have a safe adult in your life, you can borrow from them. If not, borrow from a mentor, an older brother/sister, your imagined future self, your god/goddess or higher power, a value you have, a plan you have written, etc. It’s ok to just borrow what you need right now, warmth or a boundary or direction, just like you would borrow a cup of sugar from a neighbor.

  3. Next, put on your adult hat as if you are an apprentice adult, and pick the words or the action yourself. Then do at least part of it.

  4. Last, return the adult hat, noticing what you borrowed and what you were able to do on your own (even if it’s only 5%).

The adult hat is only borrowed: the person you borrowed it from stays beside you, and you still make the choice. The longer version of this exercise can be found in my Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide.

You don’t have to have trustworthy people in your history. Use someone you remember when that helps, or just imagine a good adult whose intention is to take good care of you. See if you can imagine what they would say/do in your situation, with your history, limitations, options, etc. If you can’t imagine a good adult, or just imagining a caring grown-up makes you feel more threatened, or lonelier, start smaller, with the least cruel response you can believe. Once you’ve found something good, try it out instead of repeating the exercise.

It can be helpful to use borrowing when you’re in the adult position and don’t know what a decent adult would do, especially when your inner child doesn’t trust you and every response you can think of is retaliating, defending your goodness, collapsing or leaving. How would a decent adult hear that distrust? Borrow that one response. You may still feel young when you do it. Feeling like an adult can come after you act like one.

Love doesn’t have to wait for trust

What if your little one doesn't believe you love them? It is possible to love them even if they don't believe you love them. Their trust can question your promises, remember when you failed to protect them, and take its time.

You could say something like, "Okay. I believe that you don't believe me. I still love you. You don't have to love me back. Tell me what feels unsafe." Try not to turn "I still love you" into a line you say on cue, though, because then it can brush the anger off, and what it's really saying is "so stop being angry."

Then respond to the complaints that are sure to come. You don't have to accept every conclusion they reach about you, but do listen for the concrete complaint without fighting the yelling and swearing around it, because even if they are coming from a place that is not completely "right," there will likely be something true in what they are saying. If it's something serious, answer it seriously. Do what you can to show your love for them. This might include some or all of the following: Owning what was not okay. Working on changing yourself so that you are less likely to turn on them. Finding other ways to make up to them for what you did. Finding other ways to show your love for them (this might include taking care of them, such as by buying and cooking food for them, setting limits for them, taking them to the doctor when they need it, and doing what you said you would do). Just not hitting back is enough to start being the adult, even if it isn't enough yet for them to trust you. Even that takes some warmth, and if you don't have any yet, borrow some from someone who's been good to you, or from an adult you imagine.

Instead of blame

Be aware of what you are teaching your other positions. For example, are you teaching your little one that you are going to love them no matter what? Or are you teaching them that it is their responsibility to believe your love for them, and that if they don't, it is their fault? Is it two separate positions, one making the vow to love your little one no matter what, and the other blaming your little one for not taking in your love? Or is it one position doing both of these things? They may be younger than you are now. Both talk in your grown-up voice, so to your little one they're the same. Be careful not to take the blaming to mean your love really is conditional, when it could just be another part talking.

When you're placing your younger self on trial, instead of asking which age of you deserves the blame, ask: What was that age of you trying to get, or get away from? What did it understand back then? What options could it actually see? Could it stand to take any of them? What happened because of the choice it made? What was missing? Some ability, or help/protection from someone else? What can you learn or repair now, and how would you act differently in such situations? Even if what it did was harmful to you and others, punishing yourself now is not going to give it the capacity it needed at the time.

Working with your inner child

The two-hand practice

There is a simple and easy body technique to connect with your inner child. Just place one hand in the center of your chest, which physically connects you to your love, and then place your other hand just below that, on your solar plexus, where your inner child can be.

Imagine a divine Love from above, with the inner child below, in the solar plexus area. The inner adult is present. You can also use the hand on the solar plexus to massage that area.

Gently breathe in lots of love and begin to send that love from your heart down to your inner child and you breathe out, noticing what comes up for you. If some comes back up, let it, and send it on down again, so it goes around like a loop. That part's a gift from them, and they don't owe it to you, so if nothing comes back, just keep sending yours. If you feel resistance, just meet it with love. If you feel or see something, just meet that with love as well. If you can't find any warmth today, just leave your hands where they are and notice what's there. Don't pretend to feel something you don't. Let it go back and forth between your heart and your inner child, like a real conversation.

The Protector

As the Protector, set boundaries, so your inner child can believe the warm stuff. They can be simple like, "I'm going to leave this conversation because it's not safe" or "I'm going to stop texting the person who destabilizes me." It can be self-care boundaries like, "I'm going to eat" or "I'm going to get some sleep." It can be trust-related boundaries like, "I'm going to keep the promise to myself" or "I'm not going to leave you to do adult things by yourself." You can protect your inner child from yourself too, like by repairing after an internal attack: calling yourself an idiot for losing your keys again, for one. Your inner child was listening, so you go back and apologize to them.

A lot of times, inner child work doesn't work because people are doing all the fluffy stuff, but then they go and text the person who is destabilizing them, or they don't eat, or they don't do whatever it is they're supposed to be doing.

If it's hard to bring up the warm stuff, you can always do something to protect your inner child. Make sure it's small enough that you can actually do it. If you're not able to, figure out what's getting in the way of you doing it. Are you scared? Tired? Do you not know how to? Is there a practical thing that's getting in your way? Have you set yourself up to do it in a way that's likely to be successful? It still counts if doing it doesn't make you feel better right away, and checking it off a list doesn't mean it helped.

The Guide

You can also work with your Guide. Call them whatever you want: Leader, Guru, Wise One, Sage, Inner Teacher, Director, Higher Adult, whatever.

Ask them for help with directing your life. The Guide holds standards, the way a good parent would, and asks hard things of your inner child so it can grow, at its own pace. Your parent’s harsh tone doesn’t come with the job, though, so be careful not to ask for the “tough it out, you don’t have needs, you just need discipline!” kind of direction. Without the Guide, you can also slide from looking after your inner child into spoiling it: sweets and screens can pass for loving yourself, and so can getting it out of anything hard. Ask them for help in directing you to do adult things in a way that takes your needs into account: asking for the help you need, expressing your preferences, letting a safe person see you in an imperfect way, finding ways to get your need for closeness met without punishing someone for their need for space, etc. etc.

Affirmations might be helpful here, too: “We’re not just here to feel good. We’re here to become whole. We’re here to belong to life, not only to people who approve of us. We’re here to serve truth/beauty/love/God/source/nature/the universe/dharma/the Tao/the benefit of all beings,” etc. etc.

Start with whatever adult function you can connect to, but most of us need enough Nurturer and Protector first, before the Guide. Otherwise the Guide just overrules your inner child in the name of something higher (spiritual bypassing, dressed up as direction). Even if you start with protection and can’t do the warmth stuff yet, make sure the direction you’re being led in is appropriate for your inner child’s growth (i.e. not depriving them of things they need, like play or support, etc.)

Those are not failures. Often what you're experiencing is a very charming guard. It's what protects you when your consciously installed parental Protector isn't functioning, the same as when you were a child. It might not be a guard at all, though. Maybe it's your inner child, mad that you took so long to come back. Maybe it's your inherited parent script, or something from earlier today, or a bit of each.

You can tell that part something like, “I see you. I'm not going to force my way past you. What's going on?” Or however you'd actually say that. Then wait and listen patiently. If nothing comes, that's fine for now. No need to answer for that part just because you'd set this up as a healing session. Later on, you can ask it, “What are you afraid would happen if you stopped protecting me by…?”

If necessary, take time and listen. (Perhaps they are afraid that you will lie to yourself again or pretend again or that it will be too painful or you will spiritually bypass again or you will forgive someone who shouldn’t be forgiven yet or you will see what you want to see or that you will leave them hanging after the session or…)

Now talk to the guard. Tell them that it makes sense that they want to protect you and that they don’t have to stop protecting you, but that you want to know what could make them trust you 1% more that you can protect yourself. If it says no, I’d listen, and if it says no to going deeper in this work or to an altered state, I’d stop there and not push past that no, even when it sounds like a prediction.

Most of the time, the guard wants you to do certain things: set boundaries, do whatever you are supposed to do, eat, not text someone, keep a promise, go to sleep, talk to the therapist, admit that you owe someone money, write something down, etc.

Your inherited parent script

One important thing to become aware of when approaching your inner child is your inherited parent script. This is the set of rules and ways of treating yourself that you internalized as a child, mostly based on how your parent(s) treated you, and it doesn’t always sound like a voice. This is not the same as your inner adult (Nurturer, Protector, Guide) that you build up on purpose. Often this script acts as a critic or warden, telling your inner child not to cry or complain. You might be hearing, "Don't be needy! Don't embarrass us! And don't be independent." So when you begin talking to your inner child in a new, loving way, that dusty old script may huff and puff that what you’re doing is “fake”.

Protection that knows when to stop scanning

Do you want to embody your Protector? Then you could practice this:

  1. orient yourself (look around, feel the support around you, etc.)

  2. assess the situation (know what you know, what you don’t know and check out what you need to)

  3. differentiate between actual/real danger, unknown, past or imagined dangers

  4. set boundaries (start with one and keep it in proportion to the danger; move, stop, delay, ask, leave, get support, etc.)

  5. verify if it is safe enough (you don't need perfect certainty, just enough real-world evidence to take the next step)

  6. stand down and rest (stop scanning once staying on alert isn't helping anymore)

Inner child reparenting

I see a lot of connection between reparenting therapy and self hypnosis. I’d learn to do inner-child therapy sober first, in your ordinary mindstate, since that’s the one you’ll be living in, and get enough Nurturer and Protector in the room before you go deep. When you go into hypnosis, your inner child might come to you. It might show up in any form. It can be an image, a posture, a voice, a memory, an emotion, etc. It can be any age. It can be any vulnerable part of you. If there is no child there, just work with a guard or whatever you find.

Don’t rush the repair

When your inner child does show up, spend some quality time with them before taking off the kid gloves, so to speak, and jumping into the healing work.

They might like to hear something like, “Little one, I’m here with you now. You don’t have to work to explain anything. I'm not asking you to impress me with how fast you can get better. Can I help you feel less alone?”

What does your inner child need? It can be anything: warmth, to play, to be protected, to mourn, to be angry, food, rest, boundaries, better relationships, to stop fantasizing, to stop working their butt off, that you keep a promise to them today, etc. etc.

When a younger you, a guard, a feeling or a signal comes up in your body, take it slowly:

A. Prepare yourself to tend to them with some privacy, but still able to access support if needed. Check how much grown-up you is really in the room first. Can you take a moment before you react? When you decide something, do you follow through? If you answered "No," because right now you’re mostly still stuck identifying as the frightened little boo, hold off on the deeper conversation. Do what makes you feel adult before you try to talk to little you.

  1. B. Look at what's there before judging or deciding what it is.

  2. C. Ask them what you can do or if you can move closer to them. Even if you feel like you already know what they need, ask if they'd prefer warmth, space, verbal reassurance, silence, distance, whatever.

  3. D. Give them what you can: pick a need and do the smallest thing that would help.

E. Give yourself some time to let what you did sink in- it may take sleep, etc.

F. Reconnect with them if they're willing- don't expect them to merge, go away, forgive you, become happy, etc.

G. Stop and rest before the caring turns into another performance, and reconnect with them later to see what was helpful.

Remember the goal isn't to make yourself good as new but to make yourself good as you- more supported and connected to yourself. If you don't like the idea of part work or it makes you feel more divided, you can work with a signal or a feeling instead of a part, or with an imaginary younger you that you feel embodies the issue you're having.

Play and rest still need an adult present

When you're reparenting, it's okay to play with things that interest your younger states, whether that means touching things of different textures, drawing, exercising, roleplaying with a plushie in a different voice, etc. As long as your reality testing is good and you remember that you aren't a child or pet or doll or whatever and that nobody owns you, it's okay to work on getting your play ability back. A lot of the best discoveries come when you're both just messing around. Play not only paves the way for therapy, but is a part of how successful therapy should be measured.

Resting doesn’t mean you have to become helpless. It’s okay to limit the number of decisions you are going to be making for a certain amount of time, it’s okay to put the things you might not get to on a list, and it’s okay to let your inner adult keep watch for a while. You can still get up and move around, stop resting, and get back to what you were doing whenever you need to.

Non-dominant hand dialogue

If talking out loud or visualizing conversations feels fake, try writing it out. Tell your inner child or guard to write with your nondominant hand, and talk to them with your dominant. It might be a little hard to write with your nondominant hand but you can kind of do it. So for example, they might say "I don't trust you" with your nondominant hand, and you can say with your dominant hand "I know you don't trust me right now but I love you and I want to know how I've fucked up. I don't expect you to trust me but I want to hear you out and give you a promise I can keep today" and they can say "what promise?" and you can say "I'm going to make sure I eat today/ clear off my desk/ not text so-and-so". Only say the "I love you" part if you can feel it. If you don't feel the love yet, say you care.

When “your voice” is everybody else’s voice

It can be difficult to connect with your inner child if your inner child didn’t have the opportunity to develop. This happens often when you grow up in a situation where you don’t have a private self, for example when a controlling family, a rigid religion or an enmeshed community takes over very early in your life. Or you are pushed to achieve very early in life. The result is that you don’t know what you feel, what you want, what you believe, what you like and dislike until you have heard someone else’s opinion about it. If you are in that situation, it can be difficult to follow the advice to ‘love yourself’. You don’t even know who ‘yourself’ is! So you can do reparenting and finding yourself in parallel. For others, it is very easy to work with the inner child because they can feel it right away.

You are in the first camp if:

Your opinion is very different when you are with different people

  • You agree with people in the moment but when you are by yourself you realize that you don’t agree with them

  • You take responsibility for the emotions of others, at the expense of yourself

  • When you try to love yourself, it feels more like a show

  • When you try to find out how your inner child feels, you feel that you are guessing instead of feeling

  • You can rebel, but you can’t choose yet

One way of explaining this is that your childhood didn’t leave you much room for finding out what you like, what you want to try out and who you are when nobody is grading you. You were too busy avoiding pain, reading what was dangerous and what you had to do to stay in a good bond, etc.

Taking a break. Sometimes it may be wise to remove yourself, for a time, from the people or group that you normally would get answers from. Some people come back later on different terms, and sometimes the break has to be long-term because the pattern of losing yourself with them is deeply entrenched. As you become more your own person, you learn to tell which of those apply. You want to let your quieter preferences bubble up to the surface so you can be sure you’re not just echoing your social group.

What is the difference between a human being and a human doing? If you don’t feel good enough as a human being, you will try to become good enough as a "human doing" by, for example, being beautiful, doing something well, being smart, having a certain status, being spiritual, etc. Those can all be real gifts, although they still become a kind of bargain for belonging when failing in these areas feels like losing what it means to have your own inherent value.

So in order to find yourself, you can ask yourself:

What do I like when nobody is looking?

What do I believe when I don’t check with other people?

What do I want that is neither in rebellion nor in compliance with others?

What can I try on that I don’t need to be good at?

With whom can I be myself?

What do I feel in my body and what do I make out of it?

Give your inner child the opportunity to do something that has no value for its resume and that it doesn’t need to be good at. For example, painting, dancing, gardening, learning a language, playing a sport, doing volunteer work, playing a musical instrument, etc. Because often your inherited parent script tells your inner child that it is supposed to be good at something as soon as it starts doing it, which is humiliating. The more consciously sculpted adult in you can answer, “We’re finding out whether we enjoy this, not proving our worth.” So try things out long enough until the humiliation is gone.

Belonging without betraying yourself

When you are a child, you feel that you need to belong to the family in order to survive. This is why people do things for their family, religion, romantic partner, political tribe, spiritual group, institution, online community etc., that they wouldn’t otherwise do. Part of adulting is working out where you are selling yourself in order to belong and choosing your own ground, and finding out you can disappoint someone and not disappear. A large part of your subconscious mind is made out of other people.

Where am I betraying myself in order to belong?

What does it feel like to belong without betraying myself?

Where do I think I am following my own guidance, but I am in fact following my belonging program?

Where do I think I am doing things out of love, but I am actually doing it out of fear of not belonging?

Where do I think I am listening to my conscience, but I am actually listening to my conditioning?

Where do I think I am doing something out of devotion, but I am actually doing it out of compliance?

Where do I think I am free, but I am actually rebelling?

And then notice what your body tells you. It is not always correct, but it is a good place to start.

De-hypnosis: wake up inside the influence

One of the best side-effects of learning self-hypnosis is that sudden "waking up" to all the ways your attention is being shaped in your day-to-day life. There are actually multiple patents on using screens to send subliminal messages, but even without that kind of potentially illegal tech, the screen is one connection to the omnipotent Algorithm. Which shows us ads and guides our preferences. Then we wonder why we vote for this or that politician. But there are also the older, more mundane influences: erotic imprinting (as if breast implants are actually sexy), religious fear, which tags along with shame, charismatic authority and pressure to belong. And all of that drilled repeatedly into us, because one exposure won't do the trick. Sometimes it’s benign – like lullabies or prayers or being taught by a good teacher or listening to a beautiful song or being in love. But you may want to ask yourself what effect it’s having on your ability to see clearly and make choices.

Manipulation often comes with any of the following: urgency, feeling special, FOMO (fear of missing out), the threat of not being included, sexual charge, shame, confusion, the promise that someone will rescue you, destiny, pressure to obey, secret knowledge, a sense of certainty about something that comes way too quickly, and a sense that one good question will break the spell.

If you sense you are being influenced

First of all, if you sense you are being hypnotically influenced, stop. Don’t obey immediately, but don’t rebel automatically, just realize that you are being influenced.

Secondly, here are some of the ways people use hypnotic language to manipulate: you consent to something without realizing it, you only get one choice, or someone slips something you never agreed to into a sentence. For example:

“You will soon realize that I am right…” Or “You may, in time, decide to admit that…” Or “The only reason you do not agree is…” Or “Part of you already knows…” Or “Your higher self wants…” Or “Everyone who is awake knows…” Or “The reason you do not believe that is true is because your ego does not want you to…”

If you are unsure, you may like to ask yourself the following questions:

What are they asking me to agree to? What choice are they giving me? What are they assuming that I may not agree with? Who benefits if I comply? What is the emotionally charged part of what they’re saying? What are they leaving out? If I say no, what will the consequences be? (Will they punish me, shame me, laugh at me, leave me out, tell me I’m not ‘awake’ etc?)

And afterwards: do I come away from them more present and grown up, or less? Be extra careful with all this when you’re in a deep or powerful state, because that’s when you need your boundaries most.

Soft language can still take away choice

Indirect suggestions can bypass the subject’s skeptical guard. They do this by not directly telling the subject what to do.

Here are some of the ways that happens:

1) Utilizing induction techniques while seemingly explaining something (even something like "anti hypnosis" can use the same mechanics of induction).

2) In any way making it so that whatever the subject says shows that a suggestion has worked, when this may not be the case (this includes answering yes, no, noticing something, not noticing something, trying not to do something, not answering, etc etc etc)

3) Including phrases such as “whether or not you notice” etc etc, so either way counts as success

  • 4) Including any concepts that may make a subject think they must obey, or take on a role, or that it is good/safe to please the guide or belong to someone etc etc.

5) Fractionating a subject without telling them that you are going to take them in and out of absorption a few times.

6) Giving a subject cues (words, gestures, blinks, sounds, listening a certain number of times etc) that you intend to use to make them do something at a later date, without informing them exactly what it is that they will be doing.

7) Including suggestions for amnesia, or using dreams, gaps in memory, imagery, body experiences etc as evidence to make a subject think that something has happened when this may not necessarily be the case.

8) Not taking a subject out at the end of a recording, or leaving them with a role to play, with their judgment down, with their physical abilities down, with their minds blank, or in any other way different for an indefinite length of time.

In order for a subject to be making a real choice while in hypnosis, there must be at least one way for them not to carry on with the hypnosis if they wish. This could be by making the hypnosis lighter, using a different method of hypnosis, being in full awareness while in hypnosis, or being able to pause or stop the hypnosis. Just so you know, if you say no, you are entitled to mean no, even if someone with a nice voice is asking you to do something. And just because you don't say anything, or don't know what to say, doesn't mean yes.

Induction: gathering and softening attention

Inductions serve to gather your awareness up into a gentler, trancier, more receptive form so you can access the inner knowing that's too quiet for our ordinary awareness to hear. Call me a heretic, but I don't think inductions should be about surrendering to the hypnotist while playing asleep.

Sometimes it's nice to do an actual induction if you're sober but distracted or analytical or otherwise not very trancey. If, on the other hand, you're already pretty trancey for whatever reason, skip straight to "Already trancey? Stabilize before dipping deeper."

There are lots of different ways to do the induction. For this method, I'm going to be counting, but you could do all sorts of things. If you want to just talk, or tell a story, or move, or make sounds, or be curious, or whatever, by all means, just do that if it works. Everyone's unique and each person might have a kind that works best for them which they'll discover as they go. The nice thing about the counting method is it's very explicitly an induction, whereas with more implicit methods you have to make sure to explain their function clearly.

You can test to see if you're at a deep enough level of hypnosis by choosing a simple target suggestion and checking if the response is the automatic subconscious kind or the analytical questioning kind.

Overly Confusing Isn't Automatically Good

"Keep it simple, stupid!" (KISS principle) actually can apply to hypnosis!

It's really easy to take a whole bunch of techniques from all of this and make something so complicated that you "hypnotize" yourself to the point where you don't even know if it worked or not. But keep in mind that especially with self-hypnosis, we can't go so deep that we are unable to continue the session! And even if it were a hypnotist bringing you under, you still need to be at an "access level" of awareness in which you can respond to questions.

So start off with setting one anchor/image to rest your mind's eye on. Decide on a hypnotic tone (warm, indirect, direct, neutral witnessing, etc.) for the session. If you're going to use metaphors, just start with one, and if you switch metaphors midway, understand the purpose of the switch. Then plan a simple stepwise walk out of the subconscious at session's end.

In fact, you don't even need to use metaphors nor imagery. You can use whatever form of inner signal that you notice. It may be a direct knowing, or an emotion, some kind of rhythmic movement or sound, or other kind of spatial perception technique like body scanning or flipping between awareness of different body parts.

Keep the language tied to the subconscious world you've chosen, so if you're counting, and there are already raindrops falling, you can count the raindrops, not the sheep which aren't in that scene. When you're done, come back out the opposite way from how you went in (we often visualize a staircase).

It's fine to borrow qualities such as warmth or steadiness from someone who models them, if you need to, but don't change who you are. We don't want you showing up at the Emmy's like Jim Carrey saying he's Nobody. 😅 Not yet anyways.

When you're writing the script, decide what the central verb you're using is to anchor it (like "letting go"). Give it an overall sensory quality, like unified or disjoint, or something fun like "fluffy wuffy."

You may have a short refrain that you come back to a few times in there as another type of anchor. Think about how you're going to slide from one idea to the next. It's much easier if you read it out loud as you're writing it, since the writing brain is sort of different from the vocal brain. Long, elegant sentences don't read well when you're being induced. You want your script to be soft, but to the point, easy to remember, easy to say, easy to grok.

Before you begin

  1. Read through the induction, and the Come all the way back section, when you're awake.

  2. Get clear on what it is you want to do during the induction. What are you leaving alone today? How long do you want to spend on this? (You don't have to do this on a problem. You can do it on something you want to experience more of: love, play, confidence, creativity, communion, whatever.) Make sure you're leaving some time at the end to come back, and reflect on what you experienced.

  3. Put yourself in a place where you can safely do this, which is not: driving a big machine, swimming with the fishies, or otherwise needing to be alert and functionally responsible for yourself or others. If you are intoxicated and more confused than usual, or for whatever reason not able to keep yourself safe, then get some immediate support instead of doing exploratory hypnosis. (The part below called "When you're already trancey" can be used to help orient you if you're already open, but don't use it to make yourself deeper if you're not stable.)

  4. Deciding to do it: how will you coax yourself to do this? Make it spacious? Make it warm and held? Make it firmer, so you stay with a feeling? Something else? Preview what you are going to do: will you close your eyes? Will you come out and go back in again? What will you do at the end? Know that you can say "stop" at any time and the induction will end. If you want to change something, say "less" if you want it to ask less of you, or say "quiet" and the talking will stop. You can also just open your eyes and stop, and then come all the way back anyway. You don't need to ask any parts for permission to stop, even if a part tells you that you do.

  5. If you are new to this or haven't done it for a while, work on the coming in and coming out part first. Don't bring in the hard stuff till later. You don't have to watch yourself all the time, but for the first few times, make sure you can change state. Let yourself make it easier and easier to do this and notice that it takes less and less of you to do it.

  6. Let your response guide you as to what to do next regardless of what the script is expecting. Utilize: use what you have (a thought, sound, movement, or objection) to go to the next place, whatever it is. Learn how to notice what is there. If nothing changes, notice that too. As my dad always said, "Be prepared to take what you get. The best might come afterwards!" For more on language you can use, see Appendix B, or keep reading below for information on using the tools after induction in Let the next response guide you. Either way, keep the instructions about how to stay safe and how to get out plain.

Sober induction: 3–5 minutes

You can read this slowly into your recorder, or you can read it out loud or say it to yourself if you are good at that. Choose a sound, or a contact, or the two-hand practice (above) or something to anchor yourself, and choose a phrase you will say to open it up. Use the same anchor wherever the script mentions it. Don't read the directions out loud, just pause between paragraphs.

Script:

Let your eyes rest on something in the room for a bit, maybe a doorknob or a spot on the wall. Notice the difference between looking for something and letting what's already there come into view.

You can allow yourself to notice something you can feel, like the sensation of something under you, and notice the sensation of the contact, the pressure. Or maybe there is a sound, or something else to notice.

Allow yourself some time to do this.

You might notice yourself thinking, and whenever you notice yourself thinking, you can allow yourself to re-engage with your anchor and leave the thought unfinished.

You might notice something that holds your interest, something about the sound, or sensation, or listening or something else.

Allow yourself some time to do this, as much time as you would for someone you care about who has started to talk and hasn't gotten to what she wants to say yet, so you are going to stick around till she gets it all out.

Repeat the phrase that you chose, if you want to. Say it as an invitation and notice how it's received.

You can close your eyes now if you want to. If not, it's fine to keep them open, or soften your gaze. The listening might go deeper while there's still a background sound you can hear.

Count 5 to 1, taking your time if you like.

  1. Let your attention come back to your chosen anchor. 4. Let more of your attention come to what you've found. 3. Leave a little space. 2. See if you can listen to a space after the words without preparing an answer. 1. Take your time.

Bring to mind, if you want, the thing that you want to talk about, or just the wish to have inner contact, and let it sit there without telling the whole story yet.

What do you notice now?

Let yourself notice what comes to you, if anything. It may not be clear yet what it is. It may be sensations, words, pictures, feelings, movements, or anything else.

Let yourself take your time to notice, with or without words.

When you’re already trancey

This is for when something has already opened you up before you start: loveyhuasca, meditation, lucid dreaming, going through grief, listening to beautiful music, prayer, erotic energy, exhaustion, seeing something you love, etc.

Know that you're already open, you just need to aim and stabilize it, and keep it kind.

Orient yourself by looking around. Where are you? What's the date or day? How does your body feel? Where are you grounded? Can you feel your feet/hands/spine/etc? Make some contact with yourself, eg press your thumbs against the tips of your index fingers gently but firmly so that you can feel friction. Focus on that.

Say something like, "I ask to experience only the therapy that I can integrate. Patience pays, so if there's too much, let it simmer. May my body stay safe, and may my little one know there's an adult here to carry the heavy stuff."

Once you’ve got your bearings, make your first wish.

"Please show me the next truth. The next symbol or feeling. The question or action that's useful for me at this time, and no more than I can handle."

Stay awake when your mind likes to escape

If your mind keeps drifting off, sit up and open your eyes. Then feel your feet on the floor, and just let your breathing go at its own pace. Tell yourself, "I'm not going anywhere. I'm happily arriving more fully here each moment."

Pick something in the room to look at, and feel your spine at the same time (focusing on two things makes it harder for the mind to use multitasking mode to wander). Then ask yourself what the daydreaming is doing for you. Is it bringing you guidance? Does it help you work through a feeling, or are you using it to avoid something?

Ask for any diffuse benefits to become more practically focused: "If this is guidance, show me what I need to do about it. If I'm avoiding something, bring me gently back to the present and show me what it is."

Make the hypnosis more alert

Aside from focusing on things in your direct experience, you can also suggest yourself into greater alertness (it's still relaxed-alert, not paranoid-alert). Keep your eyes open. Use a small hand movement each time you make the auto-suggestion that you're gaining increasing clarity. Then stop the hand movement and see where your mind is at. That hand movement is the mental escape into the body's flow.

You can tell your analytical mind to stand down, any revelations can wait.

Optional fractionation: practice coming back

Fractionation means coming partway back toward normal awareness, then deciding whether to go back into the trance. It gives the trance a revolving door, so you can check how the session is going from a few different depths instead of "flying blind." Going in and out a few times also makes going in and coming out easier and quicker, until it feels natural. At some point in a session, you can do it on purpose, like this:

“Okay, open your eyes and come back up. Feel the room around you. You can decide if you want to stop the work at this point or continue. If you want to continue, soften your eyes or close them, and notice the change that comes over you when you do. If you want to stop, that’s okay too. Either way is okay, and you can stop at either place.”

Deepening, if useful

Allow your attention to gather, if it will be of use to you. If it won’t be of use to you at this time, you can remain as awake as you need to be.

Pick a place to go to when you go inside. It might be a room in your heart, a garden, a temple, sitting in front of a fire with your Protector, down by a clear lake where you can see the bottom, in a comfortable chair where your adult is going to be, whatever you want.

Optional trance depth check

“As you wait, you release the responsibility of making anything happen.

It might be useful for your wise subconscious to let you know that your inner meeting space is ready for you to go into by making a finger twitch, pulse, lift up by itself, heat up or tingle in a way you notice.

You don’t have to make it happen, or pretend that it is happening, just wait with love and notice what happens.”

Sometimes a finger will move, sometimes an entire hand will heat up, sometimes you will notice a change in your breathing, sometimes you won’t notice anything at all. Even if something happens, it doesn’t mean that what comes next will be true, only that some part of you might be listening.

A shortcut back into hypnosis

Once you have learned a useful induction and how to come all the way back up at the end, you can do the following:

Have an unusual word, phrase, and/or motion (optional) that you will use to go into hypnosis again. Say the word and/or phrase while doing the motion (if applicable). Be sure to do this when you are in hypnosis and in a good place. Don't do this when you are in a very emotional state, child state, or thinking about something that could be considered trauma.

For example, my dad used to use a mudra (like putting two fingers together like an "OK" sign) when he needed to relax. He even did it when he was driving. Not that I recommend driving in a trance, don't do that! Make sure the cue you use to calm down while staying alert is not the same as your cue to go inward into hypnosis.

Give your cue the meaning you choose: “When I pick the time and place (an appropriate time and place) and say the word and/or phrase to begin, I can go into this state that I picked and am familiar with. I can change direction, or stop, at any point.”

Now come all the way back up again, and at a later time, when it is appropriate, start using this. Over time, you can shorten the length of the longer induction, or use the longer induction if this doesn't seem to be enough for your needs. You don't have to do this a certain number of times, go to a certain depth, or even have a certain sensation when you do this.

Also, be sure that this is working for you: you can turn it on and off at will, it isn't there when you don't want it to be, and you can change direction or come back up when you are in it. Don't use it in a way that makes you follow instructions that you are hearing from a narrator, notifications, or something that you overhear. Don't use this to go into hypnosis faster for things that you shouldn't be working on yet.

There is something you should never do with this shortcut: use it, practice it or test it while you're driving or doing something where you need to be actively paying attention. It's just not safe to be going into a trance where you are actively paying attention to what's going on inside of you when you need to be paying attention to what's going on outside of you. Even though your eyes can be open, even though you're using "if it would be useful for you to do so" type language, it's just not safe.

Turning the shortcut off, or keeping it

If you want to turn the shortcut off, simply don't practice it anymore. Take it out of your recordings, take it out of your apps if you're using it in an app, and start using your regular induction. Tell yourself, when you're awake, that you won't be using it anymore. Just because you say that it'll be gone doesn't mean that it'll be gone right away. If it's causing you a problem, if you're still responding to it when you don't want to respond to it, if you're having issues with disorientation, etc, get some help from someone who's qualified to help you with this. If, on the other hand, you want to keep it so that you can use it to go back into trance at a later date when you do want to go into a trance, that's fine too. Keeping it doesn't keep today's trance going, though.

Come all the way back

If you have done anything to alter your state when entering the trance, make sure to reverse those things. If you have floated, make sure to gain your weight again (whatever weight you want of course). If you have shrunk, make sure to grow back to your regular size. If you have met a younger state or are working with parts, make sure to come back to feeling complete in your body in the here and now, that your body belongs to you, and that it is under the control of your adult mind. If you have gone somewhere, make sure to come back the same way you went. Etcetera.

Here is a generic bring-back that you can use: "When I come out of this session, I will only bring with me that which is useful to me in my everyday life right now. Anything else I will bring with me at a later date, when I am better able to integrate it. When I come out of this session, I will be coming back to the room I am in now, and to the day in which I am participating in this session now. All the instructions to carry me deeper into this session or keep me in this state are now finished. The only intention I will take with me into my everyday life, and the only cue I will use to take me into this state at a later date, will be the ones I consciously choose, and neither of them will keep me in this state. When I come out of this session I will be me, in my body and with my adult judgment."

Then do a count up: On 1 start to feel your body again and what it's resting on and feel your weight (of your choice). On 2 start to feel your breathing return to normal. On 3 start to hear the room around you and name where you are. On 4 start to wiggle your fingers and toes, move your shoulders and your eyes. On 5 open your eyes, back in the here and now, feeling normal and clear and the session is now over.

When you come out, make sure you can name what day (or date) it is, look to your left and right, look at something near to you and something further away. Move around a bit. Don't get up until you feel like you can do so without falling over. Make sure that you are oriented and alert (counting does this, but not always). Make a mental note of just what actually came up and anything you need to do about it, then go and do something normal, like eating or washing up.

If your recording happens to cut out partway through, woops... you could end up lying there dazed & confused, waiting on a voice that isn't coming back. Don't worry, you don't have to be a vegetable forever. Just tell yourself, "The recording has stopped and we're all done now. Welcome back to me!" (that exclamation actually helps, because trance is kinda monotone). Then do the exit-trance count and the present-time checks above on your own. Once your eyes are open, "touch grass" as they say, for grounding. Or if no grass, just push your adorable feet or hands into something solid. The physical feeling connects your brain to your body.

Sleep recordings are another story, since you're assuming you'll drift off midway with those. But if the sleep recording is designed to also wake you back up, make sure it's doing so intelligently, not just using a vague exit word that might half wake you up and still leave you a bit blank or "under the spell" so to speak.

The actual inner meeting

Note: If you have a tendency to daydream, dissociate or fall asleep, it is better to do this exercise sitting up and save lying down for when you want to go to sleep, get a very deep rest or incubate dreams. If you are sitting, keep your spine straight but not rigid like a soldier. Relax your jaw and stay somewhat connected to the room you are in.

“I am not leaving life; I am listening more deeply to life.

I invoke my Higher Self, universal heart-mind, wise subconscious mind, intelligence in my body, inner child(ren), inner adult(s), guard(s) and all that is of truth and love to help me know what I need to know, only in a form and at a time that will be useful, kind, stabilizing and real for me.”

Ask your conscious mind to listen, and don't force an answer.

This does not mean you have to stop thinking to listen. Just notice your thoughts, body sensations, pictures, doubts, emotions, etc. Don’t fight what comes up, but don’t go with it either. Just notice what stands out on its own as you hold the idea of what you are doing in the back of your mind.

Internally say, “Tell me what I need to know or ask me what you need to know and I’ll sit in silence for 5 minutes and listen.”

You may hear a phrase, feel a body sensation, notice a memory, see a color, be contacted by your inner child or a guard, feel pain, warmth, complete silence, know that you need to do something, be grateful for something, feel you need to stop the listening or any number of things.

Listen to everything and believe nothing automatically.

Let the next response guide you

The induction and the inner meeting get you to where you can listen. After that, it's about answering what happens, instead of reading out a script that has already decided what every part of you is going to say. Read through these sections, and then try doing the inner meeting without looking at the screen.

Respond to each thing that comes up with an accurate notice and a small reasonable invitation (or action or taking more time etc). The thing might be a sensation, image, gesture, feeling, words, or anything. The notice and invitation don't need to cover all those forms, just notice and invite what happens.

Finding your own words

An example notice might be, "I notice my chest tightening when I imagine asking for help". Don't add interpretation like "this must be the abandoned child part of me, or a protective mechanism" - just notice what you notice. An example invitation might be, "And as I notice that, I can take some time before deciding what it means, and notice if I want to say anything, or change it, or just stay like that".

Then just listen. You don't need fancy language - often it will be better to just ask what happens next, or whatever. If you notice a feeling developing, it might be best not to say anything while it's developing (if you can help it), but some days you need to talk to understand, so just do that.

You can be pretty straightforward when that's the help you chose - "Let the explanation wait a moment. What do I feel as I say that?" can be a perfectly valid thing to say to yourself, and help you focus. As long as you're still present, and able to change if you want to, and happy to carry on, a sad feeling or tension or whatever doesn't require an exit.

Notice, meaning, and your thoughts on the meaning

It's worth trying to separate the notice, the meaning, and your thoughts on the meaning, but in practice it will often be pretty fuzzy. For example, "I noticed... this seemed to mean... here's why that might be true / false" or something like that.

Is this a notice or meaning? "My stomach went tight" is a notice, "He's a complete bastard" is a meaning, even if you didn't think it in words. You can then go on to find out what he did, notice what it was that gave you the idea, and consider if there might be other explanations. It's also ok to put a meaning into words now, to look at it, even if it came to you without words. Nonverbal thoughts and early thoughts aren't necessarily any deeper, truer, or more reliable than other thoughts.

Let the response change the invitation

Your shoulders tightening, or 'hmm, that feels kind of wrong', is a signal to investigate further, but 'No! That is Not Right!' is a signal to stop what you're doing and change it. If you don't feel like it fits, don't keep doing it, and don't try saying it again more persuasively. You don't need any other reason.

"I notice the tightness, and I can just leave it like that. Then again, is that what I really want?"

Sometimes the invitation just isn't right or helpful, even if it sounds like something a hypnotist might say. Each time we play around with this, we explore new possibilities which bring up new inner signals for us to feel out.

Doubting is sometimes a guard to listen to, and sometimes simply part of normal consideration. Lack of doubt doesn't necessarily mean the process is working in the best way, either.

Look at the situation too

If there is another person involved in the situation, it may be a good idea to look at what they are doing as well. Can they hear you disagree, take responsibility, and make room for what you're going through? Try and be specific in what you conclude about them, and be willing to change it if you find out that it was wrong. Just because someone is disagreeing with you or trying to set boundaries with you does not mean they are in their emotionally immature parts. You don’t need to subject yourself to something that may be dangerous before protecting yourself.

Look at your own side too. You may find that you feel guilty. Having feelings of guilt does not mean that you have done something wrong or that you have been manipulated. Have you done something that was wrong or that caused harm? If so, what can you do to fix it? On the other hand, did you give in to someone else just to keep them from being disappointed in you? What do you want to do or achieve the next time you interact with this person? Do you need them to understand what you are feeling/thinking? Do you just need to communicate information to them? Do you need to set boundaries with them? Do you need to end the interaction? You don’t have to make them understand before you can act.

Change the amount of contact

When you start to contact the difficult thing, if it feels too much, immediately start to widen your awareness to something else in the room, in your body, etc. Keep moving back and forth between the difficult thing and the easier thing. This is called pendulation, and approaching in smaller amounts is titration.

You can say, "I can let this much be here, and notice what's supporting me right now." And when you've had enough, "Ok, I think that is enough for now."

Feel free to go back and explore more if you wish to do so. If, at any point, you start to lose contact with the here and now, feel that things are becoming more and more unreal, or you feel that you are not able to switch to something else or stop, stop trying to explore inside of yourself and just do the entire "coming back" part. If you have trouble orienting yourself and it lasts for a prolonged period of time, please get some human help rather than trying a stronger induction.

If you feel an urge to move

If you feel an urge to move, turn, reach, push, or change your body’s position, feel free to either actually do something that will not cause your body pain or discomfort, or just imagine doing what you feel you need to do, and see what happens next. If you do move and you feel that you are causing yourself pain or other symptoms that you are concerned about, stop moving.

Offer the care that is actually wanted

If any younger parts come up, offer them the opportunity to spend time with you, or some space if they would rather. If one of them says “don’t come closer”, stay at the distance they choose. You don’t have to consider them literally younger, nor do you have to ask them why they are there or what happened to them. If you can identify any guards (childhood protector parts), talk to them in the same Nurturer voice you meant for your little one, and find out what they are concerned about. You don’t have to get rid of any of them.

If you feel that it would be useful to bring in your Nurturer, Protector or Guide to help you either change the tone of the interaction, set boundaries, or find your way toward the life you want, feel free to do so. You don’t have to talk to them, just use the one whose job you need, and if you can’t do it yet, borrow it. You only need to use the Witness if you need help getting started with a step.

Try some different ideas as to what might be helpful for this inner part to feel better (being welcomed by you, visualizing someone else, giving them the sort of words they would like to hear, you placing yourself in a different body position, you moving further away etc). Only try one idea at a time to see if it works.

It is possible that when you try and give the part what it wants, you might feel very upset. It may be that it never got what it needed at the time and now that you are giving it what it wants, it is processing the upset of not getting what it wanted before. That doesn’t mean your love isn’t working. If this happens, allow yourself to respond for as long as you want to do so, and for as long as you can stay there (ie remain in the process), but don’t push for more tears, since crying more doesn’t mean you’re healing more. If you feel that the voice, distance, touch, image etc is too much, then change it to something that is less.

Allow yourself to see where a metaphor takes you. Ask questions like ‘if the problem had an image or story, what would it be?’ or ‘what would need to happen to make things right?’ If it feels like you need to do something to give yourself the resource that you feel you need (or to put a boundary in place, or to move away or to take some other action) then try doing that, as far as it helps you. Remember that whatever you find out is likely to be metaphorical. It may be a good hypothesis for what you need to do, but it isn’t a record of what actually happened.

Alternatively, you may want to use the session to build up something you want more of (eg love, play, courage, curiosity etc). You can also use it to rehearse something that you have decided to do.

Finish without forcing a revelation

Don’t feel that you have to end the session with a great revelation (those are often flaky, not standing the test of time). When you have done as much as you want to do, ask yourself what would help you leave things where they are for now. This might include thanking parts for their help, or making sure that you do something in real life (put a boundary in place, get some rest, repair a relationship, keep a promise etc). You can tell your little one, "That’s enough for today, choochoo. Aren't you excited to get back to the life we're building?"

When you have finished, even if you stopped part way through, use the ‘Come all the way back’ procedure to bring yourself back. When you are back, it is a good idea to write a few lines in a notebook about what you have done. Just jot down the essentials (what was there before you started, what you have done to change things, what happened, what you are unsure about, any phrases that were said, any body signals, any information that the inner child said that it needed, anything that a guard wanted you to know, any boundaries that you put in place, any repairs that you did, anything you learned about what not to force etc). You don’t have to list every part of you that might have been involved, only what you actually noticed.

Throughout the exercise

Allow your analytical part to make comments and/or tell you that it doesn’t like what you are doing. Just tell it that you will check things out, and make any commitments etc, when you are back. If it says no to going deeper, or tells you to stop doing the exercise, I’d stop there. You can stop whenever you want to. Just because you see an image, or feel a body release, doesn’t mean you are right about what is going on.

The 15-minute daily practice

You don't need to learn a second script for this. It's the same process (gather your attention, notice what's here, respond, wait, and come all the way back), just for fifteen minutes or so. That is long enough to induce yourself (it only takes a few minutes), practice and let it unfold at its own pace, and come out again (that takes a few minutes too). Fifteen minutes is just a handy appointment with yourself, though, and your feelings don't have to keep to it. Five minutes is plenty to see what happens when you simply sit in silence, listening, and you can interrupt yourself at any time to change direction if that feels better.

You can also try other ways of inducing yourself, such as becoming aware of 3 things you can see, 3 things you can hear, 3 things you can feel in your body; then 2 of each, 1 of each. Then count from 5 to 1 and begin to listen. You don't need to try all of them (in fact, stacking them might be counter-productive).

Once you have practiced listening a few times with the recordings, you may want to shorten them to speed up your induction, or even practice without playing them back at all. On the other hand, if you like using the recordings, there is no reason to stop. (Once you know the induction well, the cue from "A shortcut back into hypnosis" can shorten it too.)

As you come out of the process, ask yourself what needed warmth, what needed to be protected, what you need to do differently to be more in accord with truth and love and life. Is there something you want to explore more fully or a capacity you want to build? And most importantly, what can you now carry yourself that you used to have to borrow? If you identified something you needed to do differently, make a plan with yourself to do that thing. The more specific you can be, the more likely you are to change: “If _________ (fill in the cue for the old pattern), then I will do ________ (fill in a small action that will begin to change the pattern).” Then check back later and see how you are doing – sleeping, relating, functioning, etc., not only how impressive the session felt.

The weekly deeper practice

Once or twice a week, when it’s useful, give this practice more space and time so even the unplanned, unknown and maybe hidden needs can show up. Just because it's unplanned doesn't mean it will be more shocking. Start from your Higher Self, from the field of acceptance and understanding, or however you can describe it that makes it feel accessible. If you need the Protector or another of your adult jobs, bring it in or borrow it from someone safe. Mr. or Ms. Protector can check to make sure you’re not skipping past anything important. Leave enough time at the end to come all the way back up & out.

Then, if there's something from the session that asked you to do something in real life, and your Protector agrees, then go ahead and do it when you can. That's how you show your subconscious you mean business.

Capacity before content

Note: if it is medically unsafe, or you are in a place of panic, sleep deprivation, nausea, deep destabilization, mania, psychosis-like symptoms, feeling overwhelmed by chemicals etc., focus on stabilizing first, with help if you need it (see “When this needs more support than a guide can provide”), and only then look for what your subconscious needs you to know. If knowing what your subconscious knows would destabilize you, it is not meant for you to know directly at this time. Ask for capacity before content.

Sometimes, it feels like your subconscious knows something that your conscious self is not prepared to know. It may be something related to your sexuality, grief, trauma, family issues, betrayal, shame, vocation, a relationship, illness fears, spirituality, addiction, identity, moral issues etc.

What you don’t want to do is bully your subconscious into telling you what it knows by saying things to yourself like:

“What is it you won’t tell me? What are you hiding? Tell me NOW! I am telling you to tell me!”

This can lead to flooding, panic, false certainty, confabulation, dissociation etc., and can make your day suck. When you are flooded, it is much harder to access your adult capacities, so focus on being able to regulate and on getting support first. Then ask for what you need to know in a way that you can tolerate:

“What can you tell me, that will make my conscious self 5% better at knowing the thing you want to tell me?” “Can you tell me in symbolic form so I am not completely overwhelmed by the revelation?” “Can you tell me what I need to do to be safe, without actually telling me what it is?” “Do I need to know what it is now, or do I need to get some support first, so I have the capacity to know it, and who would be a good person to help me?” etc.

Or, instead of demanding answers, say to yourself something like this:

“Dear Wise Subconscious Mind, if there is something you know that my conscious self is not ready to know, please don’t break it open violently. Do what you can to help me know it in a way that is safe. Prepare me before you break it open. Ground me before you show me the memory. Support me before you make it intense. And show me the next thing to do.” etc.

Remember: not everything in hypnotic imagery is literal, especially if it relates to trauma and memory. The mind makes it symbolic, squashes things together, distorts, dramatizes, pulls from cultural influences, fears etc. Get some help outside of yourself when you need it.

Troubleshooting

Nothing happens

Don't fret, even for the greatest masters, many times nothing happens. After all, only like 15% of people are very suggestible. But sometimes it's just confusing when you throw everything at yourself at once. Check if there's a way you could try this on a smaller scale... like is it even the right time for this? You can directly ask that.

"It feels fake"

Let's drill into that "it"... do you have a sense of what exactly feels feigned, imposterous, or preposterous? The words, an image, what you are expecting to get from this, a role you feel you have to play... am I getting warm? Not having an inner voice or images doesn't mean you're resisting the work, nor that you're missing a 'grown-up'. If it feels like you are being asked to do something you don't want to do, then correct the invitation. It could be that there is a part that is protecting you, so talk to it.

You could say something like 'What are you protecting me from, helicopter mom?' or 'Are you sus because you think this will just be a silly performance?' If it says yes, that one can sting, because there could be something to it. Maybe you've fooled yourself before, and that part remembers. But as ex-US-President George W. Bush wisely explained, "Fool me once, shame on — shame on you. Fool me — you can't get fooled again." !!!

But seriously... you can then say, "Tell me what you remember, I'm listening." It puts you into the role of the Nurturer, which alleviates the instinct to argue back.

It could also be your inherited parent script that you are picking up, and it is trying to enforce a rule about how you should be to belong to them. Find out what rule it is enforcing. Comfort your little one before you answer any of it. Your answers can be firm or funny, as long as they aren’t mean. Then work on finding some ordinary evidence to build a more believable 'grown up' voice of your own.

If the fake feeling is telling you that you don't have the grown up ability to do this yet, then don't try and convince yourself by using an inner voice. As long as you can still observe yourself well enough to do this, try and borrow what you need from someone who you can imagine is good enough to lend it to you. This could be a family member, friend, famous person etc. What you need might be warmth, boundary, direction etc. For warmth, just make sure it's someone who liked you the way you were. Try out something which rings true to you, whether it is a sentence, an action etc, and see how you get on.

The subconscious will not answer

There may not be an answer. There may not be an answer that will help you. You may need to try asking a different question or you may just need to rest or finish your inner work for the day. If trust is actually a concern, you can try asking your subconscious “What would you need to know in order to trust me?” Some examples of answers might be “I need to know you will set boundaries,” “I need to know you will do what you said you will do,” “I need to know you won’t keep leaving,” or “I need to know you won’t think that knowing something means that you don’t need to do anything about it.”

The inner child will not appear

If you can tell there is a guard in the way, talk to it. You can say to the guard “You don’t have to let me in, just tell me what I need to do to make it safe”. Even if there isn’t a child part that you can talk to, you can work with whatever you are aware of. You can work on your preferences or something that you’re curious about or something that you’re willing to try as a beginner etc. and see how it goes. Work on developing a sense of who you are and reparent yourself as best you can.

You get anxious

Find out if the anxiety is bad enough that you want to decrease contact with it or if you can manage it and let it continue. If the anxiety is getting too bad, then you need to stop searching for content and help your body first, like the following:

-Where are your feet? Where are you sitting? Focus on your body, your breathing, and the room around you.

-Tell your little one and your guards (childhood protector parts) that you’re there for your little one, that you recognize that your guards are trying to protect you, and that you don’t need them to scream at you in order for you to know that something is wrong. Ask them if they need you to do something, and if they do, ask them to show you one step at a time that you can do. If they don’t, then ask them to let your body calm down.

If you’re having chest pain, fainting, suicidal thoughts, psychosis-like symptoms, mania-like symptoms, severe dissociative symptoms, not being able to sleep, wanting to hurt yourself or somebody else, etc., then you need to get professional help. For more on handling anxiety, see the toolbox in Appendix D.

You dissociate or feel unreal

Open your eyes, move around, feel something textured, name 5 things that you can see, and end the deeper practice for the day.

You become grandiose

Go eat, go to sleep, do some chores, accept some humility, find somebody to disagree with you, etc.

What do you say to yourself in that moment? “If it’s real, it’ll still be here after a good night’s sleep, some food, some time, and some subsequent humility, feedback, etc.”

You become too analytical

“What if you didn’t try to improve the practice?” Put one hand on your heart and the other on your solar plexus, as in the two-hand practice, and say, “I don’t have to do this perfectly. I’m here for a relationship with something deeper than my surface mind.” “What can you do to make this a communion, not a performance?” Maybe it’s silence, resting, or doing something. Whatever it is, just do it.

You become too blissful or diffuse

If the bliss spreads out into a big warm haze, it can’t do much for you or anyone else yet. Bring it down to Earth by asking, “What’s this lofty love asking me to do? Is there a task I'm ignoring? Or a boundary I need to set? Am I getting enough rest and nourishment? Am I hiding from a needed conversation or relationship repair?”

You want the hidden truth immediately

Don’t pry.

“Show me the capacity before the content.”

You keep daydreaming

Sit up and open your eyes, and ask yourself, “What are you avoiding doing in the real world by doing this?”

You get stuck in spiritual bypass

“Where has divine love not yet descended into the wound?”

Maybe you had a profound opening that showed you how much more love and freedom is possible. Let that matter, but don’t demand that your inner child receive it on command. Invite your little one in with you. They might go numb, or call BS on that. If so, they might just need to see if you’re actually humble enough, and grateful enough for them, to listen to their rejection and be patient. Maybe when you prayed it seemed like you were being abandoned and condemned. Maybe you felt betrayed by a spiritual teacher. It may be worth exploring these, but you might not want to do more of the same practice.

Sometimes it’s best to get some practical help while life still feels too small, and let yourself be curious about finding more happiness, without necessarily believing the explanation of how it’s going to work. Sometimes people have enough love and meaning to just live their lives, and trying to be spiritual doesn’t have to be another assignment.

You get stuck in endless processing

You may have a real wound that you need to deal with and be caught in a checking loop at the same time. Consider asking yourself “Will processing this again actually help me feel, understand, decide, or do something differently? Or is this a form of checking to see if I can get the reassurance that something is finally okay?” Sometimes, whether you’re grieving or doing your practice, it’s okay to cover the same ground again and again.

The best thing to do, though, if you notice you’re in a checking loop, is allow the thought to be there without engaging with it and get on with what you have chosen to do.

What would the Guide choose to do now?

You keep using hypnosis but not changing life

Ask yourself, “What can I do to show my little one I’m for real?” Pick one thing to do that’s small enough to repeat, then go do it. A big gesture you later drop would only prove them right to doubt you, but a small thing you keep doing can change their mind.

What not to do

  • Don’t view your inner experiences as commands or proof. They’re internal happenings that need to be reality tested at all times.

  • Don’t go seeking “downloads” of information, or make a hobby of demons/jinn/Unattached Burdens or the like. Stabilize your life first, and get help to do so if you are not reality testing well, or not functioning well in your life.

  • Don’t do self-hypnosis while driving, bathing, swimming, operating any equipment, supervising children, or doing anything else that requires active attention.

  • Don’t try to “force” memories, to use trance to “prove” something happened to you, or to skip medical attention for symptoms just because you can “force” them down using hypnosis.

  • Don’t force yourself to ignore your body’s needs, such as eating when hungry, resting when tired, responding to pain, etc., or your emotions, such as letting yourself grieve when someone dies, feeling anger when it is appropriate, acknowledging your fears, etc., until you know more about what the signals are telling you.

  • Don’t “extend” the duration of altered states indefinitely.

  • Don’t allow your inner roles to get out of proportion (e.g., letting your inner child run your adult life, the Protector shut out all love, the Nurturer disregard consequences, or the Guide ignore the needs of your inner child). You are capable of analysis, as well as tenderness.

  • Don’t use NLP techniques to force down guard parts, to give yourself certainty you do not have, to manipulate others, or to heal yourself when your body is telling you not to.

  • Don’t consider negative indicators (doubt, distraction, resistance, lack of response, saying “No”) as good signs that the induction is working.

  • Don’t rely upon the safety of your unconscious mind as the only safety factor in any session. Be sure you have set up clear limits, are in a safe place, and have a tested way to end the session and get out.

  • Don’t believe that normal physical sensations are indications of hidden trauma, illness, toxins in your body, possession, “attachments,” “transformations,” or information from your “subconscious mind.”

Don’t believe that anything is “healed” in your body (neurologically, cellularly, etc.) simply because it is “healed” in your imagination.

Don’t put hidden commands in yourself, nor allow anyone to do so. Even if you are consciously placing something in yourself which you wish to utilize later, be sure that you know exactly what you are doing and why you are doing it, and be sure that you can review it and can “take it out” if you decide that you no longer wish to use it. It may not be as easy as simply saying, “Take out.” Nor should you allow anyone to activate these commands in you without your knowledge, or use them to make you do something you don’t want to do, to make you into someone you don’t want to be, to give you amnesia, etc.

When this needs more support than a guide can provide

You should get help now if you are in imminent danger, if you are very confused, if you cannot ensure your own safety, and/or if you are experiencing concerning physical symptoms (e.g., chest pain, fainting, etc). You should also seek professional help promptly if you are experiencing new or worsening suicidal thoughts, severe insomnia, new or worsening manic or psychotic symptoms, or if dissociative symptoms are impacting your ability to function.

Appendix A: How to ask the subconscious a useful question

Your subconscious mind is not an AI large language model. However, there is a bit of overlap in the way you can ask questions to both: how you ask shapes the answer. So, if you know how to do prompt engineering for AI, this can be very useful to help you be more present, honest, loving etc. But you should never use it to be more controlling.

To get as much precision as possible, you need to include the following in your prompt:

  1. Role: Who or what are you talking to?

  2. Context: Give some context so it knows what you’re talking about.

  3. Intention: What do you want to change or accomplish?

  4. Constraint: What do you need to do to make sure your question is safe, true, kind etc.? No forcing, no false certainty, and no asking for an answer before you can handle it.

  5. Output: How do you want to receive the answer? As an image, phrase, sensation in your body, memory, action, question, symbol, silence, rest etc.

  6. Evaluation: How are you going to evaluate the answer? How will you know if it’s true, kind, timely, stabilizing, specific, testable etc?

Iteration: Iterate by changing your prompt depending on what you get from reality as feedback.

Most importantly, always let silence count as an answer, otherwise the analytical part of your mind will make up an answer just so it fits with your prompt. I usually add the following to my prompt:

“If you need more time or the best and safest answer is silence, then I ask you to make my body go into a deeper state of comfortable rest. A peaceful silence would be a successful answer to my question.”

Once you do that, here are the core prompts you can use:

Core prompts

For getting clarity: “I ask you, my wise subconscious mind, to show me what is true, kind and timely. If what you want to show me is too intense for me to integrate, please transfer it to my conscious mind in a way I can integrate it, for instance as a symbol, a signal in my body, a next step I can take. Most importantly, show me first my capacity to be peaceful, and then, the content of my wounding.”

When you experience resistance: “What are you protecting me from? What would help you trust me 1% more?”

When you want to ask your inner child something: “What do you want me to know? What do you need me to do today so that you are not alone in dealing with it?”

When you want to bring in your Protector: “What would a minimally competent adult do here, in the external world, so that all of me is safe?”

When you want to ask your Guide: “What direction is the most in alignment with truth, love, life and all beings?”

When you want to listen to your intuition: “I ask my intuition to give me a signal, my analytical mind to decipher it, my body to give me feedback on whether my deciphering is correct, and reality to test it.”

When you want to know if a particular intuition you have is coming from your universal heart or from your programming: “I ask my wise subconscious mind to help me see if the intuition or impulse I have about …is coming from my universal heart or from my family, my religion, marketing, erotic programming, my political tribe, my trauma response or an old deal I did as a child to belong. Once you get an answer, you can ask: “What can I do in my ordinary life to show that I heard correctly?” If it tells you to do something, I'd only do it if it's loving and leaves you free. You use the prompt to tell your subconscious what you want in a language it can easily understand. The NLP in Appendix B below can help smooth the prompt's entry and reduce resistance. Appendix B: NLP without turning yourself into a sales seminar

NLP stands for Neuro-Linguistic Programming.

You do not need to know this stuff to use the main path, but if you want to freshen up on your skills in the art of working with imagery, body states, behavioral rehearsal, talking to yourself in effective sentences etc., then it might be useful, as long as it helps you listen. Using the techniques can be an easy way to fall into the trap of thinking that NLP is a bag of tricks rather than something that needs to be done with love and truth and contact and consent.

The four NLP layers

The four levels of NLP are:

Sensory Level of NLP: working with the form of an inner experience (where do you see or hear it, how far away, how big, how bright, how loud, how do you hold your body, where is the pressure etc.)

State Level of NLP: making useful states (warmth, adult presence, courage, love etc.) easier to get to.

Strategy Level of NLP: practicing a better response.

Language Level of NLP: pacing what is true; opening up people’s awareness; presuming only that you can do something safe; linking cause and effect carefully; embedding invitations; asking de-hypnotic questions to break the effects of vague, absolute, manipulative or grandiose language.

The Milton Model, minus the covert sales seminar

We are going to use a model that’s called the Milton model. It’s all to do with sentence architecture. When we tell ourselves to ‘be better’, ‘relax now’, ‘stop feeling anxious’ or ‘trust me’, it doesn’t work, does it?

What we need to do is use indirect language to go around the part that wants to argue with us, so that we can actually get to where we want to get to.

So let’s start with something we know to be true, let’s take the pressure off, presume that we can only do a tiny bit and let’s invite one thing:

‘As my body begins to notice the sensation of the bed underneath it, it may be possible for it to begin to realize that it is not alone in carrying the burden of the day.’

We can then test this out with the body to see if it was possible.

Pattern 1: Pacing and leading — truth before suggestion

So how might we pace and lead in a way that works with anxiety?

It might go something like this: ‘It is completely true that the sensation of ________ (or the emotion of _____) is coming up for me. It makes complete sense that my system is trying to react and respond to that by wanting to do or think ________. While that is completely true, there is another thing that is equally true, which is that my adult self is here, breathing in the room right now, and wanting to listen to what comes up for me next.’

Truth, Invite, Truth, Invite etc. is all you need to do.

Pattern 2: Awareness verbs

Open up awareness by using awareness verbs e.g. notice, realize, recognize, sense, feel, wonder, imagine, picture, listen to, allow, discover, remember, consider etc.

Example: ‘Notice how the answer comes to you… maybe as an image, or a word… maybe as a pressure, or some warmth… or a silence, or a sense of what to do next.’

Pattern 3: Presuppositions

A presupposition hides an assumption inside a sentence, and whoever hears it usually just goes along with it. That’s a lot of power, so only use it to assume good things. It should give you more agency, not less. It should make you more safe (not just feeling more safe when you're not). It should help you feel the love that's there (even if it's only available beyond the conditions). We don't use this to assume obedience, the way a less ethical hypnotist does when they say something like, ‘As you go deeper, it gets easier and easier to just do what I say.’

I'd switch that up to the safe version: ‘As you go deeper, it gets easier to commune with any part that needs your understanding before it can change.’

Pattern 4: Gentle cause-effect bridges

Bridge words, like ‘as’, ‘whilst’, ‘because’, ‘when’, ‘after’, ‘before’ and ‘which means’, link one event to the next, so the first can seem to come with the second, or even cause it. That doesn't mean it does, of course. This is another kind of more subtle presupposition technique.

We can use this technique to support communion and agency: “As I breathe, my adult self can allow itself to become present enough to hear a bit of what my wounded inner child needs.”

Pattern 5: Embedded invitations

An embedded invitation tucks a small direction inside a bigger sentence, like when you put a pill inside a little food to swallow it easier. In communion hypnosis, we choose not to use embeds like this to hide commands, but rather to soften them into nudges. Nudges give more agency, and they keep the conversation flowing without a need for a strong decision. For example, rather than, "As I sit here, I will become aware..." we can say, "As I sit here, I may begin to allow myself to become aware..."

With your inner child, it could be: "My little one doesn't have to trust me all at once. I invite them to just notice that right now, I'm still here."

Invitational language can also feel super fake though, especially when it's overused. Fakeness doesn't feel so safe. And if it's just chunking the manipulation into easier bite-sized portions without reducing the overall size of the demand, it could be less ethical, not more.

Pattern 6: Ethical double binds

This is also an actual parenting technique. Children love choices, even when they're both designed for the same pre-decided outcome. Our subconscious still works like that, too. A manipulative double bind is the name for that, although hypnotists don't call it manipulative, that's my framing. Nimja demonstrates this a lot in his hypnosis: "Do you want to go deeper now, or in a moment?" Clearly, that sends you deeper regardless what you "choose."

An ethical double bind also gives you two choices, but both of them are designed to be genuinely safe, and they also give a bit of actual agency, even if both choices serve the same end. Example:

"Welcome to McHappyness. Would you like some warmth first, or some space?" And would you like fries with that?

Pattern 7: Conversational postulates

A conversational postulate is a sneaky little yes or no question that's actually a way to ask for something indirectly. People use it all the time casually, like, "Can you pass the salt?" We already know they're not really asking if you have that ability. In its most egregious form, it could turn into a dare. Do you recall how hard it was as a child to ignore a dare?

I dare you now to practice this ethical postulate! "Can my subconscious show me the next healing stepping stone, sparing me any more painful memories than I can handle right now?"

Pattern 8: Tag questions

Tag questions, like "isn't it?" or "doesn't it?", can soften a sentence, but they get oily very quickly, so go easy on them.

"Your body knows how to relax, doesn't it?"

Pattern 9: Nominalizations

A nominalization turns something you do into a noun: you heal, and it becomes 'healing'. Others are 'safety', 'trust', 'integration', 'communion', 'clarity' and 'rest'. They're a bit vague, and sometimes that's useful, because it lets the listener's subconscious fill in something very specific for them.

"A bit more safety can come to me in whatever way my body understands."

Pattern 10: Meta Model questions — the de-hypnosis tool

Milton Model language opens things up, and Meta Model questions ask what exactly someone means.

You need both to be ethical with people, so when something sounds true but vague, ask: "What do you mean, specifically? According to who? Compared to what? Always, or never? What exactly would happen if you did? How do you know that, and what evidence would change your mind?"

So when someone is starting to tense up in their body, use the Milton Model to help them soften. And when you feel you're losing track of what's really going on, that's the time for the Meta Model.

Sensory, state, and strategy tools

Submodalities — editing the form, not denying the truth

If your fear, craving, fantasy, belief, memory, whatever, has sensory properties in your experience (it has a place in your visual field, or sounds a certain way, or is heavy, or moving, or inside / outside your body, or something like that), you can work with those properties. If there are no sensory properties that you can notice, don't try to create any -- just don't use this tool.

How is this represented for you? Can you allow yourself to move that image back a couple of feet? Can you soften the edges of that image? Can you turn the volume down on that voice in your head? Etc. What changes?

"And I can allow myself to know that I am not deleting the message, just turning down the volume so that I am able to be present enough to hear what it is."

And, always ask the guard if it's ok to do this.

"Is it ok to move that image back a couple of feet and turn the volume down a bit, so that I can be present enough to hear what you need me to hear?"

Anchoring — giving the body a doorway back

There are many cues you can connect to a state. Put your hand on your heart, touch two fingers together, use a breath, a word, a particular body posture, smell, song, whatever. Feel a state you have really been in, one you want to be able to get back to easily when you need it. Do your anchor thing and say to yourself that this is available to you. To get yourself back into a good hypnosis state easily, don’t go for a peak emotional state but for a usable, receptive one. See "A shortcut back into hypnosis" for a quick way back in. Do make sure this is different from both your alert and calm cue and your synchronicity wake up cue.

Using synchronicity as your own wake-up bell

I call this the synchronicity wake up cue.

People often ask what the synchronicity means. Sometimes they get a great insight from it, but other times they get obsessed trying to work out patterns etc. I am using the noticing of the synchronicity to wake up. It doesn't have to mean anything.

Whenever I notice it, I use it as a cue for waking up inside whatever state I am in, feeling my body, feeling the room I'm in, remembering that what I'm noticing doesn't confirm what I'm reading into the coincidences, widening my state so that I can see beyond what my current state is showing me. I then look for the truth of what is going on (What is really going on, what is just my interpretation? What needs warmth? What needs protecting? What do I need to do?) and I go into the highest state I can get into, where I can see what is going on in that situation as objectively and clearly as I can, while remaining in my body, not leaving the facts of the situation and not leaving my regular human state. From that state I feel my feelings. If the state I started in is negative, I check if it is a message from my inner child or from a childhood guard part, or if I just need to grieve. I check if it is a message from my physical body (if there is a problem with my body that I need to take care of) or if I need to do something about the situation. I choose at least one thing to do that is from a loving place. I allow myself to be in whatever state is real for me, which may include objectivity, clarity, ease, peace, freedom, joy, gratitude, etc.

Installing the synchronicity cue

Mentally rehearse the following: Imagine that you have noticed a synchronicity that is obviously harmless.

Feel the "whoa" of stimulation that you just got from noticing it.

  1. Use that stimulation to feel your feet, your body, your breathing, your room.

  2. Reiterate to yourself that whenever you notice a synchronicity, it is just a bell that rings to wake you up. It doesn’t confirm what you are reading into it.

  3. Ask yourself: "What does my Nurturer think about this?", "What does my Protector think about this?", "What does my Guide think about this?"

  4. Rehearse going back to a more grounded state and to a wider view of the situation.

Rehearse yourself taking at least one action. Rehearse yourself waking up completely.

These cues do not authorize you to make a decision. They do not stop you from hearing the message of your intuition if it is warning you about something.

This is very similar to implementation intention (an if...then... plan). I'm not aware of research into implementation intention for synchronicity cues, but the general mechanisms appear to work; Gollwitzer’s paper explains how and why they work.

False positives, and turning the cue off

A downside of synchronicity cues is that they can lead to false positives. We create an association in our head on purpose when we rehearse what to do when we notice a synchronicity; however, when you are absorbed in something and/or when you are destabilized, you might think that you saw a synchronicity when in fact there wasn't any. Research has linked apophenia, the tendency to see patterns that aren't really there, with psychosis spectrum traits (see Blain and colleagues, and Grant and colleagues). That isn't saying that noticing patterns is bad.

If you feel that synchronicity cues are making these things worse (e.g. you feel like you have to look for them all the time, you can't sleep, you feel anxious, you are sure that random things happen in your life in order to tell you something, you can't tell the difference between noticing something and interpreting what it might mean, etc.), you should deactivate synchronicity cues.

You can do that by not rehearsing them anymore and by saying to yourself (when you are fully awake) that you don't want to use synchronicity as a cue anymore (e.g. "From now on, I am no longer going to use noticing synchronicity as a cue for waking up. I let go of this association, coincidences can go back to being just coincidences."). Saying this is just you deciding, it doesn't mean the association is gone straight away.

Reframing — changing the meaning without lying

Reframes are dishonest when they declare the problem was something that was good all along ("It's good nobody loves me, because I'm happier alone"). Instead, ask yourself: "What else might be true?" or "What is this part trying to do for me?" or "How might my Nurturer reframe this?" or "How might my Protector reframe this?" or "How might my Guide reframe this?" etc., etc. So we might arrive at, "If it seems like nobody loves me, could it be a part of a vicious cycle in which I expect that, and my expectations are met?"

Perceptual positions — self, other, witness, universal heart

I go through four positions: 1) seeing from Self (what do I feel from inside myself?), 2) then seeing from Other (if I sit inside the other person, what might they be feeling or protecting?), 3) then seeing from Witness (if I watched this from across the room, what would I notice?), and 4) then seeing from Universal Heart (what serves truth, love, boundaries, and the good of everyone involved?).

I will not use this to rationalize bad behavior, but only to break out of a prison of perception.

Future pacing — let the future test the insight

Once I have seen the situation more clearly, I will imagine future scenarios where my old behavior would have been triggered, to test out if my new insight is working.

If these scenarios come up, I will choose new small behaviors using an Implementation Intention

The Swish, softened

I use an NLP technique called a "Swish Pattern" (it swaps a picture of an old reaction for a picture of a new one), but I will allow my old pattern to be there, while also holding my new pattern in my awareness. I feel what comes up somatically, and compare the differences between the future scenarios of my old and new patterns.

Parts integration, without pretending the conflict is solved

I use parts integration to ask my conflicting parts what they want, what they are afraid of, what their need is, and see if I can find a 3rd option that satisfies both parts' needs, without either part dominating my behavior.

What I will not use NLP for

Here is a list of things I don't do with NLP, whether I'm hypnotizing myself or someone else:

I don't use NLP to convince anyone an intuition is true. For example, I don't use NLP or hypnosis in general, to do more than initial memory retrieval work. Memories retrieved still need external validation, especially when they were led to (which NLP tends to).

On the other hand, I also don't use NLP to talk anyone out of a real signal. That doesn't mean we can't try to calm unbearable pain, but we must keep access to the awareness of the signal so that we can do the real root cause healing.

For example, I won’t tell anyone to push past their fear before asking what it’s protecting, and I won’t make anyone feel rushed when they want to take time to process the feelings.

I don't use NLP on people without their consent nor to bypass consent via covert commands. Nearly all hypnotists claim that hypnosis cannot violate consent, which is an absurdly ignorant and unethical claim.

That includes not manipulating anyone to feel like their "no" is the ego's resistance we need to overcome, which is another way of saying they are spiritually inferior to me if they disagree with me.

If someone is in an unstable state in which they can't reasonably give consent, I don't use NLP to pretend they did.

Running the whole NLP sequence

Here is a rough outline of the entire NLP process:

  1. Induce yourself (meaning gather your attention into a soft focus) or contain yourself (meaning stabilize yourself) first if you are already too opened up to difficult memories/experiences.

  2. Ask yourself for permission: is it useful to work with this pattern today?

Get a representation of your pattern. Do you see it as an image, with size, color and location, or do you feel it like a movement or some other kind of sensation?

Find out if that part/pattern is protecting something for you.

Gently change an aspect of your pattern's representation from the prior step.

Bring in your three good adults. The Nurturer brings love in. Next, the Protector puts your helmet on, and then the Guide points the way out of the mess. They’re really all one inner adult, but picturing each with their own function can be easier before merging them together (like Captain Planet if you saw that cartoon).

Then anchor the good state you're in with a hand on your heart, two fingers touching, a particular breath or a posture, so that doing the same thing later can bring the state back.

Imagine running into a scenario where your old pattern would have triggered, and adopt a feasible new behavior. ("If I see a red cloth, then I will choose not to charge at it. I am not a bull anymore.")

Check in with your body for signals during the re-imagining process, and what else besides body signals supports your new interpretation of the situation. Ask yourself whether the new behavior you've imagined is kind and sensible.

Then come all the way back out of the altered state (see “Come all the way back”), and go do something normal.

Appendix C: The extended de-hypnosis checklist

What to do when you feel like you’re being pushed too hard by something (person, ad, ideology, spiritual teaching, romantic attraction, internal voice, etc.): Say to yourself, “Okay, something is trying to influence me here. I don’t have to obey it or attack it. I can just slow myself down and ask some questions like, 'What would this thing influence me to believe that isn’t true in order to get what it wants from me? What is my body telling me to do about this (how does my body feel)? What would love do here? What would truth do here? How would I approach this humbly? What would life have me do here?'”

If what you’re investigating is true, it can take your questioning (as the police like to say). If it’s love, it can accommodate your boundaries. If it’s real guidance, it’ll still be there tomorrow. If it’s really for you, it won’t want you to give up all the wisdom and sovereignty you’ve worked so hard to develop.

Before you do what it wants you to do, check how it’s trying to get you there. Is it rushing you, or telling you to keep things secret? Is it shaming you, or threatening to leave you out if you don’t do what it says? Maybe it makes you feel special, or there’s a sexual charge to it, or a sense that you’re spiritually above everyone else.

Then look at what it wants from you. Is it money, or sex, or your loyalty? Is it asking you to keep quiet, or to obey it?

Keep in mind though, people who mean well can ask for some of these too. A teacher charges for their time, and your partner probably wants your loyalty. If you can say no to them and they’re fine with it, that’s a good sign.

Back to the interrogation:

Is it trying to narrow down your choices? Does questioning it feel like you’re doing something wrong, or does it act like not obeying is a problem you need to fix? Does normal life start to seem boring, stupid or irrelevant when it’s around? Is it trying to seduce, scare or rescue your inner child? And does it try to stop your adult self from saying no?

If it does a bunch of these things, slow yourself down, don’t let it push you deeper into trance, don’t do anything you can’t undo, and then ground yourself for a while (take care of your body, eat, sleep, give it time, get counsel, etc).

A good guide will make your adult self stronger. A good guide can take no. A bad guide can’t take no. A bad guide wants to take your adult self’s job. A bad guide will try to make your no something that’s bad or needs to be fixed (resistance, ego, fear, low vibration, trauma response, sin, need for more of its guidance, etc).

Hypnosis-specific audit

Before you let a hypnotic recording or a live hypnotist put manipulative suggestions into your skull, interview them a bit:

First, what are they going to do to bring you under? And if you react negatively to something, will they pause to change the script? Make sure they are fine with stopping the whole thing.

And how are they deciding it's working? Just because you seem "out of it" shouldn't be a green light. Are they using sneaky moves to get past your adult judgement, like putting you into identity roles (including age regression)? Walking you down obedience ladders? Your desire for belonging also shouldn't be used for that.

Keep in mind though, you can only answer those last questions by reading the script first. You can't expect them to know what your adult judgement would accept, so make that clear ahead of time.

Will they tell you before they use techniques that affect your memory or behaviors after the session? Like fractionation or cues.

Are they low-key using your body signals to prove their theories? Like oh your hand dropped real fast, so you ARE gay.

And when it's time to come out, will they tell you clearly that the session is over, so you get your normal waking, walking self back? After it's over, check how well your critical thinking is working when you look back on how it went.

If any answer they give you is wishy washy, beware. Ask for the exact script (or the methods used since they probably aren't exactly reading a script). If they don't want to give the secrets away, walk right out that door.

Appendix D: Anxiety — body first


Remember that often the anxiety comes from a childhood guard part of you. It’s what protects you when your inner adult’s Protector isn’t functioning, the same as in childhood. It’s trying to help, making a guess about what’s coming: that you are in danger, or may be shamed, or abandoned, or fail, or be exposed, or be sick, or be rejected, or lose control, or feel something it believes would be unbearable. It can be right! It’s probably been wrong before too, and you know some of those times. Therefore, don’t start by interrogating that part.

Work in this order – first your body, then your inner child, then some inquiry, then some action:

Reduced breathing / Buteyko-style breathing

Try breathing less. (There are some good things to be said for slowing your breathing down, if you are breathing too much, or panicked. However, please don’t hold your breath until you are air hungry, which can make you more anxious in the short term.) Breathe in small amounts through your nose and let it out quietly, through your nose too. Only pause if that's comfortable. Can you make your breathing very quiet? If you feel bad, dizzy, or panicky, or have medical issues, stop.

EFT tapping

One of the easiest ways to do EFT (Emotional Freedom Techniques, or tapping) is to say something like, “Even though I have this anxiety, I am willing to be kind to the part of me that is afraid,” while you tap gently on your body and stay with what you feel. There isn’t a ton of evidence for EFT, but there are some studies on anxiety. A 2025 systematic review found 7 randomized controlled trials for anxiety disorders, and noted that EFT was more effective than no intervention in several of them. There are first-line therapies for anxiety.

Somatic Experiencing

If you would like more information about somatic experiencing, or even a free intro session, please see my somatic guide. In somatic experiencing (SE), you are not digging into the trauma. You learn to track. So you might start by tracking, by discovering where the activation is, where a neutral place is, and then whether you can move your attention from one to the other, very slowly, like a snail.

Gendlin Focusing

Focusing is another approach where you work with your body. You might say to yourself, “What is the felt sense of this whole anxiety?” Don’t answer with your head; let your body come up with a vague sense of the whole anxiety. Then ask, “What would this felt sense like me to know?” Try a word or a phrase and see how well it fits the felt sense. But also see if what the word or phrase says is true of the situation. The word may feel ok, but say something that doesn’t fit the situation, or vice versa.

Body supports

Sometimes the mind is too activated for meditation to help, so you may need to work directly with the body to calm it down. Maybe get a massage, or massage a foot, your scalp, a temple, or the back of the neck, the top of a shoulder, etc. (Avoid massaging the front of the neck. Also avoid massaging injured or numb body parts, or bruised, inflamed, or recently surgically operated on areas, or varicose veins. Also stop if the massage makes the anxiety symptoms worse, or makes you feel bad in any way, such as painful, dizzy, tingly, etc.) You’ve got lots of other options for the body too, like heat, pressure, a weighted blanket, darkness, your favorite calming music, or a massage gun if you’re careful with it, etc. Use whatever actually helps you, and don’t go surrounding the anxiety with a heap of wellness stuff.

I have a page on my ZHoly3O product, an ozonated charcoal “holy water”, where I talk about it as one of the things that can help you relax. (See the mention of Blushield on that page, too.) But neither one of those products is necessary.

Inner child love

Put your hand over your heart and your other hand on your solar plexus (the two-hand practice in Working with your inner child), and say to yourself in a loving way, “Little one, you are not alone. You don’t have to fix this problem. I’m here now to listen to you.” Make sure you're actually talking to your inner child, not just repeating a script. If you can't genuinely say all that and feel it, then just rest your hands there and notice what you find.

Anxiety script

A script you could use for anxiety is:

Where are my body parts? What are my feet doing? How is my body positioned in the chair? How is my body breathing? How is my body situated in the room?

If you’re there, little one, I’m here. Thank you, guard, for letting me know you’re here. You didn’t have to scream. I got your message.

If there is something we need to do, let me know what the first thing is that we need to do at this moment. If there is nothing to do, help my body stand down. I’m not going to abandon you, but I’m not going to worship you either. I will listen to what you have to say and reality test it and then take some action.

Appendix E: Is the daydream helping or replacing your life?

Sometimes daydreaming is something that your subconscious mind is doing to help you. It is simulating an idea or event to help you work through grief or teach you something. The very act of daydreaming may be helping you to access your creativity or allowing your subconscious to show you a potential future. Sometimes your subconscious will show you things that you may be afraid of. Sometimes it will show you what you want.

Other times daydreaming may be taking you away from your life in a detrimental way. It may be that you are escaping reality and are addicted to fantasies rather than real life. You may be dissociating or obsessing about romantic partners or escaping from being an adult.

How do you know which it is? Simply ask yourself if the daydreaming is taking you closer to life, with more clarity, love and ability to take action, or if it is taking you away from life, in a repetitive and craving way.

If it is helping you, then simply ask yourself what the guidance is and how you can use it.

If it is taking you away from life, then change your body position by sitting upright, open your eyes, feel your feet and ask your subconscious to help you.

Then ask yourself what it is that you need to do in real life that you are avoiding by daydreaming.

You may need to send a text message or stop sending text messages. You may need to clean up your room, eat, sleep, deal with grief, create, apologize, leave, tell the truth, make a plan, get help, get back to a task that you were supposed to be doing, stop chasing love that is not available to you etc.

Appendix F: Self-trust, outside guidance, and oracle-like states

[For using synchronicity as a cue rather than an oracle, see “Using synchronicity as your own wake-up bell” in Appendix B.]

One of the issues here is that people vary dramatically in how suggestible they are, both to their own suggestions and to someone else's, and the same person can be more or less suggestible in different states. Different types of suggestion (commands, imagery, music, metaphors, spiritual, etc) can be very different for different people. So there are definite advantages to both having someone work with you (pacing, containment, another set of eyes), and doing things yourself. Doing it yourself means being able to follow up immediately, to correct something in real time, to have a line of communication with guards, etc.

That said, self hypnosis may be the best way of talking to your subconscious on a day to day basis. If you’re dealing with particularly complicated trauma, blind spots, dissociation, destabilization, or relational wounds that need another nervous system, skilled outside support may be best.

Finding a hypnotist who can work with you

Ask them:

  • What they are trained in (what they can treat)

  • How they use hypnosis

  • What they will do if a suggestion doesn't fit you

  • If they are treating trauma, whether they have specific training in trauma and dissociation (having hypnosis certifications matters, but doesn't mean they are trained in trauma care)

Tell them:

  • What has/hasn't worked for you in the past

  • How direct you want them to be

  • What kind of touch (if any) you are ok with

  • What kind of imagery (if any) you are ok with

  • What you want them to do if you want them to stop

Ask them:

  • What they will do if something unexpected happens during or after the session

  • What they do if you aren't sure if something is a memory or not

  • How they will do the full return (coming out of hypnosis) at the end of the session

You should be able to be firmly encouraged, but not forced to continue if you don't want to.

Then afterwards, check in about what was actually done, regardless of what you might call the method. Ask the person why they did what they did, and whether they told you in advance. Did you agree to it? How did the person respond to you? Keep in mind that just because something made you uncomfortable, it doesn't mean it was bad, but if they saw your discomfort as meaning that you needed more of it, that might be something to look into. You might even be able to use this app or ask around to see if someone can tell you what the things the person did meant. They probably won't be able to tell you whether the person was certified in those methods, though. Then you can decide if you want to try learning to do self-hypnosis, use both, or find someone else.

When the subconscious speaks like an oracle

Your subconscious is amazing at picking up on things your conscious mind isn't getting. Whether it's anticipations, reading patterns, or just picking up on uncanny things, it's good to respect what your subconscious is picking up on. But just because your subconscious gives you an image or tells you to do something, that doesn't mean you need to treat it like an order.

Especially if it's a big thing, or something that can't be easily undone, slow down. Look at it with your adult's eyes, and take it to the council (next section).

The council — internal first, then outward

Ask your inner child, your adult, your conscious mind, your subconscious mind, your intuition, your analytical mind. But if it's bigger than all of those, ask outside of yourself, too.

Ask people who love you, people who are like you, people who have done what you are thinking of doing. Sometimes it is right to go it alone, but I would suggest making it the last step, after you've tried to get counsel.

On the other hand, it is possible to give your power away. Seeking counsel can slide into abdicating to an authority figure without you noticing.

Sometimes you need the support of your peers. In Hearthwork, the concept is that you give each other equal time, warmth, confidentiality, and acceptance of “no” as an answer, and attention without interpretation, advice, or fixing. You lend each other the Nurturer and Protector for a while, but you don’t act as each other’s Guide. Then you reverse roles. Sometimes, with a question asked carefully and at the right time, your peers can help you hear your own Guide more clearly.

Forgiveness without bypass

We cannot force forgiveness on ourselves. It has to come from a place of understanding, otherwise it can be bypassing. However, if we remain in the anger forever, we are allowing the injury to dictate how we live our lives in the present. So how do we hold all of this? We just remember that it can all be true at once. They really did hurt us and they still have to answer for it. Our boundary with them is crucial to maintain, especially after forgiveness which can tend to weaken it. One day we may find that we have forgiven them for not knowing better, just like Jesus did on the Cross.

You might say to the part of you that got hurt, “I’m not going to use spiritual mumbo jumbo to shush your cries and make you forgive before you're ready. But I’m also not going to build our whole life around this wound forever.”

Forgiving yourself

For self-forgiveness, name what you regret doing, and take responsibility for just that part. Let yourself feel the remorse by taking in the harm done. See how it went against your values. Stop short of making yourself a permanent criminal by identifying with the behaviors you're now abandoning.

Do what you can to put it right. Every so often, come back to how you got to be someone who could do that, until you understand the pattern well enough that it can't drive you anymore.

Appendix G: Altered states

I’m not saying you should use substances, but many people have used them when they first did self-hypnosis, contacted their inner child, or talked to their Higher Self. I’d learn the inner child work sober first, with enough Nurturer and Protector in the room before you go deep (see the inner child reparenting section). If you don’t have them in you yet, borrow them from someone steady, as long as they won’t take advantage of that to try to run your trip for you.

Do not use altered states to try to force yourself to get to a certain revelation about yourself, but rather to help yourself become more in tune with love and to better your life.

A lot of people have had dramatic openings to love at some point in their life. If you've had a similar experience, you know that there is a difference between having experienced it at one point, or only being able to access it in a certain state, and being able to get to it at any point in your life. While you're building a way to reach love on a normal day, let that experience you had serve as a reference point for what can happen. (If you can't give that love to the child, try to deal with why you can't, instead of trying to ramp up the experience. Often there's a child guard part that’s seen love come with strings or get revoked before in a painful way. Love starts with hearing out the resistance to it first.)

Use your altered state of consciousness, either while you're in it or after, to help yourself by asking things like:

Help me to become more grounded. Help me to only take what I can integrate. Help me to remember love. Help me to come back when it's time. Help me to figure out how to change one thing in my life to make it better.

Don’t use self-hypnosis to try to keep the altered state of consciousness going indefinitely, to force memories to come back, to make yourself more afraid, to get by without sleep, to put off taking care of medical problems, to become less communicative, etc.

Stabilize before you interpret

Self-hypnosis can be powerful when you are in an altered state of consciousness (e.g. when you're on loveyhuasca, meditating, have a lucid dream, are dealing with grief, are dealing with sexual energy, are praying, haven't slept, are around plant medicine, etc, etc, etc). Sometimes you may open yourself up to things faster than you are able to put them into context with your adult self.

So what feels like a meaningful altered state of consciousness may or may not actually be meaningful - it could be your inner child, it could be a childhood guard part, it could be all of the things you're ashamed of / sad about / angry about / etc, coming out to be dealt with. Or it could be that your body is overloaded, your environment is chaotic, you haven't slept enough, etc, etc, etc. Regardless of how scary an image is, regardless of how amazing the lighting was on the "download", regardless of how bad you feel, it doesn't necessarily mean what you think it does.

Before you even think about interpreting what your altered state of consciousness is, ask yourself something simpler:

“What kind of problem is happening?”

Appendix H: Demons, jinn, hostile presences, and “Unattached Burdens”

I speak here from experience.

When you’re doing hypnosis, meditation, lucid dreaming, loveyhuasca-ing, going through a period of intense grief, praying, or whatever else may alter your consciousness, it’s possible you may run into something that you perceive to be a demon, a jinn, a parasitic energy, or whatever other dark entity or terrifying archetype you may encounter – it may be a voice or an image, and it doesn’t just feel like an emotion or a younger part of you, but something that feels intrusive, hateful, seductive, mocking, etc.

First of all, it doesn’t matter if they’re demons or not. They may be literal demons, for all you know. Or they may be manifestations of your mind, burdens you’re carrying from previous relationships, whatever. What matters is finding the best way to deal with them, so you end up safer, clearer, more loving, more grounded and less hooked.

Start with the room, the body, and basic safety

So what do you do when you encounter a demon, or a jinn, or something else that’s bad and intrusive?

First of all, do the usual things – make sure you’re not in danger from a medical standpoint, check your environment, check your body, make sure you’re not overstimulating yourself, make sure you’re not opening your mind too much. If you need to, open your eyes, ground yourself in your body and your environment, remove yourself from any bad influence you’re surrounding yourself with, stop stimulating your mind. Don’t go further into whatever it is you’re doing – don’t be curious, don’t try to figure out what it is.

Tell yourself, “If this can be integrated in this moment, it’s welcome. If not, it can wait until later. The adult part of me is still here, and my body can return to safety.”

If you feel like you’re in any danger of doing anything harmful to yourself or others, if you feel like you’re going psychotic and you’re afraid of it, if you feel like you won’t be able to sleep when the self-hypnosis session is over, if you feel overly paranoid, if you feel physical pain in your chest, if you feel like you’re going to have a seizure, if you feel like you’re too hot in a bad way, if you feel like you’ve taken too much of something, if you feel like you won’t be able to function in the world anymore – then the self-hypnosis session is over, and you should get help in the real world.

A response check, not a verdict about what it is

If, however, you feel like you’re stable enough to stay present, check in with yourself and see what’s best to do. Put your hand on your heart if that feels okay, breathe comfortably, and stay aware of the room.

Find out if it helps to be warm towards it or to keep it at arm’s length, and keep your boundary either way. Keep in mind that you don’t have to do what it tells you to do.

If you feel vulnerable, if you feel tightness in your throat, if you feel sadness or fear or like you’re trying to protect something, call upon your Nurturer. If you don’t feel better, or if it continues being hostile towards you, stop trying to deal with it and return to your body and the room.

Do not fight from fear

Your first instinct may be to fight it, banish it, hate it, show it that you’re more powerful than it is, argue with it, panic, turn completely into the frightened part of you, etc. Some people may want to use traditional prayer or mantras or protection or banishing or exorcism techniques, and those may well work for them. But if you’re fighting it out of fear, it can backfire:

It becomes a center of attention if you fear it, and if you hate it, you give it a hook in you.

So the next time it comes up, you can just say, “I see that there is no reason to hate and revile what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to merge with or become what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to obey what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to try to defeat what I am experiencing out of fear. I choose to return my attention to loving awareness.”

A bodily boundary: compassion present, door closed

You can also tell the presence that while you have compassion for it, the door is closed.

This is especially a good idea if the presence you’re experiencing seems even the slightest bit hostile, cold, alien, parasitic, etc. It’s not really helpful to approach it with the kind of soft, sentimental love that you’d use if you were inviting it to stay for tea.

Sit up, make sure your feet are grounded on the floor, straighten your spine, pull your shoulder blades back a little, open your chest a little, feel the boundary of your torso’s skin, feel the perimeter of your body, and tell the presence, “While I have compassion for you and any suffering you may be experiencing, this suffering needs to go back where it came from. The boundary of my skin is the boundary of an adult now, and you’re not welcome to come inside the house anymore.”

If it might be psychosis or destabilization

Everything above assumes that the “hostile presence” is symbolic, the kind that settles down when you ground yourself and meet it with love. What you’re going through might instead be psychosis, or your mind becoming unstable in some other way.

Get help right away if you feel under attack for days at a time, or you can’t sleep or function normally. The same goes if you hear voices telling you to do things, feel you need to hurt yourself or somebody else, or feel you’re going manic. And get help if you can’t communicate normally, or can’t tell the difference between what you’re experiencing symbolically and normal reality. This isn’t something to deal with in hypnosis.

The General Rule

The General Rule is: if you find that what you're doing is making you more loving, more grounded, more relational, more honest, more functional, then it's probably OK to keep doing it gently. But if it's making you more paranoid, sleepless, grandiose, isolated or scared, or things feel more and more unreal, stop going deeper and get help.

The cosmic-download humility test

Sometimes a hostile presence, a shadow force or a grandiose part comes at you with flattery instead of fear. It may try to give you a sense that you’re special, try to “download cosmic info” to you, try to give you a special mission, make you feel doing the dishes is beneath you, etc. You can just say, “Thank you for the imagery, but I don’t need your grand performance. Unless the info you’re trying to download to me can help me do the dishes, clean my room, be more honest, be more helpful, be more loving, keep my promises, take care of my body by resting and eating etc, I don’t need it.”

Don’t become an entity hobbyist

Finally, some of you may be tempted to make this an area of hobbyism – to become interested in demons or jinn or hostile presences or Unattached Burdens or whatever. I just don’t want you to do that. They all lead to the same thing, which is how to maintain a presence of love without giving up your boundaries, while also making your mind harder to hook.

Appendix J: A practical induction library

Preview the sequence. Work out in advance which one you want to do and what you want to use it for (pick just one) and whether or not you want to do it at all. Any of these families of induction can be done as light inductions, with eyes open, or you can just do them as a no-trance inner contact.

Once you start an induction, you will want to notice what’s going on (even if nothing changes) and work out what to do next: move, imagine something else, change the sensory channel you’re using, do nothing, or stop the induction sequence and do something else… Whatever you do, make sure you bring yourself fully back to waking at the end of an induction.

NB: if you can’t ‘see’ the images you’re trying to visualize, that’s fine, change them into words, rhythm, spatial relations, something to touch or move or just plain knowing…

1. Stretch and release

Work out what will be supporting you, and focus on the feeling of support. You can also focus on where one of your hands or feet is. Either actually, or in your imagination, stretch a little bit, either stretching that part of your body slightly or pressing gently down into the support, and then let go of the effort, just relax. See if there is any difference (it’s fine if there isn’t). Do this for no more than 3 places on your body. Let releasing guide you as you go through the sequence. Which bit of your body, or inner voice, needs this? What do you need to support it? Bring your body back to normal movement and return.

2. Supported floating

Work out what is supporting you and where your weight is. Imagine that the support is holding a tiny bit (1%) more of your effort. It’s like floating but you don’t have to lose contact, or you can just keep it at the same level, with just enough weight/pressure. See what happens. You can fractionate this if you want, perhaps noticing your normal awareness when you breathe in and the support when you breathe out. Tell yourself you are going to fractionate before you do it. What can you hear more easily now? Keep the support, come back to your normal weight and firm contact, and return.

3. Rhythmic breath or pulse

Use your breathing or a slight hum in your throat for a natural rhythm. You could also tap your finger, or follow a sound outside. You can even do it by counting to yourself (silently) or with no rhythm at all. Say “here, and here again” over and over, and notice what you pay attention to in between the phrases. Then stop saying it. Notice what you become aware of in the first beat – you don’t need to make your mind blank, just notice what comes up. Does it make any words, feelings, needs or actions more clear? Then let everything go back to its normal rhythm, and come back.

4. Unwinding object

Unwinding an object is good for when you have a few different strands to one thing you want to work on. Imagine an object unwinding – maybe some yarn, some bubbles, or some knots that you place beside you. Once you’ve placed them all, give yourself a minute to look at them all. You don’t have to change anything, but you can label them if you need to (just a word or two). How many did you put down? Which one do you need to work on, and what does it need – warmth, protection, action? Keep one strand in your attention and put the others somewhere you can see them and return.

5. Sensory journey

Pay attention to the sounds in the room you’re in and how your body feels, then imagine yourself walking somewhere. Where do you want to go? Listen to the sounds of your footsteps and take in your surroundings as you walk. What do objects feel like if you touch them? What landmarks do you see? When you feel you have gathered enough of your attention, imagine the scenery changing until you are in a place where you can talk to whoever you need to (or you can just cut to the chase, if you prefer). Pay attention to something without worrying about what it means. Allow yourself to be curious about what’s going to happen next, but don’t make it a “what are they going to do to you?” kind of thing. When you are ready to finish, imagine yourself walking back the way you came and close the space.

6. Gradual wave

Start with a very small neutral area, feel into it a little and then imagine a wave of warmth, steadiness, or attention spreading out to a new area that is still ok. Sense into the new area (or continue using the old). See if there is anything different at each step. Does the body want any contact, space, words, other care? Let the mental image go, sense your body, sense your contact points, come back.

7. Care workshop

Prepare the workshop (time, place, tools, boundaries, etc). Invite the signal/feeling/young part to come only as close as is welcome. Try to avoid any language that makes you feel you're not one person. Care for it as best you can, by asking what it wants and giving that if you can, or sensing into it and responding, or just being with it. Take breaks, get back in contact if that's ok, take more breaks, etc. Keep bringing in adult functions (or borrow someone else's function). Have the adult part of you run the show. The goal is to make it "good as you" rather than "good as new", not to erase history. Close the workspace, reassure yourself that you are one whole person, that you're an adult now, and come back.

8. Resource embodiment

Pick a quality you understand and want more of, like warmth, being able to hold a boundary, steadiness, curiosity or dignity. Imagine yourself with just 1% more of it. (If you'd rather not step into it, you can watch an imagined you have it instead.) What's different? How do you sit, and how does your voice sound? Try it a few times; each time, it gets a little easier to find. Come back, keeping the quality and a positive action that you would do with it, but leaving the imagined you behind, saying "I am me".

9. No-trance inner contact

We don't need to go into trance to get a lot of the benefits of the communion technique! Here's my no-trance version:

For pacing (warm-up), notice reality as it is now. What do you see around you? Is today Monday, your favorite day of the week? Hopefully your name didn't suddenly change to Larry.

For the suggestion: in your mind, ask a clear question and then listen gently.

Now keep your sense of agency during the answer phase. You might notice an answer popped up, or you might decide you don't know. You can always take a break or rephrase the question. Sometimes you might ask advice from someone else.

Keep aware of the subtle inner signal: write whatever you get. It might be a word or sensation. An impulse or objection. Sometimes there's no signal, just silence, itself a kind of signal. Don't jump to interpreting it as validation of some theory.

Hand off the role: have your adult self answer that same question in whatever way is appropriate, preferably by writing or doing.

Integration/exit: reality test the signal you noticed. As part of testing, if it seems appropriate, you can do something simple based on it.

There's no elaborate exit protocol here since you didn't go into trance. Just close the exercise and carry on with your day.
