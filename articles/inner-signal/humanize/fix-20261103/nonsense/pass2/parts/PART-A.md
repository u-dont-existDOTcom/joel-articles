[26] # What I mean by hypnosis

[27] APA Division 30 commonly defines hypnosis as focused attention, reduced peripheral awareness, and increased responsiveness to suggestion. That’s basically true, but there’s a lot more than meets the eye. In fact, hypnosis is such a broad and common type of altered state that it’s hard to confine it to one use. But I’m using hypnosis here as a therapy.

[28] And hypnosis as a therapy is as old as time itself. While the modern technique of it was discovered by Franz Anton Mesmer in the 1770s, shamans have been using dancing, music, and drugs to induce hypnosis for therapeutic purposes forever. We enter into trance states every night which resemble hypnosis as we go to sleep and as we wake up as well. Those states can also be used for deep therapy.

[44] My dad always said: “Always, and in everything, expect the very best. But be prepared to take what you get. [45] Another quote I use all the time from my dad… when someone asks me “How’s it going?” I say “Better & better!” This really confuses a lot of people. [50] # Hypnosis Evidence

[51] Is there real (not magical) evidence for hypnosis? Yes. In 2024, there was an overview of 49 meta-analyses containing a total of 261 distinct primary studies. [73] # A useful map of the mind

[74] Note: whenever I refer to the subconscious, I’m referring to all of the processes under the surface of your conscious awareness: perceptions, emotions, desires, avoidance tendencies, imagery, memories, body signals, habits, intuitions, automatic behaviors and so forth. You can work with all of these together as if it’s all one mind, or you can work with it as a set of parts, an ecology if you like. There isn’t a right or wrong way to approach it. They’re both maps. Don’t form a priesthood around either one!

[75] Working with parts gives you precision, and working with the Higher Self gives you coherence. Communion is going back and forth between the two.

[76] If you need to work with the whole, the subconscious as one:

[77] a) Invoke the subconscious in some way. Something simple like ‘Hello, wise subconscious, please help me understand…’ or whatever works for you.

[78] If you need to work with the ecology in the subconscious, notice what each part wants: the child parts usually want love, the guards want you safe, the critic wants you to meet some standard, the fantasy making parts are after possibility, body memories want to be recognized, the social part wants to belong, the spiritual part wants God or source or what’s best for all beings, and the wiser part just wants what’s true. Then:

[79] a) It’s usually a good idea to start with unity. So invoke your Higher Self: “Hello, my Higher Self/universal heart-mind/wise subconscious, please guide me…”

[80] b) Move into differentiation. “Which part is afraid? Which part wants to protect you? Which part wants to avoid the whole thing? Which part knows what to do? Which part is too young for this?” Etc.

[81] c) Move back into unity again. “And all of these parts belong to the one life, and as the adult in charge of this life, you can lead with love…” Etc.

[82] ## Sometimes start with the whole

[83] Some people make themselves too fragmented by starting to work with parts. ‘Here’s a part! Here’s another one! I’ll get twelve of them in a room and give them all an office to hang around in!’ If that happens to you, it’s better to start from the sacred whole.

[84] Start like this: ‘I’m approaching this from the universal heart. I’m approaching this from my Higher Self. I’m approaching this from the part of me that knows it belongs to life before it belongs to any wound, or role, or family, or group, or identity, or ideology, or story. So please help me see what’s true and kind and timely and stabilizing and useful here.’ Etc.

[85] It can be that you can’t even access your Higher Self, or the universal heart, or whatever you call it. (By the way, if it doesn’t work for you, don’t use it!) In that case, start by just witnessing whatever is coming up. Notice the room, your body, the fact that you’re in a younger state etc. Being able to notice is enough adult presence for the first step.

[86] Once you have the ability to notice, and to differentiate, you don’t need to keep rehearsing it. Just work on whatever it is you need to work on (often being warm to yourself, protecting yourself and knowing what to do etc). So even though it can be useful to talk about your Higher Self, it doesn’t mean you already have all the good inner parenting functions etc that you need. And I don’t think the Higher Self has any specific wisdom. It’s a vantage point, where more wisdom and compassion can come up, but problems can also look like they were never problems at all from up there, and that can ironically lead instead to more foolish indifference.

[87] When something specific comes up, that’s the time to sort out what it is. “Is this my inner child? Or is this a guard? Is this my body? Is this fear? Is this intuition? Is this my social part? Is this divine guidance, or is this my interpretation of divine guidance?”

[92] ## When the adult role is not available yet

[93] The problem is that some people do not have access to their adult functions at will. Instead, they may go blank, access an inherited inner critic, or feel like a child part who may be playing at being an adult (what I call forced adulthood). Telling them to just access their adult is like telling a baby bird to just fly.

[94] For that baby bird, I use a kind of provisional bridge (also found in Schema Therapy) that I call “borrowed adulthood,” which I'll detail here.

[95] First, access your witnessing position if necessary, just noticing what’s there, without needing it to be warm or wise or certain. If you already know how to access different internal positions, move straight to the adult function you need to borrow.

[96] If you have a safe adult in your life, you can borrow from them. If not, borrow from a mentor, an older brother/sister, your imagined future self, your god/goddess or higher power, a value you have, a plan you have written, etc. It’s ok to just borrow what you need right now, warmth or a boundary or direction, just like you would borrow a cup of sugar from a neighbor.

[97] Next, put on your adult hat as if you are an apprentice adult, and pick the words or the action yourself. Then do at least part of it.

[98] Last, return the adult hat, noticing what you borrowed and what you were able to do on your own (even if it’s only 5%).

[99] The adult hat is only borrowed: the person you borrowed it from stays beside you, and you still make the choice. The longer version of this exercise can be found in my Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide.

[100] You don’t have to have trustworthy people in your history. Use someone you remember when that helps, or just imagine a good adult whose intention is to take good care of you. See if you can imagine what they would say/do in your situation, with your history, limitations, options, etc. If you can’t imagine a good adult, or just imagining a caring grown-up makes you feel more threatened, or lonelier, start smaller, with the least cruel response you can believe. Once you’ve found something good, try it out instead of repeating the exercise.

[101] It can be helpful to use borrowing when you’re in the adult position and don’t know what a decent adult would do, especially when your inner child doesn’t trust you and every response you can think of is retaliating, defending your goodness, collapsing or leaving. How would a decent adult hear that distrust? Borrow that one response. You may still feel young when you do it. Feeling like an adult can come after you act like one.

[109] ## Instead of blame

[110] Be aware of what you are teaching your other positions. For example, are you teaching your little one that you are going to love them no matter what? Or are you teaching them that it is their responsibility to believe your love for them, and that if they don't, it is their fault? Is it two separate positions, one making the vow to love your little one no matter what, and the other blaming your little one for not taking in your love? Or is it one position doing both of these things? They may be younger than you are now. Both talk in your grown-up voice, so to your little one they're the same. Be careful not to take the blaming to mean your love really is conditional, when it could just be another part talking.

[111] When you're placing your younger self on trial, instead of asking which age of you deserves the blame, ask: What was that age of you trying to get, or get away from? What did it understand back then? What options could it actually see? Could it stand to take any of them? What happened because of the choice it made? What was missing? Some ability, or help/protection from someone else? What can you learn or repair now, and how would you act differently in such situations? Even if what it did was harmful to you and others, punishing yourself now is not going to give it the capacity it needed at the time.

[112] # Working with your inner child

[113] ## The two-hand practice

[114] There is a simple and easy body technique to connect with your inner child. Just place one hand in the center of your chest, which physically connects you to your love, and then place your other hand just below that, on your solar plexus, where your inner child can be.

[115] Imagine a divine Love from above, with the inner child below, in the solar plexus area. The inner adult is present. You can also use the hand on the solar plexus to massage that area.

[116] Gently breathe in lots of love and begin to send that love from your heart down to your inner child as you breathe out, noticing what comes up for you. If some comes back up, let it, and send it on down again, so it goes around like a loop. That part's a gift from them, and they don't owe it to you, so if nothing comes back, just keep sending yours. If you feel resistance, just meet it with love. If you feel or see something, just meet that with love as well. If you can't find any wealth today, just leave your hands where they are and notice what's there. Don't pretend to feel something you don't. Let it go back and forth between your heart and your inner child, like a real conversation.

[126] ## The Protector

[127] As the Protector, set boundaries, so your inner child can believe the warm stuff. Have you set yourself up to do it in a way that's likely to be successful? It still counts if doing it doesn't make you feel better right away, and checking it off a list doesn't mean it helped.

Your parent’s harsh tone doesn’t come with the job, though, so be careful not to ask for the “tough it out, you don’t have needs, you just need discipline!” kind of direction. Without the Guide, you can also slide from looking after your inner child into spoiling it: sweets and screens can pass for loving yourself, and so can getting it out of anything hard. Ask them for help in directing you to do adult things in a way that takes your needs into account: asking for the help you need, expressing your preferences, letting a safe person see you in an imperfect way, finding ways to get your need for closeness met without punishing someone for their need for space, etc. etc.

[133] Affirmations might be helpful here, too: “We’re not just here to feel good. We’re here to become whole. We’re here to belong to life, not only to people who approve of us. We’re here to serve truth/beauty/love/God/source/nature/the universe/dharma/the Tao/the benefit of all beings,” etc. etc.

[134] Start with whatever adult function you can connect to, but most of us need enough Nurturer and Protector first, before the Guide. Otherwise the Guide just overrules your inner child in the name of something higher (spiritual bypassing, dressed up as direction). Even if you start with protection and can’t do the warmth stuff yet, make sure the direction you’re being led in is appropriate for your inner child’s growth (i.e. not depriving them of things they need, like play or support, etc.)

[135] ## Working with your guards

[136] Use parts language here too, if it helps. Often, we see a sensation or a distraction or an objection as something that needs to be explored, when in fact it might just be hunger, or reasonable skepticism about something, or a sentence that doesn’t fit. Still, notice it, and then look at what might be going on.

[137] If you're not careful, you might find yourself expecting that when you go within to meet your inner child, you'll find your inner child.

[138] Instead, you might feel numb, cynical, angry, suddenly hungry, like you need to look at your phone, sexually aroused, like you need to make plans, sleepy, dissociated, or you might even think that what you're doing is stupid. Those are not failures. Often what you're experiencing is a very charming guard. It's what protects you when your consciously installed parental Protector isn't functioning, the same as when you were a child. It might not be a guard at all, though. Maybe it's your inner child, mad that you took so long to come back. Maybe it's your inherited parent script, or something from earlier today, or a bit of each.

[139] You can tell that part something like, “I see you. I'm not going to force my way past you. What's going on?” Or however you'd actually say that. Then wait and listen patiently. If nothing comes, that's fine for now. No need to answer for that part just because you'd set this up as a healing session. Later on, you can ask it, “What are you afraid would happen if you stopped protecting me by…?”

[140] If necessary, take time and listen. (Perhaps they are afraid that you will lie to yourself again or pretend again or that it will be too painful or you will spiritually bypass again or you will forgive someone who shouldn’t be forgiven yet or you will see what you want to see or that you will leave them hanging after the session or…)

[141] Now talk to the guard. Tell them that it makes sense that they want to protect you and that they don’t have to stop protecting you, but that you want to know what could make them trust you 1% more that you can protect yourself. If it says no, I’d listen, and if it says no to going deeper in this work or to an altered state, I’d stop there and not push past that no, even when it sounds like a prediction.

[142] Most of the time, the guard wants you to do certain things: set boundaries, do whatever you are supposed to do, eat, not text someone, keep a promise, go to sleep, talk to the therapist, admit that you owe someone money, write something down, etc.

[143] ## Your inherited parent script

[144] One important thing to become aware of when approaching your inner child is your inherited parent script. This is the set of rules and ways of treating yourself that you internalized as a child, mostly based on how your parent(s) treated you, and it doesn’t always sound like a voice. This is not the same as your inner adult (Nurturer, Protector, Guide) that you build up on purpose. Often this script acts as a critic or warden, telling your inner child not to cry or complain. You might be hearing, "Don't be needy! Don't embarrass us! And don't be independent." So when you begin talking to your inner child in a new, loving way, that dusty old script may huff and puff that what you’re doing is “fake”.

[145] ## Protection that knows when to stop scanning

[146] Do you want to embody your Protector? Then you could practice this:

[147] orient yourself (look around, feel the support around you, etc.)

[148] assess the situation (know what you know, what you don’t know and check out what you need to)

[149] differentiate between actual/real danger, unknown, past or imagined dangers

[150] set boundaries (start with one and keep it in proportion to the danger; move, stop, delay, ask, leave, get support, etc.)

[151] verify if it is safe enough (you don't need perfect certainty, just enough real-world evidence to take the next step)

[152] stand down and rest (stop scanning once staying on alert isn't helping anymore)

[153] ## Inner child reparenting

[154] I see a lot of connection between reparenting therapy and self hypnosis. I’d learn to do inner-child therapy sober first, in your ordinary mindstate, since that’s the one you’ll be living in, and get enough Nurturer and Protector in the room before you go deep. When you go into hypnosis, your inner child might come to you. It might show up in any form. It can be an image, a posture, a voice, a memory, an emotion, etc. It can be any age. It can be any vulnerable part of you. If there is no child there, just work with a guard or whatever you find.

[155] ## Don’t rush the repair

[156] When your inner child does show up, spend some quality time with them before taking off the kid gloves, so to speak, and jumping into the healing work.

[157] They might like to hear something like, “Baby boopaloopa, I’m here with you now. You don’t have to work to explain anything or impress me with how fast you can get better. Can I help you feel less alone?”

[158] What does your inner child need? It can be anything: warmth, to play, to be protected, to mourn, to be angry, food, rest, boundaries, better relationships, to stop fantasizing, to stop working their butt off, that you keep a promise to them today, etc. etc.

[159] When a younger you, a guard, a feeling or a signal comes up in your body, take it slowly:

[160] A. Prepare yourself to tend to them with some privacy, but still able to access support if needed. Check how much grown-up you is really in the room first. Can you take a moment before you react? When you decide something, do you follow through? If you answered "No," because right now you’re mostly still stuck identifying as the frightened little boo, hold off on the deeper conversation. Do what makes you feel adult before you try to talk to little you.

[161] B. Look at what's there before judging or deciding what it is.

[162] C. Ask them what you can do or if you can move closer to them. Even if you feel like you already know what they need, ask if they'd prefer warmth, space, verbal reassurance, silence, distance, whatever.

[163] D. Give them what you can: pick a need and do the smallest thing that would help.

[164] E. Give yourself some time to let what you did sink in- it may take sleep, etc.

[165] F. Reconnect with them if they're willing- don't expect them to merge, go away, forgive you, become happy, etc.

[166] G. Stop and rest before the caring turns into another performance, and reconnect with them later to see what was helpful.

[167] Remember the goal isn't to make yourself good as new but to make yourself good as you- more supported and connected to yourself. If you don't like the idea of part work or it makes you feel more divided, you can work with a signal or a feeling instead of a part, or with an imaginary younger you that you feel embodies the issue you're having.

[168] ## Play and rest still need an adult present

[169] When you're reparenting, it's okay to play with things that interest your younger states, whether that means touching things of different textures, drawing, exercising, roleplaying with a plushie in a different voice, etc. As long as your reality testing is good and you remember that you aren't a child or pet or doll or whatever and that nobody owns you, it's okay to work on getting your play ability back. A lot of the best discoveries come when you're both just messing around. Play not only paves the way for therapy, but is a part of how successful therapy should be measured.

[170] Resting doesn’t mean you have to become helpless. It’s okay to limit the number of decisions you are going to be making for a certain amount of time, it’s okay to put the things you might not get to on a list, and it’s okay to let your inner adult keep watch for a while. You can still get up and move around, stop resting, and get back to what you were doing whenever you need to.

[171] ## Non-dominant hand dialogue

[172] If talking out loud or visualizing conversations feels fake, try writing it out. Tell your inner child or guard to write with your nondominant hand, and talk to them with your dominant. It might be a little hard to write with your nondominant hand but you can kind of do it. So for example, they might say "I don't trust you" with your nondominant hand, and you can say with your dominant hand "I know you don't trust me right now but I love you and I want to know how I've fucked up. I don't expect you to trust me but I want to hear you out and give you a promise I can keep today" and they can say "what promise?" and you can say "I'm going to make sure I eat today/ clear off my desk/ not text so-and-so". Only say the "I love you" part if you can feel it. If you don't feel the love yet, say you care.

[173] # When “your voice” is everybody else’s voice

[174] It can be difficult to connect with your inner child if your inner child didn’t have the opportunity to develop. This happens often when you grow up in a situation where you don’t have a private self, for example when a controlling family, a rigid religion or an enmeshed community takes over very early in your life. Or you are pushed to achieve very early in life. The result is that you don’t know what you feel, what you want, what you believe, what you like and dislike until you have heard someone else’s opinion about it. If you are in that situation, it can be difficult to follow the advice to ‘love yourself’. You don’t even know who ‘yourself’ is! So you can do reparenting and finding yourself in parallel. For others, it is very easy to work with the inner child because they can feel it right away.

[175] You are in the first camp if:

[176] Your opinion is very different when you are with different people

[177] You agree with people in the moment but when you are by yourself you realize that you don’t agree with them

[178] You take responsibility for the emotions of others, at the expense of yourself

[179] When you try to love yourself, it feels more like a show

[180] When you try to find out how your inner child feels, you feel that you are guessing instead of feeling

[181] You can rebel, but you can’t choose yet

[182] One way of explaining this is that your childhood didn’t leave you much room for finding out what you like, what you want to try out and who you are when nobody is grading you. You were too busy avoiding pain, reading what was dangerous and what you had to do to stay in a good bond, etc.

[183] Taking a break. Sometimes it may be wise to remodel yourself, for a time, from the people or group that you normally would get answers from. Some people come back later on different terms, and sometimes the break has to be long-term because the pattern of losing yourself with them is deeply entrenched. As you become more your own person, you learn to tell which of those apply. You want to let your quieter preferences bubble up to the surface so you can be sure you’re not just echoing your social group.

[184] What is the difference between a human being and a human doing? If you don’t feel good enough as a human being, you will try to become good enough as a "human doing" by, for example, being beautiful, doing something well, being smart, having a certain status, being spiritual, etc. Those can all be real gifts, although they still become a kind of bargain for belonging when failing in these areas feels like losing what it means to have your own inherent value.

[185] So in order to find yourself, you can ask yourself:

[186] What do I like when nobody is looking?

[187] What do I believe when I don’t check with other people?

[188] What do I want that is neither in rebellion nor in compliance with others?

[189] What can I try on that I don’t need to be good at?

[190] With whom can I be myself?

[191] What do I feel in my body and what do I make out of it?

[192] Give your inner child the opportunity to do something that has no value for its resume and that it doesn’t need to be good at. For example, painting, dancing, gardening, learning a language, playing a sport, doing volunteer work, playing a musical instrument, etc. Because often your inherited parent script tells your inner child that it is supposed to be good at something as soon as it starts doing it, which is humiliating. The more consciously sculpted adult in you can answer, “We’re finding out whether we enjoy this, not proving our worth.” So try things out long enough until the humiliation is gone.

[193] ## Belonging without betraying yourself

[194] When you are a child, you feel that you need to belong to the family in order to survive. This is why people do things for their family, religion, romantic partner, political tribe, spiritual group, institution, online community etc., that they wouldn’t otherwise do. Part of adulting is working out where you are selling yourself in order to belong and choosing your own ground, and finding out you can disappoint someone and not disappear. A large part of your subconscious mind is made out of other people.

[195] Where am I betraying myself in order to belong?

[196] What does it feel like to belong without betraying myself?

[197] Where do I think I am following my own guidance, but I am in fact following my belonging program?

[198] Where do I think I am doing things out of love, but I am actually doing it out of fear of not belonging?

[199] Where do I think I am listening to my conscience, but I am actually listening to my conditioning?

[200] Where do I think I am doing something out of devotion, but I am actually doing it out of compliance?

[201] Where do I think I am free, but I am actually rebelling?

[202] And then notice what your body tells you. It is not always correct, but it is a good place to start.

[203] # De-hypnosis: wake up inside the influence

[204] One of the best side-effects of learning self-hypnosis is that sudden "waking up" to all the ways your attention is being shaped in your day-to-day life. There are actually multiple patents on using screens to send subliminal messages, but even without that kind of potentially illegal tech, the screen is one connection to the omnipotent Algorithm. Which shows us ads and guides our preferences. Then we wonder why we vote for this or that politician. But there are also the older, more mundane influences: erotic imprinting (as if breast implants are actually sexy), religious fear, which tags along with shame, charismatic authority and pressure to belong. And all of that drilled repeatedly into us, because one exposure won't do the trick. Sometimes it’s benign – like lullabies or prayers or being taught by a good teacher or listening to a beautiful song or being in love. But you may want to ask yourself what effect it’s having on your ability to see clearly and make choices.

[205] Manipulation often comes with any of the following: urgency, feeling special, FOMO (fear of missing out), the threat of not being included, sexual charge, shame, confusion, the promise that someone will rescue you, destiny, pressure to obey, secret knowledge, a sense of certainty about something that comes way too quickly, and a sense that one good question will break the spell.

[206] ## If you sense you are being influenced

[207] First of all, if you sense you are being hypnotically influenced, stop. Don’t obey immediately, but don’t rebel automatically, just realize that you are being influenced.

[208] Secondly, here are some of the ways people use hypnotic language to manipulate: you consent to something without realizing it, you only get one choice, or someone slips something you never agreed to into a sentence. For example:

[209] “You will soon realize that I am right…” Or “You may, in time, decide to admit that…” Or “The only reason you do not agree is…” Or “Part of you already knows…” Or “Your higher self wants…” Or “Everyone who is awake knows…” Or “The reason you do not believe that is true is because your ego does not want you to…”

[210] If you are unsure, you may like to ask yourself the following questions:

[211] What are they asking me to agree to? What choice are they giving me? What are they assuming that I may not agree with? Who benefits if I comply? What is the emotionally charged part of what they’re saying? What are they leaving out? If I say no, what will the consequences be? (Will they punish me, shame me, laugh at me, leave me out, tell me I’m not ‘awake’ etc?)

[212] And afterwards: do I come away from them more present and grown up, or less? Be extra careful with all this when you’re in a deep or powerful state, because that’s when you need your boundaries most.

[213] ## Soft language can still take away choice

[214] Indirect suggestions can bypass the subject’s skeptical guard. They do this by not directly telling the subject what to do.

[215] Here are some of the ways that happens:

[216] 1) Utilizing induction techniques while seemingly explaining something (even something like "anti hypnosis" can use the same mechanics of induction).

[217] 2) In any way making it so that whatever the subject says shows that a suggestion has worked, when this may not be the case (this includes answering yes, no, noticing something, not noticing something, trying not to do something, not answering, etc etc etc)

[218] 3) Including phrases such as “whether or not you notice” etc etc, so either way counts as success

[219] 4) Including any concepts that may make a subject think they must obey, or take on a role, or that it is good/safe to please the guide or belong to someone etc etc.

[220] 5) Fractionating a subject without telling them that you are going to take them in and out of absorption a few times.

[221] 6) Giving a subject cues (words, gestures, blinks, sounds, listening a certain number of times etc) that you intend to use to make them do something at a later date, without informing them exactly what it is that they will be doing.

[222] 7) Including suggestions for amnesia, or using dreams, gaps in memory, imagery, body experiences etc as evidence to make a subject think that something has happened when this may not necessarily be the case.

[223] 8) Not taking a subject out at the end of a recording, or leaving them with a role to play, with their judgment down, with their physical abilities down, with their minds blank, or in any other way different for an indefinite length of time.

[224] In order for a subject to be making a real choice while in hypnosis, there must be at least one way for them not to carry on with the hypnosis if they wish. This could be by making the hypnosis lighter, using a different method of hypnosis, being in full awareness while in hypnosis, or being able to pause or stop the hypnosis. Just so you know, if you say no, you are entitled to mean no, even if someone with a nice voice is asking you to do something. And just because you don't say anything, or don't know what to say, doesn't mean yes.

[226] # Induction: gathering and softening attention

[227] Inductions serve to gather your awareness up into a gentler, trancier, more deceptive form so you can access the inner knowing that's too quiet for our ordinary awareness to hear. Call me a heretic, but I don't think inductions should be about surrendering to the hypnotist while playing asleep.

[228] Sometimes it's nice to do an actual induction if you're sober but distracted or analytical or otherwise not very trancey. If, on the other hand, you're already pretty trancey for whatever reason, skip straight to "Already trancey? Stabilize before dipping deeper."

[229] There are lots of different ways to do the induction. For this method, I'm going to be counting, but you could do all sorts of things. If you want to just talk, or tell a story, or move, or make sounds, or be curious, or whatever, by all means, just do that if it works. Everyone's unique and each person might have a kind that works best for them which they'll discover as they go. The nice thing about the counting method is it's very explicitly an induction, whereas with more implicit methods you have to make sure to explain their function clearly.

[230] You can test to see if you're at a deep enough level of hypnosis by choosing a simple target suggestion and checking if the response is the automatic subconscious kind or the analytical questioning kind.

[231] ## Overly Confusing Isn't Automatically Good

[232] "Keep it simple, stupid!" (KISS principle) actually can apply to hypnosis!

[233] It's really easy to take a whole bunch of techniques from all of this and make something so complicated that you "hypnotize" yourself to the point where you don't even know if it worked or not. But keep in mind that especially with self-hypnosis, we can't go so deep that we are unable to continue the session! And even if it were a hypnotist bringing you under, you still need to be at an "access level" of awareness in which you can respond to questions.

[234] So start off with setting one anchor/image to rest your mind's eye on. Decide on a hypnotic tone (warm, indirect, direct, neutral witnessing, etc.) for the session. If you're going to use metaphors, just start with one, and if you switch metaphors midway, understand the purpose of the switch. Then plan a simple stepwise walk out of the subconscious at session's end.

[235] In fact, you don't even need to use metaphors nor imagery. You can use whatever form of inner signal that you notice. It may be a direct knowing, or an emotion, some kind of rhythmic movement or sound, or other kind of spatial perception technique like body scanning or flipping between awareness of different body parts.

[236] Keep the language tied to the subconscious world you've chosen, so if you're counting, and there are already raindrops falling, you can count the raindrops, not the sheep which aren't in that scene. When you're done, come back out the opposite way from how you went in (we often visualize a staircase).

[237] It's fine to borrow qualities such as warmth or steadiness from someone who models them, if you need to, but don't change who you are. We don't want you showing up at the Emmy's like Jim Carrey saying he's Nobody. 😅 Not yet anyways.

[238] When you're writing the script, decide what the central verb you're using is to anchor it (like "letting go"). Give it an overall sensory quality, like unified or disjoint, or something fun like "fluffy wuffy."

[239] You may have a short refrain that you come back to a few times in there as another type of anchor. Think about how you're going to slide from one idea to the next. It's much easier if you read it out loud as you're writing it, since the writing brain is sort of different from the vocal brain. Long, elegant sentences don't read well when you're being induced. You want your script to be soft, but to the point, easy to remember, easy to say, easy to grok.

[240] ## Before you begin

[241] Read through the induction, and the Come all the way back section, when you're awake.

[242] Get clear on what it is you want to do during the induction. What are you leaving alone today? How long do you want to spend on this? (You don't have to do this on a problem. You can do it on something you want to experience more of: love, play, confidence, creativity, communion, whatever.) Make sure you're leaving some time at the end to come back, and reflect on what you experienced.

[243] Put yourself in a place where you can safely do this, which is not: driving a big machine, swimming with the fishies, or otherwise needing to be alert and functionally responsible for yourself or others. If you are intoxicated and more confused than usual, or for whatever reason not able to keep yourself safe, then get some immediate support instead of doing exploratory hypnosis. (The part below called "When you're already trancey" can be used to help orient you if you're already open, but don't use it to make yourself deeper if you're not stable.)

[244] Deciding to do it: how will you coax yourself to do this? Make it spacious? Make it warm and held? Make it firmer, so you stay with a feeling? Something else? Preview what you are going to do: will you close your eyes? Will you come out and go back in again? What will you do at the end? Know that you can say "stop" at any time and the induction will end. If you want to change something, say "less" if you want it to ask less of you, or say "quiet" and the talking will stop. You can also just open your eyes and stop, and then come all the way back anyway. You don't need to ask any parts for permission to stop, even if a part tells you that you do.

[245] If you are new to this or haven't done it for a while, work on the coming in and coming out part first. Don't bring in the hard stuff till later. You don't have to watch yourself all the time, but for the first few times, make sure you can change state. Let yourself make it easier and easier to do this and notice that it takes less and less of you to do it.

[246] Let your response guide you as to what to do next regardless of what the script is expecting. Utilize: use what you have (a thought, sound, movement, or objection) to go to the next place, whatever it is. Learn how to notice what is there. If nothing changes, notice that too. As my dad always said, "Be prepared to take what you get. The best might come afterwards!" For more on language you can use, see Appendix B, or keep reading below for information on using the tools after induction in Let the next response guide you. Either way, keep the instructions about how to stay safe and how to get out plain.
