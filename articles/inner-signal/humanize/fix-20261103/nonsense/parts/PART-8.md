## The part to read

[594] ## Hypnosis-specific audit

[595] Before you let a hypnotic recording or a live hypnotist put manipulative suggestions into your skull, interview them a bit:

[596] First, what are they going to do to bring you under? And if you react negatively to something, will they pause to change the script? Make sure they are fine with stopping the whole thing.

[597] And how are they deciding it's working? Just because you seem "out of it" shouldn't be a green light. Are they using sneaky moves to get past your adult judgement, like putting you into identity roles (including age regression)? Walking you down obedience ladders? Your desire for belonging also shouldn't be used for that.

[598] Keep in mind though, you can only answer those last questions by reading the script first. You can't expect them to know what your adult judgement would accept, so make that clear ahead of time.

[599] Will they tell you before they use techniques that affect your memory or behaviors after the session? Like fractionation or cues.

[600] Are they low-key using your body signals to prove their theories? Like oh your hand dropped real fast, so you ARE gay.

[601] And when it's time to come out, will they tell you clearly that the session is over, so you get your normal waking, walking self back? After it's over, check how well your critical thinking is working when you look back on how it went.

[602] If any answer they give you is wishy washy, beware. Ask for the exact script (or the methods used since they probably aren't exactly reading a script). If they don't want to give the secrets away, walk right out that door.

[603] # Appendix D: Anxiety — body first

[604] image 4

[605] Remember that often the anxiety comes from a childhood guard part of you. It’s what protects you when your inner adult’s Protector isn’t functioning, the same as in childhood. It’s trying to help, making a guess about what’s coming: that you are in danger, or may be shamed, or abandoned, or fail, or be exposed, or be sick, or be rejected, or lose control, or feel something it believes would be unbearable. It can be right! It’s probably been wrong before too, and you know some of those times. Therefore, don’t start by interrogating that part.

[606] Work in this order – first your body, then your inner child, then some inquiry, then some action:

[607] ## Reduced breathing / Buteyko-style breathing

[608] Try breathing less. (There are some good things to be said for slowing your breathing down, if you are breathing too much, or panicked. However, please don’t hold your breath until you are air hungry, which can make you more anxious in the short term.) Breathe in small amounts through your nose and let it out quietly, through your nose too. Only pause if that's comfortable. Can you make your breathing very quiet? If you feel bad, dizzy, or panicky, or have medical issues, stop.

[609] ## EFT tapping

[610] With EFT (Emotional Freedom Techniques, or tapping), you tap gently on your body while you say something like, “Even though I have this anxiety, I am willing to be kind to the part of me that is afraid,” and you stay with what you feel. There isn’t a ton of evidence for EFT, but there are some studies on anxiety. A 2025 systematic review found 7 randomized controlled trials for anxiety disorders, and noted that EFT was more effective than no intervention in several of them, though it isn't one of the first-line therapies for anxiety.

[611] ## Somatic Experiencing

[612] If you would like more information about somatic experiencing, or even a free intro session, please see my somatic guide. In somatic experiencing (SE), you are not digging into the trauma. You learn to track. So you might start by tracking, by discovering where the activation is, where a neutral place is, and then whether you can move your attention from one to the other, very slowly, like a snail.

[613] ## Gendlin Focusing

[614] Focusing is another approach where you work with your body. You might say to yourself, “What is the felt sense of this whole anxiety?” Don’t answer with your head; let your body come up with a vague sense of the whole anxiety. Then ask, “What would this felt sense like me to know?” Try a word or a phrase and see how well it fits the felt sense. But also see if what the word or phrase says is true of the situation. The word may feel ok, but say something that doesn’t fit the situation, or vice versa.

[615] ## Body supports

[616] Sometimes the mind is too activated for meditation to help, so you may need to work directly with the body to calm it down. Maybe get a massage, or massage a foot, your scalp, a temple, or the back of the neck, the top of a shoulder, etc. (Avoid massaging the front of the neck. Also avoid massaging injured or numb body parts, or bruised, inflamed, or recently surgically operated on areas, or varicose veins. Also stop if the massage makes the anxiety symptoms worse, or makes you feel bad in any way, such as painful, dizzy, tingly, etc.) You’ve got lots of other options for the body too, like heat, pressure, a weighted blanket, darkness, your favorite calming music, or a massage gun if you’re careful with it, etc. Use whatever actually helps you, and don’t go surrounding the anxiety with a heap of wellness stuff.

[618] ## Inner child love

[619] Put your hand over your heart and your other hand on your solar plexus (the two-hand practice in Working with your inner child), and say to yourself in a loving way, “Little one, you are not alone. You don’t have to fix this problem. I’m here now to listen to you.” Make sure you're actually talking to your inner child, not just repeating a script. If you can't genuinely say all that and feel it, then just rest your hands there and notice what you find.

[620] ## Anxiety script

[621] A script you could use for anxiety is:

[622] Where are my body parts? What are my feet doing? How is my body positioned in the chair? How is my body breathing? How is my body situated in the room?

[623] If you’re there, little one, I’m here. Thank you, guard, for letting me know you’re here. You didn’t have to scream. I got your message.

[624] If there is something we need to do, let me know what the first thing is that we need to do at this moment. If there is nothing to do, help my body stand down. I’m not going to abandon you, but I’m not going to worship you either. I will listen to what you have to say and reality test it and then take some action.

[625] # Appendix E: Is the daydream helping or replacing your life?

[626] Sometimes daydreaming is something that your subconscious mind is doing to help you. It is simulating an idea or event to help you work through grief or teach you something. The very act of daydreaming may be helping you to access your creativity or allowing your subconscious to show you a potential future. Sometimes your subconscious will show you things that you may be afraid of. Sometimes it will show you what you want.

[627] Other times daydreaming may be taking you away from your life in a detrimental way. It may be that you are escaping reality and are addicted to fantasies rather than real life. You may be dissociating or obsessing about romantic partners or escaping from being an adult.

[628] How do you know which it is? Simply ask yourself if the daydreaming is taking you closer to life, with more clarity, love and ability to take action, or if it is taking you away from life, in a repetitive and craving way.

[629] If it is helping you, then simply ask yourself what the guidance is and how you can use it.

[630] If it is taking you away from life, then change your body position by sitting upright, open your eyes, feel your feet and ask your subconscious to help you.

[631] Then ask yourself what it is that you need to do in real life that you are avoiding by daydreaming.

[632] You may need to send a text message or stop sending text messages. You may need to clean up your room, eat, sleep, deal with grief, create, apologize, leave, tell the truth, make a plan, get help, get back to a task that you were supposed to be doing, stop chasing love that is not available to you etc.

[633] # Appendix F: Self-trust, outside guidance, and oracle-like states

[634] [For using synchronicity as a cue rather than an oracle, see “Using synchronicity as your own wake-up bell” in Appendix B.]

[635] One of the issues here is that people vary dramatically in how suggestible they are, both to their own suggestions and to someone else's, and the same person can be more or less suggestible in different states. Different types of suggestion (commands, imagery, music, metaphors, spiritual, etc) can be very different for different people. So there are definite advantages to both having someone work with you (pacing, containment, another set of eyes), and doing things yourself. Doing it yourself means being able to follow up immediately, to correct something in real time, to have a line of communication with guards, etc.

[636] That said, self hypnosis may be the best way of talking to your subconscious on a day to day basis. If you’re dealing with particularly complicated trauma, blind spots, dissociation, destabilization, or relational wounds that need another nervous system, skilled outside support may be best.

[637] ## Finding a hypnotist who can work with you

[638] Ask them:

[639] What they are trained in (what they can treat)

[640] How they use hypnosis

[641] What they will do if a suggestion doesn't fit you

[642] If they are treating trauma, whether they have specific training in trauma and dissociation (having hypnosis certifications matters, but doesn't mean they are trained in trauma care)

[643] Tell them:

[644] What has/hasn't worked for you in the past

[645] How direct you want them to be

[646] What kind of touch (if any) you are ok with

[647] What kind of imagery (if any) you are ok with

[648] What you want them to do if you want them to stop

[649] Ask them:

[650] What they will do if something unexpected happens during or after the session

[651] What they do if you aren't sure if something is a memory or not

[652] How they will do the full return (coming out of hypnosis) at the end of the session

[653] You should be able to be firmly encouraged, but not forced to continue if you don't want to.

[654] Then afterwards, check in about what was actually done, regardless of what you might call the method. Ask the person why they did what they did, and whether they told you in advance. Did you agree to it? How did the person respond to you? Keep in mind that just because something made you uncomfortable, it doesn't mean it was bad, but if they saw your discomfort as meaning that you needed more of it, that might be something to look into. You might even be able to use this app or ask around to see if someone can tell you what the things the person did meant. They probably won't be able to tell you whether the person was certified in those methods, though. Then you can decide if you want to try learning to do self-hypnosis, use both, or find someone else.

[655] ## When the subconscious speaks like an oracle

[656] Your subconscious is amazing at picking up on things your conscious mind isn't getting. Whether it's anticipations, reading patterns, or just picking up on uncanny things, it's good to respect what your subconscious is picking up on. But just because your subconscious gives you an image or tells you to do something, that doesn't mean you need to treat it like an order.

[657] Especially if it's a big thing, or something that can't be easily undone, slow down. Look at it with your adult's eyes, and take it to the council (next section).

[658] ## The council — internal first, then outward

[659] Ask your inner child, your adult, your conscious mind, your subconscious mind, your intuition, your analytical mind. But if it's bigger than all of those, ask outside of yourself, too.

[660] Ask people who love you, people who are like you, people who have done what you are thinking of doing. Sometimes it is right to go it alone, but I would suggest making it the last step, after you've tried to get counsel.

[661] On the other hand, it is possible to give your power away. Seeking counsel can slide into abdicating to an authority figure without you noticing.

[662] Sometimes you need the support of your peers. In Hearthwork, the concept is that you give each other equal time, warmth, confidentiality, and acceptance of “no” as an answer, and attention without interpretation, advice, or fixing. You lend each other the Nurturer and Protector for a while, but you don’t act as each other’s Guide. Then you reverse roles. Sometimes, with a question asked carefully and at the right time, your peers can help you hear your own Guide more clearly.

[663] ## Forgiveness without bypass

[664] We cannot force forgiveness on ourselves. It has to come from a place of understanding, otherwise it can be bypassing. However, if we remain in the anger forever, we are allowing the injury to dictate how we live our lives in the present. So how do we hold all of this? We just remember that it can all be true at once. They really did hurt us and they still have to answer for it. Our boundary with them is crucial to maintain, especially after forgiveness which can tend to weaken it. One day we may find that we have forgiven them for not knowing better, just like Jesus did on the Cross.

[665] You might say to the part of you that got hurt, “I’m not going to use spiritual mumbo jumbo to shush your cries and make you forgive before you're ready. But I’m also not going to build our whole life around this wound forever.”

[666] ## Forgiving yourself

[667] For self-forgiveness, name what you regret doing, and take responsibility for just that part. Let yourself feel the remorse by taking in the harm done. See how it went against your values. Stop short of making yourself a permanent criminal by identifying with the behaviors you're now abandoning.

[668] Do what you can to put it right. Every so often, come back to how you got to be someone who could do that, until you understand the pattern well enough that it can't drive you anymore.

[669] # Appendix G: Altered states

[670] I’m not saying you should use substances, but many people have used them when they first did self-hypnosis, contacted their inner child, or talked to their Higher Self. I’d learn the inner child work sober first, with enough Nurturer and Protector in the room before you go deep (see the inner child reparenting section). If you don’t have them in you yet, borrow them from someone steady, as long as they won’t take advantage of that to try to run your trip for you.

[671] Do not use altered states to try to force yourself to get to a certain revelation about yourself, but rather to help yourself become more in tune with love and to better your life.

[672] A lot of people have had dramatic openings to love at some point in their life. If you've had a similar experience, you know that there is a difference between having experienced it at one point, or only being able to access it in a certain state, and being able to get to it at any point in your life. While you're building a way to reach love on a normal day, let that experience you had serve as a reference point for what can happen. (If you can't give that love to the child, try to deal with why you can't, instead of trying to ramp up the experience. Often there's a child guard part that’s seen love come with strings or get revoked before in a painful way.)

[673] Use your altered state of consciousness, either while you're in it or after, to help yourself by asking things like:

[674] Help me to become more grounded. Help me to only take what I can integrate. Help me to remember love. Help me to come back when it's time. Help me to figure out how to change one thing in my life to make it better.

[675] Don’t use self-hypnosis to try to keep the altered state of consciousness going indefinitely, to force memories to come back, to make yourself more afraid, to get by without sleep, to put off taking care of medical problems, to become less communicative, etc.

[676] ## Stabilize before you interpret

[677] Self-hypnosis can be powerful when you are in an altered state of consciousness (e.g. when you're on loveyhuasca, meditating, have a lucid dream, are dealing with grief, are dealing with sexual energy, are praying, haven't slept, are around plant medicine, etc, etc, etc). Sometimes you may open yourself up to things faster than you are able to put them into context with your adult self.

[678] So what feels like a meaningful altered state of consciousness may or may not actually be meaningful - it could be your inner child, it could be a childhood guard part, it could be all of the things you're ashamed of / sad about / angry about / etc, coming out to be dealt with. Or it could be that your body is overloaded, your environment is chaotic, you haven't slept enough, etc, etc, etc. Regardless of how scary an image is, regardless of how amazing the lighting was on the "download", regardless of how bad you feel, it doesn't necessarily mean what you think it does.

[679] Before you even think about interpreting what your altered state of consciousness is, ask yourself something simpler:

[680] “What kind of problem is happening?”
