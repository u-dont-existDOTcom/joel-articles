## Context before this part (read it, don't report on it)

[86] Once you have the ability to notice, and to differentiate, you don’t need to keep rehearsing it. Just work on whatever it is you need to work on (often being warm to yourself, protecting yourself and knowing what to do etc). So even though it can be useful to talk about your Higher Self, it doesn’t mean you already have all the good inner parenting functions etc that you need. And I don’t think the Higher Self has any specific wisdom. It’s a vantage point, where more wisdom and compassion can come up, but problems can also look like they were never problems at all from up there, and that can ironically lead instead to more foolish indifference.

[87] When something specific comes up, that’s the time to sort out what it is. “Is this my inner child? Or is this a guard? Is this my body? Is this fear? Is this intuition? Is this my social part? Is this divine guidance, or is this my interpretation of divine guidance?”

## The part to read

[88] # The inner child, the guards, and the inner adult’s three jobs

[89] Your inner child won’t trust you right away. It’s not going to be enough for you to meditate, say three nice sentences to the inner child and expect to have its trust. What’s going to help your inner child to trust you is you, the person you are right now, becoming reliable.

[90] Here I’m going to talk about what you develop in yourself as you reparent your inner child. I’m going to talk about it in terms of three jobs: your loving Nurturer, your safe Adult Protector (hereafter just the Protector) and your wise Guide. It’s still one grown-up doing three jobs (nobody new is moving in to help with the rent), and each job keeps the others in balance. Say a party gets to be too much for your little one. The Protector gets you both out of there, and on the way home the Nurturer tells them they did great, even though mostly they hid in the bathroom. And the Guide helps them think twice about the next weird party they might go to, avoiding another cycle of bathroom vacationing. 🙈

[91] Also, your inner child’s worth is not dependent on these qualities… It has its own worth, and its own qualities, like playing, being tender, not taking life too seriously, being creative and wondering at the world. Those belong in your relationship with it too, not just the wounds.

[92] ## When the adult role is not available yet

[93] The problem is that some people do not have access to their adult functions at will. Instead, they may go blank, access an inherited inner critic, or feel like a child part who may be playing at being an adult (what I call forced adulthood). Telling them to just access their adult is like telling a baby bird to just fly.

[94] For that baby bird, I use a kind of provisional bridge (also found in Schema Therapy) that I call “borrowed adulthood,” which I'll detail here.

[95] First, access your witnessing position if necessary, just noticing what’s there, without needing it to be warm or wise or certain. If you already know how to access different internal positions, move straight to the adult function you need to borrow.

[96] If you have a safe adult in your life, you can borrow from them. If not, borrow from a mentor, an older brother/sister, your imagined future self, your god/goddess or higher power, a value you have, a plan you have written, etc. It’s ok to just borrow what you need right now, warmth or a boundary or direction, just like you would borrow a cup of sugar from a neighbor.

[97] Next, put on your adult hat as if you are an apprentice adult, and pick the words or the action yourself. Then do at least part of it.

[98] Last, return the adult hat, noticing what you borrowed and what you were able to do on your own (even if it’s only 5%).

[99] The adult hat is only borrowed: the person you borrowed it from stays beside you, and you still make the choice. The longer version of this exercise can be found in my Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide.

[100] You don’t have to have trustworthy people in your history. Use someone you remember when that helps, or just imagine a good adult whose intention is to take good care of you. See if you can imagine what they would say/do in your situation, with your history, limitations, options, etc. If you can’t imagine a good adult, or just imagining a caring grown-up makes you feel more threatened, or lonelier, start smaller, with the least cruel response you can believe. Once you’ve found something good, try it out instead of repeating the exercise.

[101] It can be helpful to use borrowing when you’re in the adult position and don’t know what a decent adult would do, especially when your inner child doesn’t trust you and every response you can think of is retaliating, defending your goodness, collapsing or leaving. How would a decent adult hear that distrust? Borrow that one response. You may still feel young when you do it. Feeling like an adult can come after you act like one.

[102] ## Let the child speak while you define the goal

[103] Your inner child wants to be heard, but they don't want to feel like they have to steer the ship while they're in the emotional storm. By providing this protective healing guidance for them, you gain access to all of the positive aspects of what it can be like to be as free as an unburdened child. All the wonder, silliness, approaching things through the senses more, feeling it’s ok to be vulnerable, to want someone to take care of you, to feel need without the stigma of "needy," and to want to offload some decision making, while your adult part stays present. At the same time you get to leave behind the difficulties that came with really being a kid: having to obey, not being able to do much for yourself, getting scolded, and not understanding how things work yet.

[104] "It is okay for a younger part of me to talk. I understand that the adult in me is always going to be here, listening, evaluating, protecting me and stopping me if needed. I'm not handing my adult decisions over to a younger part of me, or to a hypnotist or a recording, or to anyone else."

[105] ## Love doesn’t have to wait for trust

[106] What if your little one doesn't believe you love them? It is possible to love them even if they don't believe you love them. Their trust can question your promises, remember when you failed to protect them, and take its time.

[107] You could say something like, "Okay. I believe that you don't believe me. I still love you. You don't have to love me back. Tell me what feels unsafe." Try not to turn "I still love you" into a line you say on cue, though, because then it can brush the anger off, and what it's really saying is "so stop being angry."

[108] Then respond to the complaints that are sure to come. You don't have to accept every conclusion they reach about you, but do listen for the concrete complaint without fighting the yelling and swearing around it, because even if they are coming from a place that is not completely "right," there will likely be something true in what they are saying. If it's something serious, answer it seriously. Do what you can to show your love for them. This might include some or all of the following: Owning what was not okay. Working on changing yourself so that you are less likely to turn on them. Finding other ways to make up to them for what you did. Finding other ways to show your love for them (this might include taking care of them, such as by buying and cooking food for them, setting limits for them, taking them to the doctor when they need it, and doing what you said you would do). Just not hitting back is enough to start being the adult, even if it isn't enough yet for them to trust you. Even that takes some warmth, and if you don't have any yet, borrow some from someone who's been good to you, or from an adult you imagine.

[109] ## Instead of blame

[110] Be aware of what you are teaching your other positions. For example, are you teaching your little one that you are going to love them no matter what? Or are you teaching them that it is their responsibility to believe your love for them, and that if they don't, it is their fault? Is it two separate positions, one making the vow to love your little one no matter what, and the other blaming your little one for not taking in your love? Or is it one position doing both of these things? They may be younger than you are now. Both talk in your grown-up voice, so to your little one they're the same. Be careful not to take the blaming to mean your love really is conditional, when it could just be another part talking.

[111] When you're placing your younger self on trial, instead of asking which age of you deserves the blame, ask: What was that age of you trying to get, or get away from? What did it understand back then? What options could it actually see? Could it stand to take any of them? What happened because of the choice it made? What was missing? Some ability, or help/protection from someone else? What can you learn or repair now, and how would you act differently in such situations? Even if what it did was harmful to you and others, punishing yourself now is not going to give it the capacity it needed at the time.

[112] # Working with your inner child

[113] ## The two-hand practice

[114] There is a simple and easy body technique to connect with your inner child. Just place one hand in the center of your chest, which physically connects you to your love, and then place your other hand just below that, on your solar plexus, where your inner child can be.

[115] Imagine a divine Love from above, with the inner child below, in the solar plexus area. The inner adult is present. You can also use the hand on the solar plexus to massage that area.

[116] Gently breathe in lots of love and begin to send that love from your heart down to your inner child as you breathe out, noticing what comes up for you. If some comes back up, let it, and send it on down again, so it goes around like a loop. That part's a gift from them, and they don't owe it to you, so if nothing comes back, just keep sending yours. If you feel resistance, just meet it with love. If you feel or see something, just meet that with love as well. If you can't find any warmth today, just leave your hands where they are and notice what's there. Don't pretend to feel something you don't. Let it go back and forth between your heart and your inner child, like a real conversation.

[117] This practice works as an induction too: the Sober induction below has it as one of the anchors you can pick.

[118] ## The Nurturer

[119] Some of the work you can do as the Nurturer is to enjoy your inner child. You don't have to try to make their feelings go away. Just sit back and enjoy their company. Say things to your inner child like:

[120] It's okay to feel the way you're feeling.

[121] I want you to know that I'm here for you.

[122] You don't need to do anything right now.

[123] It's okay. You're not too much. You can be small here.

[124] You can be angry here.

[125] You can be sad here.

[126] ## The Protector

[127] As the Protector, set boundaries, so your inner child can believe the warm stuff. They can be simple like, "I'm going to leave this conversation because it's not safe" or "I'm going to stop texting the person who destabilizes me." It can be self-care boundaries like, "I'm going to eat" or "I'm going to get some sleep." It can be trust-related boundaries like, "I'm going to keep the promise to myself" or "I'm not going to leave you to do adult things by yourself." You can protect your inner child from yourself too, like by repairing after an internal attack: calling yourself an idiot for losing your keys again, for one. Your inner child was listening, so you go back and apologize to them.

[128] A lot of times, inner child work doesn't work because people are doing all the fluffy stuff, but then they go and text the person who is destabilizing them, or they don't eat, or they don't do whatever it is they're supposed to be doing.

[129] If it's hard to bring up the warm stuff, you can always do something to protect your inner child. Make sure it's small enough that you can actually do it. If you're not able to, figure out what's getting in the way of you doing it. Are you scared? Tired? Do you not know how to? Is there a practical thing that's getting in your way? Have you set yourself up to do it in a way that's likely to be successful? It still counts if doing it doesn't make you feel better right away, and checking it off a list doesn't mean it helped.

[130] ## The Guide

[131] You can also work with your Guide. Call them whatever you want: Leader, Guru, Wise One, Sage, Inner Teacher, Director, Higher Adult, whatever.

[132] Ask them for help with directing your life. The Guide holds standards, the way a good parent would, and asks hard things of your inner child so it can grow, at its own pace. Your parent’s harsh tone doesn’t come with the job, though, so be careful not to ask for the “tough it out, you don’t have needs, you just need discipline!” kind of direction. Without the Guide, you can also slide from looking after your inner child into spoiling it: sweets and screens can pass for loving yourself, and so can getting it out of anything hard. Ask them for help in directing you to do adult things in a way that takes your needs into account: asking for the help you need, expressing your preferences, letting a safe person see you in an imperfect way, finding ways to get your need for closeness met without punishing someone for their need for space, etc. etc.

[133] Affirmations might be helpful here, too: “We’re not just here to feel good. We’re here to become whole. We’re here to belong to life, not only to people who approve of us. We’re here to serve truth/beauty/love/God/source/nature/the universe/dharma/the Tao/the benefit of all beings,” etc. etc.

[134] Start with whatever adult function you can connect to, but most of us need enough Nurturer and Protector first, before the Guide. Otherwise the Guide just overrules your inner child in the name of something higher (spiritual bypassing, dressed up as direction). Even if you start with protection and can’t do the warmth stuff yet, make sure the direction you’re being led in is appropriate for your inner child’s growth (i.e. not depriving them of things they need, like play or support, etc.)

[135] ## Working with your guards

[136] Use parts language here too, if it helps. Often, we see a sensation or a distraction or an objection as something that needs to be explored, when in fact it might just be hunger, or reasonable skepticism about something, or a sentence that doesn’t fit. Still, notice it, and then look at what might be going on.

[137] If you're not careful, you might find yourself expecting that when you go within to meet your inner child, you'll find your inner child.

[138] Instead, you might feel numb, cynical, angry, suddenly hungry, like you need to look at your phone, sexually aroused, like you need to make plans, sleepy, dissociated, or you might even think that what you're doing is stupid. Those are not failures. Often what you're experiencing is a very charming guard. It's what protects you when your consciously installed parental Protector isn't functioning, the same as when you were a child. It might not be a guard at all, though. Maybe it's your inner child, mad that you took so long to come back. Maybe it's your inherited parent script, or something from earlier today, or a bit of each.

[139] You can tell that part something like, “I see you. I'm not going to force my way past you. What's going on?” Or however you'd actually say that. Then wait and listen patiently. If nothing comes, that's fine for now. No need to answer for that part just because you'd set this up as a healing session. Later on, you can ask it, “What are you afraid would happen if you stopped protecting me by…?”

[140] If necessary, take time and listen. (Perhaps they are afraid that you will lie to yourself again or pretend again or that it will be too painful or you will spiritually bypass again or you will forgive someone who shouldn’t be forgiven yet or you will see what you want to see or that you will leave them hanging after the session or…)

[141] Now talk to the guard. Tell them that it makes sense that they want to protect you and that they don’t have to stop protecting you, but that you want to know what could make them trust you 1% more that you can protect yourself. If it says no, I’d listen, and if it says no to going deeper in this work or to an altered state, I’d stop there and not push past that no, even when it sounds like a prediction.

[142] Most of the time, the guard wants you to do certain things: set boundaries, do whatever you are supposed to do, eat, not text someone, keep a promise, go to sleep, talk to the therapist, admit that you owe someone money, write something down, etc.

[143] ## Your inherited parent script

[144] One important thing to become aware of when approaching your inner child is your inherited parent script. This is the set of rules and ways of treating yourself that you internalized as a child, mostly based on how your parent(s) treated you, and it doesn’t always sound like a voice. This is not the same as your inner adult (Nurturer, Protector, Guide) that you build up on purpose. Often this script acts as a critic or warden, telling your inner child not to cry or complain. You might be hearing, "Don't be needy! Don't embarrass us! And don't be independent." So when you begin talking to your inner child in a new, loving way, that dusty old script may huff and puff that what you’re doing is “fake”.

[145] ## Protection that knows when to stop scanning

[146] Do you want to embody your Protector? Then you could practice this:

[147] orient yourself (look around, feel the support around you, etc.)

[148] assess the situation (know what you know, what you don’t know and check out what you need to)

[149] differentiate between actual/real danger, unknown, past or imagined dangers

[150] set boundaries (start with one and keep it in proportion to the danger; move, stop, delay, ask, leave, get support, etc.)

[151] verify if it is safe enough (you don't need perfect certainty, just enough real-world evidence to take the next step)

[152] stand down and rest (stop scanning once staying on alert isn't helping anymore)

[153] ## Inner child reparenting

[154] I see a lot of connection between reparenting therapy and self hypnosis. I’d learn to do inner-child therapy sober first, in your ordinary mindstate, since that’s the one you’ll be living in, and get enough Nurturer and Protector in the room before you go deep. When you go into hypnosis, your inner child might come to you. It might show up in any form. It can be an image, a posture, a voice, a memory, an emotion, etc. It can be any age. It can be any vulnerable part of you. If there is no child there, just work with a guard or whatever you find.

[155] ## Don’t rush the repair

[156] When your inner child does show up, spend some quality time with them before taking off the kid gloves, so to speak, and jumping into the healing work.

[157] They might like to hear something like, “Baby boopaloopa, I’m here with you now. You don’t have to work to explain anything or impress me with how fast you can get better. Can I help you feel less alone?”

[158] What does your inner child need? It can be anything: warmth, to play, to be protected, to mourn, to be angry, food, rest, boundaries, better relationships, to stop fantasizing, to stop working their butt off, that you keep a promise to them today, etc. etc.

[159] When a younger you, a guard, a feeling or a signal comes up in your body, take it slowly:

[160] A. Prepare yourself to tend to them with some privacy, but still able to access support if needed. Check how much grown-up you is really in the room first. Can you take a moment before you react? When you decide something, do you follow through? If you answered "No," because right now you’re mostly still stuck identifying as the frightened little boo, hold off on the deeper conversation. Do what makes you feel adult before you try to talk to little you.

[161] B. Look at what's there before judging or deciding what it is.

[162] C. Ask them what you can do or if you can move closer to them. Even if you feel like you already know what they need, ask if they'd prefer warmth, space, verbal reassurance, silence, distance, whatever.

[163] D. Give them what you can: pick a need and do the smallest thing that would help.

[164] E. Give yourself some time to let what you did sink in- it may take sleep, etc.

[165] F. Reconnect with them if they're willing- don't expect them to merge, go away, forgive you, become happy, etc.

[166] G. Stop and rest before the caring turns into another performance, and reconnect with them later to see what was helpful.

[167] Remember the goal isn't to make yourself good as new but to make yourself good as you- more supported and connected to yourself. If you don't like the idea of part work or it makes you feel more divided, you can work with a signal or a feeling instead of a part, or with an imaginary younger you that you feel embodies the issue you're having.

## Context after this part (read it, don't report on it)

[168] ## Play and rest still need an adult present

[169] When you're reparenting, it's okay to play with things that interest your younger states, whether that means touching things of different textures, drawing, exercising, roleplaying with a plushie in a different voice, etc. As long as your reality testing is good and you remember that you aren't a child or pet or doll or whatever and that nobody owns you, it's okay to work on getting your play ability back. A lot of the best discoveries come when you're both just messing around. Play not only paves the way for therapy, but is a part of how successful therapy should be measured.
