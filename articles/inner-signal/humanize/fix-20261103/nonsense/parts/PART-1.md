[17] # Quick start: do this first

[18] To work on difficulties or cultivate positive qualities (love, curiosity, play, confidence, etc.), simply do these steps, in order: 1) Gather attention; 2) Notice what is here; 3) Offer a fitting response; 4) Wait; 5) Come all the way back. Not much needs to happen in each session!

[19] Prior to your first session, read through the induction and the full return to waking life, so you’re more familiar with them. Then put the screen away. You’re learning to run the practice yourself, not reading from a teleprompter like a news anchor person.

[20] Follow whatever shows up, even if it’s an objection or a long silence, and let it shape what you invite next. Vary the invites as needed to be responsive & warm, to create a boundary, to give direction or more time, etc. Complete the return to normal waking consciousness. After, note what happened & any real-world actions it calls for.

[21] If at any point you feel panicky, disoriented, more unreal, or less functional, this is not the time for bravely sticking with difficult feelings (that's another choice, made freely & while staying present). Stop deepening, return to the room & get appropriate support.

[26] # What I mean by hypnosis

[27] APA Division 30 commonly defines hypnosis as focused attention, reduced peripheral awareness, and increased responsiveness to suggestion. That’s basically true, but there’s a lot more than meets the eye. In fact, hypnosis is such a broad and common type of altered state that it’s hard to confine it to one use. But I’m using hypnosis here as a therapy.

[28] And hypnosis as a therapy is as old as time itself. While the modern technique of it was discovered by Franz Anton Mesmer in the 1770s, shamans have been using dancing, music, and drugs to induce hypnosis for therapeutic purposes forever. We enter into trance states every night which resemble hypnosis as we go to sleep and as we wake up as well. Those states can also be used for deep therapy.

[43] # Affirmations Done Right

[44] My dad always said: “Always, and in everything, expect the very best. But be prepared to take what you get. The best might come afterwards.” That’s a better affirmation than what most people think of as affirmation, because it’s not telling you something which you know to be false in the present. If you say “I am beautiful” but you know that’s not true, then you’re challenging your beliefs, and if you’re challenging your beliefs you should have some argument or reason why they are wrong, otherwise it’s bullying.

[45] Another quote I use all the time from my dad… when someone asks me “How’s it going?” I say “Better & better!” This really confuses a lot of people. Some think it’s amazing that I’m always doing better. Some ask if I was sick or something. But it’s doing multiple jobs. On the one hand, it’s deflecting from the fake question because most people don’t actually want to hear how you are doing in detail anyway. On the other hand, more importantly, it’s reprogramming your mind to remove you from tunnel vision. You might be sad now, but if you say, “I am sad” then you’re subtly identifying with the sadness and it keeps you stuck. It’s best to say, “I’m feeling sad now, but I’m doing better and better, every day, in every way!” That’s more like a mission statement than a historical analysis. Although you can also re-evaluate your history as lessons to make that true.

[50] # Hypnosis Evidence

[51] Is there real (not magical) evidence for hypnosis? Yes. In 2024, there was an overview of 49 meta-analyses containing a total of 261 distinct primary studies. Evidence was strongest for pain, medical procedures, and work with children and adolescents, but varied with different outcomes and study design quality.

[52] Is there evidence for self-hypnosis? Yes. There has been a systematic review of Randomized Controlled Trials of self-hypnosis, finding benefit in studies of pain, childbirth, children, stress, anxiety etc. Self-hypnosis seemed more useful when people learned it as a real skill and practiced it several times than when they just listened to an audio file.

[55] The Mayo Clinic warns that using hypnosis to deal with stressful events from earlier in life may bring up strong emotions and that there are other types of treatments that may be more helpful with historical trauma.

[73] # A useful map of the mind

[74] Note: whenever I refer to the subconscious, I’m referring to all of the processes under the surface of your conscious awareness: perceptions, emotions, desires, avoidance tendencies, imagery, memories, body signals, habits, intuitions, automatic behaviors and so forth. You can work with all of these together as if it’s all one mind, or you can work with it as a set of parts, an ecology if you like. There isn’t a right or wrong way to approach it. They’re both maps. Don’t form a priesthood around either one!

[75] Working with parts gives you precision, and working with the Higher Self gives you coherence. Communion is going back and forth between the two.

[76] If you need to work with the whole, the subconscious as one:

[77] a) Invoke the subconscious in some way. Something simple like ‘Hello, wise subconscious, please help me understand…’ or whatever works for you.

[78] If you need to work with the ecology in the subconscious, notice what each part wants: the child parts usually want love, the guards want you safe, the critic wants you to meet some standard, the fantasy making parts are after possibility, body memories want to be recognized, the social part wants to belong, the spiritual part wants God or source or what’s best for all beings, and the wiser part just wants what’s true. Then:

[79] a) It’s usually a good idea to start with unity. So invoke your Higher Self: “Hello, my Higher Self/universal heart-mind/wise subconscious, please guide me…”

[80] b) Move into differentiation. “Which part is afraid? Which part wants to protect you? Which part wants to avoid the whole thing? Which part knows what to do? Which part is too young for this?” Etc.

[81] c) Move back into unity again. “And all of these parts belong to the one life, and as the adult in charge of this life, you can lead with love…” Etc.

[82] ## Sometimes start with the whole

[83] Some people make themselves too fragmented by starting to work with parts. ‘Here’s a part! Here’s another one! I’ll get twelve of them in a room and give them all an office to hang around in!’ If that happens to you, it’s better to start from the sacred whole.

[84] Start like this: ‘I’m approaching this from the universal heart. I’m approaching this from my Higher Self. I’m approaching this from the part of me that knows it belongs to life before it belongs to any wound, or role, or family, or group, or identity, or ideology, or story. So please help me see what’s true and kind and timely and stabilizing and useful here.’ Etc.

[85] It can be that you can’t even access your Higher Self, or the universal heart, or whatever you call it. (By the way, if it doesn’t work for you, don’t use it!) In that case, start by just witnessing whatever is coming up. Notice the room, your body, the fact that you’re in a younger state etc. Being able to notice is enough adult presence for the first step.

[86] Once you have the ability to notice, and to differentiate, you don’t need to keep rehearsing it. Just work on whatever it is you need to work on (often being warm to yourself, protecting yourself and knowing what to do etc). So even though it can be useful to talk about your Higher Self, it doesn’t mean you already have all the good inner parenting functions etc that you need. And I don’t think the Higher Self has any specific wisdom. It’s a vantage point, where more wisdom and compassion can come up, but problems can also look like they were never problems at all from up there, and that can ironically lead instead to more foolish indifference.

[87] When something specific comes up, that’s the time to sort out what it is. “Is this my inner child? Or is this a guard? Is this my body? Is this fear? Is this intuition? Is this my social part? Is this divine guidance, or is this my interpretation of divine guidance?”
