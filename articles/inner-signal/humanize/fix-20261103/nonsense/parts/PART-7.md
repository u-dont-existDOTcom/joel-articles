## The part to read

[485] ## The Milton Model, minus the covert sales seminar

[486] We are going to use a model that’s called the Milton model. It’s all to do with sentence architecture. When we tell ourselves to ‘be better’, ‘relax now’, ‘stop feeling anxious’ or ‘trust me’, it doesn’t work, does it?

[487] What we need to do is use indirect language to go around the part that wants to argue with us, so that we can actually get to where we want to get to.

[488] So let’s start with something we know to be true, let’s take the pressure off, presume that we can only do a tiny bit and let’s invite one thing:

[489] ‘As my body begins to notice the sensation of the bed underneath it, it may be possible for it to begin to realize that it is not alone in carrying the burden of the day.’

[490] We can then test this out with the body to see if it was possible.

[491] ### Pattern 1: Pacing and leading — truth before suggestion

[492] So how might we pace and lead in a way that works with anxiety?

[493] It might go something like this: ‘It is completely true that the sensation of ________ (or the emotion of _____) is coming up for me. It makes complete sense that my system is trying to react and respond to that by wanting to do or think ________. While that is completely true, there is another thing that is equally true, which is that my adult self is here, breathing in the room right now, and wanting to listen to what comes up for me next.’

[494] Truth, Invite, Truth, Invite etc. is all you need to do.

[495] ### Pattern 2: Awareness verbs

[496] Open up awareness by using awareness verbs e.g. notice, realize, recognize, sense, feel, wonder, imagine, picture, listen to, allow, discover, remember, consider etc.

[497] Example: ‘Notice how the answer comes to you… maybe as an image, or a word… maybe as a pressure, or some warmth… or a silence, or a sense of what to do next.’

[498] ### Pattern 3: Presuppositions

[499] A presupposition hides an assumption inside a sentence, and whoever hears it usually just goes along with it. That’s a lot of power, so only use it to assume good things. It should give you more agency, not less. It should make you more safe (not just feeling more safe when you're not). It should help you feel the love that's there (even if it's only available beyond the conditions). We don't use this to assume obedience, the way a less ethical hypnotist does when they say something like, ‘As you go deeper, it gets easier and easier to just do what I say.’

[500] I'd switch that up to the safe version: ‘As you go deeper, it gets easier to commune with any part that needs your understanding before it can change.’

[501] ### Pattern 4: Gentle cause-effect bridges

[502] Bridge words, like ‘as’, ‘whilst’, ‘because’, ‘when’, ‘after’, ‘before’ and ‘which means’, link one event to the next, so the first can seem to come with the second, or even cause it. That doesn't mean it does, of course. This is another kind of more subtle presupposition technique.

[503] We can use this technique to support communion and agency: “As I breathe, my adult self can allow itself to become present enough to hear a bit of what my wounded inner child needs.”

[504] ### Pattern 5: Embedded invitations

[505] An embedded invitation tucks a small direction inside a bigger sentence, like when you put a pill inside a little food to swallow it easier. In communion hypnosis, we choose not to use embeds like this to hide commands, but rather to soften them into nudges. Nudges give more agency, and they keep the conversation flowing without a need for a strong decision. For example, rather than, "As I sit here, I will become aware..." we can say, "As I sit here, I may begin to allow myself to become aware..."

[506] With your inner child, it could be: "My little one doesn't have to trust me all at once. I invite them to just notice that right now, I'm still here."

[507] Invitational language can also feel super fake though, especially when it's overused. Fakeness doesn't feel so safe. And if it's just chunking the manipulation into easier bite-sized portions without reducing the overall size of the demand, it could be less ethical, not more.

[508] ### Pattern 6: Ethical double binds

[509] This is also an actual parenting technique. Children love choices, even when they're both designed for the same pre-decided outcome. Our subconscious still works like that, too. A manipulative double bind is the name for that, although hypnotists don't call it manipulative, that's my framing. Nimja demonstrates this a lot in his hypnosis: "Do you want to go deeper now, or in a moment?"

[510] An ethical double bind also gives you two choices, but both of them are designed to be genuinely safe, and they also give a bit of actual agency, even if both choices serve the same end. Example:

[511] "Welcome to McHappyness. Would you like some warmth first, or some space?" And would you like fries with that?

[512] ### Pattern 7: Conversational postulates

[513] A conversational postulate is a sneaky little yes or no question that's actually a way to ask for something indirectly. People use it all the time casually, like, "Can you pass the salt?" In its most egregious form, it could turn into a dare. Do you recall how hard it was as a child to ignore a dare?

[514] I dare you now to practice this ethical postulate! "Can my subconscious show me the next healing stepping stone, sparing me any more painful memories than I can handle right now?"

[515] ### Pattern 8: Tag questions

[516] Tag questions, like "isn't it?" or "doesn't it?", can soften a sentence, but they get old very quickly, so go easy on them.

[517] "Your body knows how to relax, doesn't it?"

[518] ### Pattern 9: Nominalizations

[519] A nominalization turns something you do into a noun: you heal, and it becomes 'healing'. Others are 'safety', 'trust', 'integration', 'communion', 'clarity' and 'rest'. They're a bit vague, and sometimes that's useful, because it lets the listener's subconscious fill in something very specific for them.

[520] "A bit more safety can come to me in whatever way my body understands."

[521] ### Pattern 10: Meta Model questions — the de-hypnosis tool

[522] Milton Model language opens things up, and Meta Model questions ask what exactly someone means.

[523] You need both to be ethical with people, so when something sounds true but vague (like the new-age slogans: e.g. "everyone is your mirror"), ask: "What do you mean, specifically, my mirror to what extent? According to whom, Madame Blavatsky, AI, or...? How do you know that, and what evidence would change your mind?"

[524] So when someone is starting to tense up in their body, use the Milton Model to help them soften. And when you feel you're losing track of what's really going on, that's the time for the Meta Model.

[525] ## Sensory, state, and strategy tools

[526] ### Submodalities — editing the form, not denying the truth

[527] If your fear, craving, fantasy, belief, memory, whatever, has sensory properties in your experience (it has a place in your visual field, or sounds a certain way, or is heavy, or moving, or inside / outside your body, or something like that), you can work with those properties. If there are no sensory properties that you can notice, don't try to create any -- just don't use this tool.

[528] How is this represented for you? Can you allow yourself to move that image back a couple of feet? Can you soften the edges of that image? Can you turn the volume down on that voice in your head? Etc. What changes?

[529] "And I can allow myself to know that I am not deleting the message, just turning down the volume so that I am able to be present enough to hear what it is."

[530] And, always ask the guard if it's ok to do this.

[531] "Is it ok to move that image back a couple of feet and turn the volume down a bit, so that I can be present enough to hear what you need me to hear?"

[532] ### Anchoring — giving the body a doorway back

[533] There are many cues you can connect to a state. Put your hand on your heart, touch two fingers together, use a breath, a word, a particular body posture, smell, song, whatever. Feel a state you have really been in, one you want to be able to get back to easily when you need it. Do your anchor thing and say to yourself that this is available to you. To get yourself back into a good hypnosis state easily, don’t go for a peak emotional state but for a usable, receptive one. See "A shortcut back into hypnosis" for a quick way back in. Do make sure this is different from both your alert and calm cue and your synchronicity wake up cue.

[534] ### Using synchronicity as your own wake-up bell

[535] I call this the synchronicity wake up cue.

[536] People often ask what the synchronicity means. Sometimes they get a great insight from it, but other times they get obsessed trying to work out patterns etc. I am using the noticing of the synchronicity to wake up. It doesn't have to mean anything.

[537] Whenever I notice it, I use it as a cue for waking up inside whatever state I am in, feeling my body, feeling the room I'm in, remembering that what I'm noticing doesn't confirm what I'm reading into the coincidences, widening my state so that I can see beyond what my current state is showing me. I then look for the truth of what is going on (What is really going on, what is just my interpretation? What needs warmth? What needs protecting? What do I need to do?) and I go into the highest state I can get into, where I can see what is going on in that situation as objectively and clearly as I can, while remaining in my body, not leaving the facts of the situation and not leaving my regular human state. From that state I feel my feelings. If the state I started in is negative, I check if it is a message from my inner child or from a childhood guard part, or if I just need to grieve. I check if it is a message from my physical body (if there is a problem with my body that I need to take care of) or if I need to do something about the situation. I choose at least one thing to do that is from a loving place. I allow myself to be in whatever state is real for me, which may include objectivity, clarity, ease, peace, freedom, joy, gratitude, etc.

[538] #### Installing the synchronicity cue

[539] Mentally rehearse the following: Imagine that you have noticed a synchronicity that is obviously harmless.

[540] Feel the "whoa" of stimulation that you just got from noticing it.

[541] Use that stimulation to feel your feet, your body, your breathing, your room.

[542] Reiterate to yourself that whenever you notice a synchronicity, it is just a bell that rings to wake you up. It doesn’t confirm what you are reading into it.

[543] Ask yourself: "What does my Nurturer think about this?", "What does my Protector think about this?", "What does my Guide think about this?"

[544] Rehearse going back to a more grounded state and to a wider view of the situation.

[545] Rehearse yourself taking at least one action. Rehearse yourself waking up completely.

[546] These cues do not authorize you to make a decision. They do not stop you from hearing the message of your intuition if it is warning you about something.

[547] This is very similar to implementation intention (an if...then... plan). I'm not aware of research into implementation intention for synchronicity cues, but the general mechanisms appear to work; Gollwitzer’s paper explains how and why they work.

[548] #### False positives, and turning the cue off

[549] A downside of synchronicity cues is that they can lead to false positives. We create an association in our head on purpose when we rehearse what to do when we notice a synchronicity; however, when you are absorbed in something and/or when you are destabilized, you might think that you saw a synchronicity when in fact there wasn't any. Research has linked apophenia, the tendency to see patterns that aren't really there, with psychosis spectrum traits (see Blain and colleagues, and Grant and colleagues). That isn't saying that noticing patterns is bad.

[550] If you feel that synchronicity cues are making these things worse (e.g. you feel like you have to look for them all the time, you can't sleep, you feel anxious, you are sure that random things happen in your life in order to tell you something, you can't tell the difference between noticing something and interpreting what it might mean, etc.), you should deactivate synchronicity cues.

[551] You can do that by not rehearsing them anymore and by saying to yourself (when you are fully awake) that you don't want to use synchronicity as a cue anymore (e.g. "From now on, I am no longer going to use noticing synchronicity as a cue for waking up. I let go of this association, coincidences can go back to being just coincidences."). Saying this is just you deciding, it doesn't mean the association is gone straight away.

[552] ### Reframing — changing the meaning without lying

[553] Reframes are dishonest when they declare the problem was something that was good all along ("It's good nobody loves me, because I'm happier alone"). Instead, ask yourself: "What else might be true?" or "What is this part trying to do for me?" or "How might my Nurturer reframe this?" or "How might my Protector reframe this?" or "How might my Guide reframe this?" etc., etc. So we might arrive at, "If it seems like nobody loves me, could it be a part of a vicious cycle in which I expect that, and my expectations are met?"

[554] ### Perceptual positions — self, other, witness, universal heart

[555] I go through four positions: 1) seeing from Self (what do I feel from inside myself?), 2) then seeing from Other (if I sit inside the other person, what might they be feeling or protecting?), 3) then seeing from Witness (if I watched this from across the room, what would I notice?), and 4) then seeing from Universal Heart (what serves truth, love, boundaries, and the good of everyone involved?).

[556] I will not use this to rationalize bad behavior, but only to break out of a prison of perception.

[557] ### Future pacing — let the future test the insight

[558] Once I have seen the situation more clearly, I will imagine future scenarios where my old behavior would have been triggered, to test out if my new insight is working.

[559] If these scenarios come up, I will choose new small behaviors using an Implementation Intention

[560] ### The Swish, softened

[561] I use an NLP technique called a "Swish Pattern" (it swaps a picture of an old reaction for a picture of a new one), but I will allow my old pattern to be there, while also holding my new pattern in my awareness. I feel what comes up somatically, and compare the differences between the future scenarios of my old and new patterns.

[562] ### Parts integration, without pretending the conflict is solved

[563] I use parts integration to ask my conflicting parts what they want, what they are afraid of, what their need is, and see if I can find a 3rd option that satisfies both parts' needs, without either part dominating my behavior.

[564] ## What I will not use NLP for

[565] Here is a list of things I don't do with NLP, whether I'm hypnotizing myself or someone else:

[566] I don't use NLP to convince anyone an intuition is true. For example, I don't use NLP or hypnosis in general, to do more than initial memory retrieval work. Memories retrieved still need external validation, especially when they were led to (which NLP tends to).

[567] On the other hand, I also don't use NLP to talk anyone out of a real signal. That doesn't mean we can't try to calm unbearable pain, but we must keep access to the awareness of the signal so that we can do the real root cause healing.

[568] For example, I won’t tell anyone to push past their fear before asking what it’s protecting, and I won’t make anyone feel rushed when they want to take time to process the feelings.

[569] I don't use NLP on people without their consent nor to bypass consent via covert commands. Nearly all hypnotists claim that hypnosis cannot violate consent, which is an absurdly ignorant and unethical claim.

[570] That includes not manipulating anyone to feel like their "no" is the ego's resistance we need to overcome, which is another way of saying they are spiritually inferior to me if they disagree with me.

[571] If someone is in an unstable state in which they can't reasonably give consent, I don't use NLP to pretend they did.

[572] ## Running the whole NLP sequence

[573] Here is a rough outline of the entire NLP process:

[574] Induce yourself (meaning gather your attention into a soft focus) or contain yourself (meaning stabilize yourself) first if you are already too opened up to difficult memories/experiences.

[575] Ask yourself for permission: is it useful to work with this pattern today?

[576] Get a representation of your pattern. Do you see it as an image, with size, color and location, or do you feel it like a movement or some other kind of sensation?

[577] Find out if that part/pattern is protecting something for you.

[578] Gently change an aspect of your pattern's representation from the prior step.

[579] Bring in your three good adults. The Nurturer brings love in. Next, the Protector puts your helmet on, and then the Guide points the way out of the mess. They’re really all one inner adult, but picturing each with their own function can be easier before merging them together (like Captain Planet if you saw that cartoon).

[580] Then anchor the good state you're in with a hand on your heart, two fingers touching, a particular breath or a posture, so that doing the same thing later can bring the state back.

[581] Imagine running into a scenario where your old pattern would have triggered, and adopt a feasible new behavior. ("If I see a red cloth, then I will choose not to charge at it. I am not a bull anymore.")

[582] Check in with your body for signals during the re-imagining process, and what else besides body signals supports your new interpretation of the situation. Ask yourself whether the new behavior you've imagined is kind and sensible.

[583] Then come all the way back out of the altered state (see “Come all the way back”), and go do something normal.

[584] # Appendix C: The extended de-hypnosis checklist

[585] What to do when you feel like you’re being pushed too hard by something (person, ad, ideology, spiritual teaching, romantic attraction, internal voice, etc.): Say to yourself, “Okay, something is trying to influence me here. I don’t have to obey it or attack it. I can just slow myself down and ask some questions like, 'What would this thing influence me to believe that isn’t true in order to get what it wants from me? What is my body telling me to do about this (how does my body feel)? What would love do here? What would truth do here? How would I approach this humbly? What would life have me do here?'”

[586] If what you’re investigating is true, it can take your questioning (as the police like to say). If it’s love, it can accommodate your boundaries. If it’s real guidance, it’ll still be there tomorrow. If it’s really for you, it won’t want you to give up all the wisdom and sovereignty you’ve worked so hard to develop.

[587] Before you do what it wants you to do, check how it’s trying to get you there. Is it rushing you, or telling you to keep things secret? Is it shaming you, or threatening to leave you out if you don’t do what it says? Maybe it makes you feel special, or there’s a sexual charge to it, or a sense that you’re spiritually above everyone else.

[588] Then look at what it wants from you. Is it money, or sex, or your loyalty? Is it asking you to keep quiet, or to obey it?

[589] Keep in mind though, people who mean well can ask for some of these too. A teacher charges for their time, and your partner probably wants your loyalty. If you can say no to them and they’re fine with it, that’s a good sign.

[590] Back to the interrogation:

[591] Is it trying to narrow down your choices? Does questioning it feel like you’re doing something wrong, or does it act like not obeying is a problem you need to fix? Does normal life start to seem boring, stupid or irrelevant when it’s around? Is it trying to seduce, scare or rescue your inner child? And does it try to stop your adult self from saying no?

[592] If it does a bunch of these things, slow yourself down, don’t let it push you deeper into trance, don’t do anything you can’t undo, and then ground yourself for a while (take care of your body, eat, sleep, give it time, get counsel, etc).

[593] A good guide will make your adult self stronger. A bad guide will try to make your no something that’s bad or needs to be fixed (resistance, ego, fear, low vibration, trauma response, sin, need for more of its guidance, etc).
