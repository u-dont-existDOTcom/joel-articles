[569] ## What I will not use NLP for

[570] Here is a list of things I don't do with NLP, whether I'm hypnotizing myself or someone else:

[571] I don't use NLP to convince anyone an intuition is true. For example, I don't use NLP or hypnosis in general, to do more than initial memory retrieval work. Memories retrieved still need external validation, especially when they were led to (which NLP tends to).

[572] On the other hand, I also don't use NLP to talk anyone out of a real signal. That doesn't mean we can't try to calm unbearable pain, but we must keep access to the awareness of the signal so that we can do the real root cause healing.

[573] For example, I won’t tell anyone to push past their fear before asking what it’s protecting, and I won’t make anyone feel rushed when they want to take time to process the feelings.

[574] I don't use NLP on people without their consent nor to bypass consent via covert commands. Nearly all hypnotists claim that hypnosis cannot violate consent, which is an absurdly ignorant and unethical claim.

[575] That includes not manipulating anyone to feel like their "no" is the ego's resistance we need to overcome, which is another way of saying they are spiritually inferior to me if they disagree with me.

[576] If someone is in an unstable state in which they can't reasonably give consent, I don't use NLP to pretend they did.

[577] ## Running the whole NLP sequence

[578] Here is a rough outline of the entire NLP process:

[579] Induce yourself (meaning gather your attention into a soft focus) or contain yourself (meaning stabilize yourself) first if you are already too opened up to difficult memories/experiences.

[580] Ask yourself for permission: is it useful to work with this pattern today?

[581] Get a representation of your pattern. Do you see it as an image, with size, color and location, or do you feel it like a movement or some other kind of sensation?

[582] Find out if that part/pattern is protecting something for you.

[583] Gently change an aspect of your pattern's representation from the prior step.

[584] Bring in your three good adults. The Nurturer brings love in. Next, the Protector puts your helmet on, and then the Guide points the way out of the mess. They’re really all one inner adult, but picturing each with their own function can be easier before merging them together (like Captain Planet if you saw that cartoon).

[585] Then anchor the good state you're in with a hand on your heart, two fingers touching, a particular breath or a posture, so that doing the same thing later can bring the state back.

[586] Imagine running into a scenario where your old pattern would have triggered, and adopt a feasible new behavior. ("If I see a red cloth, then I will choose not to charge at it. I am not a bull anymore.")

[587] Check in with your body for signals during the re-imagining process, and what else besides body signals supports your new interpretation of the situation. Ask yourself whether the new behavior you've imagined is kind and sensible.

[588] Then come all the way back out of the altered state (see “Come all the way back”), and go do something normal.

[589] # Appendix C: The extended de-hypnosis checklist

[590] What to do when you feel like you’re being pushed too hard by something (person, ad, ideology, spiritual teaching, romantic attraction, internal voice, etc.): Say to yourself, “Okay, something is trying to influence me here. I don’t have to obey it or attack it. I can just slow myself down and ask some questions like, 'What would this thing influence me to believe that isn’t true in order to get what it wants from me? What is my body telling me to do about this (how does my body feel)? What would love do here? What would truth do here? How would I approach this humbly? What would life have me do here?'”

[591] If what you’re investigating is true, it can take your questioning (as the police like to say). If it’s love, it can accommodate your boundaries. If it’s real guidance, it’ll still be there tomorrow. If it’s really for you, it won’t want you to give up all the wisdom and sovereignty you’ve worked so hard to develop.

[592] Before you do what it wants you to do, check how it’s trying to get you there. Is it rushing you, or telling you to keep things secret? Is it shaming you, or threatening to leave you out if you don’t do what it says? Maybe it makes you feel special, or there’s a sexual charge to it, or a sense that you’re spiritually above everyone else.

[593] Then look at what it wants from you. Is it money, or sex, or your loyalty? Is it asking you to keep quiet, or to obey it?

[594] Keep in mind though, people who mean well can ask for some of these too. A teacher charges for their time, and your partner probably wants your loyalty. If you can say no to them and they’re fine with it, that’s a good sign.

[595] Back to the interrogation:

[596] Is it trying to narrow down your choices? Does questioning it feel like you’re doing something wrong, or does it act like not obeying is a problem you need to fix? Does normal life start to seem boring, stupid or irrelevant when it’s around? Is it trying to seduce, scare or rescue your inner child? And does it try to stop your adult self from saying no?

[597] If it does a bunch of these things, slow yourself down, don’t let it push you deeper into trance, don’t do anything you can’t undo, and then ground yourself for a while (take care of your body, eat, sleep, give it time, get counsel, etc).

[598] A good guide will make your adult self stronger. A good guide can take no. A bad guide can’t take no. A bad guide wants to take your adult self’s job. A bad guide will try to make your no something that’s bad or needs to be fixed (resistance, ego, fear, low vibration, trauma response, sin, need for more of its guidance, etc).

[599] ## Hypnosis-specific audit

[600] Before you let a hypnotic recording or a live hypnotist put manipulative suggestions into your skull, interview them a bit:

[601] First, what are they going to do to bring you under? And if you react negatively to something, will they pause to change the script? Make sure they are fine with stopping the whole thing.

[602] And how are they deciding it's working? Just because you seem "out of it" shouldn't be a green light. Are they using sneaky moves to get past your adult judgement, like putting you into identity roles (including age regression)? Walking you down obedience ladders? Your desire for belonging also shouldn't be used for that.

[603] Keep in mind though, you can only answer those last questions by reading the script first. You can't expect them to know what your adult judgement would accept, so make that clear ahead of time.

[604] Will they tell you before they use techniques that affect your memory or behaviors after the session? Like fractionation or cues.

[605] Are they low-key using your body signals to prove their theories? Like oh your hand dropped real fast, so you ARE gay.

[606] And when it's time to come out, will they tell you clearly that the session is over, so you get your normal waking, walking self back? After it's over, check how well your critical thinking is working when you look back on how it went.

[607] If any answer they give you is wishy washy, beware. Ask for the exact script (or the methods used since they probably aren't exactly reading a script). If they don't want to give the secrets away, walk right out that door.

[608] # Appendix D: Anxiety — body first

[609] image 4

[610] Remember that often the anxiety comes from a childhood guard part of you. It’s what protects you when your inner adult’s Protector isn’t functioning, the same as in childhood. It’s trying to help, making a guess about what’s coming: that you are in danger, or may be shamed, or abandoned, or fail, or be exposed, or be sick, or be rejected, or lose control, or feel something it believes would be unbearable. It can be right! It’s probably been wrong before too, and you know some of those times. Therefore, don’t start by interrogating that part. Help your body first.

[611] Work in this order – first your body, then your inner child, then some inquiry, then some action:

[612] ## Reduced breathing / Buteyko-style breathing

[613] Try breathing less. (There are some good things to be said for slowing your breathing down, if you are breathing too much, or panicked. However, please don’t hold your breath until you are air hungry, which can make you more anxious in the short term.) Breathe in small amounts through your nose and let it out quietly, through your nose too. Only pause if that's comfortable. Can you make your breathing very quiet? If you feel bad, dizzy, or panicky, or have medical issues, stop.

[614] ## EFT tapping

[615] One of the easiest ways to do EFT (Emotional Freedom Techniques, or tapping) is to say something like, “Even though I have this anxiety, I am willing to be kind to the part of me that is afraid,” while you tap gently on your body and stay with what you feel. There isn’t a ton of evidence for EFT, but there are some studies on anxiety. A 2025 systematic review found 7 randomized controlled trials for anxiety disorders, and noted that EFT was more effective than no intervention in several of them. So treat the evidence with caution, and know that there are first-line therapies for anxiety.

[616] ## Somatic Experiencing

[617] If you would like more information about somatic experiencing, or even a free intro session, please see my somatic guide. In somatic experiencing (SE), you are not digging into the trauma. You learn to track. So you might start by tracking, by discovering where the activation is, where a neutral place is, and then whether you can move your attention from one to the other, very slowly, like a snail.

[618] ## Gendlin Focusing

[619] Focusing is another approach where you work with your body. You might say to yourself, “What is the felt sense of this whole anxiety?” Don’t answer with your head; let your body come up with a vague sense of the whole anxiety. Then ask, “What would this felt sense like me to know?” Try a word or a phrase and see how well it fits the felt sense. But also see if what the word or phrase says is true of the situation. The word may feel ok, but say something that doesn’t fit the situation, or vice versa. Use the words and the body to mutually correct one another.

[620] ## Body supports

[621] Work with the body. Sometimes the mind is too activated for meditation to help, so you may need to work directly with the body to calm it down. Maybe get a massage, or massage a foot, your scalp, a temple, or the back of the neck, the top of a shoulder, etc. (Avoid massaging the front of the neck. Also avoid massaging injured or numb body parts, or bruised, inflamed, or recently surgically operated on areas, or varicose veins. Also stop if the massage makes the anxiety symptoms worse, or makes you feel bad in any way, such as painful, dizzy, tingly, etc.) You’ve got lots of other options for the body too, like heat, pressure, a weighted blanket, darkness, your favorite calming music, or a massage gun if you’re careful with it, etc. Use whatever actually helps you, and don’t go surrounding the anxiety with a heap of wellness stuff.

All you really need is to stabilize yourself, and then listen to the messages from your inner realm, reality test those messages, and then do something grounded about them.

[623] ## Inner child love

[624] Put your hand over your heart and your other hand on your solar plexus (the two-hand practice in Working with your inner child), and say to yourself in a loving way, “Little one, you are not alone. You don’t have to fix this problem. I’m here now to listen to you.” Make sure you're actually talking to your inner child, not just repeating a script. If you can't genuinely say all that and feel it, then just rest your hands there and notice what you find.

[625] ## Anxiety script

[626] A script you could use for anxiety is:

[627] Where are my body parts? What are my feet doing? How is my body positioned in the chair? How is my body breathing? How is my body situated in the room?

[628] If you’re there, little one, I’m here. Thank you, guard, for letting me know you’re here. You didn’t have to scream. I got your message.

[629] If there is something we need to do, let me know what the first thing is that we need to do at this moment. If there is nothing to do, help my body stand down. I’m not going to abandon you, but I’m not going to worship you either. I will listen to what you have to say and reality test it and then take some action.

[630] # Appendix E: Is the daydream helping or replacing your life?

[631] Sometimes daydreaming is something that your subconscious mind is doing to help you. It is simulating an idea or event to help you work through grief or teach you something. The very act of daydreaming may be helping you to access your creativity or allowing your subconscious to show you a potential future. Sometimes your subconscious will show you things that you may be afraid of. Sometimes it will show you what you want.

[632] Other times daydreaming may be taking you away from your life in a detrimental way. It may be that you are escaping reality and are addicted to fantasies rather than real life. You may be dissociating or obsessing about romantic partners or escaping from being an adult.

[633] How do you know which it is? Simply ask yourself if the daydreaming is taking you closer to life, with more clarity, love and ability to take action, or if it is taking you away from life, in a repetitive and craving way.

[634] If it is helping you, then simply ask yourself what the guidance is and how you can use it.

[635] If it is taking you away from life, then change your body position by sitting upright, open your eyes, feel your feet and ask your subconscious to help you.

[636] Then ask yourself what it is that you need to do in real life that you are avoiding by daydreaming.

[637] You may need to send a text message or stop sending text messages. You may need to clean up your room, eat, sleep, deal with grief, create, apologize, leave, tell the truth, make a plan, get help, get back to a task that you were supposed to be doing, stop chasing love that is not available to you etc.
