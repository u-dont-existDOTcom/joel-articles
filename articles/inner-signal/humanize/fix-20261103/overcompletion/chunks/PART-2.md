Even if what it did was harmful to you and others, punishing yourself now is not going to give it the capacity it needed at the time.

## The part to read

[112] # Working with your inner child

[113] ## The two-hand practice

[114] There is a simple and easy body technique to connect with your inner child. Just place one hand in the center of your chest, which physically connects you to your love, and then place your other hand just below that, on your solar plexus, where your inner child can be.

[115] Imagine a divine Love from above, with the inner child below, in the solar plexus area. The inner adult is present. You can also use the hand on the solar plexus to massage that area.

[116] Gently breathe in lots of love and begin to send that love from your heart down to your inner child and you breathe out, noticing what comes up for you. If some comes back up, let it, and send it on down again, so it goes around like a loop. That part's a gift from them, and they don't owe it to you, so if nothing comes back, just keep sending yours. If you feel resistance, just meet it with love. If you feel or see something, just meet that with love as well. If you can't find any warmth today, just leave your hands where they are and notice what's there. Don't pretend to feel something you don't. Let it go back and forth between your heart and your inner child, like a real conversation.

[117] This practice works as an induction too: the Sober induction below has it as one of the anchors you can pick.

[118] ## The Nurturer

[119] Some of the work you can do as the Nurturer is to enjoy your inner child. You don't have to try to make their feelings go away. Just sit back and enjoy their company. Be happy that they are there. Your inner child needs to be enjoyed as well as comforted when it hurts. Say things to your inner child like:

[120] It's okay to feel the way you're feeling.

[121] I want you to know that I'm here for you.

[122] You don't need to do anything right now.

[123] It's okay. You're not too much. You can be small here.

[124] You can be angry here.

[125] You can be sad here.

[126] ## The Protector

[127] As the Protector, set boundaries, so your inner child can believe the warm stuff. They can be simple like, "I'm going to leave this conversation because it's not safe" or "I'm going to stop texting the person who destabilizes me." It can be self-care boundaries like, "I'm going to eat" or "I'm going to get some sleep." It can be trust-related boundaries like, "I'm going to keep the promise to myself" or "I'm not going to leave you to do adult things by yourself." You can protect your inner child from yourself too, like by repairing after an internal attack: calling yourself an idiot for losing your keys again, for one. Your inner child was listening, so you go back and apologize to them.

[128] A lot of times, inner child work doesn't work because people are doing all the fluffy stuff, but then they go and text the person who is destabilizing them, or they don't eat, or they don't do whatever it is they're supposed to be doing. Your inner child starts to trust you when you start taking care of those things.

[129] If it's hard to bring up the warm stuff, you can always do something to protect your inner child. Make sure it's small enough that you can actually do it. If you're not able to, figure out what's getting in the way of you doing it. Are you scared? Tired? Do you not know how to? Is there a practical thing that's getting in your way? Have you set yourself up to do it in a way that's likely to be successful? It still counts if doing it doesn't make you feel better right away, and checking it off a list doesn't mean it helped.

[130] ## The Guide

[131] You can also work with your Guide. Call them whatever you want: Leader, Guru, Wise One, Sage, Inner Teacher, Director, Higher Adult, whatever.

[132] Ask them for help with directing your life. The Guide holds standards, the way a good parent would, and asks hard things of your inner child so it can grow, at its own pace. Your parent’s harsh tone doesn’t come with the job, though, so be careful not to ask for the “tough it out, you don’t have needs, you just need discipline!” kind of direction. Without the Guide, you can also slide from looking after your inner child into spoiling it: sweets and screens can pass for loving yourself, and so can getting it out of anything hard. Ask them for help in directing you to do adult things in a way that takes your needs into account: asking for the help you need, expressing your preferences, letting a safe person see you in an imperfect way, finding ways to get your need for closeness met without punishing someone for their need for space, etc. etc.

[133] Affirmations might be helpful here, too: “We’re not just here to feel good. We’re here to become whole. We’re here to belong to life, not only to people who approve of us. We’re here to serve truth/beauty/love/God/source/nature/the universe/dharma/the Tao/the benefit of all beings,” etc. etc.

[134] Start with whatever adult function you can connect to, but most of us need enough Nurturer and Protector first, before the Guide. Otherwise the Guide just overrules your inner child in the name of something higher (spiritual bypassing, dressed up as direction). Even if you start with protection and can’t do the warmth stuff yet, make sure the direction you’re being led in is appropriate for your inner child’s growth (i.e. not depriving them of things they need, like play or support, etc.)

[135] ## Working with your guards

[136] Use parts language here too, if it helps. Often, we see a sensation or a distraction or an objection as something that needs to be explored, when in fact it might just be hunger, or reasonable skepticism about something, or a sentence that doesn’t fit. Still, notice it, and then look at what might be going on.

[137] If you're not careful, you might find yourself expecting that when you go within to meet your inner child, you'll find your inner child.

[138] Instead, you might feel numb, cynical, angry, suddenly hungry, like you need to look at your phone, sexually aroused, like you need to make plans, sleepy, dissociated, or you might even think that what you're doing is stupid. Those are not failures. Often what you're experiencing is a very charming guard. It's what protects you when your consciously installed parental Protector isn't functioning, the same as when you were a child. It might not be a guard at all, though. Maybe it's your inner child, mad that you took so long to come back. Maybe it's your inherited parent script, or something from earlier today, or a bit of each.

[139] You can tell that part something like, “I see you. I'm not going to force my way past you. What's going on?” Or however you'd actually say that. Then wait and listen patiently. If nothing comes, that's fine for now. No need to answer for that part just because you'd set this up as a healing session. Later on, you can ask it, “What are you afraid would happen if you stopped protecting me by…?”

[140] If necessary, take time and listen. (Perhaps they are afraid that you will lie to yourself again or pretend again or that it will be too painful or you will spiritually bypass again or you will forgive someone who shouldn’t be forgiven yet or you will see what you want to see or that you will leave them hanging after the session or…)

[141] Now talk to the guard. Tell them that it makes sense that they want to protect you and that they don’t have to stop protecting you, but that you want to know what could make them trust you 1% more that you can protect yourself. If it says no, I’d listen, and if it says no to going deeper in this work or to an altered state, I’d stop there and not push past that no, even when it sounds like a prediction.

[142] Most of the time, the guard wants you to do certain things: set boundaries, do whatever you are supposed to do, eat, not text someone, keep a promise, go to sleep, talk to the therapist, admit that you owe someone money, write something down, etc.

[143] ## Your inherited parent script

[144] One important thing to become aware of when approaching your inner child is your inherited parent script. This is the set of rules and ways of treating yourself that you internalized as a child, mostly based on how your parent(s) treated you, and it doesn’t always sound like a voice. This is not the same as your inner adult (Nurturer, Protector, Guide) that you build up on purpose. Often this script acts as a critic or warden, telling your inner child not to cry or complain. You might be hearing, "Don't be needy! Don't embarrass us! And don't be independent." So when you begin talking to your inner child in a new, loving way, that dusty old script may huff and puff that what you’re doing is “fake”.

[145] ## Protection that knows when to stop scanning

[146] Do you want to embody your Protector? Then you could practice this:

[147] orient yourself (look around, feel the support around you, etc.)

[148] assess the situation (know what you know, what you don’t know and check out what you need to)

[149] differentiate between actual/real danger, unknown, past or imagined dangers

[150] set boundaries (start with one and keep it in proportion to the danger; move, stop, delay, ask, leave, get support, etc.)

[151] verify if it is safe enough (you don't need perfect certainty, just enough real-world evidence to take the next step)

[152] stand down and rest (stop scanning once staying on alert isn't helping anymore)

[153] This seems to me like a mature Protector: it’s not afraid to say it doesn’t know, and it can ask another human being, delay interpreting a situation or use a no-trance method. I am learning to use discernment when I need to, and to stop scanning the room when it isn’t needed anymore.

[154] ## Inner child reparenting

[155] I see a lot of connection between reparenting therapy and self hypnosis. They fit well together, though I’d learn to do inner-child therapy sober first, in your ordinary mindstate, since that’s the one you’ll be living in, and get enough Nurturer and Protector in the room before you go deep. When you go into hypnosis, your inner child might come to you. It might show up in any form. It can be an image, a posture, a voice, a memory, an emotion, etc. It can be any age. It can be any vulnerable part of you. It doesn’t even have to be a child. If there is no child there, just work with a guard or whatever you find.

[156] ## Don’t rush the repair

[157] When your inner child does show up, spend some quality time with them before taking off the kid gloves, so to speak, and jumping into the healing work.

[158] They might like to hear something like, “Little one, I’m here with you now. You don’t have to work to explain anything. I'm not asking you to impress me with how fast you can get better. Can I help you feel less alone?”

[159] What does your inner child need? It can be anything: warmth, to play, to be protected, to mourn, to be angry, food, rest, boundaries, better relationships, to stop fantasizing, to stop working their butt off, that you keep a promise to them today, etc. etc.

[160] When a younger you, a guard, a feeling or a signal comes up in your body, take it slowly:

[161] A. Prepare yourself to tend to them with some privacy, but still able to access support if needed. Check how much grown-up you is really in the room first. Can you take a moment before you react? When you decide something, do you follow through? If you answered "No," because right now you’re mostly still stuck identifying as the frightened little boo, hold off on the deeper conversation. Do what makes you feel adult before you try to talk to little you.

[162] B. Look at what's there before judging or deciding what it is.

[163] C. Ask them what you can do or if you can move closer to them. Even if you feel like you already know what they need, ask if they'd prefer warmth, space, verbal reassurance, silence, distance, whatever.

[164] D. Give them what you can: pick a need and do the smallest thing that would help. Don't feel like you have to fix it all.

[165] E. Give yourself some time to let what you did sink in- it may take some time, it may take sleep, etc.

[166] F. Reconnect with them- don't expect them to merge, go away, forgive you, become happy, etc. Just reconnect with them if they're willing.

[167] G. Stop and rest before the caring turns into another performance, and reconnect with them later to see what was helpful.

[168] Remember the goal isn't to make yourself good as new but to make yourself good as you- more supported and connected to yourself. If you don't like the idea of part work or it makes you feel more divided, you can work with a signal or a feeling instead of a part, or with an imaginary younger you that you feel embodies the issue you're having.

[169] ## Play and rest still need an adult present

[170] When you're reparenting, it's okay to play with things that interest your younger states, whether that means touching things of different textures, drawing, exercising, roleplaying with a plushie in a different voice, etc. As long as your reality testing is good and you remember that you aren't a child or pet or doll or whatever and that nobody owns you, it's okay to work on getting your play ability back. A lot of the best discoveries come when you're both just messing around. Play not only paves the way for therapy, but is a part of how successful therapy should be measured.

[171] Resting doesn’t mean you have to become helpless. It’s okay to limit the number of decisions you are going to be making for a certain amount of time, it’s okay to put the things you might not get to on a list, and it’s okay to let your inner adult keep watch for a while. You can still get up and move around, stop resting, and get back to what you were doing whenever you need to.

[172] ## Non-dominant hand dialogue

[173] If talking out loud or visualizing conversations feels fake, try writing it out. Tell your inner child or guard to write with your nondominant hand, and talk to them with your dominant. It might be a little hard to write with your nondominant hand but you can kind of do it. So for example, they might say "I don't trust you" with your nondominant hand, and you can say with your dominant hand "I know you don't trust me right now but I love you and I want to know how I've fucked up. I don't expect you to trust me but I want to hear you out and give you a promise I can keep today" and they can say "what promise?" and you can say "I'm going to make sure I eat today/ clear off my desk/ not text so-and-so". Only say the "I love you" part if you can feel it. If you don't feel the love yet, say you care.

[174] # When “your voice” is everybody else’s voice

[175] It can be difficult to connect with your inner child if your inner child didn’t have the opportunity to develop. This happens often when you grow up in a situation where you don’t have a private self, for example when a controlling family, a rigid religion or an enmeshed community takes over very early in your life. Or you are pushed to achieve very early in life. The result is that you don’t know what you feel, what you want, what you believe, what you like and dislike until you have heard someone else’s opinion about it. If you are in that situation, it can be difficult to follow the advice to ‘love yourself’. You don’t even know who ‘yourself’ is! So you can do reparenting and finding yourself in parallel. For others, it is very easy to work with the inner child because they can feel it right away.

[176] You are in the first camp if:

[177] You don’t know what you want until you have heard what someone else wants.

[178] Your opinion is very different when you are with different people

[179] You agree with people in the moment but when you are by yourself you realize that you don’t agree with them

[180] You take responsibility for the emotions of others, at the expense of yourself

[181] When you try to love yourself, it feels more like a show

[182] When you try to find out how your inner child feels, you feel that you are guessing instead of feeling

[183] You can rebel, but you can’t choose yet

[184] One way of explaining this is that your childhood didn’t leave you much room for finding out what you like, what you want to try out and who you are when nobody is grading you. You were too busy avoiding pain, reading what was dangerous and what you had to do to stay in a good bond, etc.

[185] Taking a break. Sometimes it may be wise to remove yourself, for a time, from the people or group that you normally would get answers from. Some people come back later on different terms, and sometimes the break has to be long-term because the pattern of losing yourself with them is deeply entrenched. As you become more your own person, you learn to tell which of those apply. You want to let your quieter preferences bubble up to the surface so you can be sure you’re not just echoing your social group.

[186] What is the difference between a human being and a human doing? If you don’t feel good enough as a human being, you will try to become good enough as a "human doing" by, for example, being beautiful, doing something well, being smart, having a certain status, being spiritual, etc. Those can all be real gifts, although they still become a kind of bargain for belonging when failing in these areas feels like losing what it means to have your own inherent value.

[187] So in order to find yourself, you can ask yourself:

[188] What do I like when nobody is looking?

[189] What do I believe when I don’t check with other people?

[190] What do I want that is neither in rebellion nor in compliance with others?

[191] What can I try on that I don’t need to be good at?

[192] With whom can I be myself?

[193] What do I feel in my body and what do I make out of it?

[194] Give your inner child the opportunity to do something that has no value for its resume and that it doesn’t need to be good at. For example, painting, dancing, gardening, learning a language, playing a sport, doing volunteer work, playing a musical instrument, etc. Because often your inherited parent script tells your inner child that it is supposed to be good at something as soon as it starts doing it, which is humiliating. The more consciously sculpted adult in you can answer, “We’re finding out whether we enjoy this, not proving our worth.” So try things out long enough until the humiliation is gone.

[195] ## Belonging without betraying yourself

[196] When you are a child, you feel that you need to belong to the family in order to survive. This is why people do things for their family, religion, romantic partner, political tribe, spiritual group, institution, online community etc., that they wouldn’t otherwise do. Part of adulting is working out where you are selling yourself in order to belong and choosing your own ground, and finding out you can disappoint someone and not disappear. A large part of your subconscious mind is made out of other people. That is why belonging gets a section in a self hypnosis guide!

[197] Where am I betraying myself in order to belong?

[198] What does it feel like to belong without betraying myself?

[199] Where do I think I am following my own guidance, but I am in fact following my belonging program?

[200] Where do I think I am doing things out of love, but I am actually doing it out of fear of not belonging?

[201] Where do I think I am listening to my conscience, but I am actually listening to my conditioning?

[202] Where do I think I am doing something out of devotion, but I am actually doing it out of compliance?

[203] Where do I think I am free, but I am actually rebelling?

[204] And then notice what your body tells you. It is not always correct, but it is a good place to start.

[205] # De-hypnosis: wake up inside the influence

[206] One of the best side-effects of learning self-hypnosis is that sudden "waking up" to all the ways your attention is being shaped in your day-to-day life. There are actually multiple patents on using screens to send subliminal messages, but even without that kind of potentially illegal tech, the screen is one connection to the omnipotent Algorithm. Which shows us ads and guides our preferences. Then we wonder why we vote for this or that politician. But there are also the older, more mundane influences: erotic imprinting (as if breast implants are actually sexy), religious fear, which tags along with shame, charismatic authority and pressure to belong. And all of that drilled repeatedly into us, because one exposure won't do the trick. Sometimes it’s benign – like lullabies or prayers or being taught by a good teacher or listening to a beautiful song or being in love. But you may want to ask yourself what effect it’s having on your ability to see clearly and make choices.

[207] Manipulation often comes with any of the following: urgency, feeling special, FOMO (fear of missing out), the threat of not being included, sexual charge, shame, confusion, the promise that someone will rescue you, destiny, pressure to obey, secret knowledge, a sense of certainty about something that comes way too quickly, and a sense that one good question will break the spell.

[208] ## If you sense you are being influenced

[209] First of all, if you sense you are being hypnotically influenced, stop. Don’t obey immediately, but don’t rebel automatically, just realize that you are being influenced.

[210] Secondly, here are some of the ways people use hypnotic language to manipulate: you consent to something without realizing it, you only get one choice, or someone slips something you never agreed to into a sentence. For example:

[211] “You will soon realize that I am right…” Or “You may, in time, decide to admit that…” Or “The only reason you do not agree is…” Or “Part of you already knows…” Or “Your higher self wants…” Or “Everyone who is awake knows…” Or “The reason you do not believe that is true is because your ego does not want you to…”

[212] If you are unsure, you may like to ask yourself the following questions:

[213] What are they asking me to agree to? What choice are they giving me? What are they assuming that I may not agree with? Who benefits if I comply? What is the emotionally charged part of what they’re saying? What are they leaving out? If I say no, what will the consequences be? (Will they punish me, shame me, laugh at me, leave me out, tell me I’m not ‘awake’ etc?)

[214] And afterwards: do I come away from them more present and grown up, or less? Be extra careful with all this when you’re in a deep or powerful state, because that’s when you need your boundaries most.

[215] ## Soft language can still take away choice

[216] Be aware that even permissive language can still take away the subject’s choice.

[217] Indirect suggestions can bypass the subject’s skeptical guard. They do this by not directly telling the subject what to do. This does not mean that indirect suggestions are not specific. It simply means that they are worded in a vague, conversational, suggestive manner.

[218] Here are some of the ways that happens:

[219] 1) Utilizing induction techniques while seemingly explaining something (even something like "anti hypnosis" can use the same mechanics of induction).

[220] 2) In any way making it so that whatever the subject says shows that a suggestion has worked, when this may not be the case (this includes answering yes, no, noticing something, not noticing something, trying not to do something, not answering, etc etc etc)

[221] 3) Including phrases such as “whether or not you notice” etc etc, so either way counts as success

[222] 4) Including any concepts that may make a subject think they must obey, or take on a role, or that it is good/safe to please the guide or belong to someone etc etc.

[223] 5) Fractionating a subject without telling them that you are going to take them in and out of absorption a few times.

[224] 6) Giving a subject cues (words, gestures, blinks, sounds, listening a certain number of times etc) that you intend to use to make them do something at a later date, without informing them exactly what it is that they will be doing.

[225] 7) Including suggestions for amnesia, or using dreams, gaps in memory, imagery, body experiences etc as evidence to make a subject think that something has happened when this may not necessarily be the case.

[226] 8) Not taking a subject out at the end of a recording, or leaving them with a role to play, with their judgment down, with their physical abilities down, with their minds blank, or in any other way different for an indefinite length of time.

[227] In order for a subject to be making a real choice while in hypnosis, there must be at least one way for them not to carry on with the hypnosis if they wish. This could be by making the hypnosis lighter, using a different method of hypnosis, being in full awareness while in hypnosis, or being able to pause or stop the hypnosis. Just so you know, if you say no, you are entitled to mean no, even if someone with a nice voice is asking you to do something. And just because you don't say anything, or don't know what to say, doesn't mean yes.

[230] Inductions serve to gather your awareness up into a gentler, trancier, more receptive form so you can access the inner knowing that's too quiet for our ordinary awareness to hear.
