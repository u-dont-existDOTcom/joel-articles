Learning, entering, and returning

Healing the Divided Self, Maggie Phillips and Claire Frederick (1995), pp 24-27, 45-50, 83-90, covers a lot of aspects of this post, such as interactive hypnosis, helping the subject learn how to learn by using other senses, learning self hypnosis, making sure the subject comes back at the end, etc. However, this is a professional clinical book, not a self help book, so be careful about trying to use the procedures used with the cases, etc in this book for solo work (and the same goes for its historical claims). Also, Adam Eason and Benjamin Parris's article about self hypnosis for the International Society of Hypnosis should be a useful thing to read (especially the parts that talk about how to learn to learn when you're directing it yourself, and about whether you need another person to help you, and using recordings to help you, etc.)

Indirect language and re-entry cues

For information about using indirect hypnotic language patterns, such as pacing, utilizing, metaphor, and associational cues, etc, see Healing the Divided Self, Phillips and Frederick, 1995, pp 53-57, 96-100, as well as Nimja's tutorial on hypnotic language patterns, and his tutorial on triggers (he didn't write or endorse this guide, by the way). They're where these techniques come from, but they're not on the hook for every NLP claim I make, or for whether every example suits trauma work. I adapted the language for my own purposes, and I deliberately used a sequence that would be easy to use for re-entry.
