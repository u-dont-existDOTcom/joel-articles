Before you begin

Read through the induction, and the Come all the way back section, when you're awake.

Get clear on what it is you want to do during the induction. What are you leaving alone today? How long do you want to spend on this? (You don't have to do this on a problem. You can do it on something you want to experience more of: love, play, confidence, creativity, communion, whatever.) Make sure you're leaving some time at the end to come back, and reflect on what you experienced.

Put yourself in a place where you can safely do this, which is not: driving a big machine, swimming with the fishies, or otherwise needing to be alert and functionally responsible for yourself or others. If you are intoxicated and more confused than usual, or for whatever reason not able to keep yourself safe, then get some immediate support instead of doing exploratory hypnosis. (The part below called "When you're already trancey" can be used to help orient you if you're already open, but don't use it to make yourself deeper if you're not stable.)

Deciding to do it: how will you coax yourself to do this? Make it spacious? Make it warm and held? Make it firmer, so you stay with a feeling? Something else? Preview what you are going to do: will you close your eyes? Will you come out and go back in again? What will you do at the end? If you are going to use a recording, read the script beforehand if you can. Know that you can say "stop" at any time and the induction will end. If you want to change something, say "less" if you want it to ask less of you, or say "quiet" and the talking will stop. You can also just open your eyes and stop, and then come all the way back anyway. You don't need to ask any parts for permission to stop, even if a part tells you that you do.

If you are new to this or haven't done it for a while, work on the coming in and coming out part first. Don't bring in the hard stuff till later. You don't have to watch yourself all the time, but for the first few times, make sure you can change state. Let yourself make it easier and easier to do this and notice that it takes less and less of you to do it. A no will stay a no. The narrator can't add stuff just because you don't say anything.

Let your response guide you as to what to do next regardless of what the script is expecting. Utilize: use what you have (a thought, sound, movement, or objection) to go to the next place, whatever it is. Learn how to notice what is there. If nothing changes, notice that too. As my dad always said, "Be prepared to take what you get. The best might come afterwards!" For more on language you can use, see Appendix B, or keep reading below for information on using the tools after induction in Let the next response guide you. Either way, keep the instructions about how to stay safe and how to get out plain.
