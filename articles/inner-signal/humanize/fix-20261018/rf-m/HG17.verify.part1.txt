Below is one section of a long self-hypnosis guide by Joel, published on his Substack. It teaches gathering attention, listening to the subconscious as an ecology of parts (the inner child, protectors or "guards", the body, intuition), three adult functions he calls the Nurturer, the Protector and the Leader/Guru, Higher Self, "borrowed adulthood", inductions and a complete return, influence literacy ("de-hypnosis": noticing and resisting other people's influence), and safety limits (when to stop, when to get help). Its quoted lines are scripts the reader says or thinks.

PUBLISHED section:
[B17] Read this slowly into a recorder, speak it aloud, or learn its movement and use silent words if that comes easily. Choose one anchor—a sound, bodily contact, or your two-hand practice—and an opening phrase. Keep using the same anchor where the words refer to it. Pause between paragraphs; don’t read the delivery directions aloud.

[B18] Let your eyes settle somewhere in the room, somewhere they can rest for a little while, and notice the difference between looking for something and letting what is already there come into view.

[B19] There is a surface beneath you. Notice where you meet it … the contact, the pressure, whatever you can actually feel. And while you notice that, a sound may reach you, or a breath may draw your attention, without needing you to arrange either one.

[B20] You can give this a little time.

[B21] A thought about the day may still be here. A thought about whether you’re doing this properly may be here too. There is room to notice a thought and return to the anchor you chose, without finishing the thought first.

[B22] And as you return, you may find one detail that holds your interest … something about the sound, or the contact, or that particular way of listening … and let attention stay with it long enough to become curious.

[B23] Perhaps you can give yourself the time you would give someone you care about who has begun to speak and hasn’t yet found the words. You can remain nearby, listening, without finishing their sentence for them.

[B24] Let your chosen phrase come to mind now. Say it as an invitation, and notice how it is received.

[B25] As you continue, your eyes can close, soften, or stay open. Let that be a choice you make. Listening may deepen while a sound from the room remains available in the background.

[B26] Count slowly from five to one. Give each number its own moment.

[B27] Five. Notice your chosen anchor again. Four. Let attention gather around what you find. Three. Leave a little space after the words. Two. Listen into that space, without preparing an answer. One. Stay here for a moment.

[B28] Bring your chosen concern lightly to mind, or remain with your wish for contact. Let it be present without rehearsing its whole story.

[B29] What am I noticing now?

[B30] Give the response time to take its own form. It might be a sensation, a few words, an image, a feeling, or an impulse to move. There may be nothing clear yet.

[B31] Leave room to notice, with words or without them.
