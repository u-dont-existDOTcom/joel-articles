Sober induction: 3–5 minutes

Guide to self-guided Silent Contact Practise reading it slowly into a recorder, or reading it aloud or (best if you can manage it) learning it and saying the words to yourself silently. Choose to use sound or bodily contact or two hands throughout and choose an opening phrase. Whenever the guide mentions ‘the anchor’ use your chosen anchor and pause at the end of each paragraph for a few moments. (Don’t say aloud any of the instructions about how to do the reading.)

Pause, and let your eyes rest somewhere in the room.

Now as you notice where you are resting on the surface beneath you, notice the feel of that contact, and notice also a sound or your breathing.

There’s all the time in the world to do this.

If there’s a thought in there, perhaps about something from your day, or even about what you’re doing now, see if you can turn your attention to the anchor even if there is a thought there.

See if you can find a way of listening to the sound or the contact (or to your listening) that captures your interest in some detail.

Give yourself as much time for this as you would for someone close to you who was trying to speak to you.

Now perhaps say or think to yourself your chosen phrase, and notice how it is received.

You can close your eyes now if you wish, or soften them, or keep proceeding with them open. Deepen your listening now, keeping in the background the sound you chose.

Count slowly to yourself from 5 down to 1.

5 Notice again your chosen anchor for today, 4 notice what you notice, 3, 2 listen into the space after these words 1 and stay here for a moment.

Now perhaps briefly bring to mind the concern you chose to work with, or just your wish to make contact.

What do you notice?

Give yourself time to notice what comes, be it sensations, words, images, feelings, urges to move or nothing.

Give yourself time to notice this, with or without words.
