Sober induction: 3–5 minutes

Practise reading this slowly into a recorder, or reading it aloud, or if you can learn it and use silent words. Choose to use one of the three anchors – sound, bodily contact or two hands - throughout and choose an opening phrase. Whenever the words below refer to ‘the anchor’ use the one you’ve chosen. Pause for a few moments at the end of each paragraph. Don’t read aloud the instructions for how to do the reading.

Let your eyes settle somewhere in the room, somewhere they can rest for a little while, and notice the difference between looking for something and letting what is already there come into view.

As you notice the surface beneath you, notice the feel of the contact, the pressure, whatever you can actually feel. And while you notice that, a sound may reach you, or a breath may draw your attention, without needing you to arrange either one.

There’s time to do this.

It might be that a thought about something that’s happened during the day is still with you, or perhaps there’s a thought about doing this properly. Whatever is there, can you find it in yourself to turn your attention back to the anchor, even if there is a thought there?

and when you do come back, you may find one detail that holds your interest, something about the sound, or the contact, or that particular way of listening, and let attention stay with it long enough to become curious

Perhaps you can give yourself the time that you might give to someone dear to you who has begun to speak and hasn’t yet found the words. You can remain nearby, listening, without finishing their sentence for them

Let the phrase you chose come to mind, say it as an invitation, and notice how it is received

You can close your eyes, soften them or keep them open as you proceed. Let that be a choice you make. Listening may deepen while a sound from the room remains available in the background

Count slowly from 5 to 1, giving each number its own moment

5, perhaps notice again what you have chosen to be your anchor for today, 4, let attention gather around what you find, 3, leave a little space after the words, 2, listen into that space, without preparing an answer, 1, stay here for a moment

Bring to mind, lightly, your chosen concern. Or simply, stay with the wish to contact. Let it be present without rehearsing its whole story.

What do you notice now?

Allow yourself some time to notice what is coming. It might be sensations, words, images, feelings, the urge to move in some way or nothing clear yet.

Allow yourself some time to notice this, with or without words.
