Indirect suggestions are generally used to by-pass the subjects skeptical conscious mind by never directly stating what it is you want him/her to do. This does not mean that the suggestions are not specific, but rather that they are worded in a vague, conversational and suggestive manner.

1) Utilizing induction techniques while seemingly explaining something (even something like "anti hypnosis" using the same mechanics of induction.

2) Making it so that any answer the subject may give will result in compliance (yes, no, noticing, not noticing, resisting, not answering etc.)

3) Using phrases like "whether or not you notice", etc.

4) Binding a concept of safety or goodness to obeying, or taking on a role, or pleasing the guide or being part of something, etc.

5) Using fractionation without informing the subject of the benefits of going in and out of absorption several times.

6) Giving the subject cues (words, gestures, blinks, sounds, just by listening to certain things etc.) that will make them do something at a later date without informing them exactly what they will be doing.

7) Suggesting amnesia, or making dream, amnesia, imagery and body experiences seem like "proof" of something that is not necessarily true.

8) Ending the recording without bringing the subject out of the altered state, or leaving them with a role to play, lessened judgment, impaired physical abilities, blankness, or otherwise altered for an indefinite period of time.
