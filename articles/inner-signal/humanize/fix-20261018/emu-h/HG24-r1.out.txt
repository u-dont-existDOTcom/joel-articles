You dissociate or feel unreal

Open your eyes, move your body, feel something textured, and name five objects out loud. Then finish up for the day.

You’re being too grandiose

Go eat some food, go to sleep, do chores, accept some humility, get some feedback.

Silently say, “I need to know that these insights can survive sleep, food, time, humility, and feedback.”

You’re being too analytical

Stop trying to do the practice better. Put one hand on your heart and one on your solar plexis and say,

“I’m not supposed to be doing this well. I’m supposed to be entering into relationship with what’s deeper than my surface mind.

“What would help me turn this into a communion rather than a performance?”

Sometimes the answer is to be silent, to rest, to do something. Do the thing that comes to you.

You’re being too blissful or diffuse

Bring it down to a specific level.

“What would love do right now?”

You’re trying to get a quick peek at some hidden truth

Refrain. Say, “First show me the capacity, and you can show me the content later.”

You’re daydreaming again

Sit up and open your eyes. Then ask, “What real action am I avoiding with this fantasy?”

You’re spiritually bypassing

Ask, “Where is divine love not yet descending to meet my pain?”

You may have had a genuinely profound opening that revealed to you how much more love and freedom are possible. It’s also possible that, as a child, you prayed and then felt abandoned or condemned; that you went to a teacher and were betrayed. It’s worth asking some questions about what happened. But doing more of the same may not be the answer.

Sometimes the best thing is to do something practical in your life, while pretending that there isn’t a larger horizon, but pretending that you’re curious about what it would be like to step into it. Other people seem to have enough love and meaning. You don’t have to do anything about it. Spirituality doesn’t have to be another thing you’re supposed to do.

You’re processing forever

You may have a real wound to deal with, but you may also have a checking loop. Ask yourself, “Am I looking for something new here, or am I checking again to see if it’s gone yet?” Sometimes, both in grieving and in practice, you’re supposed to cover the same ground over and over again.

If you discover that you are in a checking loop, then let the thought be there but don’t argue with it. Return to some chosen activity or relationship. You don’t have to hold a meeting with every thought that arises. You don’t have to do some magical thing before you can start your life.

Ask yourself, “What would the Leader do?”

You’re doing all this hypnosis and not changing your life

Ask, “What would show my child that I’m for real?”

Then do it.
