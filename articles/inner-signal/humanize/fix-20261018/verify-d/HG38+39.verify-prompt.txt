Below is one section of a long self-hypnosis guide by Joel, published on his Substack. It teaches gathering attention, listening to the subconscious as an ecology of parts (the inner child, protectors or "guards", the body, intuition), three adult functions he calls the Nurturer, the Protector and the Leader/Guru, Higher Self, "borrowed adulthood", inductions and a complete return, influence literacy ("de-hypnosis": noticing and resisting other people's influence), and safety limits (when to stop, when to get help). Its quoted lines are scripts the reader says or thinks.

PUBLISHED section:
[B0] Appendix I: Sleep hypnosis

[B1] For sleep hypnosis, use Nimja’s sleep playlist.

[B2] Appendix J: A practical induction library

[B3] Preview the sequence, choose one purpose, and decide whether you want to begin. Each family can be light, eyes-open, or replaced by no-trance contact. Notice the actual response—including no change—and let it determine the next move. Move, imagine, change the sensory channel, remain still, or stop. Use the complete waking return after every induction.

[B4] If vivid images don’t come, translate the metaphor into words, rhythm, spatial sense, touch, movement, or simple knowing. The mind doesn’t owe anybody a movie.

[B5] Stretch and release

[B6] Feel the support beneath you and the position of one hand or foot. Press gently, stretch slightly, and release—or imagine it. Notice ease, tension, warmth, breath, or no change. Repeat with no more than three areas, letting “release” guide the sequence. Which area or inner voice wants attention? What support is needed? Restore ordinary movement and return.

[B7] Supported floating

[B8] Notice where your weight is carried. Let support carry 1% more effort, as if floating without losing contact, or keep ordinary weight and pressure. Notice the response. You may deliberately alternate attention toward ordinary awareness on the inhale and support on the exhale; name this fractionation beforehand. What becomes easier to hear? Keep the support, regain chosen weight and firm contact, then return.

[B9] Rhythmic breath or pulse

[B10] Accompany a natural rhythm: breath, a comfortable hum, a finger tap, outside sound, or silent counting. No rhythm is also an option. Repeat “here, and here again,” noticing where attention lands between repetitions. Stop the deliberate rhythm and notice the first quiet beat, without trying to become blank. Is a word, feeling, need, or action clearer? Let ordinary rhythm resume and return.

[B11] Unwinding object

[B12] Notice a concern with several strands. Imagine yarn unwinding, bubbles settling, or knots placed visibly beside you. Leave the object unchanged or label it in words when that fits better. Count only strands you actually set down; nothing is erased. Which needs warmth, protection, or action? Keep one useful strand, give the others a visible holding place, and return.

[B13] Sensory journey

[B14] Hear the actual room and feel the body before imagining a step toward a chosen destination. Use footsteps, textures, or landmarks to gather attention. Change scenery or skip directly to dialogue when useful. Notice a detail without assigning hidden meaning; the mystery is how the scene develops, not what will be done to you. Who or what needs to speak? Return by the same route and close the imagined space.

[B15] Gradual wave

[B16] Start with a small neutral area and what is already there. Imagine warmth, steadiness, or attention spreading to the next comfortable area, or keep it localized. Pause at each step and notice what changes without claiming medical repair. Does the body want contact, space, words, or ordinary care? Let the image recede, feel the whole body and real contact points, and return.

[B17] Care workshop

[B18] Prepare time, a place, tools, and boundaries for patient care. Let a signal, feeling, or younger state approach only as closely as welcome; choose language that doesn’t make you feel fragmented. Ask before responding, wait, reconnect only where welcome, rest, and reassess. An available inner adult or one borrowed function can help; you choose words, pace, and action. Aim for “good as you,” not erased history. Close the workspace, affirm wholeness and adult presence, and return.

[B19] Resource embodiment

[B20] Choose qualities you understand: warmth, boundary, steadiness, curiosity, or dignity. Imagine posture, voice, or attention with 1% more access to one quality. Observe rather than embody it when preferred. Notice a movement, word, image, or no response. Repeat to strengthen access, not replace identity. How would that quality meet today’s situation? Keep the quality and one useful action, release the role, affirm “I am myself,” and return.

[B21] No-trance inner contact

[B22] Observable pacing: Keep the eyes open, name the room and day, feel the feet, and read or write in ordinary awareness. Invitation: Ask one clear question and wait without narrowing attention further. Genuine choice: Answer, write “unsure,” rest, change the question, seek another person’s view, or stop. Autonomous signal: Record a word, sensation, impulse, objection, or silence without interpreting it as proof. Deepener: None. Full alertness is not an inferior version of the practice. Handoff: Let the internal adult respond in writing or through one practical choice. Integration and exit: Reality-test the signal and do one small action. Because no altered state was cultivated, simply close the exercise and continue the day.

[B9, as published] Rhythmic breath or pulse

[CHANGED, replaces B10]
Try it with a natural rhythm; perhaps the rhythm of breathing or a slight hum in the throat. Try it with a more artificial rhythm, such as tapping a finger or listening to sounds outside. Even try it counting (silently) to yourself, or even with no rhythm at all. Say the words 'here, and here again' over and over, noticing where your attention lands in between, until suddenly, stop the rhythm. Notice the first quiet beat - do not try and make your mind blank. This might bring a bit more clarity to what word, feeling, need or action is coming up. Then let your normal rhythm come back, and return.

[B11, as published] Unwinding object

[CHANGED, replaces B12]
A quick exercise for when one concern has several strands. Imagine yarn unwinding, bubbles settling, or knots placed where you can see them beside you. When you've placed them all, take a minute to look at them all (without changing them, though you could label them if needed, by saying a word or two). Count only the strands you actually set down; nothing is erased. Which one needs warmth, protection, or action? Now keep one useful strand, put the others somewhere you can see them, and return.

[B13, as published] Sensory journey

[CHANGED, replaces B14]
Listen to the sounds of the room you are currently in and feel your body. Now imagine yourself walking somewhere, where do you want to go? As you walk, listen to the sounds of your footsteps and take in your surroundings. What texture are the objects you pass? What landmarks do you see? Now that you've collected enough attention, imagine the scenery changing until you are in a place where whoever or whatever needs to speak can speak. Or you can just cut straight to the chase if you want. Notice a detail without giving it a hidden meaning, just don't make it into a "what are they going to do to you?" kind of thing. When you're ready to finish, imagine walking back the way you came and close the space.

[B15, as published] Gradual wave

[CHANGED, replaces B16]
Begin with the smallest area possible that is neutral. Sense what is already there, and imagine warmth, steadiness or attention radiating outward to the next area, which should still feel comfortable. Continue this, pausing and sensing in each new area, or continue with the original area, only. Notice what changes, without claiming any medical repair. Does the body want contact, space, words, or ordinary care? Allow the image to fade, sense the whole body, the contact points, and return.

[B17, as published] Care workshop

[CHANGED, replaces B18]
First get ready for some patient, unhurried care: time, place, tools and boundaries. Let the signal, the feeling or the younger state come only as close as is welcome. Be careful to not use language which makes you fragmented. Ask before you respond, wait, reconnect only where that’s welcome, rest, and see again. If you have an adult part which can do this or borrow a function from someone else, let that help. You decide the words, the pace and what you want to do. Aim for “good as you,” not an erased history. Finally, close the workspace. Say that you are whole, an adult and come back.

[B19, as published] Resource embodiment

[CHANGED, replaces B20]
Choose a quality you understand, like warmth, a boundary, steadiness, curiosity or dignity. Close your eyes and imagine your posture, voice or attention with just 1% more access to it, or if you are uncomfortable with this, simply observe it. Notice what happens. Repeat a few times, to strengthen your access to it, not to become someone else. How would that quality meet the situation you’re in today? [B21, as published] No-trance inner contact

[CHANGED, replaces B22]
Observable pacing: keep the eyes open, notice where the practice is taking place and on what day, notice the feet, read something, write something, notice that one is still relatively normally aware.

Invitation: choose a question and keep it in mind, do not specifically focus or narrow the awareness further.

Genuine choice: answer the question or write it down, write that one does not know, rest, change the question, ask someone else, stop the practice, etc.

Autonomous signal: write down any word, sensation, impulse, objection, or silence that comes, without taking it as proof.

Deepener: none, it is not necessary to reach a state that is less alert.

Handoff: have the adult part of yourself answer the question in any way, preferably in writing or by taking an action.

Integration/exit: reality test the autonomous signal and take an action based on that, end the practice (no special exit is needed since no state was achieved that one needs to exit from).
