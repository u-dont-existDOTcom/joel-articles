[B2] Appendix A: How to ask the subconscious a useful question

[B3] The subconscious isn’t an AI model, but prompt engineering offers one useful lesson: the shape of a question changes what kind of answer can appear. A prompt should make you more present, honest, loving, and able to hear the next signal—never more controlling.

[B4] I use seven elements when I want a prompt to be precise:

[B5] Role: who or what you are addressing.

[B6] Context: what situation you are bringing.

[B7] Intention: what kind of change is desired.

[B8] Constraint: safety, truth, kindness, no forcing, no false certainty, and capacity before content.

[B9] Output: image, phrase, body signal, memory, next action, question, symbol, silence, or rest.

[B10] Evaluation: true, kind, timely, stabilizing, specific, testable.

[B11] Iteration: revise after life gives feedback.

[B12] Always include a safe fallback so the analytical mind doesn’t treat silence as failure and manufacture an answer.

[B13] “And if the system needs more time to process this, or if silence is the safest answer today, let my body settle into deeper comfortable rest instead. A peaceful silence is a fully successful answer.”

[B14] Sometimes nothing answers because nothing is happening. Sometimes the answer is not yet, rest first, build capacity, or do the ordinary thing you already know to do.

[B15] Core prompts

[B16] For clarity:

[B17] “Wise subconscious, please show me what is true, kind, and timely. If the full truth of this situation is too intense for my body to integrate today, pass it to my conscious mind only as a gentle symbol, a body signal, or a single next step. Show me my capacity to hold peace before you show me the content of the wound.”

[B18] For resistance:

[B19] “What are you protecting me from, and what would help you trust me 1% more?”

[B20] For the inner child:

[B21] “Little one, what do you need me to understand, and what can I do today so you are not alone with it?”

[B22] For the Protector:

[B23] “What real-world action would make this system safer?”

[B24] For the Leader/Guru:

[B25] “What direction serves truth, love, life, and the benefit of all beings?”

[B26] For intuition:

[B27] “Let intuition offer a signal, let analysis examine its meaning, let the body give feedback, and let reality test the interpretation.”

[B28] For separating intuition from conditioning:

[B29] “Wise subconscious, please help me look at this impulse clearly. If part of this signal belongs to family programming, religious fear, marketing, erotic imprinting, political tribe, trauma response, or a childhood bargain for belonging, let that part become visible as conditioning. Let it soften or step back only as much as is safe. Leave behind whatever belongs to true guidance, the universal heart, and a better ordinary life. Let my body feel the difference.”

[B30] For daydreaming:

[B31] “If this daydream is useful, reveal the guidance inside it. If it is avoidance, bring me gently into the present and help me deal with what I need to deal with.”

[B32] For anxiety:

[B33] “What is anxiety trying to protect, and what would help it trust the adult self?”

[B34] For hidden truth:

[B35] “If the full truth would overwhelm me, please do not reveal it all. Show me the capacity before the content.”

[B36] For integration:

[B37] “What ordinary-life action would prove that I heard correctly?”

[B38] The prompt defines the search. NLP language affects how gently the question enters. Used together, they can create a safer field for an answer without pretending the answer is automatically true.

[B2, as published] Appendix A: How to ask the subconscious a useful question

[CHANGED, replaces B3-B14]
The subconscious is not an AI language model, but there is a certain overlap in how you can structure your questions to it to get the most useful answers. Prompt engineering can be very useful if used to become more present, more honest, more loving and better able to hear the next signal. But useless or dangerous if used to be more controlling.

For maximum precision, include these 7 elements in your prompt:

Role (who/what are you talking to?)

Context (what is the situation?)

Intention (what do you want to change?)

Constraint (what keeps it safe, true and kind: no forcing, no false certainty, capacity before content)

Output (how do you want the answer: as an image, phrase, body signal, memory, action, question, symbol, silence, rest…?)

Evaluation (how do you evaluate the answer: is it true? kind? timely? stabilizing? specific? testable?)

Iteration (revise the prompt based on real life feedback)

Always include a “safe” fallback. If you don’t do that, the analytical part of your mind might treat silence as failure and fabricate an answer to fit your prompt. Here is one fallback:

“If the system needs more time to process this, or if silence is the best and safest answer today, then let my body go into a deeper state of comfortable rest. Peaceful silence is a successful answer.”

Sometimes there is no answer because nothing is happening. Sometimes the answer is that it is not time yet, or that you need to rest, build capacity, or do whatever it is you are supposed to be doing.

[B15, as published] Core prompts

[CHANGED, replaces B16-B38]
Here are the core prompts that can be mixed and matched:

Clarity: Wise subconscious mind, show me what is true, kind and timely. If there is something I need to know that is too intense to integrate, please transfer it to my conscious mind only as a gentle symbol, a body signal, or a single next step. Show me my capacity to be peaceful first, and then the content of whatever is wounding me.

Resistance: What are you protecting me from? How can you trust me 1% more?

Inner child: Little one, what do you need me to know? What can I do today so you’re not alone with it?

Protector: What do you need me to do in the real world to make this system safer?

Leader/Guru: What is the direction that will be in alignment with truth, love, life and all beings?

Intuition: Let the intuition give me a signal. Let the analytical mind decipher it. Let the body give me feedback on my deciphering. Let reality inform me whether my interpretation is correct or not.

How to distinguish intuition from inherited programming: Wise subconscious mind, help me look at this impulse clearly. If part of it comes from family programming, religious fear, marketing, erotic imprints, political tribe, trauma responses, or a childhood need for belonging, show me that part so I can see it as programming. Let it soften or step back only as much as is safe, so that I can receive the true guidance from the universal heart that will make my life better in simple, ordinary ways. Let my body feel the difference.

Daydreaming: If this daydream is useful, show me the guidance in it. If it is a way of not dealing with my life, bring me gently into the present and help me deal with what I need to deal with.

Anxiety: What are you protecting me from? How can you trust my adult self more?

Truth that is hidden from you: If the full truth would overwhelm me, please don’t show me all of it. Show me my capacity to be peaceful first, and then the content of whatever is wounding me.

Integration: What can I do in ordinary life that would prove I heard correctly?

The prompt will tell your subconscious what you are looking for. The NLP will help you approach it in a way that will be safer. Use both, without pretending the answer is automatically true.
