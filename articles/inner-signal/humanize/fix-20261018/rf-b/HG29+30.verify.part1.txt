PUBLISHED section:
[B3] The subconscious isn’t an AI model, but prompt engineering offers one useful lesson: the shape of a question changes what kind of answer can appear. A prompt should make you more present, honest, loving, and able to hear the next signal—never more controlling.

[B4] I use seven elements when I want a prompt to be precise:

[B5] Role: who or what you are addressing.

[B6] Context: what situation you are bringing.

[B7] Intention: what kind of change is desired.

[B8] Constraint: safety, truth, kindness, no forcing, no false certainty, and capacity before content.

[B9] Output: image, phrase, body signal, memory, next action, question, symbol, silence, or rest.

[B10] Evaluation: true, kind, timely, stabilizing, specific, testable.

[B11] Iteration: revise after life gives feedback.

[B12] Always include a safe fallback so the analytical mind doesn’t treat silence as failure and manufacture an answer.

[B13] “And if the system needs more time to process this, or if silence is the safest answer today, let my body settle into deeper comfortable rest instead. A peaceful silence is a fully successful answer.”

[B14] Sometimes nothing answers because nothing is happening. Sometimes the answer is not yet, rest first, build capacity, or do the ordinary thing you already know to do.

[B16] For clarity:

[B17] “Wise subconscious, please show me what is true, kind, and timely. If the full truth of this situation is too intense for my body to integrate today, pass it to my conscious mind only as a gentle symbol, a body signal, or a single next step. Show me my capacity to hold peace before you show me the content of the wound.”

[B18] For resistance:

[B19] “What are you protecting me from, and what would help you trust me 1% more?”

[B20] For the inner child:

[B21] “Little one, what do you need me to understand, and what can I do today so you are not alone with it?”

[B22] For the Protector:

[B23] “What real-world action would make this system safer?”

[B24] For the Leader/Guru:

[B25] “What direction serves truth, love, life, and the benefit of all beings?”

[B26] For intuition:

[B27] “Let intuition offer a signal, let analysis examine its meaning, let the body give feedback, and let reality test the interpretation.”

[B28] For separating intuition from conditioning:

[B29] “Wise subconscious, please help me look at this impulse clearly. If part of this signal belongs to family programming, religious fear, marketing, erotic imprinting, political tribe, trauma response, or a childhood bargain for belonging, let that part become visible as conditioning. Let it soften or step back only as much as is safe. Leave behind whatever belongs to true guidance, the universal heart, and a better ordinary life. Let my body feel the difference.”

[B30] For daydreaming:

[B31] “If this daydream is useful, reveal the guidance inside it. If it is avoidance, bring me gently into the present and help me deal with what I need to deal with.”

[B32] For anxiety:

[B33] “What is anxiety trying to protect, and what would help it trust the adult self?”

[B34] For hidden truth:

[B35] “If the full truth would overwhelm me, please do not reveal it all. Show me the capacity before the content.”

[B36] For integration:

[B37] “What ordinary-life action would prove that I heard correctly?”

[B38] The prompt defines the search. NLP language affects how gently the question enters. Used together, they can create a safer field for an answer without pretending the answer is automatically true.

[CHANGED, replaces B3-B14]
Your subconscious mind is NOT a large language model of AI. However, there is a bit of overlap in the way you can ask questions to both. So, if you know how to do prompt engineering for AI, this can be very useful to use it to help you be more present, honest, loving etc. On the other hand, if you use it to be more controlling, it will NOT work.

To get as much precision as possible, you need to include the following in your prompt:

Role: Who or what are you talking to?

Context: Give some context so it knows what you’re talking about.

Intention: What do you want to change or accomplish?

Constraint: What do you need to do to make sure your asking is safe, true, kind etc.

Output: How do you want to receive the answer? As an image, phrase, sensation in your body, memory, action, question, symbol, silence, rest etc.

Evaluation: How are you going to evaluate the answer? How will you know if it’s true, kind, timely, stabilizing, specific, testable etc?

Iteration: Iterate by changing your prompt depending on what you get from reality as feedback.

Most importantly, you always need to make your prompt “safe” otherwise, the analytical part of your mind will “make up” an answer just so it fits with your prompt. I usually add the following to my prompt:

“If you need more time or the best and safest answer is silence, then I ask you to make my body go into a deeper state of comfortable rest. A peaceful silence would be a successful answer to my question.”

There is a very good chance there is no answer for your question because there is nothing happening. It might be that it’s not time yet, or that you need to go rest or do whatever you need to do.

Once you do that, here are the core prompts you can use:

For getting clarity: “I ask you, my wise subconscious mind, to show me what is true, kind and timely. When you want to ask your leader or guru self: “What direction is the most in alignment with truth, love, life and all beings?”

When you want to listen to your intuition: “I ask my intuition to give me a signal, my analytical mind to decipher it, my body to give me feedback as to whether my deciphering is correct and reality to give me feedback as to whether my deciphering is correct.”

When you want to know if a particular intuition you have is coming from your universal heart or from your programming (this one is longer and more complicated): “I ask my wise subconscious mind to help me see if the intuition or impulse I have about …is coming from my universal heart or from my family, my religion, marketing, erotic programming, my political tribe, my trauma response or an old deal I did as a child to belong. If it’s coming from my programming, I ask the parts responsible for it to step back and soften so that I can have true guidance from my universal heart that will help me make my life better in the most simple ways possible. Let my body give me feedback as to the difference.”

When you are daydreaming: “If this daydream has useful guidance for me, I ask my wise subconscious mind to show it to me. If this is my way of not dealing with my life, I ask my wise subconscious mind to help me come back to the present and deal with my life.”

When you are anxious: “What are you protecting me from? How can you trust more my adult self?”

When you suspect there is something true that you don’t know: “If knowing the truth is too much for me, please don’t show me everything. Show me first my capacity to be peaceful, and then, the content of my wounding.”

Once you get an answer, you can ask: “What can I do in my ordinary life to integrate what I have heard?”

The prompt will tell your subconscious what you want. The NLP will make sure you are getting it in a safe way.
