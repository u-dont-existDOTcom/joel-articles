PUBLISHED section:
[B1] Altered-state rule: do not ask for hidden truth while medically unsafe, panicking, sleep-deprived, nauseated, destabilized, manic, psychosis-like, or chemically overwhelmed. First stabilize. Truth that requires destabilization is not ready to be known directly. Ask for capacity, not content.

[B2] Sometimes it feels as though the subconscious knows something the conscious mind can’t yet tolerate. The subject may be sexuality, grief, trauma, family truth, betrayal, shame, vocation, relationship reality, illness fears, spiritual calling, addiction, identity, or moral conflict.

[B3] Interrogation is the dangerous response:

[B4] “Tell me now.” “Reveal everything.” “Stop hiding.” “I demand the truth.”

[B5] That pressure can produce flooding, panic, false certainty, confabulation, dissociation, or a generally worse Tuesday. Flooding can make the adult capacities needed for pausing, comparing options, reality-testing, planning, and choosing much harder to reach. In that state, regulation and support come before deeper dialogue. Silence may be protection when the adult lacks enough regulation, support, humility, or practical capacity to receive the material.

[B6] Ask for the next tolerable piece:

[B7] “Do I need to know the content now, or do I need to build capacity first?”

[B8] “What would make the conscious mind 5% more able to receive this?”

[B9] “Can you show me the truth as a symbol, not a full revelation?”

[B10] “Can you give me the next protective action without revealing the whole story?”

[B11] “Who, if anyone, should help me hold this?”

[B12] A safer script:

[B13] “Wise subconscious, if there is something I cannot yet handle, please do not break it open violently. Help me become someone who can know it safely. Give me preparation before revelation. Give me grounding before memory. Give me support before intensity. Give me one next action.”

[B14] Hypnotic imagery can be meaningful without being literal. The mind symbolizes, compresses, distorts, dramatizes, and borrows from culture and fear. Around trauma and memory especially, treat an image as an image and involve outside support when needed.

REWRITE:
[CHANGED, replaces B1-B14]
NOTE: If it is medically unsafe or you are in a place of panic, sleep deprivation, nausea, deep destabilization, mania, psychosis like symptoms, feeling overwhelmed by chemicals etc. then first focus on stabilizing and then look for what your subconscious needs you to know! If it takes you destabilizing to know what your subconscious knows, it is not meant for you to know directly at this time! Focus on asking for capacity over content!

Sometimes, your subconscious knows something that your conscious self is not prepared to know. It may be something related to your sexuality, grief, trauma, family issues, betrayal, shame, vocation, a relationship, illness fears, spirituality, addiction, identity, moral issues etc.

What you DON’T want to do is bully your subconscious into telling you what it knows by saying things to yourself like:

“What is it you won’t tell me? What are you hiding? Tell me NOW! I am telling you to tell me!”

This often leads to flooding, panic, false certainty, confabulation, dissociation etc. and can make your day SUCK! When you are flooded, you cannot access your adult capacities so focus on being able to regulate and getting support! Sometimes, it is better not to know what is not being said until your adult has the capacities to regulate, get support, be humble, etc. Just ask for what you need to know in a way that you CAN tolerate:

“What do I need to know that will help me get the capacity to know the thing you know that I need to know?” “What can you tell me, that will make my conscious self 5% better at knowing the thing you want to tell me?” “Can you tell me in symbolic form so I am not completely overwhelmed by the revelation?” “Can you tell me what I need to do to be safe, without actually telling me what it is”? “Do I need to know WHAT it is, or do I need to know that I need to get some support to have the capacity to know what it is, and who would be a good person to help me?” etc.

Instead, say to yourself something like this:

“Dear Wise Subconscious Mind, if there is something you know that my conscious self is not ready to know, please don’t break it open violently! Do what you can to make me able to know it in a way that is safe! Prepare me BEFORE you break it open! Ground me BEFORE you show me the memory! Support me BEFORE you make it intense!” etc.

REMEMBER: Not everything in hypnotic imagery is literal, especially if it relates to trauma and memory. The mind makes it symbolic, squashes things together, distorts, dramatizes, pulls from cultural influences, fears etc. So, treat it like an image and get some help outside of yourself!
