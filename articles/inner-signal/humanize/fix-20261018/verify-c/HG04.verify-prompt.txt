Below is one section of a long self-hypnosis guide by Joel, published on his Substack. It teaches gathering attention, listening to the subconscious as an ecology of parts (the inner child, protectors or "guards", the body, intuition), three adult functions he calls the Nurturer, the Protector and the Leader/Guru, Higher Self, "borrowed adulthood", inductions and a complete return, influence literacy ("de-hypnosis": noticing and resisting other people's influence), and safety limits (when to stop, when to get help). Its quoted lines are scripts the reader says or thinks.

PUBLISHED section:
[B0] Quick start: do this first

[B1] Gather attention, notice what is here, offer one fitting response, wait, then come all the way back. You can meet a difficulty or develop love, curiosity, play, or confidence. Nothing dramatic has to happen.

[B2] Before the first session, read the induction and full return below while awake. Set aside time somewhere physically secure. Choose one purpose and what you are leaving alone today. Then put the screen aside; the practice is something you learn to conduct, not a checklist to keep reading.

[B3] As you listen, a sensation, image, phrase, feeling, objection, or silence may become noticeable. Let the next invitation follow that response. Use warmth, a boundary, direction, or more time as needed. Finish with the complete return, then note what happened and any real-world action it calls for.

[B4] When you become panicky, disoriented, increasingly unreal, or less able to function, stop deepening and return to the room and appropriate support. A difficult feeling you freely choose to stay with while present is a different situation.

[B5] Do you usually think in words?

[B6] Before choosing a script, notice how words actually work in your mind. Do silent words or phrases arise often, occasionally, rarely, or hardly at all? They needn’t sound like an audible voice. Does this vary when you plan, remember, reflect, or feel upset? Can you deliberately form a silent sentence, even if words seldom arrive on their own?

[B7] That is different from feeling something before words come in a particular moment. Neither question requires deciding that feelings always come first. “Not sure” is a useful answer; this is a way to choose workable instructions, not a diagnosis.

[B8] Read “say,” “ask,” and “listen” in this guide as invitations, not a requirement for an inner narrator. Speak aloud, write, use a recording, or follow the invitation without rehearsing a sentence. The child and protector needn’t speak in sentences either. Don’t assume an image must appear instead. Try whatever is accessible, and change the route when it stops helping.

[B0, as published] Quick start: do this first

Gather attention, notice, respond, wait, come all the way back. Use these to work a difficulty, or to cultivate love, curiosity, play, confidence, and other qualities. In every session, not much has to happen!

[B5, as published] Do you usually think in words?

[CHANGED, replaces B6]
Before choosing a script, notice: do you talk to yourself in your head -- silent words & phrases -- a lot, some, little, never? Does it vary depending on what you're doing or thinking about? If it doesn't come naturally, can you make yourself think in words, silently? (Thinking in words doesn't mean you hear them.)

[CHANGED, replaces B7]
This doesn't mean that in any given moment you may not notice feelings before words. You don’t have to decide that feelings always come first, and you may not know. This isn't a diagnosis, just a way of finding what's useful. "Not sure" is an answer.

[CHANGED, replaces B8]
If you don't normally think in words, you may want to say things aloud, in writing, on a recording, etc., when this guide says "say." Or, just do what it invites, without preparing what you're going to say. When it says "ask" the child or protector, or "listen" for a response, it doesn't mean they'll be saying full sentences to you. It also doesn't mean you'll get pictures in your head instead. Just do whatever you can, and try different ways as needed.
