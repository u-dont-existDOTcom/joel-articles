Below is one section of a long self-hypnosis guide by Joel, published on his Substack. It teaches gathering attention, listening to the subconscious as an ecology of parts (the inner child, protectors or "guards", the body, intuition), three adult functions he calls the Nurturer, the Protector and the Leader/Guru, Higher Self, "borrowed adulthood", inductions and a complete return, influence literacy ("de-hypnosis": noticing and resisting other people's influence), and safety limits (when to stop, when to get help). Its quoted lines are scripts the reader says or thinks.

PUBLISHED section:
[B0] Troubleshooting

[B1] Nothing happens

[B2] Start smaller:

[B3] “Show me one body sensation.” “Show me one word.” “Show me whether today is for listening or resting.”

[B4] It feels fake

[B5] First ask what feels fake: the wording, the image, the expectation, or the role you’re trying to perform. Not experiencing an inner voice or image isn’t, by itself, resistance or a missing adult capacity. Correct an explicitly unwanted invitation. If you recognize a protective part, you can talk to it:

[B6] “What are you protecting me from?” “Are you afraid I will lie to myself again?” “Are you afraid this is performance?”

[B7] The voice may be the inherited parent enforcing an old belonging rule. Don’t argue with it. Ask which rule it is enforcing and what ordinary evidence would make the new adult voice more believable.

[B8] The fake feeling can also mean that a needed adult capacity isn’t available yet. Stop demanding a convincing voice. If you can already observe what is happening, borrow the particular warmth, boundary, or direction you lack. A real or imagined caring figure can help. Try one believable sentence or action and see how it is received.

[B9] The subconscious will not answer

[B10] There may be no answer, or the question may not help. You can change it, rest, or finish. If a concern about trust is actually present, ask:

[B11] “What do you need to know before you trust me?”

[B12] The reply may be extremely un-mystical: “Will you set the boundary?” “Will you keep the promise?” “Will you stop abandoning us?” “Will you stop substituting insight for action?”

[B13] The inner child will not appear

[B14] If a guard is recognizable, speak to it; otherwise stay with the feeling or preference that is actually present.

[B15] “You do not have to let me in. Just tell me what would make the doorway safer.”

[B16] If no clear child-state appears, use the session to notice one preference, curiosity, or beginner experiment instead of forcing an image. Identity-building and reparenting can happen in the same practice.

[B17] You get anxious

[B18] Notice whether the anxiety is manageable and you want to stay with it, or whether you need less contact. When it is escalating or overwhelming, stop searching for content and help the body first.

[B19] Feet. Chair. Breath. Room.

[B20] “Little one, I am here. Protector, thank you for warning me. You do not have to scream for me to listen. If there is real action needed, show me one step. If there is no action needed, help the body stand down gently.”

[B21] Get professional help when anxiety includes chest pain, fainting, suicidal thoughts, psychosis-like or mania-like symptoms, severe dissociation, inability to sleep, or urges to harm yourself or someone else. Appendix D has the longer anxiety toolbox.

[B22] You dissociate or feel unreal

[B23] Open your eyes. Move. Touch something textured. Name five objects. End the deeper practice for the day.

[B24] You become grandiose

[B25] Return to food, sleep, chores, humility, and somebody who will disagree with you.

[B26] “If this insight is real, it will survive sleep, food, time, humility, and feedback.”

[B27] You become too analytical

[B28] When the practice turns into a technical performance, stop improving it. Put one hand on the heart and one on the solar plexus.

[B29] “I am not here to do this perfectly. I am here to be in relationship with what is deeper than my surface mind.”

[B30] “What would make this feel more like communion and less like performance?”

[B31] Silence may be the answer. Rest may be the answer. One honest action may be the answer. Take the answer that actually arrived.

[B32] You become too blissful or diffuse

[B33] Ask the bliss to become specific.

[B34] “What task, boundary, meal, rest, repair, or conversation does love require next?”

[B35] You want the hidden truth immediately

[B36] Don’t pry.

[B37] “Show me the capacity before the content.”

[B38] You keep daydreaming

[B39] Sit up and open your eyes.

[B40] “What real action is this fantasy replacing?”

[B41] You get stuck in spiritual bypass

[B42] “Where has divine love not yet descended into the wound?”

[B43] A genuine opening can show you a depth of love or freedom you hadn’t known was possible. Let that matter without demanding the child receive it on command. Prayer can also evoke abandonment or condemnation, or a teacher’s betrayal can damage something sacred. Ask what was lost and what needs questioning; more of the same practice isn’t automatically the answer.

[B44] Sometimes practical care helps while the available horizon of life still feels too small. Curiosity about deeper happiness is worth following without pretending to believe an explanation. Other people already have enough love and meaning for the life they want. Spirituality doesn’t need to become another assignment.

[B45] You get stuck in endless processing

[B46] A real wound and a checking loop can coexist. Ask whether this round helps you feel, understand, decide, or respond differently—or whether you’re checking the same question for reassurance. Grief and practice can revisit familiar ground; novelty isn’t required every time.

[B47] When it is a checking loop, let the thought remain without arguing with it or chasing it away, and return to a chosen activity or relationship. You don’t have to hold an inner meeting every time a thought appears or complete a ritual before you’re allowed to live.

[B48] “What direction would the Leader choose now?”

[B49] You keep using hypnosis but not changing life

[B50] “What would prove to the inner child that the adult is real?”

[B51] Then do the thing.
