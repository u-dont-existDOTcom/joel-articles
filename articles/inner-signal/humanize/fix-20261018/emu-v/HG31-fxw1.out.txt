Appendix B - NLP (Neuro-Linguistic Programming)

You do not need to know this stuff to do it but if you want to freshen up on your skills in the art of working with imagery, body states, behavioural rehearsal, talking to yourself in effective sentences etc then it might be useful. Using the techniques can be an easy way to fall into the trap of thinking that NLP is a bag of tricks rather than something that needs to be done with love and truth and contact and consent.

The four levels of NLP are:

Sensory Level of NLP: working with the form of an inner experience (where do you see or hear it, how far away, how big, how bright, how loud, how do you hold your body, where is the pressure etc.)

State Level of NLP: accessing states of warmth, adultness, etc

Strategy level of NLP: practising a better response.

Language Level of NLP: pacing what is true; opening up people’s awareness; presuming you can do something safe; linking cause and effect; embedding invitations; asking de-hypnotic questions to break the effects of vague, absolutes, manipulative or grandiose language.

The Milton Model (how to talk to yourself without it being like a covert sales seminar)

What’s the problem? When you say “ Relax now” or “Stop being anxious” or “Trust me” there is often a part of me that wants to argue with you.

How do you get past the part that wants to argue with you without overpowering it so you can get to the place you want to get to?

You start with something you know to be true, you take the pressure off, you presume only a small safe capacity and you invite just one movement. E.g. “ As the body notices the bed beneath it it may become a little easier to stop holding the whole day alone.”

Notice that the sentence: Paces what is already true; Opens up awareness; Validates the function of what you want to change; Presupposes (or takes for granted) a capacity for change; Bridges the two ideas together; Invites only one movement; Tests whether it has happened or not.

It follows the pattern of pacing and leading.

An example pacing and leading with anxiety: “ It is completely true that the sensation of anxiety is here right now. It is completely true that the body has activated to meet this anxiety. It is completely true that there is a protector that wants to do this for you. It is completely true that the adult part of you can start to listen, one breath at a time.”

So the pattern is Truth, Invite, Truth, Invite etc.

An example script: “ It is completely true that the sensation of _______ (or the emotion of _____) is coming up for me right now. It makes complete sense that my system is reacting by doing/thinking ________. While that is completely true, there is also another completely true thing that my adult self is here, breathing in this room right now, and willing to listen to what comes up next.

Awareness’ verbs: notice, realize, recognize, sense, feel, wonder, imagine, picture, listen, allow, discover, remember, consider . E.g. “ Notice what your body notices…”

Presuppositions - Be very careful how you use presuppositions. They should only ever be used to presume that you have chosen, or that you will choose, or that you will have chosen to do something. Or to presume that you will, can, are able to do something safe like get in touch with a part, or be in your adult self. Or to presume that there is love, or that the adult can be in charge etc. Never use it for anything else. E.g. “ Before you change you will have understood why you were doing it in the first place”
