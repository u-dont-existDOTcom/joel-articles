Appendix B: NLP without turning yourself into a sales seminar

You don’t have to know the terms to practice the core method. If you want to brush up on the art of imagery, bodily states, how to effectively rehearse new behaviors, how to choose more effective sentences to say to yourself, etc., then you might want to read this appendix. NLP is useful when it helps the system listen, but it’s easy to turn it into a bag of tricks and forget that love, truth, contact, and consent are essential.

The four NLP layers

First, it works on a Sensory Level of NLP: changing the form of an inner experience, where people see or hear an image, the distance or size or brightness, the volume of a sound, posture, pressure.

Second, it works on a State Level of NLP: helping them to access warmth, or a feeling of adult-ness, or courage, or steadiness, or love, or clarity.

Third, it works on a Strategy level of NLP: helping people to practice a better response: when this cue appears, the adult does this.

Fourth, it works on a Language Level of NLP: pacing what is already true, opening up people’s awareness, presuming only their ability to do something safe, linking cause and effect carefully, embedding invitations, or asking de-hypnotic questions to break the effects of vague, absolutes, manipulative, or grandiose language.

The Milton Model, minus the covert sales seminar

It's called the Milton Model and has to do with the architecture of the sentence. When I say "Relax now", or "Stop being anxious", or even "Trust me" a part of me often braces against it.

The idea is to use indirect language to get past the part that wants to argue with me without having to overpower it, and get to the place I want to go to.

To do that, I start with something I know to be true, I take the pressure off, presume only a small safe capacity and invite just one movement:

"As the body notices the bed beneath it, it may become a little easier to stop holding the whole day alone."

That way I pace reality, I open up awareness, I validate the function of the reaction, I presuppose capacity, I use a bridge to make the link easier, I invite only one small movement, and I have a way of testing with the body if it's happening.

Pattern 1: Pacing and leading — truth before suggestion

What is it looks like to pace and lead? It means stating what is already true before inviting anything new.

Here is an example of pacing and leading for anxiety: “Anxiety is here. The body is activated. A protector is trying to help. And one breath at a time, the adult can start to listen.”

It can be as simple as: Truth, Invite, Truth, Invite etc.

“It is completely true that the sensation of _______ (or emotion of _____) is coming up for me right now. It makes complete sense that my system is reacting by doing/thinking ________. While that is completely true, another completely true thing is that my adult self is here, breathing in this room right now, and willing to listen to whatever comes up next.”

Pattern 2: Awareness verbs

‘Awareness’ verbs point attention without yelling at it: notice, realize, recognize, sense, feel, wonder, imagine, picture, listen, allow, discover, remember, consider

“Notice whether the answer comes as an image, a word, a pressure, a warmth, a silence, or a next action.”

Pattern 3: Presuppositions

Caution: The powerful technique of presupposition (embedding a statement as if it were true) should be used only to presume choice, when it will occur, ability, safety, love, and adult leadership. Never use presupposition to smuggle in obedience.

“Before any part has to change, it can first become more understood.”

Pattern 4: Gentle cause-effect bridges

Be careful with how language link events. Words like “as, while, because, when, after, before, which means” etc are all words that can be used to link events. A gentle voice doesn’t make a false causal claim true.

In general, the problem with the first example (“Because I breathe, the trauma leaves.”) is that it’s too strong. It’s unlikely that simply breathing will get rid of the trauma. The second example is better because it’s more accurate: “As I breathe, the adult self may become present enough to hear one small piece of what the wound needs.”
