PUBLISHED section:
[B1] For sleep hypnosis, use Nimja’s sleep playlist.

[B3] Preview the sequence, choose one purpose, and decide whether you want to begin. Each family can be light, eyes-open, or replaced by no-trance contact. Notice the actual response—including no change—and let it determine the next move. Move, imagine, change the sensory channel, remain still, or stop. Use the complete waking return after every induction.

[B4] If vivid images don’t come, translate the metaphor into words, rhythm, spatial sense, touch, movement, or simple knowing. The mind doesn’t owe anybody a movie.

[B6] Feel the support beneath you and the position of one hand or foot. Press gently, stretch slightly, and release—or imagine it. Notice ease, tension, warmth, breath, or no change. Repeat with no more than three areas, letting “release” guide the sequence. Which area or inner voice wants attention? What support is needed? Restore ordinary movement and return.

[B8] Notice where your weight is carried. Let support carry 1% more effort, as if floating without losing contact, or keep ordinary weight and pressure. Notice the response. You may deliberately alternate attention toward ordinary awareness on the inhale and support on the exhale; name this fractionation beforehand. What becomes easier to hear? Keep the support, regain chosen weight and firm contact, then return.

[B10] Accompany a natural rhythm: breath, a comfortable hum, a finger tap, outside sound, or silent counting. No rhythm is also an option. Repeat “here, and here again,” noticing where attention lands between repetitions. Stop the deliberate rhythm and notice the first quiet beat, without trying to become blank. Is a word, feeling, need, or action clearer? Let ordinary rhythm resume and return.

[B12] Notice a concern with several strands. Imagine yarn unwinding, bubbles settling, or knots placed visibly beside you. Leave the object unchanged or label it in words when that fits better. Count only strands you actually set down; nothing is erased. Which needs warmth, protection, or action? Keep one useful strand, give the others a visible holding place, and return.

[B14] Hear the actual room and feel the body before imagining a step toward a chosen destination. Use footsteps, textures, or landmarks to gather attention. Change scenery or skip directly to dialogue when useful. Notice a detail without assigning hidden meaning; the mystery is how the scene develops, not what will be done to you. Who or what needs to speak? Return by the same route and close the imagined space.

[B16] Start with a small neutral area and what is already there. Imagine warmth, steadiness, or attention spreading to the next comfortable area, or keep it localized. Pause at each step and notice what changes without claiming medical repair. Does the body want contact, space, words, or ordinary care? Let the image recede, feel the whole body and real contact points, and return.

[CHANGED, replaces B3-B4]
Practise! Work out in advance which one you want to do and what you want to use it for (pick just one) and whether or not you want to do it at all! Any of these ‘families’ of induction can be done as light inductions, with eyes open, or instead of trance you can just do them as a contact. Once you start an induction, you will want to notice what’s going on (even if nothing changes) and work out what to do next, move, imagine something else, change the sensory channel you’re using, do nothing, stop the induction sequence and do something else… . Whatever you do, make sure you bring yourself back to waking at the end of an induction.

NB: if you can’t ‘see’ the images you’re trying to visualise, that’s fine, change them into words, rhythm, spatial relations, something to touch or move or just plain ‘knowing’… .

[CHANGED, replaces B6]
Stretch and Release: Work out what will be supporting you, focus on the feeling of support. You can also focus on where one of your hands or feet is. Either actually, or in your imagination, stretch a little bit, either stretching that part of your body slightly or pressing down into the support, and then let go of the effort, just relax. See if there is any difference, even if there isn’t. Do this for no more than 3 places on your body. Think of it as ‘releasing’ as you go through the sequence. Which bit of your body, or voice, needs this? What do you need to support it? Bring your body back to normal movement and return.

[CHANGED, replaces B8]
Supported Floating: Work out what is supporting you and where your weight is, imagine that the support is holding a tiny bit (1%) more of your effort. It’s like floating but you don’t have to lose contact, or just keep it at the same level, just enough weight/pressure. See what happens, you can fractionate this if you want, perhaps noticing your normal awareness when you breathe in and the support when you breathe out. Tell yourself you are going to do this before you do it. What can you hear more easily now? Bring yourself back to normal feeling of support and return.

[CHANGED, replaces B10]
Rhythmic: Use your breathing or slightly hum in your throat to make a natural rhythm, or tap your finger or listen to sounds outside to make an artificial one. You can even do it by counting to yourself (silently) or with no rhythm at all. Tell yourself you are going to say “here, and here again” over and over, and notice what you pay attention to in between the phrases, then stop saying it. Notice what you become aware of in the first beat – you don’t need to make your mind blank, just notice what comes up. Does it make any words, feelings, needs or actions more clear? Bring back your normal rhythm and return.

[CHANGED, replaces B12]
Unwinding Object: Good for when you have a few different strands to one thing you want to work on. Imagine an object unwinding – maybe some yarn, some bubbles, or some knots that you place beside you. Once you’ve placed them all, give yourself a minute to look at them all, you don’t have to change anything, but you can label them if you need to (just a word or two). How many did you put down? Which one do you need to work on, what does it need – warmth, protection, action? Keep one strand in your attention and put the others somewhere you can see them and return.

[CHANGED, replaces B14]
Sensory Journey: Pay attention to the sounds in the room you’re in and how your body feels, then imagine yourself walking somewhere. Where do you want to go? Listen to the sounds of your footsteps and take in your surroundings as you walk, what do objects feel like if you touch them, what landmarks do you see? When you feel you have enough of your attention, imagine the scenery changing until you are in a place where you can talk to whoever you need to (or you can just cut to the chase, if you prefer). Pay attention to something without worrying about what it means. Allow yourself to be curious about what’s going to happen next, but don’t make it a “what are they going to do to you?” kind of thing. When you are ready to finish, imagine yourself walking back the way you came and close the space.

[CHANGED, replaces B16]
Gradual wave: start with a very small neutral area, feel into it a little and then imagine a wave spreading out to a new area that is still ok. Sense into the new area (or continue using the old). See if there is anything that could use medical attention, and if you imagine it being fixed, what is different afterwards? Does the body want any contact, space, words, other care? Let the mental image go, sense your body, sense your contact points, come back.
