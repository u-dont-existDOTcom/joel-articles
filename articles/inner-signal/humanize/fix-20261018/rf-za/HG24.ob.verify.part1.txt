PUBLISHED section:
[B2] Start smaller:

[B3] “Show me one body sensation.” “Show me one word.” “Show me whether today is for listening or resting.”

[B5] First ask what feels fake: the wording, the image, the expectation, or the role you’re trying to perform. Not experiencing an inner voice or image isn’t, by itself, resistance or a missing adult capacity. Correct an explicitly unwanted invitation. If you recognize a protective part, you can talk to it:

[B6] “What are you protecting me from?” “Are you afraid I will lie to myself again?” “Are you afraid this is performance?”

[B7] The voice may be the inherited parent enforcing an old belonging rule. Don’t argue with it. Ask which rule it is enforcing and what ordinary evidence would make the new adult voice more believable.

[B8] The fake feeling can also mean that a needed adult capacity isn’t available yet. Stop demanding a convincing voice. If you can already observe what is happening, borrow the particular warmth, boundary, or direction you lack. A real or imagined caring figure can help. Try one believable sentence or action and see how it is received.
