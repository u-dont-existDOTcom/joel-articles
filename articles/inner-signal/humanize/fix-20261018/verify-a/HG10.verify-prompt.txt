PUBLISHED section:
[B0] Hypnosis Evidence

[B1] Hypnosis has real evidence, although it doesn’t have magical evidence. A 2024 overview of 49 meta-analyses, covering 261 distinct primary studies, found the strongest support in pain, medical procedures, and work with children and adolescents. The quality still varied by outcome and study design.

[B2] Self-hypnosis has its own evidence. A systematic review of randomized controlled trials reported benefits in studies involving pain, childbirth, pediatric applications, stress, and anxiety. It appeared more useful when people learned it as a real self-directed skill and practiced it several times, rather than merely pressing play on an audio file.

[B3] That distinction matters here. I want you to learn an inner skill, not become a consumer of increasingly elaborate trance recordings.

[B4] Clinical caution still matters. Mayo Clinic warns that hypnosis used around stressful earlier-life events can trigger strong emotional reactions, and that other treatments may fit historical trauma work better.

[B5] Use self-hypnosis for regulation, dialogue, reparenting, clarity, habit change, present safety, emotional integration, and ordinary-life action.

[B6] Do not use it to force trauma memories, prove what happened, suppress medical symptoms, destabilize yourself, or replace appropriate clinical care.
