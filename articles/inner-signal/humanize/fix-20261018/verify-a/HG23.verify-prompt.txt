[B0] Capacity before content

[B1] Altered-state rule: do not ask for hidden truth while medically unsafe, panicking, sleep-deprived, nauseated, destabilized, manic, psychosis-like, or chemically overwhelmed. First stabilize. Truth that requires destabilization is not ready to be known directly. Ask for capacity, not content.

[B2] Sometimes it feels as though the subconscious knows something the conscious mind can’t yet tolerate. The subject may be sexuality, grief, trauma, family truth, betrayal, shame, vocation, relationship reality, illness fears, spiritual calling, addiction, identity, or moral conflict.

[B3] Interrogation is the dangerous response:

[B4] “Tell me now.” “Reveal everything.” “Stop hiding.” “I demand the truth.”

[B5] That pressure can produce flooding, panic, false certainty, confabulation, dissociation, or a generally worse Tuesday. Flooding can make the adult capacities needed for pausing, comparing options, reality-testing, planning, and choosing much harder to reach. In that state, regulation and support come before deeper dialogue. Silence may be protection when the adult lacks enough regulation, support, humility, or practical capacity to receive the material.

[B6] Ask for the next tolerable piece:

[B7] “Do I need to know the content now, or do I need to build capacity first?”

[B8] “What would make the conscious mind 5% more able to receive this?”

[B9] “Can you show me the truth as a symbol, not a full revelation?”

[B10] “Can you give me the next protective action without revealing the whole story?”

[B11] “Who, if anyone, should help me hold this?”

[B12] A safer script:

[B13] “Wise subconscious, if there is something I cannot yet handle, please do not break it open violently. Help me become someone who can know it safely. Give me preparation before revelation. Give me grounding before memory. Give me support before intensity. Give me one next action.”

[B14] Hypnotic imagery can be meaningful without being literal. The mind symbolizes, compresses, distorts, dramatizes, and borrows from culture and fear. Around trauma and memory especially, treat an image as an image and involve outside support when needed.

[B0, as published] Capacity before content

[CHANGED, replaces B1-B14]
NOTE: If it is medically unsafe for you to do this, or you are panicking, sleep deprived, nauseated, deeply destabilized, manic, in a psychosis-like state, or overwhelmed by chemicals, please stabilize first before seeking out what your subconscious needs you to know. It is not ready for you to know it if it requires you to destabilize to know it! Ask for capacity, not content.

There are times when your subconscious seems to know something that your conscious mind can’t handle yet. It may be about your sexuality, a grief, trauma, your family, betrayal, shame, vocation, a relationship, fears about illness, spirituality, an addiction, your identity, or a moral issue.

What you don’t want to do is bully your subconscious with statements like,

“Why don’t you just tell me?” “What are you hiding?” “Tell me right now!” or “I demand that you reveal the truth!”

This can lead to flooding, panic, false certainty, confabulation, dissociation, and just generally making your day suck more. When you’re flooded, it can be hard to access your adult capacities to pause, choose, reality test, plan and make choices for yourself. In that state, regulation and support come before going deeper. The silence may be protecting you while your adult doesn’t have enough regulation, support, humility or practical capacity to take it in. But when your adult does have enough regulation, support, humility, and other capacities, you can talk with your subconscious.

“Do I need to know the content now, or do I need to build capacity first?” “What do you need for my conscious mind to be able to know what you’re saying 5% better?” “Can you tell me through a symbol?” “Can you tell me what you need me to do to be safe without telling me what it is?” “Do you need me to get support to be able to know what it is? Who?”

You may even want to say, “Dear wise subconscious mind, if there is something you know that my conscious mind can’t handle, please don’t just break it open in a way that isn’t good for me. Help me become someone who can know it safely. Prepare me before you reveal it, ground me before the memory, support me before the intensity. Give me one next thing to do.”

REMEMBER: Hypnotic imagery can mean something without being literal, especially around trauma and memory. The mind turns things into symbols, squashes them together, distorts and dramatizes them, and borrows from culture and fear. Treat an image as an image, and get outside support when you need it.
