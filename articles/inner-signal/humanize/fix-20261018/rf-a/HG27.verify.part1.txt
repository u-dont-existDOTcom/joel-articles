It teaches gathering attention, listening to the subconscious as an ecology of parts (the inner child, protectors or "guards", the body, intuition), three adult functions he calls the Nurturer, the Protector and the Leader/Guru, Higher Self, "borrowed adulthood", inductions and a complete return, influence literacy ("de-hypnosis": noticing and resisting other people's influence), and safety limits (when to stop, when to get help). PUBLISHED section:
[B1] The subconscious isn’t the enemy. Neither are the body, the sharp analytical mind, or the protector that has been braced for years. Most of them have been trying to help without ever being properly introduced.

[B2] I’m less interested in how deeply you can get hypnotized than whether you become an adult you can trust.

[B3] Listen until the kid is willing to come closer. Keep enough small promises that the guards can occasionally sit down. Love yourself enough that thought stops bracing against tenderness, and think clearly enough that love doesn’t drift into fantasy. Then take the practice back into ordinary life, where the adult keeps the promise.

REWRITE:
[CHANGED, replaces B1]
Start from the basis that the sub, the body, the sharp mind, and the guy who's tensed up and ready for the other shoe to drop for a long time are all not your enemies. Most of them are actually trying to help you.

[CHANGED, replaces B2]
Whether or not you can "get hypnotized" very deeply is somewhat irrelevant to whether or not you can become a trustworthy adult.

[CHANGED, replaces B3]
Listen to it for as long as it takes until it's comfortable enough to move closer to you. This is easiest to do if you're making and keeping lots of small promises to the kid, so the various guards feel they can relax. Work on loving yourself in a way that your thinking doesn't object to; work on thinking in a way that your love for yourself doesn't become fantasy; work on doing this in the course of regular life so that your adult self can keep the promise.
