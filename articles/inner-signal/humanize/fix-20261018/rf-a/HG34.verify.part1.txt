PUBLISHED section:
[B1] Daydreaming can be intuition, simulation, grief digestion, creative rehearsal, symbolic teaching, or the subconscious showing a possible future, fear, or desire. It can also become avoidance, fantasy addiction, dissociation, romantic obsession, or an escape hatch from adult life.

[B2] Does the daydream return me to life with more clarity, love, and action? Or does it pull me away from life into repetition, craving, and avoidance?

[B3] When it seems useful, ask:

[B4] “What is the guidance inside this?”

[B5] When it seems to be pulling you away, change the body before debating the story. Sit upright. Open the eyes a little. Feel the feet.

[B6] “Subconscious, thank you for showing me images. If this daydream is useful, please reveal the guidance inside it. If it is avoidance, please bring me gently into the present and help me deal with what I need to deal with in a gentle but structured way.”

[B7] Then get embarrassingly practical:

[B8] “What real-life action is this daydream replacing?”

[B9] The answer may be: send the message, stop sending the message, clean the room, eat, sleep, grieve, create, apologize, leave, tell the truth, make the plan, ask for help, return to the task, or stop chasing unavailable love.

REWRITE:
[CHANGED, replaces B1-B9]
Sometimes daydreaming is something that your subconscious mind is doing to help you. It is simulating an idea or event to help you digest grief or teach you something. The very act of daydreaming may be helping you to access your creativity or allowing your subconscious to show you a potential future. Sometimes your subconcious will show you things that you may be afraid of. Sometimes it will show you what you want.

Other times daydreaming may be taking you away from your life in a detrimental way. It may be that you are escaping reality and are addicted to fantasies rather than the real life. You may be dissociating or obsessing about romantic partners or escaping from being an adult.

How do you know which it is? Simply ask yourself if the daydreaming is taking you closer to life, with more clarity, love and ability to take action or if it is taking you away from life, in a repetitive and craving way that takes you away from living your life.

If it is helping you then simply ask yourself what the guidance is and how you can use it.

If it is taking you away from life then change your body position, open your eyes and ask your subconscious to help you. Simply say to yourself, “Dear subconscious if what you are showing me is helpful then please also show me the helpfulness in it. If what you are showing me is a way of avoiding dealing with life then please show me in a gentle but structured way how to deal with life as it is right now.”

Then ask yourself what it is that you need to do in “real” life that you are avoiding by daydreaming.

You may need to send a text message or stop sending text messages. You may need to clean up your room, eat, sleep, deal with grief, create, apologize, leave, tell the truth, make a plan, get help, get back to a task that you were supposed to be doing, stop chasing love that is not available to you etc.
