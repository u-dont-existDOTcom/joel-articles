PUBLISHED section:
[B15] Letting a younger feeling-state speak doesn’t mean handing adult judgment to an outside caretaker or trying to become literally younger. Wonder, play, vivid sensation, vulnerability, honest need, or the wish to carry fewer decisions can all come forward while the internal adult remains present.

[B19] When love is available, the younger state doesn’t have to believe in it before you can offer it. Trust can question your promises, remember failures of protection, and take its time. Love can remain present while that happens.

[B20] “Okay. I believe that you don’t believe me. I still love you. You don’t have to love me back. Tell me what feels unsafe.”

[B21] That isn’t a way to end the complaint. Listen for what is true in it and answer seriously. Hearing anger without retaliation, admitting a real failure, and repairing after an internal attack are evidence alongside meals, boundaries, appointments, and kept promises. Non-retaliation expresses love; it shouldn’t replace love with a colder performance of good behavior.

[B22] Notice what each position teaches the others. A vow followed by blame when it is doubted teaches that love depends on receiving it correctly. The blaming voice and the voice making the vow may be different positions, the same position, or a blend. Don’t decide from the person’s chronological age.

[B23] When a younger version of you becomes the defendant, ask what they understood then, what options they could see and tolerate, and what happened because of the choice. Harm can require responsibility and repair. Punishment cannot retroactively create the capacity that was missing.

[B25] Place one hand at the center of the chest as a physical connection to love. Put the other at the solar plexus, just beneath the chest, where you can relate to the vulnerable child.

[B26] Send warmth from the heart toward the child. Notice whatever comes back—a sensation, image, feeling, resistance, or nothing—and answer with warmth again. Let it become a conversation rather than a performance.

[B27] Love above. Child below. Adult present.

[B29] The Nurturer brings warmth without trying to rush the feeling away, and takes pleasure in the child’s company: “I’m glad you’re here.” The child needs to be enjoyed, not only comforted when hurt.

[B30] “It’s okay to feel this.” “I’m here.” “You don’t have to do anything right now.” “You are not too much.” “You can be small here.” “You can be angry here.” “You can be sad here.” “I will stay.”

REWRITE:
[CHANGED, replaces B15]
It’s possible to let the ‘younger’ parts of you express themselves without surrendering your adult judgement to them, and without literally reverting to a younger age. Instead, the aim is to access the positive aspects of ‘younger’ feeling states, such as a sense of wonder or playfulness, more direct sensory experiences, vulnerability, need, and a desire to let someone else make decisions for you.

[CHANGED, replaces B19-B23]
Even if your inner child does not believe that you love him or her, you can still love him or her.

You can say, out loud or in your head, “Okay. I believe that you don’t believe me, but I still love you. You don’t have to love me back. Please tell me what is not okay with you.”

Respond to complaints, which are likely to include something true. Showing that you love him or her can include some or all of the following: listening to him or her when he or she is angry without hitting back, owning up to what was not okay, working on being less likely to turn on him or her, and other ways of making it up to him or her. Other ways of showing that you love him or her include taking care of him or her by buying and cooking food, setting limits, taking him or her to doctor’s appointments, and doing what you say you will do. Just not hitting back is not enough; you also need to love your inner child.

Be aware of what you are teaching your other positions. You might be teaching your inner child that you are going to love him or her no matter what, but you might also be teaching him or her that he or she is responsible for believing your love, and that if he or she does not, it is his or her fault. The position that is making the vow to love the inner child no matter what, and the position that is blaming the inner child for not taking in the love, might be separate positions, or they might be one position, or you might be blending them. It might not be based on chronological age.

When you're placing your younger self on trial, how valuable is it for you to consider how valuable it might have been for your younger self at the time to be doing what they did, given what they knew at the time? How valuable is it for you to consider how valuable it might have been for them to think what they thought, given what they thought at the time? How valuable is it for you to consider what options were available to them at the time, and how tolerable they were? How valuable is it for you to consider the consequences of their actions at the time? How valuable is it for you to consider that whilst their actions might have caused harm to you and others, punishing yourself now will not give them the capacity they needed at the time, and that it is more useful to work on what you can do now to deal with the harm that was caused?

WORKING WITH YOUR INNER CHILD:

[CHANGED, replaces B25-B27]
There is a simple and easy body technique to connect with your inner child. Just place one hand in the center of your chest which is physically connecting you to your love and then place your other hand just below that which is your solar plexis and where your child can be.

Begin to send love from your heart down to your child and just notice what comes up for you. It doesn't matter if something comes up or not. If you feel resistance, just meet it with love. If you feel or see something, just meet that with love as well. This is a conversation with your child from your heart.

Remember, Love is on top, your child is on the bottom and you are there with them.

THE NURTURER: As a Nurturer one of the most important things that you can do is to enjoy your inner child, to sit back and not feel like you have to move their feelings and to say to them that you are really glad that they are here in this moment. Children need to be enjoyed.

It’s okay that you’re feeling the way you are feeling.

I want you to know I’m here for you.

You don’t need to do anything right now.

It’s okay that you’re not too much. It’s okay to be small. You can be small here.

It’s okay to be angry. You can be angry here.

It’s okay to be sad. You can be sad here.
