It teaches gathering attention, listening to the subconscious as an ecology of parts (the inner child, protectors or "guards", the body, intuition), three adult functions he calls the Nurturer, the Protector and the Leader/Guru, Higher Self, "borrowed adulthood", inductions and a complete return, influence literacy ("de-hypnosis": noticing and resisting other people's influence), and safety limits (when to stop, when to get help). Its quoted lines are scripts the reader says or thinks.

PUBLISHED section:
[B1] Once or twice a week, give the same practice more time when that is useful. More time needn’t mean greater intensity. Start from Higher Self or neutral witnessing, follow what appears, borrow an adult function when needed, and ask whether anything important is being bypassed. Leave room for the full exit and any ordinary action needed afterward.

[B2] Do the ordinary action immediately when possible. That teaches the subconscious that the adult heard and acted, instead of staging another beautiful internal meeting and then leaving the dishes exactly where they were.

REWRITE:
[CHANGED, replaces B1]
It’s good to do this once or twice a week, though more is fine. It doesn’t have to be very intense but it is good to start from Higher Self or at least a neutral witnessing place. It’s also good to bring in some adult function to make sure nothing is being bypassed and so that at the end of the exercise, when the experience is over, there is the possibility of coming out completely and doing whatever normal thing needs doing.

[CHANGED, replaces B2]
If possible, it’s good to do the normal thing immediately after the exercise, so that the subconscious sees that the adult’s actions have been heard. Otherwise, the dishes won’t get done.
