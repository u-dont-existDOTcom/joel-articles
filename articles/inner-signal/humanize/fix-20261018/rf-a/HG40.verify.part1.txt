PUBLISHED section:
[B1] The personal accounts and the way I arrange this practice are mine. The revised teaching brings them together with body-oriented and hypnotic approaches described below. That combination is an adaptation, not a treatment validated as a whole. These notes are for readers who want to follow an idea further; you don’t need them open while practicing.

[B3] Maggie Phillips and Claire Frederick, Healing the Divided Self (1995), especially pp.24–27, 45–50, and 83–90, discusses interactive hypnosis, sensory learning, self-hypnosis, and a definite return. It is a professional clinical book, not a self-help manual; its case procedures and historical claims shouldn’t be imported wholesale into solo practice. Adam Eason and Benjamin Parris’s ISH article on self-hypnosis discusses self-directed learning and its relationship to another hypnotist and recordings.

[B5] Phillips and Frederick, pp.53–57 and 96–100, explain pacing, utilization, metaphor, and associational cues. Nimja’s hypnotic-language tutorial and trigger tutorial describe language and cueing techniques. They are technique sources, not evidence that every NLP claim is established or every example suits trauma work. The original wording and deliberately chosen re-entry sequence here are adaptations; Nimja did not write or endorse this guide.

[B7] Peter Levine, In an Unspoken Voice (2010), chapters 5 and 7; Levine and Maggie Phillips, Freedom from Pain (2012), chapters 3–4; and Levine, Healing Trauma, chapter 4, inform bodily tracking, resources, titration, pendulation, and attention across sensation, imagery, behavior, emotion, and meaning. The invitation to try a movement or image is not a claim that a particular physiological repair has occurred.

[B9] The companion Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide gives the fuller treatment of borrowed adulthood, love without compulsory trust, practical and relational repair, spiritual struggle, differentiation, and mutual peer support. The corresponding practices here follow that account while retaining the self-hypnosis entry, responsive language, and complete return.

[B10] Healing the Divided Self, pp.148–153 and 171–174, discusses renurturing, somatic work, and developing nurturing capacities. Levine and Maggie Kline’s Trauma Through a Child’s Eyes, especially chapters 4–5 and 10, discusses attunement and individual responses in actual children and caregiving. Applying those ideas to an adult’s younger feeling-states is an adaptation. My three-adult arrangement and borrowed-adulthood sequence are not presented as those authors’ tested protocol.

[B12] Nedergaard and Lupyan’s Not Everybody Has an Inner Voice (2024) studied people reporting different amounts of inner speech, not self-hypnosis adaptations. The questions in Quick start are a practical self-check, not a validated screening instrument or a test of hypnotic ability.

[B14] Levine’s Trauma and Memory (2015), chapter 7, is useful on the difference between compelling experience and historical verification. The VA’s hypnosis overview includes cautions about strong reactions and false memories. ASCH’s explanation of certification distinguishes professional preparation from a guarantee of someone’s competence. The existing links in the evidence and specialist sections point to their particular claims rather than validating every use of hypnosis.

Learning to learn, how to get in and out

Healing the Divided Self , Maggie Phillips and Claire Frederick, (1995) pp 24-27, 45-50, 83-90, covers a lot of aspects of this post, such as interactive hypnosis, helping the subject learn how to learn by using other senses, learning self hypnosis, making sure the subject comes back at the end, etc. However, this is a professional clinical book, not a self help book, so be careful about trying to use the procedures used with the cases, etc in this book for solo work (the same applies to historical information it contains). Also, Adam Eason's work with Benjamin Parris in the International Society of Hypnosis, as well as his article about self hypnosis , should be a useful thing to read (especially the parts that talk about how to learn to learn when you're directing it yourself, and about whether you need another person to help you, and using recordings to help you, etc.)
