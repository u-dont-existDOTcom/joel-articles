PUBLISHED section:
[B32] The Protector turns warmth into something the child can believe.

[B33] “I will set the boundary.” “I will leave the unsafe conversation.” “I will stop texting the person who destabilizes us.” “I will eat.” “I will sleep.” “I will keep a small promise.” “I will not leave the child alone with adult consequences.”

[B34] This is where inner-child practice often turns decorative. The voice says loving things while the adult sends the destabilizing message, skips the meal, avoids the task, and breaks the promise. Trust begins when you handle the consequence.

[B35] If warmth is unavailable, a protective act can begin the relationship. Make it small enough to carry out and review what actually happened. A missed action might reflect fear, exhaustion, missing skills, a practical barrier, or a poor plan. Completion without immediate relief isn’t automatically failure, and a tick on a list doesn’t prove the action helped.

[B37] The Leader gives direction. You can call this function Leader, Guide, Guru, Wise One, Sage, Inner Teacher, Director, or Higher Adult. The label matters much less than the ability to orient the whole system toward a life.

[B38] Direction can ask for something difficult without turning life into perfect discipline and hidden needs. Sometimes the next adult move is asking for help, expressing a preference, or letting a safe person see an imperfection. Wanting closeness is legitimate; punishing someone for needing space is a different strategy. Find a way to meet the need without erasing either person.

[B39] “We are not only here to feel better.” “We are here to become whole.” “We are here to belong to life itself, not only to people who approve of us.” “We are here to serve truth, beauty, love, God, source, nature, universe, dharma, Tao, and the benefit of all beings.”

[B40] Begin with the adult function you can actually reach. Protection may come before warmth. Direction should help the child grow without erasing care, play, or support.

[B42] Use this parts language when you actually recognize a protective pattern or find it useful to explore. A sensation, distraction, or objection doesn’t establish its cause. Notice it first; hunger may need food, skepticism may be reasonable, and a sentence may simply not fit.

[B46] “I notice you. You are trying to protect something. I want to understand. What are you afraid would happen if you stopped?”

[B47] Then wait. The answer may be: “You’ll lie to yourself again.” “This will become another performance.” “If you feel the pain, you’ll be destroyed.” “You’ll use spirituality to avoid reality.” “You’ll forgive too early.” “You’ll call fantasy guidance.” “You’ll abandon us as soon as the session ends.”

[B48] “That makes sense. You do not have to move yet. What would help you trust me 1% more?”

[B49] Usually the protector needs evidence more than another spiritual insight. Hold the boundary. Handle the task. Eat the meal. Don’t send the message. Keep the promise. Sleep. Contact the therapist. Acknowledge the debt. Write down the truth.

[B51] The Protector is more than an inner voice saying no. You can practice it as a bodily sequence:

[B52] Orient: look around and feel the support beneath you.

[B53] Assess: ask what is known, what is uncertain, and what needs checking.

[B54] Distinguish: separate present danger from uncertainty, old alarm, or imagined danger.

[B55] Set one proportionate boundary: move, stop, delay, ask, leave, or get support.

[B56] Verify enough safety: not perfect certainty, just enough reality-based evidence for the next step.

[B57] Stand down and rest: protection includes ending the scan when continued vigilance is no longer helping.

[B58] A mature Protector can say, “I don’t know.” It can ask another human, delay interpretation, or choose a no-trance practice. I’m aiming for discernment that acts when needed and then quits scanning the room after the danger has passed.

The Protector:

Work with your Protector to set boundaries together. They might be small, like “I’m going to leave this conversation because it’s not safe,” or “I’m going to stop texting someone who destabilizes me.” They might be self-care related, like “I’m going to eat” or “I’m going to get sleep.” They might be trust-related, like “I’m going to keep this promise to myself” or “I’m not going to leave you alone to deal with these adult things.”

A lot of inner child work fails because we do all the fluffy stuff, but then we go text the person who destabilizes us or don’t eat or don’t do whatever thing we were meant to be doing. Trust comes from being able to handle adult things.

If you can’t bring up the warmth stuff yet, that’s okay, you can always do something to be protective of your inner child. Make sure they’re small enough that you can do them. If you can’t, figure out what’s getting in the way of doing them. Are you scared? Tired? Don’t know how? Is there a practical obstacle in your way? Did you set yourself up to do it successfully? Be careful not to define doing the thing successfully as “feeling good about it” or “being able to check it off a list.”

The Leader/Guide/Guru:

You can also work with your Leader. Call them whatever you want, Guide, Guru, Wise One, Sage, Inner Teacher, Director, Higher Adult, whatever.

Ask them for help with directing your life. Be careful not to ask for the kind of “tough it out, you don’t have needs, you just need discipline!” kind of direction, though. Ask them for help in directing you to do adult things in a way that takes your needs into account: asking for the help you need, expressing your preferences, letting someone see you in an imperfect way, finding ways to get your need for closeness met without punishing someone for their need for space, etc. etc.

Affirmations might be helpful here, too: “We’re not just here to feel good. We’re here to become whole. We’re here to be part of life. We’re here to serve truth/beauty/love/God/source/nature/the universe/dharma/the Tao/the benefit of all beings,” etc. etc.

Start with whatever adult function you can connect to. Even if you start with protection and can’t do the warmth stuff yet, make sure the direction you’re being led in is appropriate for your inner child’s growth (i.e. not depriving them of things they need, like play or support, etc.)

Do you want to “embody” your Protector? Then you could practice this:

1. orient self (look around, feel the support around you, etc.)

2. assess the situation (know what you know, what you don’t know and check out what you need to)

3. differentiate between actual/real danger, unknown, past or imagined dangers

4. set boundaries (start with one and make it relative to the danger; move, stop, delay, ask, leave, get support, etc.)

5. verify if it is safe enough

6. stand down and rest

This seems to me like a “mature” Protector. One that is not afraid to say that he doesn’t know, or one that can ask another human being, or one that can delay interpreting a situation or one that can use a no-trance method.
