PUBLISHED section:
[B1] Sometimes the inner-child question lands nowhere because a private self had very little room to develop. In controlling families, rigid religions, enmeshed communities, or high-pressure achievement systems, performance can begin so early that a person doesn’t know what they feel, want, believe, or prefer until somebody else tells them first. Then “love yourself” produces a reasonable question: love whom? Reparenting and identity-building can proceed together. For some people the child-self is already easy to feel. For others, creating enough safety for preference, curiosity, and experimentation is part of how that self becomes easier to hear.

[B2] This may apply when:

[B3] You do not know what you want unless someone else says what they want first.

[B4] Your opinions shift dramatically depending on who is in the room.

[B5] You agree in the moment and later realize you did not agree.

[B6] You feel responsible for other people’s emotions to the point of self-erasure.

[B7] Self-love feels performative.

[B8] The question “What does my inner child feel?” produces guesses, not felt answers.

[B9] You can rebel, but you cannot yet choose.

[B10] A useful developmental explanation is that when childhood is organized around avoiding pain and preserving attachment, attention goes into reading danger, meeting expectations, and keeping the bond. That can leave less room for the ordinary experiments that answer, “What do I like? What do I want to try? Who am I when nobody is grading me?” This is a practical model, not a claim about one specific brain system.

[B11] Sometimes a temporary, intentional period of distance from the people or group supplying the answers helps quieter preferences surface. Use that distance only long enough to tell your own signal from the social echo; prolonged isolation would undermine the purpose.

[B12] The difference between a human doer and a human being helps here. When worth isn’t felt internally, it gets earned through beauty, performance, intellect, status, spirituality, or becoming indispensable to everybody else. Those can all be real gifts. They become belonging bargains when failure in the arena feels like losing yourself.

[B13] Useful questions:

[B14] “What do I like when no one is watching?” “What do I believe before I check whether others approve?” “What do I want that is neither rebellion nor compliance?” “Where am I buying belonging with self-betrayal?” “Which arena am I using to earn the right to belong?” “What would I try if nobody needed to be impressed?” “Which relationships let me remain myself?” “What am I noticing in my body, and what meaning am I giving it?”

[B15] Give the inner child a small experiment with no résumé value: painting, dancing, gardening, a language, a sport, volunteering, music, or anything else you’re allowed to be bad at. The inherited parent may demand immediate talent. The adult can answer, “We’re finding out whether we enjoy this, not proving our worth.” Give it enough repetitions to get past the humiliation of being a beginner.

[B16] For a child, belonging can feel like survival, so families, religions, romances, political tribes, spiritual groups, institutions, and online communities can reach astonishingly far into identity. Adulting means noticing where you buy belonging with self-betrayal, choosing your own ground, and learning that you can disappoint someone without disappearing. The subconscious contains a crowd of borrowed voices, which is why this belongs in a self-hypnosis guide.

[B17] “Where do I become less true in order to remain included?”

[B18] “What would belonging look like if I did not have to disappear?”

[B19] “Is this guidance, or is this a belonging program?” “Is this love, or is this fear of exclusion?” “Is this conscience, or is this conditioning?” “Is this devotion, or is this compliance?” “Is this rebellion, or is this freedom?”

[B20] Then wait for the body. It isn’t infallible, but it deserves a seat at the hearing.

[CHANGED, replaces B1-B20]
It can be difficult to connect with your inner child if your inner child didn’t have the opportunity to develop. This happens often when you grow up in a situation where you don’t have a private self, for example when your family, religion, or community ‘takes over’ very early in your life. Or you are pushed to achieve very early in life. The result is that you don’t know what you feel, what you want, what you believe, what you like and dislike until you have heard someone else’s opinion about it. If you are in that situation, it can be difficult to follow the advice to ‘love yourself’. You don’t even know who ‘yourself’ is! So you need to do reparenting and finding yourself in parallel. For others, it is very easy to work with the inner child, they can feel it easily.

You are in this camp if:

You don’t know what you want, until you have heard what someone else wants.

Your opinion is very different when you are with different people

You agree with people at the moment but when you are by yourself you realize that you don’t agree with them

You take responsibility for the emotions of others, at the expense of yourself

When you try to love yourself, it feels more like a show

When you try to find out how your inner child feels, you feel that you are guessing instead of feeling

You can rebel, but you can’t choose

This happens because when you were a child, you didn’t have the opportunity to explore what you like, what you want to try out and who you are. This is not a criticism of your brain but a practical way to look at it.

Sometimes you need to distance yourself for a certain time from the people or group that you normally get your answers from. But not for too long, as then you defeat the purpose of this exercise! You need to differentiate yourself a bit from your social environment.

What is the difference between a human doer and a human being? If you don’t feel good enough as a human being, you will try to become good enough as a human doer by for example being beautiful, doing something well, being smart, having a certain status, being spiritual, etc. In these areas, you made a bargain with yourself that as long as you are good in these areas, you will be good enough.

So in order to find yourself, you can ask yourself:

What do I like when nobody is looking?

What do I believe when I don’t check with other people?

What do I want that is neither in rebellion nor in compliance with others?

Where am I buying my sense of belonging at the expense of betraying myself?

In what arena am I trying to buy my sense of belonging?

What can I try on that I don’t need to be good at?

With whom can I be myself?

What do I feel in my body and what do I make out of it?

Give your inner child the opportunity to do something that has no value on its own and that it doesn’t need to be good at for its resume. For example painting, dancing, gardening, learning a language, playing a sport, doing volunteer work, playing a musical instrument, etc. Because often your inner parent tells your inner child that it is supposed to be good at something as soon as it starts doing it. And that’s not good because your inner child will get humiliated that it isn’t good at it. So try things out long enough until the humiliation is gone.

When you are a child, you will feel that you need to belong to a family in order to survive. That is why many people do things for their family, their religion, their romantic partner, their political tribe, their spiritual group, their institution or their online community that they otherwise wouldn’t do! Part of adulting is finding out where you sell yourself in order to belong, and then making the conscious choice where you want to belong. Because a large part of your subconscious mind, is made out of other people. This is why you are reading a self hypnosis guide!

Where am I betraying myself in order to belong?

What does it feel like to belong without betraying myself?

Where do I think I am following my own guidance, but I am in fact following my belonging program?

Where do I think I am doing things out of love, but I am actually doing it out of fear of not belonging?

Where do I think I am listening to my conscience, but I am actually listening to my conditioning?

Where do I think I am doing something out of devotion, but I am actually doing it out of compliance?

Where do I think I am rebelling, but I am actually free?

And then look what your body tells you. It is not always correct, but it is a good place to start.
