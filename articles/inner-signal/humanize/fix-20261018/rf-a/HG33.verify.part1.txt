PUBLISHED section:
[B1] Anxiety is often a Protector trying to prevent danger, shame, abandonment, failure, exposure, illness, rejection, loss of control, or a feeling it believes would be unbearable. Don’t begin by cross-examining it. Help the body first.

[B2] Body first. Inner child second. Inquiry third. Action fourth.

[B4] Start with the hand-on-heart, hand-on-solar-plexus practice.

[B5] “Little one, you are not alone. You do not have to solve this. I am here now. The adult is listening.”

[B6] Don’t recite the words like an incantation you’re hoping the subconscious will grade. Say them the way you’d speak to a frightened child you actually love.

[B8] Use this gently. Buteyko-style reduced breathing can help some people who overbreathe or panic, but don’t force breath holds. Air hunger can briefly increase anxiety before it settles, so go slowly.

[B9] Breathe through the nose. Let the inhale be small. Let the exhale be quiet. Pause only if comfortable. Let the breath become less dramatic. Stop if dizzy, strained, panicky, or medically concerned.

[B11] EFT can be a simple bodily ritual: name the fear, tap gently, and stay in contact with what you feel. The evidence is still developing. A 2025 systematic review found seven randomized controlled trials involving EFT for anxiety disorders; EFT beat no intervention in several of them, although the evidence should still be treated cautiously beside first-line therapies.

[B12] “Even though this anxiety is here, I am willing to be kind to the part of me that is afraid.”

[B14] More info, and a free intro session on my Somatic Guide. With SE, we track gently. Don’t turn the exercise into trauma excavation.

[B15] “Where is the activation?” “Where is the neutral place?” “Can attention move slowly between them?”

[B17] “What is the felt sense of this whole anxiety?”

[B18] Don’t answer from the head immediately. Give the body time to form one vague sense of the whole situation, then ask:

[B19] “What would this felt sense like me to know?”

[B20] Try a word or phrase and check how well it fits the felt sense. Then examine what it suggests about the situation; feeling right doesn’t settle that. Words and bodily attention can help correct each other.

[B22] Sometimes the mind is too activated to meditate usefully. Then help through the body: foot, scalp, temple, neck, or shoulder massage; warmth; pressure; a weighted blanket; darkness; familiar calming music; or a massage gun used carefully. Avoid the front of the neck, injuries, numb areas, bruises, inflamed tissue, recent surgery sites, varicose veins, and anything that produces pain, dizziness, tingling, or a symptom that feels wrong.

[B23] Other people settle with warmth, darkness, music, massage, weighted blankets, breathwork, familiar objects, or personal relaxation technology. Use the support that actually helps rather than assembling a tiny wellness trade show around the anxiety.

[B24] Personal relaxation-tech note: On my ZHoly3O page, I discuss ozonated charcoal “holy water” as one relaxation support, and I also mention Blushield. These remain optional supports. They don’t prove that any inner signal is true. Stabilize the body, listen gently, reality-test, and return to one grounded action.

[B26] First, body. Feet, chair, breath, room.

[B27] Little one, I am here. Protector, thank you for warning me. You do not have to scream for me to listen.

[B28] If there is real action needed, show me one step. If there is no action needed, help the body stand down gently.

[B29] I will not abandon the signal. I will not worship the signal. I will listen, test, and act.

REWRITE:
[CHANGED, replaces B1-B2]
Remember that often the anxiety comes from a protector part, a part of yourself who is trying to help you, but who believes you are in danger, or may be shamed, or abandoned, or fail, or be exposed, or become sick, or rejected, or lose control, or suffer something else unbearable. Therefore, instead of an interrogation, start with yourself:

Always work with yourself by first taking care of your body, then your inner child, then some inner inquiry, and then some action:

[CHANGED, replaces B4-B6]
Inner child love: Put a hand over your heart, and a hand on your solar plexis, and say lovingly, “Little one, you are not alone. You don’t have to fix this. I’m here now. The adult is listening.” Don’t just say the words, but say them in a way that you are talking to the inner child, who is frightened.

[CHANGED, replaces B8-B9]
Reduce your breathing. (NOTE: there are some good things to be said for slowing down the breathing, for some who overbreathe or are panicked, and this part is based on the Buteyko approach. But please don’t force yourself to hold your breath and suffer air hunger, which is going to make you more anxious for a period. Proceed slowly.) Take small inhales through the nose, and quiet exhales, with pauses if you can. Can you quiet your breathing? STOP if you feel dizzy or experience any other medical issues.

[CHANGED, replaces B11-B12]
EFT tapping: One of the easiest ways to do EFT is to say something like, “Even though I have this anxiety, I am willing to be kind to the part of me that is afraid,” while you tap on your body. There isn’t a ton of evidence for EFT, but there are some studies on anxiety. A 2025 systematic review found 7 randomized control trials for anxiety disorders, and noted that EFT was more effective than no intervention in a few studies. So use the evidence with caution, and know that there are first line therapies for anxiety.

[CHANGED, replaces B14-B15]
Somatic experiencing: If you would like more information about somatic experiencing, or even a free intro session, please see my somatic guide . In somatic experiencing (SE), you are not thrusting into the trauma. You learn to track. So you might start by tracking, by discovering where the activation is, where a neutral place is, and then whether you can move your attention from one to the other, very slowly, like a snail.

[CHANGED, replaces B17-B20]
Gendlin Focusing: Focusing is another approach where you work with your body. You might say to yourself, “What is the felt sense of this whole anxiety?” Don’t answer with your head; let your body come up with a vague sense of the whole anxiety. Then ask, “What would this felt sense like me to know?” Try a word or a phrase and see if it feels good. But also see if what the word or phrase says is true of the situation. The word may feel ok, but say something that doesn’t fit the situation, or vice versa. Use the words and the body to mutually correct one another.

[CHANGED, replaces B22-B24]
Work with the body. Sometimes the mind is too activated for meditation to help, so you may need to work directly with the body to calm it down. Maybe get a massage, or massage a foot, your scalp, a temple, or the back of the neck, the top of a shoulder, etc. (Avoid massaging the front of the neck. Also avoid massaging injured or numb body parts, or bruised, inflamed, or recently surgically operated on areas, or varicose veins. Also stop if the massage makes the anxiety symptoms worse, or makes you feel bad in any way, such as painful, dizzy, tingly, etc.) There are many other somatic approaches you can try, like heat, pressure, a weighted blanket, darkness, your favorite calming music, a careful use of a massage gun, etc.

You may find that you settle down with some of these things: warmth, darkness, favorite music, massage, a weighted blanket, some breathwork, a favorite object, technology you use to relax, etc.

I have a page on my ZHoly3O product, which is ozonated charcoal, and I use a bit of this “holy water” as one of the things that helps me relax. (See the mention of Blushield on that page, too.) But neither one of those products are necessary. All you really need is to stabilize yourself, and then listen to the messages from your inner realm, reality test those messages, and then take some action.

A script you could use for anxiety is:

[CHANGED, replaces B26-B29]
Where am my body? What are my feet doing? How am I sitting in the chair? How am I breathing? How is my body in the room?

If you can hear me, I want you to know that I am here. Thank you for alerting me. You don’t have to scream. I got your message.

Now if there is something we need to do, show me what the first thing is that we should do, right now. And if there is nothing we need to do, help my body stand down, and know that I am not going to abandon you, but I am also not going to worship you. I am going to listen to you, reality test what you are saying, and then take some action.
