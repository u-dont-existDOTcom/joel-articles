Below is one section of a long self-hypnosis guide by Joel, published on his Substack. It teaches gathering attention, listening to the subconscious as an ecology of parts (the inner child, protectors or "guards", the body, intuition), three adult functions he calls the Nurturer, the Protector and the Leader/Guru, Higher Self, "borrowed adulthood", inductions and a complete return, influence literacy ("de-hypnosis": noticing and resisting other people's influence), and safety limits (when to stop, when to get help). Its quoted lines are scripts the reader says or thinks.

PUBLISHED section:
[B1] Use the same practice rather than learning a second script: gather attention, listen, respond, wait, return. Fifteen minutes is a convenient appointment with yourself, not a therapeutic dose or a timetable your feelings must follow. Use a few minutes to enter, leave several minutes to return, and let the middle unfold at its own pace. Five minutes of silent listening is available when useful; you can interrupt it to change direction.

[B2] Another simple entry is to notice three things you see, three you hear, and three bodily sensations; then two of each, then one. Count from five to one and begin listening. Use the entry that suits you, rather than stacking inductions.

[B3] After familiar runs, shorten a recording to the cues you still need, then try practicing without playback. Keep recordings when you enjoy them; independence means being able to guide yourself, not having to refuse help. The optional re-entry cue can shorten an already familiar induction.

[B4] Afterward, ask what needed warmth, what needed protection, and what direction serves truth, love, and life. Was there something to explore, or capacity to build? What can you carry yourself now? When action is needed, make it concrete: “If [cue] happens, then I will [small action].” Check how you sleep, relate, and function later, not only how impressive the session felt.

REWRITE:
[CHANGED, replaces B1]
Notice there is no need to learn a second script! The process is the same. You are simply practicing for fifteen minutes or so. That is long enough to induce yourself (it only takes a few minutes), practice for a short time (five minutes is plenty to see what happens when you simply sit in silence, listening, and you can interrupt yourself at any time to change direction if that feels better) and come out again (that takes a few minutes too).

[CHANGED, replaces B2]
You can also try other ways of inducing yourself such as becoming aware of 3 things you can see, 3 things you can hear, 3 things you can feel in your body; then 2 of each, 1 of each, counting from 5 to 1 and beginning to listen. Choose one way to induce yourself, you don't need to try all of them! (In fact, it might be counter-productive!)

[CHANGED, replaces B3]
Once you have practiced listening a few times with the recordings, you may want to shorten them to speed up your induction, or even practice without playing them back at all. On the other hand, if you like using the recordings, there is no reason to stop! Being able to guide yourself does not mean you have to! (NB. You may wish to use the following to help you re-enter your process, this will also shorten your induction if you already know what to do.)

[CHANGED, replaces B4]
As you come out of the process ask yourself what you learned needed warmth, what needed to be protected, what you need to do differently to be more in accord with truth and love and life. Is there something you want to explore more fully or a capacity you want to build? And most importantly, what can you carry with you as you move forward from this? If you identified something you needed to do differently, make a plan with yourself to do that thing. The more specific you can be, the more likely you are to change: “If _________ (fill in the cue for the old pattern), then I will do ________ (fill in one small action that will begin to change the pattern).” Then check back later and see how you are doing – sleeping, relating, functioning, etc.
