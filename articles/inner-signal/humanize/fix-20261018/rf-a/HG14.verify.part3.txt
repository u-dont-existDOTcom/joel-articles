PUBLISHED section:
[B60] I connect my reparenting therapy to self-hypnosis because I find they are a perfect match. The child may arrive as an age, image, posture, voice, memory, emotion, or plain vulnerability. Sometimes no child appears. Stay with a recognizable protector, or with the sensation, feeling, or preference that is actually present.

[B61] When the child does appear, resist the urge to begin a healing project immediately. Be with them first.

[B62] “Little one, I am here. You do not have to explain everything. You do not have to be healed today. You do not have to become impressive. I only want to know what would help you feel less alone.”

[B63] The need may be warmth, play, protection, grief, anger, food, rest, a boundary, a safer relationship, less fantasy, less overwork, or one adult promise kept today.

[B65] When a younger state, protector, feeling, or bodily signal needs care, move patiently:

[B66] Prepare: create enough time, privacy, support, and adult capacity.

[B67] Inspect: notice what is present before deciding what it means.

[B68] Ask permission: “May I come closer, or would you prefer warmth, space, words, silence, or no contact?”

[B69] Respond to one need: do the smallest useful thing rather than attempting a total repair.

[B70] Wait: let the response settle. Some changes need quiet, sleep, or ordinary time.

[B71] Reconnect: invite contact only where it is welcome. No part has to merge, disappear, forgive, or become cheerful.

[B72] Rest: stop before care becomes another performance.

[B73] Reassess: return later and ask what actually helped.

[B74] The goal isn’t “good as new,” as though history should disappear. Aim for good as you: more supported and connected while still recognizably yourself. If parts language makes you feel more divided, use signals, feelings, younger states, or another metaphor that helps.

[B76] Reparenting doesn’t have to become solemn. A younger state may respond to texture, drawing, movement, a plushie with a voice, or whatever feels interesting. Keep reality-testing intact. You’re recovering play, not becoming somebody’s child, pet, doll, or owned object.

[B77] Rest also doesn’t require helplessness. Reduce decisions for a defined period, put unfinished tasks somewhere visible, and let the internal adult keep watch. You remain able to move, stop, and return.

[B79] When speech or visualization feels fake, write. Let the non-dominant hand speak for the child or protector, awkward handwriting and all. Answer with the dominant hand as the adult.

[B80] Non-dominant hand: “I don’t trust you.”

[B81] Dominant hand: “I hear that you don’t trust me. I still love you. Tell me where I’ve let you down. I won’t demand trust; I’ll listen and keep one small promise today.”

[B82] Non-dominant hand: “Which promise?”

[B83] Dominant hand: “I will eat, clean the desk, and not text the person who makes us feel unsafe.”

[CHANGED, replaces B60-B63]
I see a lot of connection between reparenting therapy and self hypnosis. They fit well together. When you go into hypnosis the child might come to you. If you want, you can see that child in any form you want. It can be an image, a posture, a voice, a memory, an emotion, etc. It can be any age you want it to be. It can be any vulnerable part of you. It doesn’t even have to be a “child”, if there is no child there, just work with a protector or whatever you find.

[CHANGED, replaces B65-B74]
Just be with the child.

If you can, tell the child that you are there for them and that they don’t have to explain themselves, or be “healed” or impress you. Just tell you what they need to not be alone anymore.

It can be anything. What does the child need? warmth, to play, to be protected, to mourn, to be angry, food, rest, boundaries, better relationships, to stop fantasizing, to stop working their butt off, that you keep a promise to them for one day, etc. etc.

Don't rush it.

Find a younger you/protector/feeling/signal that comes up in your body, and

A. Prepare yourself to tend to them (get yourself into a place where you can spend time with them, have privacy, feel you can access support if you need it, and be as adult as you can be).

B. Look at what's there before judging or deciding what it is.

C. Ask them what you can do or if you can move closer to them. If you feel like you already know what they need, tell them that you're going to get them what they need- warmth, space, verbal reassurance, silence, distance, whatever.

D. Give them what you can- don't feel like you have to fix it all.

E. Give yourself some time to let what you did sink in- it may take some time, it may take you to go to sleep, etc.

F. Reconnect with them- don't expect them to merge, go away, forgive you, become happy, etc. Just reconnect with them if they're willing.

G. Rest, and reconnect with them later to see what was helpful.

Remember the goal isn't to make yourself good as new but to make yourself good as you- more supported and connected to yourself. Feel free to modify this as needed, and if you don't like the idea of part work you can substitute whatever part you're working with for a signal or a feeling, or an imaginary younger you that you feel embodies the issue you're having.

[CHANGED, replaces B76-B77]
When you're reparenting, it's okay to play with things that interest your younger states. Whether that means touching things of different textures, drawing, exercising, roleplaying with a plushie in a different voice, etc. As long as you're reality testing is good and you remember that you aren't a child or pet or doll or whatever and that nobody owns you, it's okay to work on getting your play ability back.

When you're resting, you don't have to be helpless. It's okay to cut down the amount of decisions you make for a set period of time, put things you haven't finished on a list where you can see it, and let the adult part of you watch over you.
