Love doesn’t have to wait for trust

Even if your inner child does not believe that you love him or her, you can still love him or her. Trust can question your promises, remember when you failed to protect him or her, and take its time.

You can say, out loud or in your head, “Okay. I believe that you don’t believe me, but I still love you. You don’t have to love me back. Please tell me what is not okay with you.”

Respond to complaints, which are likely to include something true. Showing that you love him or her can include some or all of the following: listening to him or her when he or she is angry without hitting back, owning up to what was not okay, working on being less likely to turn on him or her, and other ways of making it up to him or her. Other ways of showing that you love him or her include taking care of him or her by buying and cooking food, setting limits, taking him or her to doctor’s appointments, and doing what you say you will do. Just not hitting back is not enough; you also need to love your inner child.

Be aware of what you are teaching your other positions. You might be teaching your inner child that you are going to love him or her no matter what, but you might also be teaching him or her that he or she is responsible for believing your love, and that if he or she does not, it is his or her fault. The position that is making the vow to love the inner child no matter what, and the position that is blaming the inner child for not taking in the love, might be separate positions, or they might be one position, or you might be blending them. It might not be based on chronological age.

When you're placing your younger self on trial, how valuable is it for you to consider how valuable it might have been for your younger self at the time to be doing what they did, given what they knew at the time? How valuable is it for you to consider what options were available to them at the time, and how tolerable they were? How valuable is it for you to consider the consequences of their actions at the time? How valuable is it for you to consider that whilst their actions might have caused harm to you and others, punishing yourself now will not give them the capacity they needed at the time, and that it is more useful to work on what you can do now to deal with the harm that was caused?
