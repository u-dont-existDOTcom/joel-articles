The Nurturer

THE NURTURER: As a Nurturer one of the most important things that you can do is to enjoy your inner child, to sit back and not feel like you have to move their feelings and to say to them that you are really glad that they are here in this moment. Children need to be enjoyed.

It’s okay that you’re feeling the way you are feeling.

I want you to know I’m here for you.

You don’t need to do anything right now.

It’s okay that you’re not too much. You can be small here.

You can be angry here.

You can be sad here.

The Protector

I’m going to be here for you.

The Protector:

Work with your Protector to set boundaries together. They might be small, like “I’m going to leave this conversation because it’s not safe,” or “I’m going to stop texting someone who destabilizes me.” They might be self-care related, like “I’m going to eat” or “I’m going to get sleep.” They might be trust-related, like “I’m going to keep this promise to myself” or “I’m not going to leave you alone to deal with these adult things.”

A lot of inner child work fails because we do all the fluffy stuff, but then we go text the person who destabilizes us or don’t eat or don’t do whatever thing we were meant to be doing. Trust comes from being able to handle adult things.

If you can’t bring up the warmth stuff yet, that’s okay, you can always do something to be protective of your inner child. Make sure they’re small enough that you can do them. If you can’t, figure out what’s getting in the way of doing them. Are you scared? Tired? Don’t know how? Is there a practical obstacle in your way? Did you set yourself up to do it successfully? Be careful not to define doing the thing successfully as “feeling good about it” or “being able to check it off a list.”
