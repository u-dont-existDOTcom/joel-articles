Appendix J: A practical induction library

Practise: Preview, choose one purpose, decide whether to start. All ‘families’ can be light, eyes open, or replaced by no-trance contact. Monitor what happens (even if nothing seems to change) and decide on next action: move, imagine, change sensory channel, remain still, stop the sequence etc. Always do a complete waking return from induction.

NB If you cannot ‘see’ any images vividly, translate the metaphor into words, rhythm, spatial relations, touch, movement, knowing etc. The mind doesn’t owe anybody a movie.

Stretch and release

Focus on a feeling of support (and perhaps the position of one hand or foot). Either actually or in imagination stretch slightly and/or press into the support and then release. Notice any difference in feeling (even if nothing seems to change). Repeat this with no more than 3 areas of the body. Use the notion of ‘release’ to guide you through the sequence. Ask yourself which area, or which inner voice, wants attention, and what support is needed. Then bring back normal movement and return.

Supported floating

Notice where your weight is carried, and let the support carry 1% more of your effort, as if floating without losing contact, or keep the same weight and pressure. Notice what happens. It may help to fractionate, turning your attention toward normal awareness on the in-breath and toward the support on the out-breath. Tell yourself you're going to do this before you start. What becomes easier to hear? Keep the support, come back to the weight and firm contact you choose, and return.

Rhythmic breath or pulse

Try it with a natural rhythm; perhaps the rhythm of breathing or a slight hum in the throat. Try it with a more artificial rhythm, such as tapping a finger or listening to sounds outside. Even try it counting (silently) to yourself, or even with no rhythm at all. Say the words 'here, and here again' over and over, noticing where your attention lands in between, then stop the rhythm. Notice the first quiet beat - do not try and make your mind blank. This might bring a bit more clarity to what word, feeling, need or action is coming up. Then let your normal rhythm come back, and return.

Unwinding object

A quick exercise for when one concern has several strands. Imagine yarn unwinding, bubbles settling, or knots placed where you can see them beside you. When you've placed them all, take a minute to look at them all (without changing them, though you could label them if needed, by saying a word or two). Count only the strands you actually set down; nothing is erased. Which one needs warmth, protection, or action? Now keep one useful strand, put the others somewhere you can see them, and return.

Sensory journey

Listen to the sounds of the room you are currently in and feel your body. Now imagine yourself walking somewhere, where do you want to go? As you walk, listen to the sounds of your footsteps and take in your surroundings. What texture are the objects you pass? What landmarks do you see? Now that you've collected enough attention, imagine the scenery changing until you are in a place where whoever or whatever needs to speak can speak. Or you can just cut straight to the chase if you want. Notice a detail without giving it a hidden meaning, the mystery is how the scene develops, so don't make it into a "what are they going to do to you?" kind of thing. When you're ready to finish, imagine walking back the way you came and close the space.

Gradual wave

Begin with the smallest area possible that is neutral. Sense what is already there, and imagine warmth, steadiness or attention radiating outward to the next area, which should still feel comfortable. Continue this, pausing and sensing in each new area, or continue with the original area, only. Notice what changes, without claiming any medical repair. Does the body want contact, space, words, or ordinary care? Allow the image to fade, sense the whole body, the contact points, and return.

Care workshop

First get ready for some patient, unhurried care: time, place, tools and boundaries. Let the signal, the feeling or the younger state come only as close as is welcome. Be careful to not use language which makes you fragmented. Ask before you respond, wait, reconnect only where that’s welcome, rest, and see again. If you have an adult part which can do this or borrow a function from someone else, let that help. You decide the words, the pace and what you want to do. Aim for “good as you,” not an erased history. Finally, close the workspace. Say that you are whole, an adult and come back.

Resource embodiment

Choose a quality you understand, like warmth, a boundary, steadiness, curiosity or dignity. Imagine your posture, voice or attention with just 1% more access to it, or, if you’d rather, simply observe it. Notice what happens. Repeat a few times, to strengthen your access to it, not to become someone else. How would that quality meet the situation you’re in today? Finally keep the quality and one useful action, let go of the role, say to yourself "I am myself", and return.

No-trance inner contact

Observable pacing: keep the eyes open, notice where the practice is taking place and on what day, notice the feet, read something, write something, notice that one is still relatively normally aware.

Invitation: choose a question and keep it in mind, do not specifically focus or narrow the awareness further.

Genuine choice: answer the question or write it down, write that one does not know, rest, change the question, ask someone else, stop the practice, etc.

Autonomous signal: write down any word, sensation, impulse, objection, or silence that comes, without taking it as proof.

Deepener: none, full alertness isn’t a lesser version of the practice.

Handoff: have the adult part of yourself answer the question in any way, preferably in writing or by taking an action.

Integration/exit: reality test the autonomous signal and do one small action based on that, end the practice (no special exit is needed since no state was achieved that one needs to exit from).
