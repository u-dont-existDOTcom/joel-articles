Second, it works on a State Level of NLP: helping them to access warmth, or a feeling of adult-ness, or courage, or steadiness, or love, or clarity.

Third, it works on a Strategy level of NLP: helping people to practice a better response: when this cue appears, the adult does this.

Fourth, it works on a Language Level of NLP: pacing what is already true, opening up people’s awareness, presuming only their ability to do something safe, linking cause and effect carefully, embedding invitations, or asking de-hypnotic questions to break the effects of vague, absolutes, manipulative, or grandiose language.

The Milton Model, minus the covert sales seminar

It's called the Milton Model and has to do with the architecture of the sentence. When I say "Relax now", or "Stop being anxious", or even "Trust me" a part of me often braces against it.

The idea is to use indirect language to get past the part that wants to argue with me without having to overpower it, and get to the place I want to go to.

To do that, I start with something I know to be true, I take the pressure off, presume only a small safe capacity and invite just one movement:

"As the body notices the bed beneath it, it may become a little easier to stop holding the whole day alone."

That way I pace reality, I open up awareness, I validate the function of the reaction, I presuppose capacity, I use a bridge to make the link easier, I invite only one small movement, and I have a way of testing with the body if it's happening.

Pattern 1: Pacing and leading — truth before suggestion

What is it looks like to pace and lead? It means stating what is already true before inviting anything new.

Here is an example of pacing and leading for anxiety: “Anxiety is here. The body is activated. A protector is trying to help. And one breath at a time, the adult can start to listen.”

It can be as simple as: Truth, Invite, Truth, Invite etc.

“It is completely true that the sensation of _______ (or emotion of _____) is coming up for me right now. It makes complete sense that my system is reacting by doing/thinking ________. While that is completely true, another completely true thing is that my adult self is here, breathing in this room right now, and willing to listen to whatever comes up next.”

Pattern 2: Awareness verbs

‘Awareness’ verbs point attention without yelling at it: notice, realize, recognize, sense, feel, wonder, imagine, picture, listen, allow, discover, remember, consider

“Notice whether the answer comes as an image, a word, a pressure, a warmth, a silence, or a next action.”

Pattern 3: Presuppositions

Caution: The powerful technique of presupposition (embedding a statement as if it were true) should be used only to presume choice, when it will occur, ability, safety, love, and adult leadership. Never use presupposition to smuggle in obedience.

“Before any part has to change, it can first become more understood.”

Pattern 4: Gentle cause-effect bridges

Be careful with how language link events. Words like “as, while, because, when, after, before, which means” etc are all words that can be used to link events. A gentle voice doesn’t make a false causal claim true.

In general, the problem with the first example (“Because I breathe, the trauma leaves.”) is that it’s too strong. It’s unlikely that simply breathing will get rid of the trauma. The second example is better because it’s more accurate: “As I breathe, the adult self may become present enough to hear one small piece of what the wound needs.”

Pattern 5: Embedded invitations

What are embedded invitations, and how to use them? An embedded invitation places a small direction inside a larger sentence. It should invite a modest inner movement, not hide a demand.

Here is an example of an embedded invitation: “And somewhere inside this breath, I can let the adult self listen.”

Here’s another example of an embedded invitation: “As I sit here, I may allow one small truth to become clear.”

And yet another embedded invitation for working with inner child parts: “The child does not have to trust me all at once; it can simply notice I am still here.”

Use one when it helps. Of course, don’t use ten embedded invitations one after the other, or people will know that’ve just completed a weekend certification course!

Pattern 6: Ethical double binds

Manipulative double bind questions trap every answer inside the speaker’s agenda.

Ethical double bind questions offer two genuinely safe routes:

“Would this part prefer warmth first, or space first?”

Pattern 7: Conversational postulates

Example of a postulate, a question whose ordinary conversational form also acts as an invitation:

“Can the system show me the next step without showing me the whole wound?”

Pattern 8: Tag questions

In the same way, tag questions (“isn’t it”, “can’t it”, “doesn’t it”, etc) can soften a sentence. But they get oily really fast, don’t they? So use them sparingly.

“The body already knows something about resting, doesn’t it?”

Pattern 9: Nominalizations

Nominalizations turn processes into nouns, words like healing, safety, trust, integration, communion, clarity, rest, and so on. Their vagueness can sometimes help, because then the subconscious fills in the blank with its own concrete meaning.

“A little more safety can arrive in whatever way the body understands.”

Pattern 10: Meta Model questions — the de-hypnosis tool

To ‘Milton Model’ is to open things up. To ‘Meta Model’ is to ask “What do yo mean by that?” In order to be ethical with people, it is necessary to do both.

“How specifically? According to who? Compared to what? ‘Always’? ‘Never’? What specifically would happen? How do I know? What evidence would change my mind?”

If it feels like the body is stiffening up, ‘Milton’ it. If it feels like truth is eluding ya, ‘Meta’ it.

Sensory, state, and strategy tools

Submodalities — editing the form, not denying the truth

If your fear, craving, fantasy, belief or memory has any sensory properties (it looks or sounds near or far, bright or dim, loud or quiet, or it moves, is heavy, is inside or outside your body etc) then you can work with the properties you actually notice. If none are apparent then don't try to force it, just skip this tool rather than inventing an image or voice.

Ask yourself how the experience is represented. What changes when the image moves two feet back, the voice gets quieter, or the edge becomes less sharp?

"I am not deleting the signal, just adjusting the volume so I can listen without being overwhelmed."

Before you move one imaginary pixel, ask your guard if it is ok to do this.

"Would it be okay if we moved this image back just a little, or lowered the volume slightly, purely so my adult self can stay present enough to hear what you need to tell me?"

Anchoring — giving the body a doorway back

An anchor is a cue connected to a state. There are lots of ways to anchor something. Put your hand on your heart, touch two fingers together, use a breath, a word, body posture, smell, song, anything. Feel something that you have got to in reality that you want to get back to easily. As you feel it do the anchor thing and say to yourself, “This is available.” Make sure you do it when you have clearly got it. To get back into a hypnotic state easily don't go for a peak emotional state but find a good receptive state that you can use. See the main induction section on a shortcut back into hypnosis. Make sure that this is separate from the alert calming and the synchronicity wake up cue.

Using synchronicity as your own wake-up bell

Sometimes when people notice a synchronicity they ask straight away what it means. Sometimes it does give them a very good insight, sometimes it gives them a reason to start obsessing and look for patterns in everything. I am using noticing a synchronicity in a different way. I am using it as a signal to WAKE UP. The coincidence doesn’t have to contain a message; it has already caught my attention, and that’s enough.

It is my cue to:

Whenever I notice a synchronicity, I use it to wake up in whatever state I am in at that moment, feel my body and the room I am in, remember that the coincidence is not proof of an interpretation, let my attention widen beyond the story my current mood is telling, ask what is fact, what is interpretation, what needs warmth, what needs protection and what needs action, and go to the highest state I can where I can objectively and clearly see the situation I am in from the highest possible perspective, without leaving my body, the facts or ordinary human reality. From this place I feel whatever feelings, search for the truth of the situation, and if I am in a negative state, I check if there is a message of my Inner Child, Protector, if I need to go through some grieving, if there is a message of my physical body about some problem that needs solving, or if there is a need for me to take some action in this situation. From this place of clarity and peace I am free to choose what to do next and I choose at least one thing to do that comes from a loving place. I make room for whatever is genuinely there, like objectivity, clarity, ease, peace, freedom, joy and gratitude, without having to manufacture any of it.

In short it is my cue to: Stop-orient-go wider-reality check-listen-do at least one thing from a loving place.

It enables me to not get into identification, but still hear the message of the state if it is a negative one.

Installing the synchronicity cue

Mentally rehearse: imagine noticing an ordinary, harmless synchronicity,

feel 'whoa' as attention stimulated,

use that opening to feel feet/body/breath/room,

reiterate to yourself "when I notice synchronicity, it is a bell to wake me up, not proof of a message",

ask Nurturer/Protector/Leader their thoughts,

rehearse returning to a more grounded and wide view,

rehearse one small action to take, and finally rehearse waking completely,

Mind the caveats : it must be self-authored, visible, reversible, and restricted to just helping orientation. It must not authorize a decision, prove an intuition, suppress a warning, or let another person control the response.

This is like an implementation intention, an advance “if X, then Y” plan that links a recognizable cue with a chosen response, for which the general mechanism is supported (a good starting point is this paper by Gollwitzer). Note that this specific application of implementation intentions hasn't been studied directly.

Synchronicity cues run the risk of creating false positives because you are creating an association in your mind by rehearsing it (this is the same mechanism by which synchronicity cues work), and when you are absorbed and/or destabilized, your mind may look for that pattern and interpret it as present when it may not actually be. Research has shown that people that score highly on apophenia (the tendency to perceive meaningful patterns where none exists) also score highly on false positives for patterns that are not present, and also score highly on traits on the psychosis spectrum, although ordinary pattern noticing is not itself pathological (Blain et al., Grant et al.).

If you feel that synchronicity cues are making any of these worse (you find yourself compelled to scan your environment for them, you are unable to sleep, you are anxious, you become certain that random events are occurring to communicate something to you specifically, you have difficulty distinguishing observation from interpretation, etc.), you should deactivate them.

You can do this by simply ceasing to rehearse them, and saying, while fully awake, “Synchronicity is no longer to be used as a cue. I release this association and return coincidence to ordinary uncertainty.” Also remove it from your list of cues so that you stop rehearsing it. The statement expresses your decision; it does not prove the association has vanished immediately.

Reframing — changing the meaning without lying

Dishonest reframing declares that the problem was secretly good all along, so I prefer to ask myself questions that widen the meaning without lying, like: “What else could be true here?” or “What is this part trying to do for me?” or “How would my Nurturer reframe this?” or “How would my Protector reframe this?” or “How would my Leader reframe this?” and so on.

Perceptual positions — self, other, witness, universal heart

1. How do I feel about this from inside myself? 2. If I were inside of this other person, what might they be feeling or protecting? 3. If I were across the room watching this, what would I see? 4. From the universal heart, what serves truth, love, boundaries, and the good of everyone involved?

Don’t use these positions to excuse harm. Use them to get out of a prison of perception, to see from more than one angle.

Running the whole NLP sequence

Induce or contain. Gather attention, or stabilize first if you are already wide open.

Ask permission. Is it useful to work with this image, feeling, or pattern today?

Represent the pattern. How does it show up: image, voice, sensation, posture? How big? How close? What color? How does it move?

Ask what it protects. What is this trying to do for me?

Adjust the form of the pattern, gently (move it further away, turn down the volume, add space, change body posture, slow it down, etc.).

Introduce the three adults (Nurturer, Protector and Leader): the Nurturer warms, the Protector makes it safe, the Leader points a direction.

Anchor the useful state (heart, fingers, breath, body posture, etc.).

Future pace into a situation where this pattern used to be triggered: if the old cue shows up, what is the smallest new action?

Check the fit and the evidence: what changes in the body, is the response kind and doable, and what supports your interpretation of the situation beyond how it feels?

Exit altered state, come back to the room, and do one ordinary thing.
