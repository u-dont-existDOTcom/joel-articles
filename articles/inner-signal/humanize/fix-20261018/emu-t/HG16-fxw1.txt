Some common ways you may know you’re being manipulated include a sense of urgency, a sense of specialness, FOMO, being made to feel like you won’t be included if you don’t go along, sexual charge, shame, being confused, a fantasy of being rescued, a sense of destiny, a sense that you should obey, a sense of secret knowledge, or an excessive sense of certainty about something, far too quickly. You may also feel that one honest question would break the spell.

If you’re being hypnotically influenced, stop. Don’t obey right away. Don’t rebel automatically, either; just realize that you’re being influenced.

Some common ways hypnotic language becomes manipulative: it smuggles in your consent, makes one choice seem inevitable, or hides a disputed claim inside the sentence, like

“you’ll realize, eventually, that I’m right…” “you may eventually admit that…” “the only reason you resist is…” “part of you already knows…” “your higher self wants…” “everyone who’s truly awake knows…” “the reason you don’t believe that’s true is your ego’s fear of…”

The grammar takes the conclusion for granted, turns your resistance into proof, and makes obeying feel like waking up. Ask:

What’s being claimed here? What’s the inevitable choice being framed for me here? What’s being presupposed? Who benefits from framing it this way? What’s the emotionally charged bit? What’s not being said? Can I say no without being punished, shamed, mocked, abandoned or spiritually downgraded?

A useful guide helps bring more of the ‘adult’ into the conversation. When the state is more powerful, you need stronger boundaries.

Soft language can still take away choice

Permissive words can still hide coercion. Look for:

Indirect suggestions can by-pass the subject’s skeptical guard by never directly stating what it is you want him/her to do. This does not mean that the suggestions are not specific, but rather that they are worded in a vague, conversational and suggestive manner.
