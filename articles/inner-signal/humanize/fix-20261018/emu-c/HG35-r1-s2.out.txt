Things to ask: What do they train in? How do they use hypnosis in their practice? What will they do if something doesn’t “fit”? If you are working with trauma: Did they actually get training in trauma and dissociation in addition to whatever hypnosis certification they might have? (Remember, credentials are not indicators of quality care!)

What has and hasn’t worked for you in the past. How direct you want them to be. What kind of touch (if any) you are ok with. What kind of imagery (if any) you are ok with. What to do if you want to stop a session. What to do if you have unexpected reactions in or after a session. What to do if you aren’t sure if something is a memory or not. What to do if you need to come back “all the way” to the session. Keep in mind that a good person should be able to firmly encourage you without making it continue if you say you don’t want to.

How you felt the method went. What the method was. Why they used it (if they said). Whether or not you agreed to it. How the person responded to you (again, keep in mind that discomfort doesn’t mean it was “bad,” but seeing your objections as meaning you needed more of the same might be worth looking into). You can ask the app or others knowledgeable about dissociation and trauma to help explain what happened, but it is unlikely someone can tell you if the person was “certified” or not just based on what you tell them. Depending on what you find out, you might want to try self-hypnosis, working with someone else on your team, or finding someone else to help you.
