It's called the Milton Model and has to do with the architecture of the sentence. When I say "Relax now", or "Stop being anxious", or even "Trust me" a part of me braces against it.

The idea is to use indirect language to get past the part that wants to argue with me, and get to the place I want to go to.

To do that, I start with something I know to be true, and I take the pressure off by presuming the least possible capacity in the person and invite just one movement:

"As the body notices the bed beneath it, it may become a little easier to stop holding the whole day alone."

That way I pace reality, I open up awareness, I validate the function of what I'm doing, I presuppose the capacity to do what I want, I use a bridge to make the link easier, I invite only one small movement, and I have a way of testing with the body if it's happening.
