PUBLISHED section:
[B6] For that starting point, I use a provisional, author-developed bridge I call borrowed adulthood. It combines familiar practices—witnessing, modeling, supported action, and gradual transfer of responsibility—without claiming that the complete sequence has been validated as one treatment.

[B7] Witness, when needed. Notice what is present without demanding warmth, wisdom, or certainty. If you already distinguish the internal positions, move to the adult function you need.

[B8] Borrow one function. Choose a safe adult, mentor, older sibling, future self, spiritual figure, value, or written plan. Borrow only the warmth, boundary, or direction needed now.

[B9] Become the adult apprentice. Choose the exact words or action yourself and carry at least one small part.

[B43] People assume inner-child work begins with the child. Very often, you meet the guard at the door first.

[B44] You sit down to connect and get numbness, cynicism, anger, sudden hunger, a phone urge, a sexual urge, planning, sleepiness, dissociation, or the thought, “This is stupid.” I wouldn’t call those failures. They’re often the protector making an appearance in its usual charming way.

[B77] Rest also doesn’t require helplessness. Reduce decisions for a defined period, put unfinished tasks somewhere visible, and let the internal adult keep watch. You remain able to move, stop, and return.

Do you have an adult you can borrow from? If you can’t find one, you can always reach up and grab a little bit from a safe adult in your life, or from a mentor, or an older brother or sister, or from your future self, or from a spiritual figure, or from a value, or from a plan you’ve written out. Just borrow what you need.

[CHANGED, replaces B9]
Then, operating from this position of being an apprentice in the adult hat, do at least part of what you would want to do.

[CHANGED, replaces B43]
When you try to connect with your inner child, what do you expect to happen? Unless you’ve been doing this work for a while, you might expect that you’ll experience your inner child.

[CHANGED, replaces B44]
But, what you’re more likely to experience is numbness, cynicism, anger, a strong desire to eat, check your phone, have sex, plan your day, go to sleep, dissociate or a voice in your head that says something like, “This is stupid.” It isn’t that you’re failing to connect with your inner child, it’s that you’ve run into your guard.

[CHANGED, replaces B77]
Remember that even though you might be resting for a while, you still have options. You can choose to not make any decisions for a certain amount of time. You can put things on a list that are not completed, and hope that someday your adult self will pick them back up and finish them.
