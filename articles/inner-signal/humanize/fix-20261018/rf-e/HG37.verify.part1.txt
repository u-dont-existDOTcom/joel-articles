PUBLISHED section:
[B1] I’m speaking from experience in this appendix.

[B2] During self-hypnosis, meditation, lucid dreaming, loveyhuasca, grief, prayer, plant-medicine-adjacent practice, or other altered states, people sometimes meet something that doesn’t feel like an ordinary emotion or younger part. It may appear as a demon, jinn, parasitic energy, dark intelligence, or terrifying archetype—a voice or image that feels intrusive, hateful, seductive, mocking, or simply alien.

[B3] I don’t need this guide to settle the metaphysics. Perhaps these are literal beings. Perhaps they are symbolic mind-forms, trauma in a frightening costume, dissociated energy, or ancestral and relational burdens. The practical question is:

[B4] “What response makes me safer, clearer, more loving, more grounded, and less hooked?”

[B6] When a demonic, jinn-like, hostile, or intrusive presence appears, don’t reward it with instant metaphysical certainty. Run the triage first: medical danger, environment, body, overload, and how open the mind has become. Open the eyes, feel the feet, name the room, lower stimulation, leave hostile company, and stop adding prompts. Don’t deepen, hunt for hidden truth, or debate ontology while destabilized.

[B7] “Only what can be integrated is welcome now. Anything too much can wait. The adult self remains present. The body may return to safety.”

[B8] Danger to self or others, psychosis-like fear, inability to sleep, extreme paranoia, chest pain, seizures, serious overheating, overdose concern, or loss of basic functioning means the self-hypnosis session is over. Get real-world help.

[B10] Once you’re stable enough to stay present, notice what helps without turning the response into a metaphysical test. Put a hand on the heart if that feels welcome, take a comfortable breath, and keep the room available.

[B11] I don’t have to decide what this is right now. I can notice whether warmth or distance helps, and keep my boundary either way. Nothing here requires my obedience.

[B12] If vulnerability appears—a knot in the throat, sadness, fear, or a sense of trying to protect something—you can bring in the Nurturer. If the experience remains hostile or becomes more frightening, stop engaging with it and return to the body and room.

[B13] Neither response establishes its origin. A protective part needn’t reveal its fear quickly; a comforting response doesn’t prove an interpretation either. The useful question is whether this way of responding helps you become clearer, steadier, and less hooked. Love and boundaries don’t require a verdict first.

[B15] The first impulse may be to fight, banish, hate, prove power, argue, panic, or identify completely with the threatened self. Some traditions use prayers, mantras, protections, banishing rituals, or exorcism forms, and some people find them useful. But fighting from fear can fasten attention more tightly to the thing you want gone.

[B16] Fear gives it center stage. Hatred gives it a hook. Pride gives it an opponent. Panic gives it the nervous system.

[B17] “I do not need to hate this. I do not need to merge with this. I do not need to obey this. I do not need to defeat this from fear. I return to loving awareness.”

[CHANGED, replaces B1-B4]
I speak here from experience.

When you’re doing hypnosis, meditation, lucid dreaming, loveyhuasca-ing, going through a period of intense grief, praying, or whatever else may alter your consciousness, it’s possible you may run into something that you perceive to be a demon, a jinn, a parasitic energy, or whatever other dark entity or terrifying archetype you may encounter – it may be a voice or an image, and it does n’t just feel like an emotion or a younger part of you, but something that feels intrusive, hateful, seductive, mocking, etc.

First of all, it doesn’t matter if they’re demons or not. They may be literal demons, for all you know. Or they may be manifestations of your mind, burdens you’re carrying from previous relationships, whatever. What matters is what is the best way to deal with them.

[CHANGED, replaces B6-B8]
So what do you do when you encounter a demon, or a jinn, or something else that’s bad and intrusive?

First of all, do the usual things – make sure you’re not in danger from a medical standpoint, check your environment, check your body, make sure you’re not overstimulating yourself, make sure you’re not opening your mind too much. If you need to, open your eyes, ground yourself in your body and your environment, remove yourself from any bad influence you’re surrounding yourself with, stop stimulating your mind. Don’t go further into whatever it is you’re doing – don’t be curious, don’t try to figure out what it is.

Tell yourself, “If this can be integrated in this moment, it’s welcome. If not, it can wait until later. The adult part of me is always welcome, and so is my body.”

If you feel like you’re in any danger of doing anything harmful to yourself or others, if you feel like you’re going psychotic and you’re afraid of it, if you feel like you won’t be able to sleep when the self-hypnosis session is over, if you feel overly paranoid, if you feel physical pain in your chest, if you feel like you’re going to have a seizure, if you feel like you’re too hot in a bad way, if you feel like you’ve taken too much of something, if you feel like you won’t be able to function in the world anymore – then the self-hypnosis session is over, and you should get help in the “real world”.

[CHANGED, replaces B10-B13]
If, however, you feel like you can deal with it, check in with yourself and see what’s best to do. Again, it doesn’t matter if it’s a demon or not. Put your hand on your heart, breathe comfortably, and check in with yourself about what to do in this situation.

Internalize the fact that you don’t have to know what it is. Find out if it helps to be warm towards it or to keep it at arm’s length. Keep in mind that you don’t have to do what it tells you to do.

If you feel vulnerable, if you feel tightness in your throat, if you feel sadness or fear or a need to protect yourself from it, call upon your Nurturer. If you don’t feel better, or if it continues being hostile towards you, stop trying to deal with it.

Internalize the fact that you don’t have to know where it comes from. Check in with yourself about whether or not this is helpful. You don’t have to find out if it’s a protector immediately, and you may not be able to comfort it quickly, but work with it as if it were, at least for now.

[CHANGED, replaces B15-B17]
Your first instinct may be to fight it, banish it, hate it, show it that you’re more powerful than it is, argue with it, panic, become your own lesser self in fear of it, etc. Some people may want to use traditional prayer or mantras or protection or banishing or exorcism techniques, and those may well work for them. But if you’re fighting it out of fear…

It only becomes a center of attention if you hate it and therefore give it a hook in you. (To counter pride, or to feel you must panic and defeat it.)

So the next time it comes up you can just say, “I see that there is no reason to hate and revile what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to merge with or become what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to obey what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to try to defeat what I am experiencing out of fear. I choose to return my attention to loving awareness.”
