It may be that an adult capacity you need is not yet available to you, in that case stop trying to make yourself have an inner voice that you believe and if you have enough observing ego, borrow the warmth, boundary or direction you need from a caring someone (real or imagined) who has it. Try out one believable sentence or action etc and see how it goes.

The subconscious will not answer

There may not be an answer. There may not be a helpful answer. It may be that this isn’t a helpful question to ask at all, and you need to try a different one, or rest, or finish. But if you do sense that there’s something you need to consider about trust, what if you asked:

“What would you need to know in order to trust me?”

You might be surprised by how profoundly un-mystical the answer is. “I need to know you’ll set a boundary.” “I need to know you’ll follow through.” “I need to know you’ll stop leaving.” “I need to know you’ll stop putting insight in place of action.”

The inner child will not appear

If you can discern who the guard is, talk to it. If you can’t, then simply stay with the feeling or preference that’s actually there.

“You don’t have to let me in. Just tell me what would make the doorway safer.”

Even if there isn’t a clear child state to talk to, use the session to work with whatever you are aware of (a preference, curiosity, or a beginner’s experiment to try and see how it goes) instead of forcing an image. Building identity and reparenting can happen in the same practice.

You get anxious

Figure out whether your anxiety is manageable and you want to allow it to continue or whether you need less contact with it. If it’s escalating or overwhelming, stop searching for content and help your body first.

Where are your feet? Your chair? Focus on your breath. Be with your body in the room.

Talk to your Little one and Protector parts: “Little one, I’m here for you. Protector, I know you’re trying to protect me. Thank you for letting me know. You don’t have to scream for me to listen. Do you need me to do something? If so, show me one step. If not, can you help my body stand down gently?”

If your anxiety comes with chest pain, fainting, suicidal thoughts, psychosis-like or mania-like symptoms, severe dissociation or not being able to sleep, or makes you want to hurt yourself or someone else, get help from a professional. For a longer list of ways to work with anxiety, see the toolbox in Appendix D.

You dissociate or feel unreal

Open your eyes, move your body, feel something textured, and name five objects. Then finish up for the day.

You become grandiose

Go eat some food, go to sleep, do chores, accept some humility, find somebody who will disagree with you.

Say, “If this insight is real, it will survive sleep, food, time, humility, and feedback.”

You become too analytical

Stop trying to do the practice better. Put one hand on your heart and one on your solar plexis and say,

“I’m not here to do this perfectly. I’m here to be in relationship with what’s deeper than my surface mind.”

“What would help me turn this into a communion rather than a performance?”

Sometimes the answer is to be silent, to rest, to do one honest thing. Do the thing that comes to you.

You become too blissful or diffuse

Bring it down to a specific level.

“What task, boundary, meal, rest, repair, or conversation does love need from me right now?”

You want the hidden truth immediately

Refrain. Say, “First show me the capacity, and you can show me the content later.”

You keep daydreaming

Sit up and open your eyes. Then ask, “What real action am I avoiding with this fantasy?”

You get stuck in spiritual bypass

Ask, “Where is divine love not yet descending to meet my pain?”

You may have had a genuinely profound opening that revealed to you how much more love and freedom are possible. Let that matter, without demanding that the child receive it on command. It’s also possible that prayer left you feeling abandoned or condemned, or that a teacher’s betrayal damaged something sacred. It’s worth asking what was lost and what needs questioning. But doing more of the same may not be the answer.

Sometimes practical care helps while the horizon of your life still feels too small, and to follow your curiosity about deeper happiness without pretending to believe an explanation. Some people already have enough love and meaning for the life they want. Spirituality doesn’t have to be another thing you’re supposed to do.

You get stuck in endless processing

You may have a real wound to deal with, but you may also have a checking loop. Ask yourself, “Does this round help me feel, understand, decide or respond differently, or am I checking again for reassurance?” Sometimes, both in grieving and in practice, it’s fine to cover the same ground again; it doesn’t have to be new every time.

If you discover that you are in a checking loop, then let the thought be there but don’t argue with it or chase it away. Return to some chosen activity or relationship. You don’t have to hold a meeting with every thought that arises. You don’t have to complete some ritual before you can start your life.

Ask yourself, “What direction would the Leader choose now?”

You keep using hypnosis but not changing life

Ask, “What would show my inner child that I’m for real?”

Then do it.
