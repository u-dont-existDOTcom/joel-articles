PUBLISHED section:
[B1] An induction gathers scattered attention until quieter material becomes easier to hear. You don’t have to surrender, play asleep, or obey anyone. When you’re sober, distracted, analytical, or guarded, an actual induction may help. When meditation, loveyhuasca, lucid dreaming, grief, music, prayer, fatigue, or another intense opening has already made you trancey, begin with orientation and containment instead of adding depth.

[B2] Aim for the amount of trance that suits the practice: focused, warm, receptive, embodied, and still able to stop. Depth is optional.

[B4] Prepare the session, gather attention, deepen only when that helps, then let what you notice shape the words and the next response. Counting is one way in. Conversation, story, movement, repeated sound, and curiosity can also organize attention. Say so when you use them; elegance doesn’t excuse a covert induction.

[B5] For a simple, chosen target, you can test the suggestion before deepening. If the useful response is already happening, build from it. An induction helps you find a receptive way of listening; you don’t need to complete a ceremony before you’re allowed to respond.

[B7] Choose one purpose, one starting anchor or image, and a way back. Decide whether warmth, a firm boundary, direction, or neutral witnessing would help. Floating, stairs, gardens, inner rooms, colored light, and child dialogue do not all need to report for duty at once. Switch consciously when the metaphor stops helping.

[B8] Imagery is optional. A signal may arrive through sensation, rhythm, movement, words, spatial sense, simple knowing, or no response at all. Keep the language in the world you chose: let a count mark something meaningful there, and reverse the journey when you finish. Borrow qualities such as warmth or steadiness without replacing your identity.

[B9] Pick one central verb, one sensory quality, one short refrain, and one transition phrase, then read the script aloud; long elegant sentences become soup during an induction.

REWRITE:
[CHANGED, replaces B1-B2]
Hypnotic inductions are intended to bring your attention together so that you can hear some material that you might not hear when you are sober. It is not intended as a place where you surrender yourself, or go to sleep, or need to obey anyone. Sometimes if you are sober and finding it hard to focus because you are distracted or too analytical or putting up with resistance to working on something, an induction can be useful. However if you are already pretty trancey (because you have meditated before hand, or because you are drunk on loveyhuasca or doing lucid dreaming or working on grief or listening to music or praying or tired or whatever) you might want to skip to orienting yourself and making sure you are contained before you go any further.

If you do want to induce a trance make it as deep as you need it to be for you to be able to be focussed, warm, receptive, embodied and able to stop when you need to. It doesn't have to be very deep at all.

[CHANGED, replaces B4-B5]
It's preparation, gathering up your attention, sometimes it's deepening too. It will work better if you make it fit to what comes up for you. Counting is one way of gathering up your attention. You can also do it through talking, or telling a story, or movement, or sound, or encouraging you to be curious about something. If you do it through any of these means, say so. Just because it's done elegantly, doesn't mean it has to be covert.

It may be a good idea to try out the suggestion you are going to make on a simple target, to see if it works for you, before you go to the trouble of deepening. If you have already got what you want, you can always develop a more receptive response to it afterwards, you don't need all the ceremony to make it happen.

[CHANGED, replaces B7-B9]
Decide what you are going to do, and how you are going to anchor it at the beginning, and how you are going to get back. Do you want it to be warm or firm or directed or neutral or what? Don't use floating and going down stairs and going through gardens and going to inner places and seeing a colour and talking to a child inside, even if they all appeal to you! If you need to change metaphors, do it consciously.

It is OK to do this with no imagery, you will get the signal some other way. Try and make the language you use for counting in etc match the world you have chosen. Come back out again by reversing the journey in some way. It is ok to borrow qualities, but don't be somebody else.

Try and restrict yourself to one key verb, or a particular sense quality, or a small bit of text you repeat to yourself, or something, to get you from one bit to the next. Read it out loud before you start. Long beautiful sentences often turn to soup in an induction.
