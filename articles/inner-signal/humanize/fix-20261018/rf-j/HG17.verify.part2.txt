PUBLISHED section:
[B11] Read the induction and Come all the way back while fully awake. Choose the question or quality you want to explore, what you’re leaving alone today, and how much time you have. Love, play, confidence, creativity, and communion are worthwhile purposes; you don’t have to find a problem. Leave several minutes for returning and reflecting.

[B12] Choose a physically secure place without driving, water, machinery, or anyone needing your supervision. When intoxicated, acutely confused, or unable to keep yourself safe, deal with immediate support rather than exploratory hypnosis. The later Already open? practice is for orientation, not permission to intensify an unstable state.

[B13] Decide what kind of encouragement is welcome: spacious listening, warm company, or a firmer invitation to stay with a feeling. Preview eye closure, any repeated exit and re-entry, and the return. For a recording, read the script beforehand when possible. “Stop” ends the exercise; “less” reduces the demand; “quiet” stops the words. You can simply open your eyes and finish. No inner part has to approve your decision to stop.

[B14] When entry and return are unfamiliar, first rehearse them without a difficult subject. Once you know how to change direction, you don’t need to supervise every moment. Let a useful response become easier and less effortful. A no remains no; silence doesn’t authorize a narrator to add something you haven’t agreed to.

[B15] Let the next invitation grow from the response you got—not the response the script expected.Utilization means working with what actually happens: a thought, sound, movement, or objection may change where the practice goes. Notice what actually happens; no change doesn’t mean secret success. Keep safety and exit instructions plain. Appendix B teaches the richer language tools, and Let the next response guide you shows how to use them after the induction.

[B17] Read this slowly into a recorder, speak it aloud, or learn its movement and use silent words if that comes easily. Choose one anchor—a sound, bodily contact, or your two-hand practice—and an opening phrase. Keep using the same anchor where the words refer to it. Pause between paragraphs; don’t read the delivery directions aloud.

[B18] Let your eyes settle somewhere in the room, somewhere they can rest for a little while, and notice the difference between looking for something and letting what is already there come into view.

[B19] There is a surface beneath you. Notice where you meet it … the contact, the pressure, whatever you can actually feel. And while you notice that, a sound may reach you, or a breath may draw your attention, without needing you to arrange either one.

[B20] You can give this a little time.

[B21] A thought about the day may still be here. A thought about whether you’re doing this properly may be here too. There is room to notice a thought and return to the anchor you chose, without finishing the thought first.

[B22] And as you return, you may find one detail that holds your interest … something about the sound, or the contact, or that particular way of listening … and let attention stay with it long enough to become curious.

[B23] Perhaps you can give yourself the time you would give someone you care about who has begun to speak and hasn’t yet found the words. You can remain nearby, listening, without finishing their sentence for them.

[B24] Let your chosen phrase come to mind now. Say it as an invitation, and notice how it is received.

[B25] As you continue, your eyes can close, soften, or stay open. Let that be a choice you make. Listening may deepen while a sound from the room remains available in the background.

[B26] Count slowly from five to one. Give each number its own moment.

[B27] Five. Notice your chosen anchor again. Four. Let attention gather around what you find. Three. Leave a little space after the words. Two. Listen into that space, without preparing an answer. One. Stay here for a moment.

[B28] Bring your chosen concern lightly to mind, or remain with your wish for contact. Let it be present without rehearsing its whole story.

[B29] What am I noticing now?

[B30] Give the response time to take its own form. It might be a sensation, a few words, an image, a feeling, or an impulse to move. There may be nothing clear yet.

[B31] Leave room to notice, with words or without them.

[CHANGED, replaces B11-B15]
Before you start:

Read this induction. Come all the way back again while you are reading it. Decide what it is you want to explore, what it is you want to leave alone, how much time you have. Maybe you just want to explore having more love or play or confidence or creativity or communion or whatever, you don't have to find a problem. Make sure you have enough time to come back out again and think about it.

Make sure you are safe. Don't do this if you are driving or near water or playing with machinery or around people you need to look after. Don't do this if you are intoxicated or in any state where you might be acutely confused or unable to look after yourself - in that case you need immediate support, not exploratory hypnosis. (But see the bit above about already being open, as this is not talking about deepening when unstable).

Practise First decide on how spacious, or how firmly inviting you wish to be. Let yourself know that you will need to close your eyes, and that you may need to come out a few times, as well as coming out at the end. If you are going to record the exercise, it may be a good idea to read the script first, if possible. Remind yourself that you can always stop the exercise by saying 'stop', or 'less', or 'quiet'. Remind yourself that you can always come out simply by opening your eyes, that you do not need to get everyone's agreement inside to finish.

It is wise, until you are fully confident about coming in and out, to practise in a context where you do not need to draw on your maximum efforts. Once you know how to change direction, you will not need to supervise all the time. Your response will get easier, and less effortful. A 'no' is a 'no' and silence does not give anyone the right to add to what is not there.

You will need to develop the next part of your induction, based on what you do get. This is the principle of utilizing what you do get, and it may change what you get. If you don't get anything, then you don't get anything! Remember that you need to lay out for yourself the instructions for safety and for coming out. You will learn more about the language you can use in Appendix B. And you will learn how to use them, once the induction is over, in Let the next response guide you .

Guided Meditation: How to Listen These instructions are for use after you've read the chapter on Listening . It's best to practisise reading it slowly into a recorder, or read it aloud or if you're a pro even learn it and just say the words silently. Choose one of the three anchors to use, and read the instructions using that as 'the anchor' throughout. Also choose one of the opening phrases to use. At the end of each paragraph pause for a few moments. Don't read out loud the instructions for how to read it.

Let your eyes rest for a moment somewhere in the room.

As you do that, notice the surface that you are on, and how it feels to be in contact with it. As you do that, notice how even a sound, or a breath can bring your attention.

Remember that you have all the time in the world to do this.

Even if your attention is on a thought from the day, or even a thought about how you should be doing this, can you bring your attention back to your anchor even if there is a thought there?

In fact, notice one aspect of listening to the sound or being in contact or whatever you are doing to bring your attention which is interesting to you.

Remember that you can take as long to do this as you would give to a loved one if they were talking to you.

Now say to yourself (the opening phrase you've chosen) and notice how you receive that.

You can close your eyes or soften them or proceed with them open as you prefer. Notice how the listening opens up now, but perhaps you can still use the sound in the room in the back ground.

Now slowly count to yourself from 5 down to 1.

5 (notice your chosen anchor once more) 4 3 2 1

Bring to mind your chosen concern, or even just the wish to come into contact.

Now, what do you notice?

Take your time to notice what comes to you, whether it's sensations, words, images, feelings, urges to do something or even nothing at all.

Take your time to notice this, with or without words.

Working when you've already opened a state Before you try to deepen a state it's useful to contain it first.
