Love doesn’t have to wait for trust

If the younger part doesn’t believe you love it yet, you can still love it, when that love is there for you to give. Its trust can question your promises, remember the times you didn’t protect it, and take its time. The love can stay while that happens.

You can start with something like, “Okay. I believe that you don’t believe me. I still love you though. You don’t have to love me back. Tell me what doesn’t feel safe to you.”

That isn’t a way to end the complaint. Hear the truth in it and answer it seriously. Hearing anger without hitting back, admitting a real failure, and making repair after you’ve turned on the child inside are evidence too, alongside the meals, the limits that protect, the appointments and the kept promises. Show your love by not retaliating, but don’t substitute not retaliating for loving the younger part.

Notice what each position teaches the others: a vow of love followed by blame when it’s doubted teaches that love depends on receiving it correctly. And keep in mind that the position that made the vow and the position that blames the child for not accepting the vow may be two separate positions, or they may be the same position, or they may be a blend. Don’t go by chronological age.

When a younger you becomes the defendant in your own head, would it be of any value to try and understand what you understood back then? Would it help to try and appreciate what options you could see and tolerate? Would any useful insight be gained by considering the consequences of your actions? Whilst acknowledging the harm you may have caused, and working to address and repair that harm, remember that punishing yourself now can’t go back and give the person you were then the capacity they were missing.

The two-hand practice

So here is a simple easy little bit of body work for connecting with your inner child. All you have to do is place one hand in the center of your chest. That hand is a physical connection to love. Now place your other hand just below that, at your solar plexis, that is where you can relate to your child.

Now begin sending love down to your child from your heart and just notice what comes up for you. It doesn't matter if something comes up or not, if there is resistance, meet it with love, and if you feel or see something, meet that with love too. Let it be a conversation with your child from your heart, not a performance.

Now remember when you do this, Love is on top and your child is on the bottom and you as the adult are right there with them.

The Nurturer

As a Nurturer, you bring warmth, and one of the most important things you can do is to enjoy your inner child. To sit back and not feel like you have to move their feelings, but actually say to them, “I’m really glad you are here in this moment.” Children need to be enjoyed, not just consoled when they are hurt.

“I know it’s okay for you to feel the way you are feeling.”

“I want you to know that I’m here for you.”

“Right now, you don’t have to do anything.”

“You are not too much.”

“It’s okay to be small. You can be small here with me.”

“It’s okay to be angry. You can be angry here.”

“It’s okay to be sad. You can be sad here.”

“I’m going to be here for you.”

The Protector

Your Protector is what turns that warmth into something the child can believe.

Then set boundaries together - it can be anything from, 'I will leave the unsafe conversation', 'I will stop texting the person who destabilizes us', 'I will eat today', 'I will get some sleep tonight', 'I will keep a small promise', or, 'I won't leave you alone with adult consequences'.

This is where inner-child work often turns into fluffy decoration - people talk lovingly to their inner child, but then send the destabilizing message, skip the meal, avoid the task, and break the promise. Trust begins when you actually handle the consequence.

If warmth isn't available, that's okay, a protective act can start the relationship. Make sure they are small enough to carry out, and look at what actually happened. If you don't do them, work out why - was it fear, exhaustion, lack of skills, a practical barrier, poor planning, etc.? Doing it without feeling better right away isn't automatically failure, and ticking it off a list doesn't prove it helped.

The Leader, Guide, or Guru

Working with the Leader function, who provides direction. You can give it the name you like: Leader, Guide, Guru, Wise One, Sage, Inner Teacher, Director, Higher Adult, etc. The name matters much less than whether it can point the whole system toward a life.

Invite it to give direction, even ask for something difficult, not in a way that expects you to be perfectly disciplined and not have any needs. Ask it to help you find a way to do the adult thing and ask for help, express a preference, allow a safe person to see your imperfections, etc. Find ways to get your need for closeness met without punishing the other person for needing space, and without erasing either of you. Etc.

Affirmations to support the Leader may include: “We are not only here to feel good.” “We are here to become whole.” “We are here to belong to life itself, not only to people who approve of us.” “We are here to serve truth, beauty, love, God, source, nature, universe, dharma, Tao, the benefit of all beings.” Etc.

Begin with the adult function you can actually reach; protection may come before warmth. Be sure that the direction serves your child’s growth, but doesn’t deprive your child of necessary care, play, support, etc.

Working with protectors

Feel free to use this parts language when you actually recognize a protective pattern, or when it’s useful to explore. A sensation, a distraction or an objection doesn’t tell you its own cause. Notice it first: hunger may just need food, skepticism may be reasonable, and a sentence may simply not fit.
