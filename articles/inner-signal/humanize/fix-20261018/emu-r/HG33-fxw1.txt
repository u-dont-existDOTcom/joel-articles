Appendix D: Anxiety — body first

Remember that the anxiety is often coming from a part of yourself that is a Protector who believes they are trying to protect yourself from danger, shame, abandonment, failure, exposure, illness, rejection, losing control, or something that the protector believes will be unbearable for yourself. Rather than getting into a dialogue where it feels as though yourself is interrogating the protector, start with yourself by taking care of the body.

Always work with yourself by taking care of the body first, then the inner child, then doing some inner inquiry work, and then taking some kind of action.

Inner child love

Start by putting your hand over your heart and your other hand on your solar plexis and say lovingly, “Little one, you are not alone. You don’t have to fix this. I’m here now. The adult is listening.”

Don’t say the words, just hoping that they will work on your subconscious mind. Say them to yourself like you are talking to a frightened little child that you love.

Reduced breathing / Buteyko-style breathing

NOTE: There are some good things to be said for slowing down the breathing, for some people who overbreathe or are subject to panic. Some of this is based on the Buteyko approach. However, don’t force breath holds, and creating air hunger can increase anxiety for a short period. Take things slowly.

Try breathing through the nose. Try small inhales. Try quiet exhales. Try pausing if it is comfortable. See if it is possible to quiet the breathing. STOP if this creates dizziness, strain, a sense of panic or any other medical concerns.

EFT tapping

One of the simplest aspects of EFT is that it’s something you can do in your body. If you’re worried about something, you can name it, tap gently on your body, and get in touch with the feeling. There isn’t a ton of evidence, but a 2025 systematic review of EFT and anxiety disorders found seven randomized control trials and noted that EFT was more effective than no intervention in several studies. Still, the evidence should be handled with a bit of caution next to first-line therapies.

Gendlin Focusing

“What is the felt sense of this whole anxiety?”

Don’t answer from your head right away. Give your body time to get one vague sense of the whole thing. Then, ask:

“What would this felt sense like me to know?”

Try out a word or phrase to see if it fits, but also look at what it says about the situation. A word that feels ok might say something that doesn’t fit. It’s good to use both, the words and the body, to correct each other.

Body supports

Sometimes the mind is too activated to let meditation to take care of it. It may be useful to start from the body instead. Consider massage. Maybe massage a foot, or the scalp, or a temple, or the back of the neck, or the top of a shoulder. (Avoid the front of the neck. Avoid injured, numb, bruised, inflamed, recently surgically-operated-on areas, and varicose veins. Stop if it makes the anxiety symptoms or anything else feel bad, such as painful, dizzy, tingly, etc.) Other body approaches that may help include heat, pressure, weighted blanket, darkness, favorite calming music, massage gun used carefully, etc.

Others settle with warmth, darkness, favorite music, massage, weighted blanket, breathwork, favorite object, or personal relaxation technology. Use the support that actually helps, rather than building a tiny wellness trade show around the anxiety.

See my ZHoly3O page for more on the use of ozonated charcoal, “holy water” as one relaxation support. I also mention Blushield there, and both are optional, not at all essential. Neither one proves that any inner signal is true. The important thing is to get the body to a state of stabilization, listen gently, reality-test the messages from the “inner realm”, and come back to one grounded action.

Anxiety script

First, where is my body? What are my feet doing? How am I sitting in the chair? How am I breathing? What is my body doing in this room?

And if you can hear me, little one, know that I am here. Thank you, protector, for alerting me. You do not need to scream. I got the message.

Now if there is real action needed, show me the first step to take right now. And if no action is needed, help my body stand down gently.

I won’t abandon the signal, but I won’t worship it either. I’ll listen, test, and act.
