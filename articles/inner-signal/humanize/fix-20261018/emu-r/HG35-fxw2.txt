Finding a hypnotist who can work with you

Things to ask: What are they trained to treat? How do they use hypnosis in their practice? What will they do if something doesn’t “fit”? If you are working with trauma: Did they actually get training in trauma and dissociation in addition to whatever hypnosis certification they might have? (Remember, credentials matter, but they don’t guarantee competent care!)

Tell them what has and hasn’t worked for you in the past. How direct you want them to be. What kind of touch (if any) you are ok with. What kind of imagery (if any) you are ok with. What to do if you want to stop a session. And ask them what they do about unexpected reactions in or after a session, about things you aren’t sure are memories, and how they do the full return at the end. Keep in mind that a good person should be able to firmly encourage you without making it continue if you say you don’t want to.

Afterwards, look at what actually happened, not the method’s name. Why they used it (if they said). Whether or not you agreed to it. How the person responded to you (again, keep in mind that discomfort doesn’t mean it was “bad,” but seeing your objections as meaning you needed more of the same might be worth looking into). You can ask the app or others knowledgeable about dissociation and trauma to help explain what happened, but it is unlikely someone can tell you if the person was “certified” or not just based on what you tell them. Depending on what you find out, you might want to try self-hypnosis, combining the two, or finding another practitioner. Those stay your choices.

When the subconscious speaks like an oracle

The subconscious can notice things the conscious mind misses. Anticipations, pattern reads, or just things that seem a bit uncanny that come from it should be respected. The subconscious may send out the image of something or tell you to do something, but that does n't mean it is an order.

If it is something big, or can not be undone easily, it needs to be slowed down to the scale of its consequences, looked at with the adult’s questions, and brought to a council.

The council — internal first, then outward

First go within; ask the child in you, the adult in you, the conscious and subconscious minds, your intuition and analysis. But if the thing is larger than any of those, go outside for more help. The bigger and more irreversible the move, the more you need to look outside.

Go to people who love you, people who are like you, or people who have traveled that path. Of course sometimes going alone is the right thing, but I would do that as a last step, after first seeking counsel.
