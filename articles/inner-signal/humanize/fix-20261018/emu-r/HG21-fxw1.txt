The 15-minute daily practice

You don't need to learn a second script for this: it's the same practice (gather attention, listen, respond, wait, return). Fifteen minutes or so is just a convenient appointment with yourself, not a therapeutic dose or a timetable your feelings have to follow. It's enough time to induce yourself (a few minutes), let the middle unfold at its own pace (five minutes of sitting in silence and listening is there when it's useful), and then bring yourself back out again (several minutes). You can interrupt the listening at any time to change direction.

Another simple way in: notice three things you can see, then three things you can hear and three sensations you can feel in your body. Now do two of each, then one of each. Count from 5 to 1 and start listening. Pick the entry that suits you, don't stack inductions.

After practicing a few times with a recording, feel free to cut it down to the cues you still need. Then try practicing without playing it back. But keep using recordings if you enjoy them: being independent means you can guide yourself, not that you have to refuse help. (Note: the optional re-entry cue can shorten an induction you already know well.)

When you come out of the process, ask what needed warmth, what needed protection, and what direction serves truth and love and life. Perhaps there is something to explore, or capacity to build. And what can you carry yourself now? If there is something you need to do, then make a plan with yourself to do that thing. Be concrete and say something like, "If _________ (cue) happens, then I will do ________ (one small action)." Then see how things are later on: how you are sleeping, relating and functioning, not only how impressive the session felt.
