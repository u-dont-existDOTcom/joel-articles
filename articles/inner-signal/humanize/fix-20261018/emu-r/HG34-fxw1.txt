Appendix E: Is the daydream helping or replacing your life?

Sometimes, daydreaming is intuitive. Your subconscious is simulating an idea or event to help you digest grief or teach you something. The act of daydreaming may also be a creative exercise or a way your subconscious is showing you a possible future. Sometimes your subconscious will show you things you’re afraid of. Sometimes it shows you what you want.

Other times, it’s a detriment. An escape from reality. An addiction to fantasy rather than reality. Dissociation. Obsession with romantic partners. An escape hatch from adult life.

How do you know which one it is? Ask yourself, “Is my daydreaming bringing me back into life with more clarity, love and action, or pulling me away from it into repetition, craving and avoidance?”

If it’s helping you, ask: “What is the guidance inside this?”

If it’s taking you away from life, change your body before you start arguing with the story: sit up straight, open your eyes a little, feel your feet. Then ask your subconscious for help. “Hey subconscious, thanks for all these interesting images. If they’re helpful, please show me the guidance inside them. If they’re just a way for me to avoid dealing with life, please bring me gently into the present and help me deal with what I need to deal with in a gentle but structured way.”

Then get embarrassingly practical and ask yourself, “What real-life action is this daydream replacing?”

It could be anything from “send a text message” or “stop sending text messages” to “clean my room” or “eat” or “sleep” or “deal with my grief” or “create something” or “apologize” or “leave” or “tell the truth” or “make a plan” or “get help” or “return to the task at hand” or “stop chasing unavailable love.”
