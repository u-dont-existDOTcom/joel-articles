Induction: gathering attention without disappearing

Hypnotic inductions are techniques to gather your attention so you can get to the good stuff quicker. They are NOT about giving in, playing asleep, being gullible or letting someone control you. The "actual induction" part is most useful when you are sober, distracted, analytical or closed down. If you are already trancey (meditation, loveyhuasca, lucid dreams, falling apart from grief, music, prayer, tired or other intense experiences) skip straight to the "orientation and containment" part.

Generally, you want to go just deep enough to be very focused, warm, receptive, embodied and able to stop when you want to. You can go deeper if you want but it is not needed.

What are you doing when you do an induction?

You are preparing for a session. This can include getting focused, getting deeper and using utilization (shaping what you say according to what you are noticing happening). There are many ways to "get in" - counting is often used, but conversational, stories, movement, sounds, curiosity etc. can work. Using other ways is fine, just mention you are doing it - don't try to be "clever" and do it behind someone's back!

You can do a simple target and test for it to see if you need to go deeper to get the response you are after. If you are getting it, you may not need to. Learning to listen in a receptive way is one of the benefits of doing an induction. It is not a ritual or ceremony that has to be "completed" before you can respond.

Please, no more than 6 metaphors in one trance!

Have a purpose - do one thing. Have a way to start - an anchor or an image that orients you. Have a way to get back. Have a tone - it can be warm and caring or more solid and boundary oriented or it can provide direction or it can be neutral and witnessing. Choose your world / metaphor / image / feeling (floating, stairs, gardens, going to an inner place, seeing colors, talking to a child part etc. It is OK to change them but do it consciously.

You do not have to use imagery. You can receive signals in many ways: sensations, rhythms, movements, words, spatial, knowing, lack of response etc. Keep your language in your chosen world. If you count, relate the counting to your world and remember to do the opposite to get back. You can borrow qualities (warmth, steadiness etc.) but not identities.

Use a central verb, a sensory quality, a short refrain and a transition to put you in your chosen world. Read the induction script out loud. Long elegant sentences turn to soup during an induction.

What to do before an induction

Read the induction and "come all the way back" parts while fully awake. Choose what you want to do - a question to ask, a quality to bring in etc. Also choose what you are NOT going to bring in this session. Have a purpose that lights you up - love, play, confidence, creativity, communion etc. Consider how long you want to spend and allow a few minutes to come back and reflect afterwards.

Make sure you are in a safe physical place - don't do this if you are driving, in the water, near machinery or need to take care of people. Don't do this if you are intoxicated or acutely confused - if you need support, get it. (The section on "Already open?" at the end is designed to orient, not intensify an unstable state).

Choose a supportive tone for yourself - warm and caring, solid, directional or neutral. Prepare for what is going to happen - close your eyes, go in and out a few times and come all the way back. If you are going to use a recording, read the script if possible. Know that you can stop at any time by saying "Stop", you can reduce the demands on you by saying "Less", you can have the words stop by saying "Quiet" or you can just open your eyes and be done. You don't even have to get the OK from any part inside.

If you have never tried this, practice going in and coming back before you bring in a difficult subject. Once you know how to change states, you don't need to be a supervisor to your own life. You can learn to allow things to happen with less effort. The answer "No" will stay "No" and someone won't pop into your head and start talking if no one was there before.

To learn how to shape what you are saying to fit what is happening (utilization), see Appendix B and how to use it after the induction, see "Let the next response guide you". Notice what does happen - sometimes nothing will change and that may mean that the change already happened. If you are not clear about the safety and exit instructions, read them again.

An induction to use when sober (3 - 5 minutes)

Read this slowly into a recorder or read it out loud. You can even do it in your head if you are skilled at silent words. Choose an anchor (sound, body contact, two hands practice etc.) and an opening phrase and stick with them when you come to the anchoring phrases in the script. Don't read the directions aloud. Pause between paragraphs.

Where would your eyes like to rest right now? Perhaps just staring off into the room a bit?

Where are you making contact with the surface beneath you, right now? Can you just sit with that contact a bit, while perhaps turning your attention to some sounds you hear, or the feeling of your breath?

Give this a bit of time.

Even if there are thoughts that come to mind about your day, about doing this right, etc., can you find a way to move your attention back to that anchoring point, while just noticing the thought a bit?

Just letting one particular aspect of that sound, or contact with the surface, or the contact of listening itself, hold your attention for a bit?

Give yourself the time you would give a loved one who was beginning to speak to you but hadn’t quite finished yet. Just being with them while they spoke.

Perhaps by saying to yourself, in your head, some phrase you’ve chosen in advance, and noticing how that phrase is received by you.

You can let your eyes close, or soften, or be open, as you feel you like, while continuing to listen, perhaps letting the sound you’ve chosen become more like background noise.

Perhaps counting backwards from 5 to 1, slowly.

Noticing again your chosen anchoring point, as you count 5, 4, allowing the noticing to gather a bit, 3, allowing a bit of space after you speak the word, 2, listening into the space, 1, and just being there for a moment.

Bring to mind, perhaps, something you would like to bring into contact with, or an issue you’ve chosen to bring to this.

What do you notice in response to that?

Give yourself some time to notice what form that response takes, if any. Sensations, words, images, feelings, moves, etc.

You are already in an open state (caused by loveyhuasca, a previous meditation, an impending lucid dream, an intense moment of grief, beautiful music, prayer, erotic energy, exhaustion, experiencing something of great beauty, etc), and want to contain it enough to then deepen it.

Recognize that you are already in an open state and don’t need to enhance the openness of the state, just aim and stabilize it in a kind way.

Open your eyes. Say to yourself, “As I press my thumb and forefinger together, I will become more and more clear in my body and head. Fractionation (optional)

Once you've taught it, you can use fractionation. It's not good to sneak it in, since not everything needs to be deeper. Here's how to teach it:

Open your eyes and come back up. You can stop the exercise now, if you want to. You can also, if you want to continue, close or soften your eyes, and notice how your attention is gathering again. You can stop again at this point.

You can continue to let your focus gather, if it's useful for you to do so. If it's not useful, you'll be as awake as you need to be.

Picture yourself in your inner meeting place. It might be a room in your heart, a garden, a temple, sitting by a fire with your Protector nearby, at the side of a clear lake, sitting in a chair as your adult self. Just pick one, you don't need more.

Ask yourself who or what might need to talk first.

Trance check (optional)

See if your letting go of the responsibility for making it happen

That if it's useful for you to do so, your wise subconscious can make one of your fingers twitch, pulse, lift up, warm up, tingle or do something subtle to let you know that you are ready to begin meeting with yourself inside.

That you won't force it or fake it, you'll just wait with love and see what happens.

It might be that your finger does something, your hand warms up, you notice your breathing or it might be that nothing happens. None of these proves anything, except perhaps that something inside is listening to you.

Shortcut to get back into hypnosis

This one you should only play with once you know an induction well, and how to come back up from it completely. My dad would often snap his middle finger and thumb together, kind of like a mudra or the OK signal, to help him relax, even while driving.

Pick a word or phrase, and a gesture if you want to. Pick something that is not likely to come up often in normal conversation. When you are in a nice state of hypnosis, not at a peak of emotional intensity, not in a child state or viewing a traumatic scene, but just generally in a state of being more receptive to what you are saying to yourself, say to yourself something like:

When I deliberately use this signal to myself at a time when I have set aside time to do this work, and at a place where it is appropriate to do this work, I will come back into this state of being more receptive to my inner guidance. I can always change that or come out of it, just like I can now.

Then come all the way back up. At another appropriate time, when you are deliberately starting the work, practice using your shortcut. It may take a while to establish, there is no need for it to be instant, there is no need for a specific number of repetitions to establish it, and there is no need for any particular sensation to be associated with it. If you find that the shortcut works well, you can make the longer induction shorter. If you find that it isn't working for you, you can always use the longer induction.

See if it is useful for you, and see if it is selective enough to not be activated when you don't want it to. Make sure that you can change it and that you can come back up from it. It should not be used to make you do something that you might not want to do when you hear the word, phrase or gesture. It should not make you go into a hypnotic state when you see a notification on your phone, hear someone else say the word or phrase, or overhear it in conversation. How fast you get into hypnosis does not determine how deep you can go with the work.

Do not practice or use this to get yourself back into hypnosis while you are driving or doing something that requires your active attention, even if you open your eyes and even if you phrase it conditionally.

If you want to eliminate the shortcut, stop rehearsing it, stop including it in recordings or active instructions in apps and just go back to the original induction. You don't need to say anything to eliminate it, although saying something to yourself when you are fully awake won't hurt. If you find that you are still responding to it when you don't want to, or if you find yourself disoriented, you can always seek help from someone qualified. If you do want to keep it around, you should only use it to start a new session at a later date, and not to alter your state while you are already working.
