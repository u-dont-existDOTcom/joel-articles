Appendix D: Anxiety — body first

Anxiety is usually a Protector trying to prevent something bad from happening, such as danger, shame, abandonment, failure, exposure, illness, rejection, loss of control, or anything else it believes would be intolerable. Don't cross-examine it! Instead, start with:

Body, Inner child, Inquiry, Action.

Inner child love:

Try the hand on heart and hand on solar plexus technique :

Little one, it's ok, you don't have to solve this, you don't have to do anything. I am here. I am with you. You are loved.

Make sure you say this with all your love for the little one, not as some incantation you hope will trick your subconscious into shutting up.

Reduced breathing:

If you tend to overbreathe and panic, you may want to try the Buteyko method of reduced breathing . Be aware that increased air hunger can increase anxiety at first, so go slow:

Inhale a small amount through your nose, exhale quietly, and pause for a little bit before you inhale again. If this starts to make you feel panicky, dizzy, or if you have other medical reasons to suspect it's a bad idea, stop.

EFT Tapping :

This is another mostly bodily technique that may be worth trying while we wait for more research. A 2025 systematic review showed that, at that time, there were seven RCTs done on EFT for anxiety disorders. Although the evidence should be taken with a grain of salt compared to first-line therapies, EFT helped more than no intervention in several trials.

Even though I am afraid of X, I am still willing to love myself.

Gendlin Focusing:

What is your felt sense of the anxiety as a whole?

Again, don't try to answer this from your head. Get a vague overall felt sense of the whole thing in your body, and then:

What does this felt sense need me to know?

Try out some words or phrases to see if they match the felt sense. Then investigate the situation. Just because something matches the felt sense doesn't mean it's correct, but it's a place to start. And if your words don't match your felt sense, you can reexamine your words.

Other things you can try with your body when your mind is too activated for meditation to help: massage your foot, scalp, temple, neck (not the front!), or shoulders; apply warmth or pressure; use a weighted blanket; turn off the lights; put on music that usually calms you down; or use a massage gun (avoid injured, numb, bruised, inflamed, or recently surgically operated areas, as well as varicose veins, and stop if you feel pain, dizziness, tingling, or other bad symptoms).

Different people respond to different things, so find what works for you: lights off, warmth, music, massage, weighted blanket, breathwork, comforting object, or your favorite piece of relaxation technology (ozonated charcoal "holy water" and Blushield are two of my favorites; more info on my ZHoly3O page).

Finally, here's a script to talk yourself through anxiety:

I'll start with my body.

Little one, it's ok, I'm here. Protector, it's ok, I hear you, thank you for looking out for me.

If there's something I need to do, I'll do it. If there's nothing to do, I'll stand my body down.

Either way, I won't abandon you or worship you, I'll just listen, reality check, and do what's needed.
