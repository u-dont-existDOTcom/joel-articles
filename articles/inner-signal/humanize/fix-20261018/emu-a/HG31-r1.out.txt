Appemdix B: Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP)

You don't have to learn NLP to do this work. But, if you feel it would be useful to become more skilled at specific language patterns and sensory processing techniques, you may find some of the NLP information here useful. Just remember: it is a tool, and it is potentially dangerous when used to replace rather than enhance love, truth, contact, and consent. NLP is not to be used as a bag of tricks.

Neuro-Linguistic Programming is comprised of at least four layers of information. The sensory layer deals with changing sensory aspects of an experience, such as the size, distance, brightness, volume, posture, pressure, etc. The state layer is about accessing states, such as warmth, adult, courage, etc. The strategy layer is about rehearsing new, more effective strategies for responding to triggers, etc. The language layer is about learning to speak in a specific way that allows you to pace reality, open awareness, presume useful capacities, make cause-effect links, create embedded invitations, and use de-hypnotic questions to stop vague, absolute, manipulative, and grandiose language.

The Milton Model is part of the language layer. One of the problems with command language, such as "Relax now," "Stop being anxious," "Trust me," etc., is that it activates parts that don't want to comply. So, a key to Milton Model language is to speak in ways that get past the part that is engaged in skeptical arguments. While there is a subtle difference between being subtle about where you want to go and being covert about where you want to go, the Milton Model is geared toward being subtle. A good pattern for parts work is:

Start by pacing what is already true in a low-pressure way; presume small and safe capacities, and invite only one movement at a time. For example, "As you notice your body sinking into the bed, you may notice how it can feel to stop holding up the sky all by itself." Or pace reality, open awareness, validate function, presume capacity, bridge cause-effect gently, invite one small movement at a time, and always test with the body for whether this is useful.

Some specific Milton Model patterns include:

Pacing and leading : Pace by stating what is already true, then lead with an invitation to the future. "Even though you are anxious and your body is activated and this is a protector that wants to protect you, you can begin to listen to your adult self, one breath at a time." The pacing and leading pattern is a simple loop of stating the truth, then inviting a change. "It is completely true that ______________________________. It is completely true that ___________________________. And, it is also true that your adult self is here now and can begin to listen to the anxiety and the __________________________, one breath at a time."

Awareness Verbs : Verbs of awareness include notice, realize, recognize, sense, feel, wonder, imagine, picture, listen, allow, discover, remember, consider, etc. Use these verbs to open awareness without yelling at parts to pay attention. "You may notice whether you can picture it, hear it, feel it, know what to do next, or whatever."

Presuppositions : These are assumptions built into sentences. Always use them ethically to presume capacities such as choice, timing, capacity, safety, love, adult leadership, etc. "Even before you change, you can begin to become more understood."

Cause-Effect Bridges : Words such as as, while, because, when, after, before, which means, etc., connect one event to the next. Be careful not to use these in a way that is too strong. "As you breathe, your adult self can begin to hear the wounded part."

Embedded Invitations : These are invitations to small changes embedded inside of larger sentences. "As you breathe, you can let your adult self begin to listen." Or, "As you sit here in this chair, you may allow yourself to become clear about one small thing that is true." Or, "Even if the small girl doesn't trust you completely, she can begin to notice that you are still here." Don't use more than one at a time, or you'll sound like a scumbag who recently took a weekend certification.

Ethical Double Binds : There are manipulative double binds and ethical double binds. "Would you rather feel a warm glow on your body or have more space around you?"

Conversational Postulates : These are invitations framed as questions. "Is there a way for your system to let you know what to do next without having to re-show you the whole wound?"

Tag Questions : These are questions tagged onto the ends of statements, such as "isn't it," "can't it," "doesn't it," etc. Use sparingly or you will sound like a used car salesman.

10. Questions from the Meta Model - the de-hypnosis tool. Just as you need both the Milton Model and the Meta Model for ethical NLP, you need both for ethical hypnosis. So when you want to zero in on what's really true, just ask questions like

How specifically? According to whom? Compared to what? Is that always true? Have there been times when it wasn't? Is that never false? What specifically would happen? How do you know that? How could you tell? What evidence would convince you otherwise?

and so on. So, you can use the Milton Model to let yourself be softened by hypnosis, and the Meta Model to make sure you're always coming back to what's true.

Anchoring can also be useful - you can use a physical gesture (putting your hand on your heart, touching two fingers together, etc.) or a breath or a word or a posture or a scent or a song or whatever. To create an anchor, first find a state that you want an anchor for. When you're in that state, apply the anchor and say "this is available to you". If you're anchoring a way to go back into hypnosis, don't go for the peak of the emotional state, but instead use a state that's actually useful to you where you're receptive to hypnosis (see the main induction section, "A shortcut back into hypnosis"). You can also anchor a way to be both alert and calm, and you can anchor a way to wake up using synchronicity (see below).

I've talked before about synchronicity . One of the problems is that when you notice one, you usually want to figure out what it means, and that can lead to finding patterns everywhere, often compulsively. One way to deal with this is to use noticing synchronicity as a wake-up bell. It doesn't necessarily mean anything, it just catches your attention, and that's all you need.

Anchor a wake-up routine for whenever you notice a synchronicity. The script might be:

Whenever I notice a synchronicity, it can wake me up out of whatever state I'm in. I can feel my body, feel the room I'm in, and remember that the synchronicity doesn't actually mean anything. I can expand my focus to be broader than whatever state I'm currently in. I can consider what's actually true, separate from my current emotional state. I can see what needs nurturing, what needs protection, what needs action, from my highest perspective, while still being in my body, in the real world, surrounded by other human beings. I can access whatever state is useful to me - objectivity, clarity, ease, peace, freedom, joy, gratitude, etc.

So when you notice a synchronicity, you pause, orient yourself, expand your focus, reality test, listen to whatever you need to listen to, and then take one useful action from love.

That way, you're not being swept up in the state, but you're still able to see if there's something the inner child wants, or something a Protector wants to tell you, or something you need to grieve, or something that's wrong in your body, or something you need to act on.

To install this anchor, imagine noticing a synchronicity - it doesn't have to be a special one, just imagine noticing one. Notice the "whoa" moment, when you realize that you noticed the synchronicity. At that point, feel your feet on the floor, feel your whole body, take a breath, feel the room, and repeat to yourself that noticing the synchronicity doesn't mean it has a message for you, but it is a wake-up bell. Then do whatever you want to do to wake up - ask your Nurturer what it sees, ask your Protector, ask your Leader, etc. Practice getting back to a more grounded state, with a broader perspective. Then, take one action, and fully exit hypnosis, waking up completely.

That way, the anchor is under your control, visible to you, revocable, and narrowly-focused.

This is similar to what in psychology is called implementation intentions . I haven't seen anyone using synchronicity as a cue for an implementation intention, but the general idea has been studied, and there's good evidence that it works (starting with this paper by Gollwitzer).

The problem is that the same mechanism which makes this technique powerful, makes it potentially dangerous. Just like an alien abduction implant, if you rehearse that a synchronicity is a cue, your brain may start scanning for that cue, and if you are particularly suggestible, absorbable, or destabilizable, you may start to see patterns where there are none and interpret them to have a meaning that they don't. Research shows that the same part of the brain that sees patterns, sees patterns where there are none leading to false positive interpretations, and this trait is correlated with psychosis spectrum traits. (See Blain et al. and Grant et al.)

If you find that this technique makes you scan for patterns, lose sleep, feel anxious, think that random occurrences are meaningful, or in any other way, make it harder to distinguish what is really meaningful from what is not, you should deactivate this technique.

You can deactivate this technique by simply stopping the rehearsal of this technique, and saying in a fully awake state, "Seeing synchronicities is no longer going to be a cue for me, I'm releasing that association now." Then simply remove it from your list and stop rehearsing it.

Reframe: Rather than reframing in the way that some NLP trainings suggest, where you take what you think is a bad thing and convince yourself that it was good all along (lying to yourself is never helpful), I prefer to reframe by asking, "What else could be true?" or "Why might this be a good thing?" or to ask other parts of myself like the Nurturer, Protector, and Leader how to reframe it.

Perceptual Positions: Sometimes, I can't see something in a new way until I see it from someone else's point of view, so I put myself in the other person's shoes and see how they might perceive the situation. I also try looking at it from a third person's perspective or the perspective of the universal heart.

Future Pace: After you think you've learned something, think about the situations where you're likely to be tested on what you've learned, and imagine yourself in that situation. Imagine the cue that will trigger the behavior coming up, and then rehearse doing the thing you want to do. "If I see x, I'm going to do y." Keep it simple.

Swish Pattern: This is a classic NLP technique for getting yourself to visualize yourself in a new way rather than an old way.

Parts Integration: When two parts of you are in conflict, figure out what each part wants, then figure out if there's a way to meet both of their needs, or find a third option that does.

How I Do NLP:

Induce or contain

Get permission to use it

Figure out how the pattern you want to change is represented internally (is it visual, auditory, kinesthetic, what posture is the representation in, how big/small, far/close, black and white/color, etc)

Find out why the pattern exists (what is it protecting you from)

Change the representation in whatever ways will make it more adaptive (move it further away, make the voice quieter, slouch, slow it down, etc)

Call on the Nurturer, Protector, and Leader versions of yourself to help with whatever you need

Decide what state you want to anchor, and anchor it with a physical action (pressing your hand to your heart, touching your fingers together, changing your posture, taking a deep breath, etc)

Future pace to test yourself (see above)

Check in with yourself to see if this feels right and if you have other evidence to support the conclusions you came to.

Exit the state and take action.
