Appendix K: Sources and further reading

The stories, arrangement and some details here are my own, as are the adaptations of the approaches described below. You don't need this open when you do this.

Learning, Entering and Returning

Adapted from Healing the Divided Self by Maggie Phillips and Claire Frederick (1995) pages 24-27, 45-50, 83-90 on interactive hypnosis, learning by the senses, self hypnosis and returning to full alertness in a definite way. It's a pro book not self-help, so it doesn't cover everything straightforwardly, and includes some procedures they do with each other that you probably shouldn't. On learning with and from yourself and other hypnostists and recordings see this ISH piece by Adam Eason and Benjamin Parris.

They use indirect techniques, described on pages 53-57 and 96-100, including pacing, utilization, metaphor and associational cues for re-entry. For discussion of those and other techniques see Nimja's hypnotic language tutorial and trigger tutorial . The language here is my own, adapted from those, and the sequence for returning, which is intentional, is likewise. Nimja neither wrote nor endorses this guide.

For body-oriented techniques see In an Unspoken Voice by Peter Levine (2010) ch 5, 7; Freedom from Pain by Levine and Maggie Phillips (2012) ch 3, 4; and Healing Trauma by Levine ch 4 for tracking and working with your body and its resources, titration, pendulation, paying attention to all the levels of sensation, image, behaviour, emotion, meaning.

For more on borrowed adulthoods, getting love without learning to trust it, making things right practically, spiritually, interpersonally, with other parts of you, dealing with spiritual bullshit, separating yourself out from other people, each other, and helping each other as peers, see the Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide .

For more on renurturing, working with the body, and learning the capacities to nurture, see Healing the Divided Self pp148-153, 171-174; and Trauma Through a Child's Eyes by Levine and Maggie Kline, esp. ch 4-5, 10 for how to attune to and work with actual children, appreciating that they are different from each other and seeing what works when you're with someone who needs caring for. But the three-adult arrangement and borrowed-adulthood sequence are my own, and while those authors talk about working with parts, this is my adaptation of their ideas to that approach, not one they've done themselves.

Not everybody has an inner voice . Nedegard & Lupyan, 2024. The study isn't about adapting hypnosis, but the quick start contains some questions you can use to check.

On memory see Trauma and Memory by Levine (2015) ch 7. The VA has a page with cautions about hypnosis including strong reactions and false memories. The American Society of Clinical Hypnosis explains the difference between certification and competence. The links under evidence and finding a specialist are whatever they link to.
