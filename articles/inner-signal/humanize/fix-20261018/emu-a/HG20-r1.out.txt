Let the next response guide you

This post is to be read after the induction and inner meet and is mostly about how to respond when you are actually doing the work. It would be a good idea to read through it before you start and then not have it on screen when you do the actual work.

Responding The most basic way of responding is to notice something accurately and then either offer a new invitation or something that brings in more and then acknowledge what happens and wait. When something comes up that you can notice like a sensation, image, gesture, feeling or words you can acknowledge that, for example:

Finding your own words

It feels like my chest is tightening when I imagine asking for help

Can I get some time before I decide what that means and notice what comes up.

And then just listen. It's perfectly OK to not be very good at constructing beautiful sentences to invite things. In fact it's probably better that you don't. Sometimes it is useful to stop talking when something is developing to let it get on with it without interruption, other times you need the words to understand what is happening.

If you want to be more forceful with yourself then be more forceful, for example:

Don't try to tell me what it means, just notice what you feel when you say that.

Be direct. You don't need to make everything you feel nice, you just need to not feel it the same way and be able to change direction if you want to. Being sad or feeling tension doesn't mean you have to change it.

Separating things out can be hard and they can be jumbled together. It's not essential that you separate them out but sometimes it's useful to do so and ask yourself some questions to check if your interpretations are correct, for example:

When I say "My stomach tightened" that's just a description of what happened inside you. When you say "That person is evil" you are making a conclusion. You may not even be saying it in words but it still counts. So then you can go back and look at what that person did, work out what cues you were responding to and whether there could be other reasons for what they did. You don't need to have said the sentence at the time, saying it now will help you examine it. The fact that something happened inside you and was non verbal doesn't mean that it is either more or less true than if it was verbal. It's just a feeling and feelings are fine to analyse.

Let the response change the invitation

When you notice something like "My shoulders tightened" or "It feels like that sentence is wrong" then you can work out if what you are saying is the right thing to say or not. If what you are saying doesn't fit then work out what does, just like in normal conversation. If the answer is an explicit NO then don't ask for anymore reasons, just change what you are doing.

Why does my shoulders tighten, can I leave it tightening and see if my invitation fits or if there is something else I want to do.

Quite often the sentence was wrong. Other times it's just that the idea that you are looking at is unfamiliar and you need to let that feeling develop. Remember that just because you are skeptical about something doesn't mean that you have an inner child or that it is protecting you, you might just be silent.

Look at the situation

If you are looking at something that involves other people then you can also look at what the other person is doing. It's OK to decide that someone else is not able to take responsibility or isn't emotionally mature enough to have a normal conversation with you. You don't have to be absolutely sure that they are, just that you think that they are, you don't have to repeatedly expose yourself to dangers.

You can also look at what you are doing in the situation. It's OK to feel guilty but remember that feeling guilty doesn't mean that you are doing something wrong or that someone else is trying to manipulate you. Look at what you are doing, are you doing something to someone else that you should address or are you letting someone else make you feel bad so that you give in to them. When you go into an interaction with someone else decide why you are doing it. Do you want to improve mutual understanding, are you trying to convey information or are you trying to say no and leave. You don't have to make someone understand something before you do something.

Change the amount of contact

If you find that you need to reduce contact then try and expand your attention to other things in the room or in your body to bring in some support. Pendulation and titration can be useful to learn about, they basically mean that you alternate between difficult things and things that are more manageable.

of those, you can let be present, here are some tips to help

If at any point you feel you are losing your sense of ‘now’, starting to feel unreal, and you cannot change it or stop it then stop doing the exploring and do the whole Come all the way back thing. If you still don’t feel in touch with reality you need real human help.

If you feel like moving, try a small, physically safe movement in the direction your body wants to move or just imagine making that movement. You do not have to move if you don’t want to or if you feel you need to shake or ‘let something out’. Don’t move if you feel it will cause pain or other bad symptoms.

Give what you are asked for

If you feel younger, try not to make yourself younger still or find out what ‘really’ happened. Just be with your younger self. If you feel like you have a protector, listen to what it has to say, but don’t force it to let you go on. Don’t force any ‘part’ of you to leave.

If there’s anything you need to say, say it, such as: ‘I’m here with you. Do you want me with you or do you want some space?’

If you are told not to get any nearer then don’t get any nearer and explore what it’s like to have said that. If you feel you have different parts of you, such as a Nurturer, Protector and Leader, then you can ask one to speak in the voice of another if that’s appropriate, otherwise just use their function. If you don’t have one of these then borrow one and use it for that function only. You don’t need to use the Witness unless you have to in order to start. If you can start without using the Witness don’t bother to start over.

If it feels like it might help, try putting your hands on yourself, or imagining someone else there, or saying something you think might help, or moving into a different position, or moving further away, or anything else you think might help just to see what effect it has.

Giving yourself love may bring up sadness about not having had enough, it doesn’t mean the love wasn’t there. Explore as long as you want to but don’t force yourself. If it feels like it’s not helping, try something different. If not being able to accept love is a problem for you and the way you’re doing this feels like it’s not helping then try something else.

If you prefer to think in metaphors you can explore things that way but don’t try to find out what it ‘means’. Just imagine what you think might help and see what happens. Don’t interpret what happens, it’s just a useful metaphor. It is not proof of what ‘really’ happened. Just try doing what you think might help if it hasn’t already been tried and see what happens.

If you want to develop more of a good feeling, such as love or playfulness, you can spend the whole session on that if you want to. You can use the time to rehearse something you want to do in ‘real life’.

Finish the session without forcing anything

When you feel like you’ve had enough, stop. You don’t have to find out anything else. You can ask if there’s anything you need to do to ‘put this away for now’. If you’ve talked to ‘parts’ of yourself, thank them for helping you. You don’t have to do anything, if there’s something you want to do in ‘real life’ such as eat something nice, or say no to someone, or rest, or repair something that’s broken, or keep a promise you’ve made to yourself, then that’s fine. If there isn’t anything you need to do that’s fine too.

Remember that you can use your ‘thinking brain’ throughout this. If there’s anything you want to find out the facts about, wait until you’ve come all the way back again. Don’t believe everything you ‘find out’ in this exercise.

When you’ve finished use the Come all the way back thing even if you stopped earlier than you intended. It may help to write down a few notes about what happened so you can remember it. You don’t need to make a detailed note of everything, just a few words to remind you of what happened. If there was a useful phrase that came up, or a bodily sensation, or a need from a ‘child’ part, or something a protector said, or a boundary you need to put in place, or something you need to repair, or something you need to not force yourself to do, write that down so you can remember it.
