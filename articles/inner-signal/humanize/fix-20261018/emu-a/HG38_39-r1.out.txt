Appendix J - Try a few inductions out: a quick guide library

This time, look over the induction sequence, decide if you want to try it and for what purpose, and start it if you wish. (You can always consider converting one of these to be a lighter induction, done with eyes open, or to no-trance contact at all. If there is no apparent response from the induction, that is a response, and you may proceed to the next step. If you wish to stop at any point, move your body, imagine moving, switch sensory channel, stay still, or simply stop, and then proceed to the return, which should be a full waking return each time.

Note that if you are not getting good imagery with any of these, it is OK to imagine them in some other channel: hearing the words, feeling a rhythm or location, physical touch or movement, or even just "knowing" it is happening.

1) Stretching and relaxing. Feel yourself supported and conscious of a limb (hand or foot are easiest). Stretch it out a little and then feel it relaxing into the ground or other support. You can do this by physically moving, or by just imagining it. See what happens (you may feel a relaxation, tension, warmth, a change in your breathing, or nothing at all), and repeat with up to 3 different areas of your body, feeling yourself letting go of whatever you need to let go of, perhaps asking yourself or the limb what it needs or what it has to say. Then return yourself to normal by moving your body as you choose, imagining moving, or some other way.

2) Floating. Notice yourself in your position, perhaps with eyes closed and supported. Imagine yourself floating up a bit, leaving only 1% of your effort to maintain contact with the support, and notice what happens. If you like, you can imagine yourself not floating, and maintain contact with the same weight and pressure you started with. You may wish to fractionate, by deliberately moving your attention to floating on an out breath, and back to your normal level of awareness on an in breath (note that you should tell yourself that this is what you're doing before you start doing it). Notice what you hear or experience at each level. Then deliberately drop yourself back to your chosen level of contact with the ground, feel your weight again, or ground yourself however you wish, and return.

3) Rhythmic breathing. You may use your breath, a hum, tapping a finger, outside rhythm, counting in your head, or even silence, or the deliberate lack of any rhythm. Pick a rhythm and repeat it a few times, perhaps saying to yourself (silently or aloud) "here, and here again". After a few times, deliberately stop doing the rhythm, and see if anything comes to mind when you hear the first silent or non-deliberate beat. Then return to your regular rhythm and return to normal.

4) Worry object. Imagine an object, such as a ball of yarn, perhaps representing a particular problem that has many strands. Unravel it in your mind's eye, or imagine blowing soap bubbles, or some other technique, and set aside each worry or strand where you can see it, perhaps saying its name or just counting it. Notice if there are any that need special care, and continue until you have only one left, or until you feel like stopping. You may also wish to name each part, or leave the object unchanged if you prefer. Then return to normal.

5) Journey. Hear your surroundings, feel your body, and then imagine walking to a place. You may wish to imagine the scene changing, or talk to someone there. Notice the mysterious unfolding of the scene, and then imagine returning along the same route.

6) Spreading wave. Imagine a small part of your body that is neutral to you, and notice what you feel or experience there. Then imagine a wave or spreading of that sensation to another part of your body, and notice what you experience there. (It is OK to imagine it not spreading, and just to keep it localized.) You may wish to repeat this for a few areas, but do not imagine yourself healing any medical conditions. You may wish to ask yourself what each area needs, or notice what happens as you go. When finished, imagine the sensation fading or receding, feel your body and your contact points, and return to normal.

7) Workshop. Imagine that you are creating a workshop for taking care of some patient. Arrange the time, place, tools, and boundaries as you need them. Invite your patient (signal, feeling, child, etc. - use whatever language you prefer here) to come into the room, and work with it as you see fit. You may wish to ask it what it needs, wait and see what it does, reconnect with it, rest, reassess, or do any number of things. It is OK to invite another inner adult or borrowed part to help. You are not trying to make it better, just as good as you. When you are done, thank the patient for coming, return to your body, and remind yourself that you are all one whole, and that you are all adults now. Return to normal.

8) Wise part. Imagine some good qualities you wish to have (wisdom, power, strength, etc.). Imagine yourself having one percent more of that quality, and notice what you experience (movement, words, images, no change, etc.). You may also wish to imagine someone else with those qualities, and just observe them without embodying them yourself. You may wish to repeat this a few times. Then, imagine that quality fading, remind yourself of who you are, and return to normal.

9) Non-trance inner parts contact method. Pacing: Open your eyes, note your environment, the day, feel your feet, read something, write something, just be awake and aware. Deepener: Ask a question, wait, offer a choice to do some or all of: answer, write something, say you don't know, rest, change the question, ask someone else, stop the process. Handoff: note a response, which can be: word(s), feeling, motion, image, lack thereof, dislike of something, etc. Integration: Reality test, take an action. Exit: There is no altered state to exit from, just finish up and get on with your day.
