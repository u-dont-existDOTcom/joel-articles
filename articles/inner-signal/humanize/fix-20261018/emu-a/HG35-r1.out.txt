Appendix F: Self-trust, outside guidance, and oracle-like states

(See Synchronicity Cue above for helpful ways to interpret this.)

Some people are more suggestible than others; some people are more suggestible to outside hypnotists, some to self hypnosis; some to different methods of entraining, in different mental states. Some are not suggestible to commands at all, but may be very suggestible to imagery, music, metaphors, deep spiritual thoughts, etc. There are advantages to working with a professional when you need their skills, their ability to pace things, their ability to contain you, their ability to see things from outside, etc. There are advantages to working with yourself, as you can do followup immediately, you can alter things to what you need in real time, your protectors can trust you most, etc.

Self-hypnosis is probably best for daily communication with your subconscious, and working with a professional who is skilled may be best for complex trauma, blind spots, dissociations, destabilization, working with relationships, etc.

How do you find someone who can work well with you?

Ask around about what sort of training they have with what sorts of issues, how they use hypnosis, etc. If they are working with trauma, make sure they are trained in trauma and dissociation, not just hypnosis. Look at their credentials, etc., but know that even those are not a guarantee of how good they are.

Talk with them about what you need, what has worked or not for you, how direct you want to be, how much touch you are ok with (if any), what imagery you are open to, how you will end the session if you don’t like it, etc. Also ask them what they will do if you have unexpected reactions, aren’t sure if a memory is real or not, or if they can make sure you come all the way out of the session. They should be able to help you come all the way out, even if you tell them you don’t want to, but they shouldn’t continue if you say you don’t want to. They may be firm in encouraging you to work through things, but if you say no, they should stop.

After you have worked with someone, decide if the methods they used worked well for you. Did they explain to you what they were doing and get your agreement? Did they work with you well, or did they just push through your discomfort? If they only pushed through your discomfort, you may want to question their methods. (In fact, if anyone is pushing through your discomfort with their methods, you may want to question their methods.) See if you can find out why they used a certain method. The app can help explain some things, or ask someone who has worked with them before, etc. Remember, you won’t be able to certify someone based on a little bit of work, so if it doesn’t seem like what you want, try working with yourself, working with them in a different way, or working with someone else.

Working with your Subconscious: Your Inner Oracle

When you feel your subconscious telling you to do something, and it has large implications, take your time. Ask questions, work with your council of parts, etc.

Your council may include your inner parts, your children and adult parts, your subconscious and conscious mind, your intuitive and analytical sides, etc. If the implications are large, it may be a good idea to go outside yourself as well. The larger the implications, especially if they can’t be reversed, the more important it is to talk to others.

You can talk to people who love you, people who are like-minded, people who have been there before, etc. While it is sometimes legitimate to go it alone, I recommend doing that as the last step, after you’ve gotten advice from others.

On the other hand, be careful not to give your power away when you talk with others. Just because someone is an authority in an area doesn’t mean you have to trust them, just as it isn’t wise to dismiss them out of hand.

You can also talk with your peers, whether that be in a Hearthwork group, an equal time group, or other place where you have safety, warmth, confidentiality, etc. You can talk with your peers about how you are going to work on something, and they can be your nurturer and protector while you work. They can remind you of your guide when you forget, as long as they don’t take over your guide, and then you can switch roles. Sometimes just asking the right question at the right time can help someone to find their own way. It isn’t up to your peers to interpret your experiences for you, but it may be up to them to ask you the right questions to help you find your own answers.

On Forgiveness, a Paradox or Three (Why not to Bypass)

You can’t force yourself to forgive; you have to understand first. But trying to force yourself to forgive is bypassing, just as staying angry forever is bypassing. You can hold all these paradoxes at once: it was wrong, you still need to hold a boundary with the person, they still need to be accountable for it, and you may or may not forgive them later.

You may come to understand the person better, their conditioning, their history, their pain, their limitations, etc. and be able to understand that, given the total sum of who they were at the time, this is the only way they could have acted. That doesn’t make it right; they still need to be accountable and face the consequences of their actions and work on becoming someone who can act differently. You can still understand their limitations and have appropriate expectations for them.

I will not use spirituality to force you to forgive when you are not ready, but I will also not let you make your entire life around not forgiving.

What really happened? What was the impact of what happened? What boundaries do you need to maintain around this? What led this person to be the way they were? What is this person’s responsibility in this? What is your responsibility in this? What does this person need to do to be accountable? How can you release this without denying the impact it had?

What did you do? What wasn’t your responsibility in this? Did what you do violate your own values? Did it cause harm? Allow yourself to feel remorse if you violated your own values or caused harm. Remorse is not the same thing as saying you are a criminal for what you did. What do you need to do to take responsibility for this? Allow yourself to feel remorse for what you did. What do you need to do to rectify what you did or to prevent you from doing it again? Once you have taken responsibility, how can you stop beating yourself up for what you did?
