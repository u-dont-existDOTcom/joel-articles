PUBLISHED section:
[B0] When this needs more support than a guide can provide

[B1] Use self-hypnosis as a relationship with yourself. When the situation needs actual care, the guide shouldn’t become a substitute for it.

[B2] Get urgent help now for immediate danger, severe confusion, inability to keep yourself safe, or concerning physical symptoms such as chest pain or fainting. New or worsening suicidal thoughts, severe insomnia, mania-like or psychosis-like symptoms, or dissociation that disrupts functioning need prompt professional assessment, not a four-to-twelve-week practice schedule.

[B3] For ordinary learning, start with shorter sessions, warmth, a manageable subject, and a full return. Over a week or two, track one target—anxiety, daydreaming, relationship repair, an avoided task, sleep, emotional regulation, or intuitive accuracy. Worsening function or growing certainty about unverifiable memories calls for reassessment, not more intensity.

[B4] Seek suitable ongoing help for recurring panic, compulsive behavior, substance-related destabilization, or major relationship instability.

[B5] Methods to explore with a trained person present

[B6] Use a trauma-informed clinician trained in the method, present with you, for deliberate trauma-focused regression, searching for unknown traumatic origins through an affect or somatic bridge, or work with substantial flashbacks or dissociative reactions. A bridge follows associations from a feeling or sensation; regression revisits recalled or imagined younger experience. Either can have different uses, including resourcing. Ask what is intended and how uncertainty and unexpected reactions will be handled. Hypnosis cannot prove a historical event, even with a clinician present.

[B7] Trauma history, tears, or wanted difficult feelings don’t automatically exclude self-practice. Choose the working range with attention to the actual response and available support. Some people need a narrow range; others much broader. Confidence alone doesn’t supply missing support.

[B8] Major trauma guidelines emphasize treatments such as trauma-focused CBT variants, CPT, PE, and EMDR depending on the person and guideline. Self-hypnosis and inner-child practice may support that process, but high-risk trauma work needs a larger container.

[B9] If the practice makes ordinary life more loving, honest, functional, relational, grounded, and precise, continue gently. If it makes ordinary life less stable, stop deepening and return to sleep, food, grounding, boundaries, and outside support.
