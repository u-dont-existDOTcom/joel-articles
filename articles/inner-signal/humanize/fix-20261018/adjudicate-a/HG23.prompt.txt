NOTE: If it is medically unsafe for you to do this, or you are panicking, sleep deprived, nauseated, deeply destabilized, manic, in a psychosis-like state, or overwhelmed by chemicals, please stabilize first before seeking out what your subconscious needs you to know. It is not ready for you to know directly if it requires you to destabilize to know it! Ask for capacity, not content.

There are times when your subconscious seems to know something that your conscious mind can’t handle yet. It may be about your sexuality, a grief, trauma, your family, betrayal, shame, vocation, a relationship, fears about illness, spirituality, an addiction, your identity, or a moral issue.

What you don’t want to do is bully your subconscious with statements like,

“Why don’t you just tell me?” “What are you hiding?” “Tell me right now!” or “I demand that you reveal the truth!”

This can lead to flooding, panic, false certainty, confabulation, dissociation, and just generally making your day suck more. When you’re flooded, it can be hard to access your adult capacities to pause, compare options, reality test, plan and make choices for yourself. In that state, regulation and support come before going deeper. The silence may be protecting you while your adult doesn’t have enough regulation, support, humility or practical capacity to take it in. Ask for the next piece you can tolerate:

“Do I need to know the content now, or do I need to build capacity first?” “What do you need for my conscious mind to be able to know what you’re saying 5% better?” “Can you tell me through a symbol, not the whole revelation?” “Can you tell me what you need me to do to be safe without telling me what it is?” “Do you need me to get support to be able to know what it is? Who?”

You may even want to say, “Dear wise subconscious mind, if there is something you know that my conscious mind can’t handle, please don’t break it open violently. Help me become someone who can know it safely. Prepare me before you reveal it, ground me before the memory, support me before the intensity. Give me one next thing to do.”

REMEMBER: Hypnotic imagery can mean something without being literal, especially around trauma and memory. The mind turns things into symbols, squashes them together, distorts and dramatizes them, and borrows from culture and fear. Treat an image as an image, and get outside support when you need it.
