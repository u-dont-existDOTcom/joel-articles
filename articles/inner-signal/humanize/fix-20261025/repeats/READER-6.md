## 1
SECTION: Hypnosis Evidence
QUOTE: DO NOT use Self Hypnosis to help you: force yourself to remember trauma, force yourself to “prove” trauma through memories, suppress medical symptoms, de-stabilize your self, or NOT seek correct treatment.
KEPT: DO NOT: Attempt to “force” memories, to use trance to “prove” something happened to you, or to not get medical attention for symptoms, because you can “force” them down using hypnosis. [What not to do]
WHY: An early preview of the don't-list that What not to do then gives in full (no forcing memories, no proving trauma, no skipping medical care).
LOSES: An early warning for readers who stop before the end, and the DO line is left without its contrast; only "de-stabilize your self" has no twin in the list (Capacity before content covers it).

## 2
SECTION: A shortcut back into hypnosis
QUOTE: There is something you should never do while using this induction. And that's use it, practice it or test it while you're driving or doing something where you need to be actively paying attention. It's just not safe to be going into a trance where you are actively paying attention to what's going on inside of you when you need to be paying attention to what's going on outside of you. Even though your eyes can be open, even though you're using "if it would be useful for you to do so" type language it's just not safe.
KEPT: DO NOT: Participate in self-hypnosis while operating a car, bathing, swimming, operating any equipment, supervising children, or engaged in any activity which requires active attention. [What not to do]
WHY: The no-driving, no-active-attention ban a third and fourth time (also Before you begin step 3, and the "don't do that!" aside earlier in this same section).
LOSES: The ban tied to this cue, and the point that open eyes and permissive wording do not make it safe; the aside above and the list still carry the ban.

## 3
SECTION: Finish without forcing a revelation

The mind makes it symbolic, squashes things together, distorts, dramatizes, pulls from cultural influences, fears etc. So, treat it like an image and get some help outside of yourself! [Capacity before content]
WHY: Images are not literal memories or proof of what is going on, said here and again under Capacity before content (and "likely to be metaphorical" in Offer the care that is actually wanted).

## 9

QUOTE: DO NOT use self-hypnosis to try to keep the altered state of consciousness going indefinitely, to try to recover memories, to make yourself MORE afraid, to help you not need to sleep, to help you not need to take care of medical problems, to help you become LESS communicative, etc.
KEPT: DO NOT: Attempt to “force” memories, to use trance to “prove” something happened to you, or to not get medical attention for symptoms, because you can “force” them down using hypnosis. [What not to do]
WHY: Prolonging the state, recovering memories and ignoring sleep or medical needs, all already listed under What not to do (the "Extend the duration" and "ignore your body's needs" items).
LOSES: "Make yourself MORE afraid" and "become LESS communicative" have no twin in What not to do; otherwise nothing.

## 10
SECTION: Appendix G: Altered states
QUOTE: In other words, if using self-hypnosis while you're in an altered state of consciousness is NOT helping you become MORE loving, MORE grounded, MORE honest with yourself, helping you SLEEP, become MORE communicative, MORE able to do your everyday responsibilities, etc, then stop trying to go DEEPER into your altered state of consciousness, and start working on integrating what you've already gotten from it.
KEPT: Always keep in mind that your functioning in ordinary life is the feedback. If you find yourself more loving, more honest, more functional, more relational, more grounded, more precise, etc. than when you started this process, keep on keeping on gently. If you notice that your ordinary life is becoming less stable, don't try to go deeper. [Methods to explore with a trained person present]
WHY: A summing-up of the DO NOT line before it that also restates the main-body rule: judge the practice by ordinary life, and do not go deeper when it is not helping.
LOSES: The advice to switch to integrating what you already got; the next section, Stabilize before you interpret, makes the same turn toward grounding.
