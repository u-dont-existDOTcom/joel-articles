## 1
QUOTE: Know that you can say stop at any time and the induction will end. If you want to change something, say less if you want it to be asking for less from you, or quiet and the talking will stop.
HARD: "say less ... or quiet" has no quotation marks and the second "say" is missing, so "say less" reads as "talk less" and "or quiet" hangs without a verb; the reader has to work out that "less" and "quiet" are words to say, like "stop".
SCORE: 2
PLAIN: Know that you can say "stop" at any time and the induction will end. If you want to change something, say "less" if you want it to be asking for less from you, or say "quiet" and the talking will stop.

## 2
QUOTE: Not that I recommend driving in a trance, don't do that! Make sure that your relaxation/ stay alert cue is not the same as your go inward to hypnosis one.
HARD: "cue" is new here (the shortcut itself only comes in the next paragraph), "relaxation/ stay alert cue" squeezes two things into a slash, and "your go inward to hypnosis one" hangs a long phrase before "one", so the reader can't tell what has to differ from what, or why.
SCORE: 2
PLAIN: Not that I recommend driving in a trance, don't do that! Make sure that the word or motion you use to relax or stay alert is not the same as the one you use to go inward to hypnosis.

## 3
QUOTE: As you move one of your hands, you're going to move deliberately, and as soon as you stop moving deliberately, you'll focus on what you've got.
HARD: "move one of your hands ... move deliberately" says "move" twice before saying what is being done, and "what you've got" has no referent (got what?); the exercise only becomes clear from the script that follows.
SCORE: 2
PLAIN: You're going to move one of your hands deliberately, and as soon as you stop moving it deliberately, you'll focus on what's still there.

## 4
QUOTE: No will stay no. The narrator can't add stuff just because you don't say anything.
HARD: "No will stay no" can be misread (a noun "No will"), "the narrator" was never introduced (it is the voice reading the induction), and "add stuff just because you don't say anything" packs the idea that silence isn't consent into a few vague words.
SCORE: 2
PLAIN: A no will stay a no. The narrator (the voice reading the induction, even if that voice is yours) can't add stuff just because you don't say anything.

## 5
QUOTE: Choose a sound, or a contact, or the two hands thing (below) or something to anchor yourself and the thing you will say to open it up.
HARD: One run-on list: "and the thing you will say to open it up" attaches to the list of anchors, so the reader can't tell it is a second choice, and "it" in "open it up" has no clear referent (the same thing is only named "the phrase that you chose" a few lines later).
SCORE: 2
PLAIN: Choose something to anchor yourself, like a sound, a contact, or the two hands thing (below), and choose the phrase you will say to open it up.

## 6
QUOTE: (The later part of this can be used to help orient you if you're already open, but don't use it to make yourself deeper if you're not stable.)
HARD: "The later part of this" doesn't say which part, and this is a safety note, so the reader can't tell where to go if they are already open but not stable.
SCORE: 1
PLAIN: (The Already open? Contain before you deepen part below can be used to help orient you if you're already open, but don't use it to make yourself deeper if you're not stable.)

## 7
QUOTE: When you're writing this, think about what the verb you're using is, what the sensory quality is, what the short refrains are, and how you're going to transition between ideas.
HARD: "writing this" doesn't say what is being written (your own induction), and "what the verb you're using is" is compressed enough that the reader can't tell which verb or why it matters.
SCORE: 1
PLAIN: When you're writing your own induction, think about which verb you're using, what the sensory quality is, which short refrains you'll repeat, and how you're going to move between ideas.
