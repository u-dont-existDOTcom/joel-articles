## 1
SECTION: Forgiveness without bypass
QUOTE: For self-forgiveness, name what you did, but don't take responsibility for what wasn't yours.
MOVE: HEADING
CHANGE: Put a heading, "Forgiving yourself" (marked "## " and in sentence case like the other headings in this part), before the paragraph that begins with the quoted sentence. It sits over the last two paragraphs of the section: that one and the next, which begins "Take responsibility, feel the remorse". No words in either paragraph change.
WHY: The section is about forgiving someone who hurt you and then turns to forgiving yourself with no break, so a reader scanning the headings can't find the second subject (question 2).
KEEPS: Both paragraphs word for word, including "name what you did, but don't take responsibility for what wasn't yours" as the first line under the heading (the opener "For self-forgiveness," stays even though it echoes the heading). The existing heading and everything above it stay. The new heading names everything under it: naming what you did, not over-owning it, remorse, repair, letting go.

## 2
SECTION: Forgiveness without bypass
QUOTE: Take responsibility, feel the remorse, then rectify if you can, and then let it go.
MOVE: CUT-REPEAT
CHANGE: Cut that sentence, the first of its paragraph. The very next sentence in the same paragraph already carries all of it at greater length: "Be specific about the action you took, and the impact it had, feel the remorse, do what you can to rectify or keep it from happening again, and then stop holding yourself hostage." (owning the action = "Be specific about the action you took"; "feel the remorse" is in both; "rectify if you can" = "do what you can to rectify"; "let it go" = "stop holding yourself hostage"). The paragraph then opens at "Be specific about the action you took,". No other words change.
WHY: The reader gets the same four steps twice in a row, short and then long, before the paragraph adds anything (question 5).
KEEPS: The long sentence untouched, with its one addition ("or keep it from happening again") and its closing line about holding yourself hostage. The paragraph before it (name it, don't over-own it, don't become "a permanent criminal") stays as it is. I am not proposing to cut the long sentence too, even though it still re-walks that paragraph, because it adds repair.

## 3
SECTION: A response check, not a verdict about what it is
QUOTE: Internalize the fact that you don’t have to know where it comes from.
MOVE: CUT-REPEAT
CHANGE: In the section's last paragraph, cut the first two sentences: "Internalize the fact that you don’t have to know where it comes from. Check in with yourself about whether or not this is helpful." Both are said earlier in the section: "Internalize the fact that you don’t have to know what it is." and "Find out if it helps to be warm towards it or to keep it at arm’s length" (paragraph 2), and "check in with yourself and see what’s best to do" (paragraph 1). The paragraph then opens at "You don’t have to find out if it’s a protector immediately, and you may not be able to comfort it quickly, but whether warmth or distance helps, that doesn’t prove what it is." No other words change, and nothing moves.
WHY: After the instruction to stop and return to the room, the section goes back over two steps it already gave, so the reader re-reads instructions they have (question 5).
KEEPS: The stop instruction that ends the previous paragraph ("If you don’t feel better, or if it continues being hostile towards you, stop trying to deal with it and return to your body and the room."), exactly where it is. The last sentence of the paragraph in full: the permission not to identify it or comfort it quickly, and "that doesn’t prove what it is", which is the heading's promise. Paragraph 1's "Again, it doesn’t matter if it’s a demon or not." stays too, since it re-anchors the reader right after the exit list.
