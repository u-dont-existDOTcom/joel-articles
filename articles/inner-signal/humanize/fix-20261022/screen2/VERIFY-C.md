## Pair 1

"Script" below means the quoted first-person lines the reader says to themselves. Points are numbered in the published order.

1. Use the checklist when an influence is pressing too hard. KEPT.
2. The influence may be a person, advertisement, ideology, spiritual teaching, romantic pull, or inner voice. KEPT (all six named; list opened, see ADDED).
3. Script: "I notice influence is happening." KEPT ("I'm being influenced here"). The script is now offered as "something like", not as fixed words.
4. Script: "I do not need to obey it or attack it." KEPT.
5. Script: "I can slow down." CHANGED: now "for a minute", which read literally makes the pause short; the published gives no length and later points to sleep and time.
6. Script: "I can ask what is being presupposed." KEPT ("what it's taking for granted").
7. Script: "I can ask who benefits." KEPT.
8. Script: "I can notice what my body is signaling without assuming what it proves." CHANGED: the hedge stays, but the step of noticing becomes the statement "My body's telling me something", so a signal is taken as already there.
9. Script: "I can ask what love, truth, humility, and ordinary life would require." KEPT ("need from me").
10. "If this is true, it will survive questions." KEPT ("Can it handle your questions? Something true can.").
11. "If this is love, it will survive boundaries." KEPT ("Love can live with your boundaries").
12. "If this is guidance, it will survive sleep." KEPT ("real guidance will still be there after you sleep on it").
13. "If this is for me, it will not require me to abandon my adult self." KEPT (stated the other way round, which is logically the same: "If it asks you to give up your adult self, it's not for you").
14. Lines 10-13 are part of the same script the reader says to themselves. CHANGED: they now sit outside the quote as the author's statements under "Then test it a little", so the reader is no longer given them as lines to say, and "a little" makes the test sound light where the published says what the thing must survive.
15. "Before acting, ask:" KEPT ("Before you do anything, notice if..."). The later groups open with "Other things to notice" and carry no timing of their own.
16. Urgency, secrecy, shame, threat of exclusion, promise of specialness, sexual charge, spiritual superiority. KEPT as items; framing CHANGED, see 24.
17. Demand for money, sex, loyalty, silence, or obedience. KEPT (all five); framing CHANGED, see 24.
18. Narrowing of choices. KEPT (wording only: "urging you to limit your choices" names an active push, where a narrowing could also happen without anyone urging it).
19. Questioning treated as betrayal. KEPT (wording only: "framed as bad (a betrayal)"; "bad" is wider, "framed" covers words more than actions).
20. Resistance pathologized. KEPT (wording only: "treated as bad (pathologized)"; the term survives in brackets).
21. Ordinary life being downgraded. CHANGED: reduced to two specific framings ("too small", "something you're supposed to outgrow"); other ways of downgrading it are no longer named.
22. Child part seduced, scared, or rescued. KEPT (all three verbs; "inner child" for "child part", same referent as far as I can tell).
23. Adult self still allowed to say no. CHANGED: "allowed" (a permission the situation could withhold) becomes "remain an option"; "adult self" becomes "being an adult (and saying no)", so the no is now a bracket.
24. The audit items are neutral features to look for ("Is there...?"), whoever or whatever produces them. CHANGED for the first group (16-17): they are now "tricks... being used", i.e. deliberate tactics, so urgency, shame and the like that come from the situation, a romantic pull or one's own inner voice may no longer register.
25. Several yes answers mean slow down. KEPT ("If you see several of these, slow down").
26. Don't deepen trance. KEPT.
27. Don't make an irreversible decision. KEPT ("a decision you can't undo").
28. Return to the room, body, food, sleep, time, and counsel. CHANGED: the six are no longer a set; room and body come first "for a while", then "Then" the rest in sequence; "get some counsel" for "return to... counsel"; list opened with "and so on".
29. A good guide increases your adult self. KEPT (framed as a way to tell: "You can tell a good guide because your adult self gets stronger with them").
30. A bad guide replaces it. KEPT ("They take your adult self's place").
31. A good guide can tolerate your no. KEPT (implied by "can they take your no? A bad guide can't").
32. A bad guide calls your no resistance, ego, fear, low vibration, trauma, sin, or proof that you need more of their guidance. KEPT (all six labels and the proof clause; list opened, see ADDED).

LOST: none.

ADDED:
- "anything like that" after the list of sources: ADDED (the trigger is no longer limited to the six named; low stakes).
- "for a minute" on slowing down: ADDED-CLAIM (a reader may take a short pause as the step).
- "(usual) tricks... being used": ADDED-CLAIM (says these are the usual tricks and that someone is using them deliberately).
- "Just... for a while" on returning to room and body: ADDED (a duration and a minimizer).
- "and so on": ADDED (open list of things to return to; low stakes).
- "you name it": ADDED (open list of labels a bad guide uses; low stakes).

VERDICT: CHANGED
