# Stance check: guards, the inner adult's three jobs, the inherited parent script, the pre-induction safety line

I checked every position, rule and claim in the rewrite against the whole published guide and against Joel's positions stated after publication (2026-10-21 and 2026-10-22).

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1.** Rewrite (inner adult's three jobs): "what you need to develop in yourself in order to be able to reparent your inner child". Later (Working with childhood protector parts): "your inner adult (Nurturer, Protector, Guide) that you will eventually build up".
Essay: "They’re three ways the present-day person reparents the inner child" (The inner child, the guards, and the three inner adults); "Begin with the adult function you can actually reach" (The Leader, Guide, or Guru).
Reader would believe: reparenting has to wait until the inner adult is built. The essay says you start now with whatever function you can reach or borrow.

**2.** Rewrite (When "your voice" is everybody else's voice): "Give your inner child the opportunity to do something that has no value on its own"
Essay (same section): "a small experiment with no résumé value"; "We’re finding out whether we enjoy this, not proving our worth." The rewrite's own list also includes volunteer work.
Reader would believe: the activity should be worthless in itself. The essay means it has no achievement value and may be enjoyed for its own sake.

**3.** Rewrite (Appendix B, the NLP sequence): "Put 3 good adults in with the person (Nurturer, Protector, and Guide)."
Essay: "They aren’t three literal beings" (The inner child, the guards, and the three inner adults). Joel, 2026-10-22: "Three functions of that same Inner Adult—not three additional people". It also clashes with the rewrite's own "I don’t mean them as if there were really three people in you".
Reader would believe: these are three separate adults placed with someone.

**4.** Rewrite (What not to do): "the “Protector” to preclude all loving relationships, the “Nurturer” to disregard consequences to others"
Essay: "the Protector cannot exclude all love; the Nurturer cannot erase consequences" (What not to do); "I will not leave the child alone with adult consequences" (The Protector).
Reader would believe: the Nurturer is limited only where other people get hurt, so it can soothe away your own consequences. The Protector is limited only in outside relationships, which leaves out love toward the child.

**5.** Rewrite (Appendix B, synchronicity): "I go into the highest state I can get into where I can objectively and clearly see what is going on"
Essay: "I return to the highest perspective I can access"; "I make room for whatever is genuinely available … I do not have to manufacture any of them" (Using synchronicity as your own wake-up bell). The cue is "limited to orientation" (Installing the synchronicity cue). The rewrite's own last sentence ("may include objectivity, clarity") takes the claim back.
Reader would believe: Joel's cue takes him into a peak state that reliably gives objective, clear sight. In the essay it orients him and leaves room for whatever clarity is actually there.

**6.** Rewrite (Appendix G): "Regardless of how scary an image is, regardless of the lighting … regardless of how “bad” you feel, it doesn't necessarily mean what you think"
Essay (Appendix G, Stabilize before you interpret): "A frightening image isn’t automatically truth, a bad feeling isn’t automatically a lesson"; a download "doesn’t deserve belief merely because it came with excellent lighting".
Reader would believe: there is one loose warning ("may not mean what you think"). The essay makes three separate claims: fear is not evidence of truth, a bad feeling is not automatically a lesson, and vividness is not a reason to believe.

**Guards called the Protector.** Joel's 2026-10-21 position is that the guards are not the Protector. On 2026-10-22 (q9) he called anxiety "a childhood protector part".

2. Appendix D, Anxiety script: the same line. The rewrite doesn't cover it, so it's still there.
3. Appendix D, opening: "Anxiety is often a Protector trying to prevent danger". The rewrite fixes it.
4. Appendix G, Stabilize before you interpret: "Sometimes a Protector is showing fear". The rewrite fixes it.

This comes right after the three adult-function sections, and only a capital letter tells the guard from the Protector. Read aloud, nothing does. 7. Offer the care that is actually wanted: "When a protector is recognizable, listen to its concern" comes a few lines before "the Protector can carry out a boundary". The rewrite covers only the second paragraph, so the confusable pair is still there.

8. When "your voice"…: "The inherited parent may demand immediate talent. The adult can answer". The rewrite has Joel's fix.
9. Sometimes start with the whole: "it doesn’t establish that a perfectly capable inner parent is already available". Here "inner parent" means the inner adult, but Joel's newer names for the caregiver script are "inherited inner parent" and "inherited parent script". Both conflict with Joel's convention ("not necessarily a literal voice") and with Quick start ("The child and protector needn’t speak in sentences either"). The rewrite fixes both.

11. Heading: "The inner child, the guards, and the three inner adults". The rewrite fixes it.
12. Appendix B, Running the whole NLP sequence: "Bring in the three adults." The rewrite still has it ("Put 3 good adults in").
