Quick start: do this first

P1To work on difficulties or cultivate positive qualities (love, curiosity, play, confidence, etc.), simply do these steps, in order: 1) Gather attention; 2) Notice what is here; 3) Offer a fitting response; 4) Wait; 5) Come all the way back. Not much needs to happen in each session!

P2Prior to your first session, read through the induction and the full return to waking life, so you’re more familiar with them. Then put the screen away. You’re learning to run the practice yourself, not reading from a teleprompter like a news anchor person.

P4If at any point you feel panicky, disoriented, more unreal, or less functional, this is not the time for bravely sticking with difficult feelings (that's another choice, made freely & while staying present). Stop deepening, return to the room & get appropriate support.

What I mean by hypnosis

P1APA Division 30 commonly defines hypnosis as focused attention, reduced peripheral awareness, and increased responsiveness to suggestion. That’s basically true, but there’s a lot more than meets the eye. In fact, hypnosis is such a broad and common type of altered state that it’s hard to confine it to one use. But I’m using hypnosis here as a therapy.

P2And hypnosis as a therapy is as old as time itself. While the modern technique of it was discovered by Franz Anton Mesmer in the 1770s, shamans have been using dancing, music, and drugs to induce hypnosis for therapeutic purposes forever. We enter into trance states every night which resemble hypnosis as we go to sleep and as we wake up as well. Those states can also be used for deep therapy.

Affirmations Done Right

P1My dad always said: “Always, and in everything, expect the very best. But be prepared to take what you get. The best might come afterwards.” That’s a better affirmation than what most people think of as affirmation, because it’s not telling you something which you know to be false in the present. If you say “I am beautiful” but you know that’s not true, then you’re challenging your beliefs, and if you’re challenging your beliefs you should have some argument or reason why they are wrong, otherwise it’s bullying.

P2Another quote I use all the time from my dad… when someone asks me “How’s it going?” I say “Better & better!” This really confuses a lot of people. Some think it’s amazing that I’m always doing better. Some ask if I was sick or something. But it’s doing multiple jobs. On the one hand, it’s deflecting from the fake question because most people don’t actually want to hear how you are doing in detail anyway. On the other hand, more importantly, it’s reprogramming your mind to remove you from tunnel vision. You might be sad now, but if you say, “I am sad” then you’re subtly identifying with the sadness and it keeps you stuck. It’s best to say, “I’m feeling sad now, but I’m doing better and better, every day, in every way!” That’s more like a mission statement than a historical analysis. Although you can also re-evaluate your history as lessons to make that true.

Hypnosis Evidence

P1Is there real (not magical) evidence for hypnosis? Yes. In 2024, there was an overview of 49 meta-analyses containing a total of 261 distinct primary studies. Evidence was strongest for pain, medical procedures, and work with children and adolescents, but varied with different outcomes and study design quality.

P2Is there evidence for self-hypnosis? Yes. There has been a systematic review of Randomized Controlled Trials of self-hypnosis, finding benefit in studies of pain, childbirth, children, stress, anxiety etc. Self-hypnosis seemed more useful when people learned it as a real skill and practiced it several times than when they just listened to an audio file.

P3Here I will be teaching you a real skill, so you don’t end up a consumer of more and more elaborate trance recordings.

What to Use Self-Hypnosis For, and What Not To

P1The Mayo Clinic warns that using hypnosis to deal with stressful events from earlier in life may bring up strong emotions and that there are other types of treatments that may be more helpful with historical trauma.

P2Use self-hypnosis to help you: regulate yourself, talk to yourself, reparent yourself, gain clarity, change habits, make your life safer in the now, integrate emotions, and take action in ordinary life.

P3Don’t use self-hypnosis to: force yourself to remember trauma, force yourself to prove trauma through memories, push symptoms down instead of getting medical attention, destabilize yourself, or replace appropriate clinical care.

A useful map of the mind

P1Note: whenever I refer to the subconscious, I’m referring to all of the processes under the surface of your conscious awareness: perceptions, emotions, desires, avoidance tendencies, imagery, memories, body signals, habits, intuitions, automatic behaviors and so forth. You can work with all of these together as if it’s all one mind, or you can work with it as a set of parts, an ecology if you like. There isn’t a right or wrong way to approach it. They’re both maps. Don’t form a priesthood around either one!

P2Working with parts gives you precision, and working with the Higher Self gives you coherence. Communion is going back and forth between the two.

P3If you need to work with the whole, the subconscious as one:

P4a) Invoke the subconscious in some way. Something simple like ‘Hello, wise subconscious, please help me understand…’ or whatever works for you.

P5If you need to work with the ecology in the subconscious, notice what each part wants: the child parts usually want love, the guards want you safe, the critic wants you to meet some standard, the fantasy making parts are after possibility, body memories want to be recognized, the social part wants to belong, the spiritual part wants God or source or what’s best for all beings, and the wiser part just wants what’s true. Then:

P6a) It’s usually a good idea to start with unity. So invoke your Higher Self: “Hello, my Higher Self/universal heart-mind/wise subconscious, please guide me…”

P7b) Move into differentiation. “Which part is afraid? Which part wants to protect you? Which part wants to avoid the whole thing? Which part knows what to do? Which part is too young for this?” Etc.

P8c) Move back into unity again. “And all of these parts belong to the one life, and as the adult in charge of this life, you can lead with love…” Etc.

Sometimes start with the whole

P1Some people make themselves too fragmented by starting to work with parts. ‘Here’s a part! Here’s another one! I’ll get twelve of them in a room and give them all an office to hang around in!’ If that happens to you, it’s better to start from the sacred whole.

P2Start like this: ‘I’m approaching this from the universal heart. I’m approaching this from my Higher Self. I’m approaching this from the part of me that knows it belongs to life before it belongs to any wound, or role, or family, or group, or identity, or ideology, or story. So please help me see what’s true and kind and timely and stabilizing and useful here.’ Etc.

P3It can be that you can’t even access your Higher Self, or the universal heart, or whatever you call it. (By the way, if it doesn’t work for you, don’t use it! It’s no good adopting somebody else’s map.) In that case, start by just witnessing whatever is coming up. Notice the room, your body, the fact that you’re in a younger state etc. Being able to notice is enough adult presence for the first step.

P4Once you have the ability to notice, and to differentiate, you don’t need to keep rehearsing it. Just work on whatever it is you need to work on (often being warm to yourself, protecting yourself and knowing what to do etc). So even though it can be useful to talk about your Higher Self, it doesn’t mean you already have all the good inner parenting functions etc that you need. You usually need to work on developing them. And I don’t think the Higher Self has any specific wisdom. It’s a vantage point, where more wisdom and compassion can come up, but problems can also look like they were never problems at all from up there, and that can ironically lead instead to more foolish indifference. So invoking your Higher Self just means going up there for the view, and then coming back down to make your own decisions.

P5When something specific comes up, that’s the time to sort out what it is. “Is this my inner child? Or is this a guard? Is this my body? Is this fear? Is this intuition? Is this my social part? Is this divine guidance, or is this my interpretation of divine guidance?”

When the adult role is not available yet

P1The problem is that some people do not have access to their adult functions at will. Instead, they may go blank, access an inherited inner critic, or feel like a child part who may be playing at being an adult (what I call forced adulthood). Telling them to just access their adult is like telling a baby bird to just fly.

P2For that baby bird, I use a kind of provisional bridge (also found in Schema Therapy) that I call “borrowed adulthood,” which I'll detail here.

P3First, access your witnessing position if necessary, just noticing what’s there, without needing it to be warm or wise or certain. If you already know how to access different internal positions, move straight to the adult function you need to borrow.

P4If you have a safe adult in your life, you can borrow from them. If not, borrow from a mentor, an older brother/sister, your imagined future self, your god/goddess or higher power, a value you have, a plan you have written, etc. It’s ok to just borrow what you need right now, warmth or a boundary or direction, just like you would borrow a cup of sugar from a neighbor.

P5Next, put on your adult hat as if you are an apprentice adult, and pick the words or the action yourself. Then do at least part of it.

P6Last, return the adult hat, noticing what you borrowed and what you were able to do on your own (even if it’s only 5%).

P7The adult hat is only borrowed: the person you borrowed it from stays beside you, and you still make the choice. The longer version of this exercise can be found in my Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide.

P8You don’t have to have trustworthy people in your history. Use someone you remember when that helps, or just imagine a good adult whose intention is to take good care of you. See if you can imagine what they would say/do in your situation, with your history, limitations, options, etc. If you can’t imagine a good adult, or just imagining a caring grown-up makes you feel more threatened, or lonelier, start smaller, with the least cruel response you can believe. Once you’ve found something good, try it out instead of repeating the exercise.

P9It can be helpful to use borrowing when you’re in the adult position and don’t know what a decent adult would do, especially when your inner child doesn’t trust you and every response you can think of is retaliating, defending your goodness, collapsing or leaving. How would a decent adult hear that distrust? Borrow that one response. You may still feel young when you do it. Feeling like an adult can come after you act like one.

Induction: gathering and softening attention

P1Inductions serve to gather your awareness up into a gentler, trancier, more receptive form so you can access the inner knowing that's too quiet for our ordinary awareness to hear. Call me a heretic, but I don't think inductions should be about surrendering to the hypnotist while playing asleep.

P2Sometimes it's nice to do an actual induction if you're sober but distracted or analytical or otherwise not very trancey. If, on the other hand, you're already pretty trancey for whatever reason, skip straight to "Already trancey? Stabilize before dipping deeper."

P3There are lots of different ways to do the induction. For this method, I'm going to be counting, but you could do all sorts of things. If you want to just talk, or tell a story, or move, or make sounds, or be curious, or whatever, by all means, just do that if it works. Everyone's unique and each person might have a kind that works best for them which they'll discover as they go. The nice thing about the counting method is it's very explicitly an induction, whereas with more implicit methods you have to make sure to explain their function clearly.

P4You can test to see if you're at a deep enough level of hypnosis by choosing a simple target suggestion and checking if the response is the automatic subconscious kind or the analytical questioning kind.

Overly Confusing Isn't Automatically Good

P1"Keep it simple, stupid!" (KISS principle) actually can apply to hypnosis!

P2It's really easy to take a whole bunch of techniques from all of this and make something so complicated that you "hypnotize" yourself to the point where you don't even know if it worked or not. But keep in mind that especially with self-hypnosis, we can't go so deep that we are unable to continue the session! And even if it were a hypnotist bringing you under, you still need to be at an "access level" of awareness in which you can respond to questions.

P3So start off with setting one anchor/image to rest your mind's eye on. Decide on a hypnotic tone (warm, indirect, direct, neutral witnessing, etc.) for the session. If you're going to use metaphors, just start with one, and if you switch metaphors midway, understand the purpose of the switch. Then plan a simple stepwise walk out of the subconscious at session's end.

P4In fact, you don't even need to use metaphors nor imagery. You can use whatever form of inner signal that you notice. It may be a direct knowing, or an emotion, some kind of rhythmic movement or sound, or other kind of spatial perception technique like body scanning or flipping between awareness of different body parts.

P5Keep the language tied to the subconscious world you've chosen, so if you're counting, and there are already raindrops falling, you can count the raindrops, not the sheep which aren't in that scene. When you're done, come back out the opposite way from how you went in (we often visualize a staircase).

P6It's fine to borrow qualities such as warmth or steadiness from someone who models them, if you need to, but don't change who you are. We don't want you showing up at the Emmy's like Jim Carrey saying he's Nobody. 😅 Not yet anyways.

P7When you're writing the script, decide what the central verb you're using is to anchor it (like "letting go"). Give it an overall sensory quality, like unified or disjoint, or something fun like "fluffy wuffy."

P8You may have a short refrain that you come back to a few times in there as another type of anchor. Think about how you're going to slide from one idea to the next. It's much easier if you read it out loud as you're writing it, since the writing brain is sort of different from the vocal brain. Long, elegant sentences don't read well when you're being induced. You want your script to be soft, but to the point, easy to remember, easy to say, easy to grok.

A shortcut back into hypnosis

P1Once you have learned a useful induction and how to come all the way back up at the end, you can do the following:

P2Have an unusual word, phrase, and/or motion (optional) that you will use to go into hypnosis again. Say the word and/or phrase while doing the motion (if applicable). Be sure to do this when you are in hypnosis and in a good place. Don't do this when you are in a very emotional state, child state, or thinking about something that could be considered trauma. The idea is for you to be able to pick a good state to go into, and you can decide what that is.

P3For example, my dad used to use a mudra (like putting two fingers together like an "OK" sign) when he needed to relax. He even did it when he was driving. Not that I recommend driving in a trance, don't do that! Make sure the cue you use to calm down while staying alert is not the same as your cue to go inward into hypnosis.

P4Give your cue the meaning you choose: “When I pick the time and place (an appropriate time and place) and say the word and/or phrase to begin, I can go into this state that I picked and am familiar with. I can change direction, or stop, at any point.”

P5Now come all the way back up again, and at a later time, when it is appropriate, start using this. Over time, you can shorten the length of the longer induction, or use the longer induction if this doesn't seem to be enough for your needs. You don't have to do this a certain number of times, go to a certain depth, or even have a certain sensation when you do this.

P6Also, be sure that this is working for you: you can turn it on and off at will, it isn't there when you don't want it to be, and you can change direction or come back up when you are in it. Don't use it in a way that makes you follow instructions that you are hearing from a narrator, notifications, or something that you overhear. Don't use this to go into hypnosis faster for things that you shouldn't be working on yet.

P7There is something you should never do with this shortcut: use it, practice it or test it while you're driving or doing something where you need to be actively paying attention. It's just not safe to be going into a trance where you are actively paying attention to what's going on inside of you when you need to be paying attention to what's going on outside of you. Even though your eyes can be open, even though you're using "if it would be useful for you to do so" type language, it's just not safe.

Turning the shortcut off, or keeping it

P1If you want to turn the shortcut off, simply don't practice it anymore. Take it out of your recordings, take it out of your apps if you're using it in an app, and start using your regular induction. Tell yourself, when you're awake, that you won't be using it anymore. Keeping it doesn't keep today's trance going, though: once you come back, today's session is over.

Come all the way back

P1If you have done anything to alter your state when entering the trance, make sure to reverse those things. If you have floated, make sure to gain your weight again (whatever weight you want of course). If you have shrunk, make sure to grow back to your regular size. P2Here is a generic bring-back that you can use: "When I come out of this session, I will only bring with me that which is useful to me in my everyday life right now. Anything else I will bring with me at a later date, when I am better able to integrate it. When I come out of this session, I will be coming back to the room I am in now, and to the day in which I am participating in this session now. When I come out of this session I will be me, in my body and with my adult judgment."

P3Then do a count up: On 1 start to feel your body again and what it's resting on and feel your weight (of your choice). On 2 start to feel your breathing return to normal. On 3 start to hear the room around you and name where you are. On 4 start to wiggle your fingers and toes, move your shoulders and your eyes. On 5 open your eyes, back in the here and now, feeling normal and clear and the session is now over.

A vague reset word is no good either, and neither is being left in that state with no end to it.

The actual inner meeting

P1Note: If you have a tendency to daydream, dissociate or fall asleep, it is better to do this exercise sitting up and save lying down for when you want to go to sleep, get a very deep rest or incubate dreams. If you are sitting, keep your spine straight but not rigid like a soldier. Relax your jaw and stay somewhat connected to the room you are in.

P2“I am not leaving life; I am listening more deeply to life.

P3I invoke my Higher Self, universal heart-mind, wise subconscious mind, intelligence in my body, inner child(ren), inner adult(s), guard(s) and all that is of truth and love to help me know what I need to know, only in a form and at a time that will be useful, kind, stabilizing and real for me.”

P4Ask your conscious mind to listen, and don't force an answer.

P5This does not mean you have to stop thinking to listen. Just notice your thoughts, body sensations, pictures, doubts, emotions, etc. Don’t fight what comes up, but don’t go with it either. Just notice what stands out on its own as you hold the idea of what you are doing in the back of your mind.

P6Internally say, “Tell me what I need to know or ask me what you need to know and I’ll sit in silence for 5 minutes and listen.”

P7You may hear a phrase, feel a body sensation, notice a memory, see a color, be contacted by your inner child or a guard, feel pain, warmth, complete silence, know that you need to do something, be grateful for something, feel you need to stop the listening or any number of things.

P8Listen to everything and believe nothing automatically.

Let the next response guide you

P1The induction and the inner meeting get you to where you can listen. After that, it's about answering what happens, instead of reading out a script that has already decided what every part of you is going to say. Read through these sections, and then try doing the inner meeting without looking at the screen.

P2Respond to each thing that comes up with an accurate notice and a small reasonable invitation (or action or taking more time etc). The thing might be a sensation, image, gesture, feeling, words, or anything. The notice and invitation don't need to cover all those forms, just notice and invite what happens.

Finding your own words

P1An example notice might be, "I notice my chest tightening when I imagine asking for help". Don't add interpretation like "this must be the abandoned child part of me, or a protective mechanism" - just notice what you notice. An example invitation might be, "And as I notice that, I can take some time before deciding what it means, and notice if I want to say anything, or change it, or just stay like that".

P2Then just listen. You don't need fancy language - often it will be better to just ask what happens next, or whatever. If you notice a feeling developing, it might be best not to say anything while it's developing (if you can help it), but some days you need to talk to understand, so just do that.

P3You can be pretty straightforward when that's the help you chose - "Let the explanation wait a moment. What do I feel as I say that?" can be a perfectly valid thing to say to yourself, and help you focus. As long as you're still present, and able to change if you want to, and happy to carry on, a sad feeling or tension or whatever doesn't require an exit.

Notice, meaning, and your thoughts on the meaning

P1It's worth trying to separate the notice, the meaning, and your thoughts on the meaning, but in practice it will often be pretty fuzzy. When you can, it can be helpful to separate it out like that though - "I noticed... this seemed to mean... here's why that might be true / false" or something like that.

P2Is this a notice or meaning? "My stomach went tight" is a notice, "He's a complete bastard" is a meaning, even if you didn't think it in words. You can then go on to find out what he did, notice what it was that gave you the idea, and consider if there might be other explanations. It's also ok to put a meaning into words now, to look at it, even if it came to you without words. Nonverbal thoughts and early thoughts aren't necessarily any deeper, truer, or more reliable than other thoughts. Feelings are good to pay attention to, but it's still good to consider what they mean.

Let the response change the invitation

P1Your shoulders tightening, or 'hmm, that feels kind of wrong', is a signal to investigate further, but 'No! That is Not Right!' is a signal to stop what you're doing and change it. If you don't feel like it fits, don't keep doing it, and don't try saying it again more persuasively. Just change it. You don't need any other reason.

P2"I notice the tightness, and I can just leave it like that. Then again, is that what I really want?"

P3Sometimes the invitation just isn't right or helpful, even if it sounds like something a hypnotist might say. Each time we play around with this, we explore new possibilities which bring up new inner signals for us to feel out.

P4Doubting is sometimes a guard to listen to, and sometimes simply part of normal consideration. Lack of doubt doesn't necessarily mean the process is working in the best way, either.

Look at the situation too

P1If there is another person involved in the situation, it may be a good idea to look at what they are doing as well. Can they hear you disagree, take responsibility, and make room for what you're going through? Try and be specific in what you conclude about them, and be willing to change it if you find out that it was wrong. Just because someone is disagreeing with you or trying to set boundaries with you does not mean they are in their emotionally immature parts. You don’t need to subject yourself to something that may be dangerous before protecting yourself.

P2Look at your own side too. You may find that you feel guilty. Having feelings of guilt does not mean that you have done something wrong or that you have been manipulated. Have you done something that was wrong or that caused harm? If so, what can you do to fix it? On the other hand, did you give in to someone else just to keep them from being disappointed in you? What do you want to do or achieve the next time you interact with this person? Do you need them to understand what you are feeling/thinking? Do you just need to communicate information to them? Do you need to set boundaries with them? Do you need to end the interaction? You don’t have to make them understand before you can act.

Change the amount of contact

P1When you start to contact the difficult thing, if it feels too much, immediately start to widen your awareness to something else in the room, in your body, etc. Keep moving back and forth between the difficult thing and the easier thing. This is called pendulation, and approaching in smaller amounts is titration. Do as much or as little as you feel you can.

P2You can say, "I can let this much be here, and notice what's supporting me right now." And when you've had enough, "Ok, I think that is enough for now."

P3Feel free to go back and explore more if you wish to do so. If, at any point, you start to lose contact with the here and now, feel that things are becoming more and more unreal, or you feel that you are not able to switch to something else or stop, stop trying to explore inside of yourself and just do the entire "coming back" part. If you have trouble orienting yourself and it lasts for a prolonged period of time, please get some human help rather than trying a stronger induction.

If you feel an urge to move

P1If you feel an urge to move, turn, reach, push, or change your body’s position, feel free to either actually do something that will not cause your body pain or discomfort, or just imagine doing what you feel you need to do, and see what happens next. You do not need to act out the urge, shake, release, etc. If you do move and you feel that you are causing yourself pain or other symptoms that you are concerned about, stop moving.

Offer the care that is actually wanted

P1If any younger parts come up, offer them the opportunity to spend time with you, or some space if they would rather. If one of them says “don’t come closer”, stay at the distance they choose. You don’t have to consider them literally younger, nor do you have to ask them why they are there or what happened to them. If you can identify any guards (childhood protector parts), talk to them in the same Nurturer voice you meant for your little one, and find out what they are concerned about. All of them are welcome to the meeting. You don’t have to get rid of any of them.

P2If you feel that it would be useful to bring in your Nurturer, Protector or Guide to help you either change the tone of the interaction, set boundaries, or find your way toward the life you want, feel free to do so. You don’t have to talk to them, just use the one whose job you need, and if you can’t do it yet, borrow it. You only need to use the Witness if you need help getting started with a step. If you are already able to do a certain step, you don’t have to do it again.

P3Try some different ideas as to what might be helpful for this inner part to feel better (being welcomed by you, visualizing someone else, giving them the sort of words they would like to hear, you placing yourself in a different body position, you moving further away etc). Only try one idea at a time to see if it works.

P4It is possible that when you try and give the part what it wants, you might feel very upset. It may be that it never got what it needed at the time and now that you are giving it what it wants, it is processing the upset of not getting what it wanted before. That doesn’t mean your love isn’t working. If this happens, allow yourself to respond for as long as you want to do so, and for as long as you can stay there (ie remain in the process), but don’t push for more tears, since crying more doesn’t mean you’re healing more. If you feel that the voice, distance, touch, image etc is too much, then change it to something that is less. Don’t think that you can’t take the love.

P5Allow yourself to see where a metaphor takes you. Ask questions like ‘if the problem had an image or story, what would it be?’ or ‘what would need to happen to make things right?’ If it feels like you need to do something to give yourself the resource that you feel you need (or to put a boundary in place, or to move away or to take some other action) then try doing that, as far as it helps you. Remember that whatever you find out is likely to be metaphorical. It may be a good hypothesis for what you need to do, but it isn’t a record of what actually happened.

P6Alternatively, you may want to use the session to build up something you want more of (eg love, play, courage, curiosity etc). You can also use it to rehearse something that you have decided to do.

The 15-minute daily practice

P1There is no need to learn a second script. The process is the same: gather your attention, notice what is here, respond, wait, and come all the way back. You are simply practicing for fifteen minutes or so. That is long enough to induce yourself (it only takes a few minutes), practice and let it unfold at its own pace, and come out again (that takes a few minutes too). Fifteen minutes is just a handy appointment with yourself, though, and your feelings don't have to keep to it. Five minutes is plenty to see what happens when you simply sit in silence, listening, and you can interrupt yourself at any time to change direction if that feels better.

P2You can also try other ways of inducing yourself, such as becoming aware of 3 things you can see, 3 things you can hear, 3 things you can feel in your body; then 2 of each, 1 of each. Then count from 5 to 1 and begin to listen. Choose one way to induce yourself. You don't need to try all of them (in fact, stacking them might be counter-productive).

P3Once you have practiced listening a few times with the recordings, you may want to shorten them to speed up your induction, or even practice without playing them back at all. On the other hand, if you like using the recordings, there is no reason to stop. (Once you know the induction well, the cue from "A shortcut back into hypnosis" can shorten it too.)

P4As you come out of the process, ask yourself what needed warmth, what needed to be protected, what you need to do differently to be more in accord with truth and love and life. Is there something you want to explore more fully or a capacity you want to build? And most importantly, what can you now carry yourself that you used to have to borrow? If you identified something you needed to do differently, make a plan with yourself to do that thing. The more specific you can be, the more likely you are to change: “If _________ (fill in the cue for the old pattern), then I will do ________ (fill in a small action that will begin to change the pattern).” Then check back later and see how you are doing – sleeping, relating, functioning, etc., not only how impressive the session felt.

The weekly deeper practice

P1Once or twice a week, if it’s useful, give the practice more time. More time doesn’t have to mean more intense. It helps to start from your Higher Self, or if that’s out of reach, from just noticing what’s there. Then follow whatever shows up. If you need one of your adult jobs, the Protector say, bring it in, or borrow it from someone safe. And check that you’re not skipping past anything important. You’ll also want enough time at the end to come all the way back out and do whatever normal thing needs doing.

P2If you can, do that normal thing right after the session. That way your subconscious sees that the adult in you heard what came up and did something about it, instead of having one more beautiful inner meeting and leaving the dishes where they were.

Capacity before content

P1Note: if it is medically unsafe, or you are in a place of panic, sleep deprivation, nausea, deep destabilization, mania, psychosis-like symptoms, feeling overwhelmed by chemicals etc., focus on stabilizing first, with help if you need it (see “When this needs more support than a guide can provide”), and only then look for what your subconscious needs you to know. If knowing what your subconscious knows would destabilize you, it is not meant for you to know directly at this time. Ask for capacity before content.

P2Sometimes, it feels like your subconscious knows something that your conscious self is not prepared to know. It may be something related to your sexuality, grief, trauma, family issues, betrayal, shame, vocation, a relationship, illness fears, spirituality, addiction, identity, moral issues etc.

P3What you don’t want to do is bully your subconscious into telling you what it knows by saying things to yourself like:

P4“What is it you won’t tell me? What are you hiding? Tell me NOW! I am telling you to tell me!”

P5This can lead to flooding, panic, false certainty, confabulation, dissociation etc., and can make your day suck. When you are flooded, it is much harder to access your adult capacities, so focus on being able to regulate and on getting support first. Then ask for what you need to know in a way that you can tolerate:

P6“What can you tell me, that will make my conscious self 5% better at knowing the thing you want to tell me?” “Can you tell me in symbolic form so I am not completely overwhelmed by the revelation?” “Can you tell me what I need to do to be safe, without actually telling me what it is?” “Do I need to know what it is now, or do I need to get some support first, so I have the capacity to know it, and who would be a good person to help me?” etc.

P7Or, instead of demanding answers, say to yourself something like this:

P8“Dear Wise Subconscious Mind, if there is something you know that my conscious self is not ready to know, please don’t break it open violently. Do what you can to help me know it in a way that is safe. Prepare me before you break it open. Ground me before you show me the memory. Support me before you make it intense. And show me the next thing to do.” etc.

P9Remember: not everything in hypnotic imagery is literal, especially if it relates to trauma and memory. The mind makes it symbolic, squashes things together, distorts, dramatizes, pulls from cultural influences, fears etc. So, treat it like an image, and get some help outside of yourself when you need it.

Troubleshooting

Nothing happens

P1Check if there's a way you could try this on a smaller scale.

P2Try out these requests: “Show me a body sensation.” “Show me just a word.” “Show me whether today is for listening or resting.”

It feels fake

P1Do you have a sense of what it is that feels fake? The words, an image, what you are expecting to get from this, a role you feel you have to play etc. Not having an inner voice or images doesn't mean you're resisting the work, or that you're missing a 'grown up'. If it feels like you are being asked to do something you don't want to do, then correct the invitation. It could be that there is a part that is protecting you, so talk to it.

P2You could say something like 'What are you protecting me from?' or 'Are you afraid I will lie to myself again?' or 'Are you afraid this will just be a performance?' If it says yes, that can sting, because there could be something to it. Maybe you've fooled yourself before, and that part of you remembers it. "Tell me what you remember, I'm listening." works well here.

P3It could also be your inherited parent script that you are picking up, and it is trying to enforce a rule about how you should be to belong to them. Find out what rule it is enforcing. Comfort your little one before you answer any of it. Your answers can be firm or funny, as long as they aren’t mean. Then work on finding some ordinary evidence to build a more believable 'grown up' voice of your own.

P4If the fake feeling is telling you that you don't have the grown up ability to do this yet, then don't try and convince yourself by using an inner voice. As long as you can still observe yourself well enough to do this, try and borrow what you need from someone who you can imagine is good enough to lend it to you. This could be a family member, friend, famous person etc. What you need might be warmth, boundary, direction etc. For warmth, just make sure it's someone who liked you the way you were. Try out something which rings true to you, whether it is a sentence, an action etc, and see how you get on.

The subconscious will not answer

P1There may not be an answer. There may not be an answer that will help you. You may need to try asking a different question or you may just need to rest or finish your inner work for the day. If trust is actually a concern, you can try asking your subconscious “What would you need to know in order to trust me?” Some examples of answers might be “I need to know you will set boundaries,” “I need to know you will do what you said you will do,” “I need to know you won’t keep leaving,” or “I need to know you won’t think that knowing something means that you don’t need to do anything about it.”

The inner child will not appear

P1If you can tell there is a guard in the way, talk to it. You can say to the guard “You don’t have to let me in, just tell me what I need to do to make it safe”. Even if there isn’t a child part that you can talk to, you can work with whatever you are aware of. You can work on your preferences or something that you’re curious about or something that you’re willing to try as a beginner etc. and see how it goes. Work on developing a sense of who you are and reparent yourself as best you can.

You get anxious

P1Find out if the anxiety is bad enough that you want to decrease contact with it or if you can manage it and let it continue. If the anxiety is getting too bad, then you need to stop searching for content and help your body first, like the following:

P2-Where are your feet? Where are you sitting? Focus on your body, your breathing, and the room around you.

P3-Tell your little one and your guards (childhood protector parts) that you’re there for your little one, that you recognize that your guards are trying to protect you, and that you don’t need them to scream at you in order for you to know that something is wrong. Ask them if they need you to do something, and if they do, ask them to show you one step at a time that you can do. If they don’t, then ask them to let your body calm down.

P4If you’re having chest pain, fainting, suicidal thoughts, psychosis-like symptoms, mania-like symptoms, severe dissociative symptoms, not being able to sleep, wanting to hurt yourself or somebody else, etc., then you need to get professional help. For more on handling anxiety, see the toolbox in Appendix D.

You dissociate or feel unreal

P1Open your eyes, move around, feel something textured, name 5 things that you can see, and end the deeper practice for the day.

You become grandiose

P1Go eat, go to sleep, do some chores, accept some humility, find somebody to disagree with you, etc.

P2What do you say to yourself in that moment? “If it’s real, it’ll still be here after a good night’s sleep, some food, some time, and some subsequent humility, feedback, etc.”

You become too analytical

P1“What if you didn’t try to improve the practice?” Put one hand on your heart and the other on your solar plexus, as in the two-hand practice, and say, “I don’t have to do this perfectly. I’m here for a relationship with something deeper than my surface mind.” “What can you do to make this a communion, not a performance?” Maybe it’s silence, resting, or doing something. Whatever it is, just do it.

You become too blissful or diffuse

P1Ask what the bliss could turn into in real life: “What’s the next small thing this love is asking me to do? A task, a boundary, a meal, some rest, a repair, a conversation?”

You want the hidden truth immediately

P1Don’t pry.

P2“Show me the capacity before the content.”

You keep daydreaming

P1Sit up and open your eyes, and ask yourself, “What are you avoiding doing in the real world by doing this?”

You get stuck in spiritual bypass

P1“Where has divine love not yet descended into the wound?”

P2Maybe you had a profound opening that showed you how much more love and freedom is possible. Let that matter, but don’t demand that your inner child receive it on command. Invite your little one in with you. They might go numb, or call BS on that. If so, they might just need to see if you’re actually humble enough, and grateful enough for them, to listen to their rejection and be patient. Maybe when you prayed it seemed like you were being abandoned and condemned. Maybe you felt betrayed by a spiritual teacher. It may be worth exploring these, but you might not want to do more of the same practice.

P3Sometimes it’s best to get some practical help while life still feels too small, and let yourself be curious about finding more happiness, without necessarily believing the explanation of how it’s going to work. Sometimes people have enough love and meaning to just live their lives, and trying to be spiritual doesn’t have to be another assignment.

You get stuck in endless processing

P1You may have a real wound that you need to deal with and be caught in a checking loop at the same time. Consider asking yourself “Will processing this again actually help me feel, understand, decide, or do something differently? Or is this a form of checking to see if I can get the reassurance that something is finally okay?” Sometimes, whether you’re grieving or doing your practice, it’s okay to cover the same ground again and again.

P2The best thing to do, though, if you notice you’re in a checking loop, is allow the thought to be there without engaging with it and get on with what you have chosen to do. You don’t have to have a meeting with every thought that pops into your head before you start your life. You don’t have to complete a ritual before you start your life.

P3What would the Guide choose to do now?

You keep using hypnosis but not changing life

P1Ask yourself, “What can I do to show my little one I’m for real?” Pick one thing to do that’s small enough to repeat, then go do it. A big gesture you later drop would only prove them right to doubt you, but a small thing you keep doing can change their mind.

What not to do

P1Don’t view your inner experiences as commands or proof. They’re internal happenings that need to be reality tested at all times.

P2Don’t go seeking “downloads” of information, or make a hobby of demons/jinn/Unattached Burdens or the like. Stabilize your life first, and get help to do so if you are not reality testing well, or not functioning well in your life.

P3Don’t do self-hypnosis while driving, bathing, swimming, operating any equipment, supervising children, or doing anything else that requires active attention.

P4Don’t try to “force” memories, to use trance to “prove” something happened to you, or to skip medical attention for symptoms just because you can “force” them down using hypnosis.

P5Don’t force yourself to ignore your body’s needs, such as eating when hungry, resting when tired, responding to pain, etc., or your emotions, such as letting yourself grieve when someone dies, feeling anger when it is appropriate, acknowledging your fears, etc., until you know more about what the signals are telling you.

P6Don’t “extend” the duration of altered states indefinitely.

P7Don’t allow your inner roles to get out of proportion (e.g., letting your inner child run your adult life, the Protector shut out all love, the Nurturer disregard consequences, or the Guide ignore the needs of your inner child). You are capable of analysis, as well as tenderness.

P8Don’t use NLP techniques to force down guard parts, to give yourself certainty you do not have, to manipulate others, or to heal yourself when your body is telling you not to.

P9Don’t consider negative indicators (doubt, distraction, resistance, lack of response, saying “No”) as good signs that the induction is working.

P10Don’t rely upon the safety of your unconscious mind as the only safety factor in any session. Be sure you have set up clear limits, are in a safe place, and have a tested way to end the session and get out.

P11Don’t believe that normal physical sensations are indications of hidden trauma, illness, toxins in your body, possession, “attachments,” “transformations,” or information from your “subconscious mind.”

P12Don’t believe that anything is “healed” in your body (neurologically, cellularly, etc.) simply because it is “healed” in your imagination.

P13Don’t put hidden commands in yourself, nor allow anyone to do so. Even if you are consciously placing something in yourself which you wish to utilize later, be sure that you know exactly what you are doing and why you are doing it, and be sure that you can review it and can “take it out” if you decide that you no longer wish to use it. It may not be as easy as simply saying, “Take out.” Nor should you allow anyone to activate these commands in you without your knowledge, or use them to make you do something you don’t want to do, to make you into someone you don’t want to be, to give you amnesia, etc.

When this needs more support than a guide can provide

P1You should get help now if you are in imminent danger, if you are very confused, if you cannot ensure your own safety, and/or if you are experiencing concerning physical symptoms (e.g., chest pain, fainting, etc). You should also seek professional help promptly if you are experiencing new or worsening suicidal thoughts, severe insomnia, new or worsening manic or psychotic symptoms, or if dissociative symptoms are impacting your ability to function.

P2You should seek suitable ongoing help if you are experiencing recurring panic attacks, compulsions, destabilization from substances, major relationship instability, etc.

P3You probably don't need any more support than this if you keep the practice shorter, make it warm, pick a small and tractable subject, make sure you come back out, and then see how it affects a specific target (anxiety, daydreaming, helping a relationship, facing a task you've been avoiding, helping you sleep, being less reactive emotionally, improving your intuitive accuracy, etc) for a week or two. If you notice that your functioning is getting worse, or that you're getting more certain about unconfirmable memories, then reassess instead of pushing harder.

Methods to explore with a trained person present

P1If you want to deliberately do trauma-focused regression (going backwards in your memory or imagination to times when you were younger), or affect and somatic bridging (following associations from an emotional or somatic experience to find the origin of that experience, particularly if it was a traumatic experience you don't know about), or if you find that you're having substantial flashbacks or dissociative reactions, then you should do this with a trauma-informed clinician present. Make sure they're trained in the specific method you want to do. Ask any potential clinician what they would do and how they deal with uncertainty and unexpected reactions. Note that even if you work with a person, hypnosis is not a way of knowing what did or did not happen to you.

P2Note that having experienced trauma, crying, and even wanting to feel difficult feelings are not necessarily signs that you need more support than working on your own. You get to pick how wide or narrow a range you want to work in, but be aware that different people need different ranges. Pay attention to how you respond and what support you have. Feeling like you are handling it isn't a substitute for having support.

P3The main clinical practice guidelines for the treatment of major trauma recommend one or more of trauma-specific variants of cognitive behavioral therapy (often referred to by specific names, such as cognitive processing therapy, prolonged exposure, etc.) or eye movement desensitization and reprocessing, depending on the person and the guideline. These approaches in turn can be further supported by techniques such as self-hypnosis and inner child work. But high-risk trauma work needs a bigger container than that.

Your functioning in ordinary life is the feedback

P1If you find yourself more loving, more honest, more functional, more relational, more grounded, more precise, etc. than when you started this process, keep on keeping on gently. If you notice that your ordinary life is becoming less stable, don't try to go deeper. Go for more sleep, more eating, more grounding, more healthy boundaries with other people, more support from other people, etc.

Appendix B: NLP without turning yourself into a sales seminar

P1NLP stands for Neuro-Linguistic Programming.

P2You do not need to know this stuff to use the main path, but if you want to freshen up on your skills in the art of working with imagery, body states, behavioral rehearsal, talking to yourself in effective sentences etc., then it might be useful, as long as it helps you listen. The four NLP layers

P1The four levels of NLP are:

P2Sensory Level of NLP: working with the form of an inner experience (where do you see or hear it, how far away, how big, how bright, how loud, how do you hold your body, where is the pressure etc.)

P3State Level of NLP: making useful states (warmth, adult presence, courage, love etc.) easier to get to.

P4Strategy Level of NLP: practicing a better response.

P5Language Level of NLP: pacing what is true; opening up people’s awareness; presuming only that you can do something safe; linking cause and effect carefully; embedding invitations; asking de-hypnotic questions to break the effects of vague, absolute, manipulative or grandiose language.

The Milton Model, minus the covert sales seminar

P1We are going to use a model that’s called the Milton model. It’s all to do with sentence architecture. When we tell ourselves to ‘be better’, ‘relax now’, ‘stop feeling anxious’ or ‘trust me’, it doesn’t work, does it?

P2What we need to do is use indirect language to go around the part that wants to argue with us, so that we can actually get to where we want to get to.

P3So let’s start with something we know to be true, let’s take the pressure off, presume that we can only do a tiny bit and let’s invite one thing:

P4‘As my body begins to notice the sensation of the bed underneath it, it may be possible for it to begin to realize that it is not alone in carrying the burden of the day.’

P5We paced it; we opened up awareness; we validated the reason for the reaction; we presumed we could do it; we used a bridge word to make it easier and we invited one thing. We can then test this out with the body to see if it was possible.

False positives, and turning the cue off

P1A downside of synchronicity cues is that they can lead to false positives. We create an association in our head on purpose when we rehearse what to do when we notice a synchronicity; however, when you are absorbed in something and/or when you are destabilized, you might think that you saw a synchronicity when in fact there wasn't any. Research has linked apophenia, the tendency to see patterns that aren't really there, with psychosis spectrum traits (see Blain and colleagues, and Grant and colleagues). That isn't saying that noticing patterns is bad. In fact, it's not a pathology.

P2If you feel that synchronicity cues are making these things worse (e.g. you feel like you have to look for them all the time, you can't sleep, you feel anxious, you are sure that random things happen in your life in order to tell you something, you can't tell the difference between noticing something and interpreting what it might mean, etc.), you should deactivate synchronicity cues.

P3You can do that by not rehearsing them anymore and by saying to yourself (when you are fully awake) that you don't want to use synchronicity as a cue anymore (e.g. "From now on, I am no longer going to use noticing synchronicity as a cue for waking up. P2Remember that often the anxiety comes from a childhood guard part of you. It’s what protects you when your inner adult’s Protector isn’t functioning, the same as in childhood. P3Work in this order – first your body, then your inner child, then some inquiry, then some action:

Reduced breathing / Buteyko-style breathing

P1Try breathing less. (There are some good things to be said for slowing your breathing down, if you are breathing too much, or panicked. However, please don’t hold your breath until you are air hungry, which can make you more anxious in the short term.) Breathe in small amounts through your nose and let it out quietly, through your nose too. Only pause if that's comfortable. Can you make your breathing very quiet? If you feel bad, dizzy, or panicky, or have medical issues, stop.

EFT tapping

P1One of the easiest ways to do EFT (Emotional Freedom Techniques, or tapping) is to say something like, “Even though I have this anxiety, I am willing to be kind to the part of me that is afraid,” while you tap gently on your body and stay with what you feel. There isn’t a ton of evidence for EFT, but there are some studies on anxiety. A 2025 systematic review found 7 randomized controlled trials for anxiety disorders, and noted that EFT was more effective than no intervention in several of them. So treat the evidence with caution, and know that there are first-line therapies for anxiety.

Somatic Experiencing

P1If you would like more information about somatic experiencing, or even a free intro session, please see my somatic guide. In somatic experiencing (SE), you are not digging into the trauma. Gendlin Focusing

P1Focusing is another approach where you work with your body. P3Tell yourself, “If this can be integrated in this moment, it’s welcome. If not, it can wait until later. The adult part of me is still here, and my body can return to safety.”

I see that there is no reason to try to defeat what I am experiencing out of fear. I choose to return my attention to loving awareness.”

A bodily boundary: compassion present, door closed

P1You can also tell the presence that while you have compassion for it, the door is closed.

P2This is especially a good idea if the presence you’re experiencing seems even the slightest bit hostile, cold, alien, parasitic, etc. It’s not really helpful to approach it with the kind of soft, sentimental love that you’d use if you were inviting it to stay for tea, but it can be useful to approach it with love while also making it clear that it needs to respect your boundaries.

P3Sit up, make sure your feet are grounded on the floor, straighten your spine, pull your shoulder blades back a little, open your chest a little, feel the boundary of your torso’s skin, feel the perimeter of your body, and tell the presence, “While I have compassion for you and any suffering you may be experiencing, this suffering needs to go back where it came from. The boundary of my skin is the boundary of an adult now, and you’re not welcome to come inside the house anymore.”

If it might be psychosis or destabilization

P1Everything above assumes that the “hostile presence” is symbolic, the kind that settles down when you ground yourself and meet it with love. What you’re going through might instead be psychosis, or your mind becoming unstable in some other way.

P2Get help right away if you feel under attack for days at a time, or you can’t sleep or function normally. The same goes if you hear voices telling you to do things, feel you need to hurt yourself or somebody else, or feel you’re going manic. And get help if you can’t communicate normally, or can’t tell the difference between what you’re experiencing symbolically and normal reality. This isn’t something to deal with in hypnosis.

The General Rule

P1The General Rule is: if you find that what you're doing is making you more loving, more grounded, more relational, more honest, more functional, then it's probably OK to keep doing it gently. But if it's making you more paranoid, sleepless, grandiose, isolated or scared, or things feel more and more unreal, stop going deeper and get help.

The cosmic-download humility test

P1Not every hostile presence, shadow force or grandiose part comes at you with fear. They may also try to give you a sense that you’re special, try to “download cosmic info” to you, try to give you a special mission, make you feel doing the dishes is beneath you, etc. You can just say, “Thank you for the imagery, but I don’t need your grand performance. Unless the info you’re trying to download to me can help me do the dishes, clean my room, be more honest, be more helpful, be more loving, keep my promises, take care of my body by resting and eating etc, I don’t need it.”

P2Guidance worth keeping should make ordinary life more beautiful, honest, relational, humble, reliable, and useful to other people.

Don’t become an entity hobbyist

P1Finally, some of you may be tempted to make this an area of hobbyism – to become interested in demons or jinn or hostile presences or Unattached Burdens or whatever. I just don’t want you to do that. They all lead to the same thing, which is how to maintain a presence of love without giving up your boundaries, while also making your mind harder to hook.

Appendix J: A practical induction library

P2Once you start an induction, you will want to notice what’s going on (even if nothing changes) and work out what to do next: move, imagine something else, change the sensory channel you’re using, do nothing, or stop the induction sequence and do something else… Whatever you do, make sure you bring yourself fully back to waking at the end of an induction.

1. Stretch and release

P1Work out what will be supporting you, and focus on the feeling of support. You can also focus on where one of your hands or feet is. Either actually, or in your imagination, stretch a little bit, either stretching that part of your body slightly or pressing gently down into the support, and then let go of the effort, just relax. See if there is any difference (it’s fine if there isn’t). Do this for no more than 3 places on your body. Let releasing guide you as you go through the sequence. Which bit of your body, or inner voice, needs this? What do you need to support it? Bring your body back to normal movement and return.

2. Supported floating

P1Work out what is supporting you and where your weight is. Imagine that the support is holding a tiny bit (1%) more of your effort. It’s like floating but you don’t have to lose contact, or you can just keep it at the same level, with just enough weight/pressure. See what happens. You can fractionate this if you want, perhaps noticing your normal awareness when you breathe in and the support when you breathe out. Tell yourself you are going to fractionate before you do it. What can you hear more easily now? Keep the support, come back to your normal weight and firm contact, and return.

3. Rhythmic breath or pulse

P1Use your breathing or a slight hum in your throat for a natural rhythm. You could also tap your finger, or follow a sound outside. You can even do it by counting to yourself (silently) or with no rhythm at all. Say “here, and here again” over and over, and notice what you pay attention to in between the phrases. Then stop saying it. Notice what you become aware of in the first beat – you don’t need to make your mind blank, just notice what comes up. Does it make any words, feelings, needs or actions more clear? Then let everything go back to its normal rhythm, and come back.

4. Unwinding object

P1Unwinding an object is good for when you have a few different strands to one thing you want to work on. Imagine an object unwinding – maybe some yarn, some bubbles, or some knots that you place beside you. Once you’ve placed them all, give yourself a minute to look at them all. You don’t have to change anything, but you can label them if you need to (just a word or two). How many did you put down? Which one do you need to work on, and what does it need – warmth, protection, action? Keep one strand in your attention and put the others somewhere you can see them and return.

5. Sensory journey

P1Pay attention to the sounds in the room you’re in and how your body feels, then imagine yourself walking somewhere. Where do you want to go? Listen to the sounds of your footsteps and take in your surroundings as you walk. What do objects feel like if you touch them? What landmarks do you see? When you feel you have gathered enough of your attention, imagine the scenery changing until you are in a place where you can talk to whoever you need to (or you can just cut to the chase, if you prefer). Pay attention to something without worrying about what it means. Allow yourself to be curious about what’s going to happen next, but don’t make it a “what are they going to do to you?” kind of thing. When you are ready to finish, imagine yourself walking back the way you came and close the space.

6. Gradual wave

P1Start with a very small neutral area, feel into it a little and then imagine a wave of warmth, steadiness, or attention spreading out to a new area that is still ok. Sense into the new area (or continue using the old). See if there is anything different at each step. Does the body want any contact, space, words, other care? Let the mental image go, sense your body, sense your contact points, come back.

7. Care workshop

P1Prepare the workshop (time, place, tools, boundaries, etc). Invite the signal/feeling/young part to come only as close as is welcome. Try to avoid any language that makes you feel you're not one person. Care for it as best you can, by asking what it wants and giving that if you can, or sensing into it and responding, or just being with it. Take breaks, get back in contact if that's ok, take more breaks, etc. Keep bringing in adult functions (or borrow someone else's function). Have the adult part of you run the show. The goal is to make it "good as you" rather than "good as new", not to erase history. Close the workspace, reassure yourself that you are one whole person, that you're an adult now, and come back.

8. Resource embodiment

P1Pick a quality you understand and want more of, like warmth, being able to hold a boundary, steadiness, curiosity or dignity. Imagine yourself with just 1% more of it. (If you'd rather not step into it, you can watch an imagined you have it instead.) What's different? How do you sit, and how does your voice sound? Try it a few times; each time, it gets a little easier to find. Come back, keeping the quality and a positive action that you would do with it, but leaving the imagined you behind, saying "I am me".
