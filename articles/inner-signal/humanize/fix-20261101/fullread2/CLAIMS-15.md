# Part 15: the guide's claims and instructions (for the contradiction read, E310)

- Somatic Experiencing (his somatic guide; a free intro session): not digging into trauma; tracking activation and a neutral place, moving attention between them very slowly.
- Gendlin Focusing: ask for the felt sense of the whole anxiety, let the body form a vague sense, ask what it would like you to know, try a word or phrase for fit, and check whether what it says is true of the situation; words and body correct each other.
- Body supports: when the mind is too activated for meditation, work with the body (massage of foot, scalp, temple, back of neck, shoulder; not the front of the neck, injured, numb, bruised, inflamed, post-surgical areas or varicose veins; stop if worse), heat, pressure, a weighted blanket, darkness, calming music, a careful massage gun; whatever helps, without piling on wellness stuff.
- His ZHoly3O page (ozonated charcoal "holy water") and Blushield: optional, not necessary; what you need is to stabilize, listen to inner messages, reality-test them, and act on them in a grounded way.
- Inner child love: the two-hand practice with "Little one, you are not alone. You don't have to fix this problem. I'm here now to listen to you."; really talk to your inner child; if you can't say it and feel it, rest your hands and notice. (S-636: one of the practice's four appearances.)
- Anxiety script: where are my body parts, feet, position, breathing, place in the room; "If you're there, little one, I'm here. Thank you, guard... You didn't have to scream. I got your message."; ask for the first thing to do, or help the body stand down; "I'm not going to abandon you, but I'm not going to worship you either"; listen, reality-test, act.
- Appendix E (daydreaming): it can help (simulating to work through grief or learn, creativity, a potential future, fears, wants) or replace life (escape, fantasy addiction, dissociation, romantic obsession, escaping adulthood). Test: closer to life (clarity, love, ability to act) or away (repetitive, craving)? If helping: ask what the guidance is and use it. If away: sit upright, eyes open, feel your feet, and ask the subconscious for the guidance or, if it's avoidance, for a gentle but structured way to deal with life now; then ask what you're avoiding (texting, cleaning, eating, sleeping, grief, creating, apologizing, leaving, truth, a plan, help, a task, unavailable love). (Overlaps Part 7's "Stay awake when your mind likes to escape" and Part 11's "You keep daydreaming".)
- Appendix F: synchronicity is a cue, not an oracle (Appendix B). Suggestibility varies a lot (to your own suggestions vs. someone else's; by state; by type of suggestion), so both outside help (pacing, containment, another set of eyes) and self-practice (immediate follow-up, real-time correction, contact with guards) have advantages; self-hypnosis may be best day to day; complicated trauma, blind spots, dissociation, destabilization or relational wounds may need skilled outside support.
- Finding a hypnotist: ask their training, how they use hypnosis, what they do if a suggestion doesn't fit you, trauma and dissociation training (a hypnosis certificate isn't trauma training); tell them what has worked, how direct, touch, imagery, how to stop; ask about the unexpected, uncertain memories, the full return. Firm encouragement, never forced continuation. Afterwards check what was actually done and why, whether you were told and agreed, how they responded; discomfort isn't automatically bad, but treating discomfort as needing more is a warning sign; the app can help interpret; then choose self-hypnosis, both, or someone else.
- When the subconscious speaks like an oracle: it picks up a lot (anticipations, patterns, the uncanny); respect it, but an image or instruction isn't an order; the bigger or less reversible the consequences, the slower you go; adult eyes; take it to the council.
