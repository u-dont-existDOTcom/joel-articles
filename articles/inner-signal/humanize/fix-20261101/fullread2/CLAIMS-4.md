# Part 4: the guide's claims and instructions (for the contradiction read, E310)

- The two-hand practice (taught here; S-636 asks to cut or merge its other three appearances): one hand on the center of the chest (love), the other just below on the solar plexus (the inner child); send love down, let what comes back loop, keep sending if nothing comes; meet resistance or anything seen or felt with love; no warmth today: rest the hands and notice, no faking. A conversation with your inner child from the heart.
- The Nurturer: enjoy your inner child (not only comfort it), don't try to make feelings go away; lines: it's okay to feel this, I'm here, you don't need to do anything, you're not too much, you can be small / angry / sad here.
- The Protector: set boundaries so the warmth is believable (leave an unsafe conversation, stop texting the destabilizing person, eat, sleep, keep promises, don't leave the inner child with adult things); repair after an internal attack (apologize). Inner child work fails when the fluffy stuff isn't backed by action; trust comes from handling those things.
- If warmth is hard, do a small protective act; if you can't, find out what's in the way (fear, tiredness, skills, a practical barrier, the setup). It counts without immediate relief; a tick on a list doesn't prove it helped.
- The Guide (Leader, Guru, Wise One, Sage, Inner Teacher, Director, Higher Adult): holds standards like a good parent, asks hard things at the inner child's pace, without the harsh parental tone or "tough it out, you don't have needs"; without it, care slides into spoiling (sweets, screens, getting out of anything hard). Direction that takes needs into account: ask for help, express preferences, let a safe person see you imperfect, meet closeness needs without punishing someone's need for space.
- Guide affirmations: not just here to feel good; to become whole; to belong to life, not only to people who approve; to serve truth/beauty/love/God/.../the benefit of all beings.
- Order: start with whatever adult function you can reach, but most need enough Nurturer and Protector before the Guide (otherwise spiritual bypassing dressed as direction); even starting with protection, make sure the direction suits the inner child's growth (play, support).
- Guards: parts language is optional; a sensation, distraction or objection might just be hunger, reasonable skepticism, or a sentence that doesn't fit; notice it and see. When you go to meet your inner child you may instead feel numb, cynical, angry, suddenly hungry, phone urges, arousal, planning, sleepy, dissociated, "this is stupid": not failures, often a guard (what protected you when no adult did). Before deciding, ask what it's trying to stop or wants; it could also be the inner child (mad you took so long), the inherited parent script, something from today, or a mix.
- Say to the guard, in the Nurturer voice: "I see you. I'm not going to force my way past you. What's going on?" Wait; nothing is fine. Later: "What are you afraid would happen if you stopped protecting me by…?" Listen (fears: you'll lie to yourself, pretend, it'll hurt, spiritual bypass, forgive too soon, see what you want, leave them hanging). Tell it it makes sense, it needn't stop protecting, ask what would make it trust you 1% more; if it says no, especially to going deeper or to an altered state, stop and don't push past it, even when the no sounds like a prediction.
- Most of the time the guard wants practical things: boundaries, what you're supposed to do, eat, not text someone, keep a promise, sleep, talk to the therapist, admit a debt, write something down.
- The inherited parent script (his version): the rules and ways of treating yourself internalized from how your parents treated you; not always a voice; not the inner adult (Nurturer, Protector, Guide) you build on purpose; often a critic or warden ("Don't be needy! Don't embarrass us! And don't be independent."); it may call the new loving voice "fake".
- A Protector that stops scanning (a practice): orient yourself; assess (what you know, don't know, need to check); differentiate real from unknown, past or imagined danger; set a boundary in proportion (move, stop, delay, ask, leave, get support); verify it's safe enough (not perfect certainty); stand down and rest. A mature Protector can say it doesn't know, ask another human, delay interpreting, use a no-trance method; discernment when needed, then stop scanning.
