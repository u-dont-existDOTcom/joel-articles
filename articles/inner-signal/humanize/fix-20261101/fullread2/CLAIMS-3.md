# Part 3: the guide's claims and instructions (for the contradiction read, E310)

- If starting with parts makes you more fragmented, start from the sacred whole: speak from the universal heart / Higher Self / the part that belongs to life before any wound, role, family, group, identity, ideology or story; ask for help seeing what's true, kind, timely, stabilizing and useful.
- If you can't reach the Higher Self or that language doesn't fit (somebody else's map), don't use it: just witness (the room, your body, that you're in a younger state). Being able to notice is enough adult presence for the first step.
- Once you can notice and differentiate, stop rehearsing the Witness; work on what's missing (warmth, protection, direction). Talking about the Higher Self doesn't mean you already have the inner parenting functions; you develop them.
- His view (from his inner child guide): the Higher Self has no specific wisdom; it's a vantage point where more wisdom and compassion can come up, but problems can look like non-problems from up there, which can lead to foolish indifference. (Check against every place the guide asks the Higher Self to "guide me" or tell what's true: Part 2's "Hello, my Higher Self ... please guide me".)
- Differentiate only as needed (inner child? guard? body? fear? intuition? social part? divine guidance or my interpretation of it?).
- Your inner child won't trust you right away; trust comes from you becoming reliable, not from a meditation or three nice sentences.
- The inner adult has three jobs: the loving Nurturer, the safe Adult Protector ("the Protector"), the wise Guide; one grown-up doing three jobs, each keeping the others in balance; you develop them.
- The inner child's worth doesn't depend on these qualities; it has its own (play, tenderness, not taking life too seriously, creativity, wonder), which belong in the relationship too.
- Some people can't reach adult functions at will (blank, inherited critic, a child part playing adult: "forced adulthood"). Borrowed adulthood (a provisional bridge, also found in Schema Therapy): 1) witness if needed (skip if you can already tell positions apart); 2) borrow one function (warmth, boundary or direction) from a safe adult, mentor, sibling, future self, god/goddess, a value, a written plan; 3) put on the adult hat as an apprentice, choose the words or action yourself, do at least part of it; 4) return the hat, noticing what you borrowed and what you did yourself (even 5%). You still choose. Longer version: his Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide.
- You don't need trustworthy people in your history: a remembered or imagined good adult. If that feels threatening or lonelier, start with the least cruel response you can believe. Once something good is found, try it instead of repeating the exercise.
- Borrowing also helps when your inner child distrusts you and your responses are retaliating, defending, collapsing or leaving: how would a decent adult hear that distrust? You may still feel young; feeling adult can come after acting adult.
- Letting younger parts speak doesn't mean handing judgment to someone else or regressing; you can have wonder, silliness, the senses, vulnerability, need, the wish to be looked after and to offload decisions, while the adult stays present (the statement: the adult is always here, listening, evaluating, protecting, stopping; no child state, guide, recording or person replaces adult judgment).
- Love doesn't have to wait for trust: you can love your little one even if they don't believe it; their trust can question your promises and take its time. Say "Okay. I believe that you don't believe me. I still love you..." but don't turn "I still love you" into a cue to stop the anger.
- Respond to the complaints: don't accept every conclusion, but listen for the concrete complaint; answer serious things seriously; show love (listening to anger without hitting back, owning what wasn't okay, changing, making up, care: food, limits, the doctor, keeping promises). Not hitting back is enough to start being the adult, not yet enough for trust; that takes warmth (borrow it).
- Instead of blame: notice what you teach your positions (unconditional love vs. "it's your fault if you don't believe me"); two positions, one, or a blend; they may be younger than you; both talk in your grown-up voice; the blaming doesn't mean your love is conditional.
- Putting your younger self on trial: ask what it was trying to get or avoid, understood, could see and tolerate, what happened, what was missing; what you can learn or repair now. Punishing yourself now won't give it the capacity it lacked.
