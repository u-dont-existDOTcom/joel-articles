# Part 6: the guide's claims and instructions (for the contradiction read, E310)

- As a child you feel you must belong to survive, so people do things for family, religion, partner, political tribe, spiritual group, institution, online community that they otherwise wouldn't. Adulting: see where you sell yourself to belong, choose your own ground, learn you can disappoint someone and not disappear. Much of the subconscious is made of other people.
- Belonging questions: where am I betraying myself to belong; what does belonging without self-betrayal feel like; guidance or belonging program; love or fear of not belonging; conscience or conditioning; devotion or compliance; free or rebelling. Then notice what the body says (not always right, a good place to start).
- De-hypnosis (his words): self-hypnosis wakes you up to how your attention is shaped (screen subliminal patents, the Algorithm, ads, politics; erotic imprinting, religious fear and shame, charismatic authority, belonging pressure; repetition). Influence can be benign (lullabies, prayers, a good teacher, a song, love); ask what it does to your ability to see and choose.
- Manipulation signs: urgency, feeling special, FOMO, threatened exclusion, sexual charge, shame, confusion, rescue promises, destiny, pressure to obey, secret knowledge, certainty that comes too quickly, the sense that one good question would break the spell.
- If you sense hypnotic influence: stop; don't obey, don't automatically rebel; realize you're being influenced.
- Manipulative hypnotic language: consent smuggled in, only one choice offered, an unagreed claim slipped into a sentence (examples: "You will soon realize that I am right…", "Part of you already knows…", "Your higher self wants…", "your ego does not want you to…").
- Questions: what am I asked to agree to; what choice am I given; what's assumed; who benefits; what's emotionally charged; what's left out; what happens if I say no.
- Anyone worth following leaves your adult self more present; the more powerful the state, the stronger the boundaries needed.
- Soft language can still remove choice: indirect suggestions bypass the skeptical guard (specific aims, vague wording). Ways: induction disguised as explanation (even "anti hypnosis"); every answer counted as success; "whether or not you notice"; obey/role/please-the-guide concepts; unannounced fractionation; hidden cues for later; amnesia or dreams/memory gaps/imagery/body experiences as proof something happened; no exit at the end (a role, judgment down, abilities down, blank, altered indefinitely).
- A real choice needs at least one way not to continue: lighter, another method, full awareness, pause or stop. No means no even with a nice voice; silence or not knowing what to say isn't yes. Longer checklist: Appendix C.
- Induction (his words): gathers awareness into a gentler, trancier, receptive form to hear quiet inner knowing; not about surrendering to a hypnotist while playing asleep. Do one if sober but distracted or analytical; if already trancey, skip to "Already trancey? Stabilize before dipping deeper."
- Many ways to induce; this method counts; talk, story, movement, sound, curiosity also fine; counting is explicitly an induction, implicit methods need their function explained. Test depth with a simple target suggestion: automatic subconscious response vs. analytical questioning.
- Keep it simple (KISS): over-complicated scripts leave you unsure if it worked; in self-hypnosis you can't go so deep you can't continue; you need an "access level" to respond. Start with one anchor/image, one tone (warm, indirect, direct, neutral witnessing), one metaphor (know why you switch), a simple stepwise walk out at the end. Metaphors and imagery are optional: any inner signal (knowing, emotion, rhythm, sound, body scanning). Keep the language inside the chosen world (count the raindrops, not sheep); come back out the way you went in (a staircase).
- Borrow qualities (warmth, steadiness) from someone who models them, but don't change who you are.
- Writing a script: a central verb ("letting go"), an overall sensory quality, a short refrain as another anchor, smooth transitions; read it aloud while writing (writing brain vs. vocal brain); no long elegant sentences; soft, to the point, easy to remember, say and grok.
