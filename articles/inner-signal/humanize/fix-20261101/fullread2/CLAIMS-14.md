# Part 14: the guide's claims and instructions (for the contradiction read, E310)

- What I will not use NLP for (his version, 1 November): not to convince anyone an intuition is true (NLP or hypnosis only for initial memory retrieval; memories need external validation, especially when led to); not to talk anyone out of a real signal (calming unbearable pain is fine, but keep access to the signal for root-cause healing); not to override fear without asking what it protects, or create urgency when someone wants time; not without consent or via covert commands (claiming hypnosis cannot violate consent is "absurdly ignorant and unethical"); not to frame someone's "no" as ego resistance; not to pretend someone in an unstable state consented.
- Running the whole NLP sequence (his version plus two kept steps): induce yourself (soft focus) or contain yourself (stabilize) first if too opened up; ask permission (is it useful today?); get a representation of the pattern (image with size, color, location, or a movement/sensation); find out if it's protecting something; gently change an aspect of the representation; bring in the 3 good adults (Nurturer warms, Protector makes safe, Guide points a direction; all one inner adult); anchor the good state (hand on heart, fingers, breath, posture) to get back to it; imagine a scenario where the old pattern would have been triggered and adopt a feasible new behavior ("I am not a bull anymore"); check body signals and other support for the new interpretation; is the new behavior kind and sensible; come all the way back and do something normal.
- Appendix C (his words, with our middle): when something pushes too hard (person, ad, ideology, teaching, romance, inner voice): "Okay, something is trying to influence me here. I don't have to obey it. I don't even have to attack it..." and questions (what would it make me believe that isn't true, what does my body say, what would love/truth/humility/life do). Truth can take questioning; love accommodates boundaries; real guidance is still there tomorrow; what's for you won't ask you to give up your wisdom and sovereignty.
- Check how it pushes (rushing, secrecy, shame, threatened exclusion, feeling special, sexual charge, spiritual superiority) and what it wants (money, sex, loyalty, silence, obedience); people who mean well ask for some of these too (a teacher's fee, a partner's loyalty); a good sign is that they're fine with your no.
- More questions: does it narrow choices, make questioning feel wrong or disobedience a problem, make normal life seem boring, seduce/scare/rescue your inner child, stop your adult from saying no? If several: slow down, don't go deeper into trance, nothing irreversible, ground yourself (body, food, sleep, time, counsel).
- A good guide strengthens your adult self and can take no; a bad guide can't take no, wants your adult's job, frames your no as resistance, ego, fear, low vibration, trauma response, sin, or need for more of its guidance.
- Hypnosis-specific audit (his words): what induction mechanics are used; can my responses pause, modify or stop it; does the hypnotist treat my not noticing as a sign it worked; identity roles, obedience ladders, age regression, belonging used to lower judgment (check the script or explain what your judgment accepts); are fractionation, cues, memory or post-session effects disclosed; are body sensations turned into evidence; does the exit clearly end the session and restore identity and judgment; if unclear, ask for the script, change the method, or walk out.
- Appendix D (anxiety, body first): anxiety often comes from a childhood guard part (protects you when the inner adult's Protector isn't working, as in childhood), guessing at danger, shame, abandonment, failure, exposure, illness, rejection, loss of control, the unbearable; it can be right, and has been wrong; don't interrogate it first; help the body. Order: body, inner child, inquiry, action.
- Reduced breathing (Buteyko-style): can help overbreathing or panic; don't hold your breath into air hunger (more anxiety short-term); small nasal inhales, quiet nasal exhales, pause only if comfortable, very quiet breathing; stop if bad, dizzy, panicky, or medical issues.
- EFT (Emotional Freedom Techniques, tapping): "Even though I have this anxiety, I am willing to be kind to the part of me that is afraid," tapping gently; limited evidence (a 2025 systematic review: 7 RCTs for anxiety disorders, better than no intervention in several); treat it with caution; first-line therapies exist.
