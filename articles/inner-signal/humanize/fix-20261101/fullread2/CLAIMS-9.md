# Part 9: the guide's claims and instructions (for the contradiction read, E310)

- The inner meeting: if you tend to daydream, dissociate or fall asleep, sit up (save lying down for sleep, deep rest, dream incubation); spine straight not rigid, jaw relaxed, connected to the room.
- Invocation: "I am not leaving life; I am listening more deeply to life. I invoke my Higher Self, universal heart-mind, wise subconscious mind, intelligence in my body, inner child(ren), inner adult(s), guard(s) and all that is of truth and love to help me know what I need to know, only in a form and at a time that will be useful, kind, stabilizing and real for me." (Check against "the Higher Self has no specific wisdom": here it is one of many asked to help.)
- Ask the conscious mind to listen; don't force an answer; you needn't stop thinking; notice thoughts, sensations, pictures, doubts, emotions without fighting or following; notice what stands out on its own.
- "Tell me what I need to know or ask me what you need to know and I'll sit in silence for 5 minutes and listen." Responses: a phrase, sensation, memory, color, your inner child or a guard, pain, warmth, silence, a task, gratitude, the need to stop. Listen to everything, believe nothing automatically.
- Let the next response guide you: answer what actually happens, not a pre-decided script; read these sections, then do the meeting without the screen; respond to each thing with an accurate notice and a small reasonable invitation (or action, or more time); any form; no need to cover all forms.
- Finding your own words: notice ("I notice my chest tightening when I imagine asking for help") without interpretation ("abandoned child", "protective mechanism"); invite ("I can take some time before deciding what it means..."); then listen; plain language ("what happens next?"); stay quiet while a feeling develops, unless you need to talk to understand; be straightforward when that's the help you chose; a sad feeling or tension doesn't require an exit while you're present, able to change and willing.
- Notice, meaning, thoughts on the meaning: separate them when you can (often fuzzy); "My stomach went tight" (notice) vs. "He's a complete bastard" (meaning, even wordless); find out what he did, what gave you the idea, other explanations; putting a wordless meaning into words now is fine; nonverbal and early thoughts aren't deeper, truer or more reliable; feelings deserve attention, and their meaning consideration.
- Let the response change the invitation: tightening or "that feels kind of wrong" = investigate; "No! That is Not Right!" = stop and change it, no other reason needed, don't repeat it more persuasively. (His words:) "I notice the tightness, and I can just leave it like that. Then again, is that what I really want?"; sometimes the invitation just isn't right even if it sounds hypnotic; doubt is sometimes a guard, sometimes normal consideration; lack of doubt doesn't mean it's working best.
- Look at the situation too: what the other person does (can they hear disagreement, take responsibility, make room?); specific, revisable conclusions; disagreement or boundaries don't mean immaturity; don't expose yourself to danger before protecting yourself. Your side: guilt doesn't prove wrongdoing or manipulation; did you cause harm (repair) or give in to avoid their disappointment? Choose the purpose of the next interaction (understanding, information, boundaries, ending it); you needn't make them understand before you act.
- Change the amount of contact: if too much, widen awareness to the room or body; move back and forth (pendulation; smaller amounts: titration); as much or little as you can; "I can let this much be here..."; "Ok, I think that is enough for now."
- If you lose contact with the here and now, things get more unreal, or you can't switch or stop: stop exploring, do the whole "coming back" part; prolonged disorientation: get human help, not a stronger induction.
- An urge to move: do it if it won't hurt, or imagine it; no need to act it out, shake or release; stop if it causes pain or worrying symptoms.
- Offer the care that is wanted: younger parts get company or space; "don't come closer" = keep their distance; no need to treat them as literally younger or ask what happened; guards: talk in the Nurturer voice, find out their concern; all welcome, none removed. Bring in the Nurturer, Protector or Guide (tone, boundaries, direction) without talking to them; borrow what you can't do yet; the Witness only to get started; don't redo a step you can do.
- Try one idea at a time (welcome, someone else, words, posture, distance). Giving care may bring upset (processing what was missing): it doesn't mean the love isn't working; stay as long as you want and can, but don't push for tears (more crying isn't more healing); if the voice, distance, touch or image is too much, change it; don't conclude you can't take love.
- Metaphor: let it develop ("if the problem had an image or story...", "what would need to happen to make things right?"); try the resource, boundary, distance or action as far as it helps; the result is metaphorical, a hypothesis, not a record of what happened.
- Or use the session to build a capacity (love, play, courage, curiosity) or rehearse a decided action.
