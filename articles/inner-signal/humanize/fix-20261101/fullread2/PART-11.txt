## The subconscious will not answer

There may be no answer, or the question may not help. You can change it, rest, or finish. If a concern about trust is actually present, ask:

“What do you need to know before you trust me?”

The reply may be extremely un-mystical: “Will you set the boundary?” “Will you keep the promise?” “Will you stop abandoning us?” “Will you stop substituting insight for action?”

## The inner child will not appear

If a guard is recognizable, speak to it; otherwise stay with the feeling or preference that is actually present.

“You do not have to let me in. Just tell me what would make the doorway safer.”

If no clear child-state appears, use the session to notice one preference, curiosity, or beginner experiment instead of forcing an image. Identity-building and reparenting can happen in the same practice.

## You get anxious

Notice whether the anxiety is manageable and you want to stay with it, or whether you need less contact. When it is escalating or overwhelming, stop searching for content and help the body first.

Feet. Chair. Breath. Room.

“Little one, I am here. Protector, thank you for warning me. You do not have to scream for me to listen. If there is real action needed, show me one step. If there is no action needed, help the body stand down gently.”

Get professional help when anxiety includes chest pain, fainting, suicidal thoughts, psychosis-like or mania-like symptoms, severe dissociation, inability to sleep, or urges to harm yourself or someone else. Appendix D has the longer anxiety toolbox.

## You dissociate or feel unreal

Open your eyes. Move. Touch something textured. Name five objects. End the deeper practice for the day.

## You become grandiose

Return to food, sleep, chores, humility, and somebody who will disagree with you.

“If this insight is real, it will survive sleep, food, time, humility, and feedback.”

## You become too analytical

When the practice turns into a technical performance, stop improving it. Put one hand on the heart and one on the solar plexus.

“I am not here to do this perfectly. I am here to be in relationship with what is deeper than my surface mind.”

“What would make this feel more like communion and less like performance?”

Silence may be the answer. Rest may be the answer. One honest action may be the answer. Take the answer that actually arrived.

## You become too blissful or diffuse

Ask the bliss to become specific.

“What task, boundary, meal, rest, repair, or conversation does love require next?”

## You want the hidden truth immediately

Don’t pry.

“Show me the capacity before the content.”

## You keep daydreaming

Sit up and open your eyes.

“What real action is this fantasy replacing?”

## You get stuck in spiritual bypass

“Where has divine love not yet descended into the wound?”

A genuine opening can show you a depth of love or freedom you hadn’t known was possible. Let that matter without demanding the child receive it on command. Prayer can also evoke abandonment or condemnation, or a teacher’s betrayal can damage something sacred. Ask what was lost and what needs questioning; more of the same practice isn’t automatically the answer.

Sometimes practical care helps while the available horizon of life still feels too small. Curiosity about deeper happiness is worth following without pretending to believe an explanation. Other people already have enough love and meaning for the life they want. Spirituality doesn’t need to become another assignment.

## You get stuck in endless processing

A real wound and a checking loop can coexist. Ask whether this round helps you feel, understand, decide, or respond differently—or whether you’re checking the same question for reassurance. Grief and practice can revisit familiar ground; novelty isn’t required every time.

When it is a checking loop, let the thought remain without arguing with it or chasing it away, and return to a chosen activity or relationship. You don’t have to hold an inner meeting every time a thought appears or complete a ritual before you’re allowed to live.

“What direction would the Leader choose now?”

## You keep using hypnosis but not changing life

“What would prove to the inner child that the adult is real?”

Then do the thing.

# What not to do

Do not treat inner voices, images, autonomous movements, or body signals as commands or proof. Listen, then reality-test.

Do not chase grand downloads or turn demons, jinn, and “Unattached Burdens” into a hobby. Stabilize first, and seek help when reality-testing or function weakens.

Do not use self-hypnosis while driving, bathing, swimming, operating equipment, supervising children, or doing anything that requires active attention.

Do not force memory recovery, use trance to prove what happened, or suppress medical symptoms.

Do not override hunger, fatigue, pain, grief, anger, or fear before understanding the signal.

Do not prolong altered states indefinitely or treat intuition as fact without testing it.

Keep the inner roles in proportion: the child does not lead adult life; the Protector cannot exclude all love; the Nurturer cannot erase consequences; the Leader cannot bypass the child; analysis and tenderness both remain available.

Do not use NLP to override protectors, manufacture certainty, manipulate anyone, or perform healing when the body says no.

Doubt, distraction, resistance, silence, and “no” do not prove that an induction is working.

“The unconscious will keep me safe” does not replace clear limits, a safe setting, the ability to stop, and a tested exit.

Ordinary sensations do not prove hidden trauma, illness, toxicity, possession, attachment, transformation, or subconscious truth.

Imagined repair does not establish physical recovery, neurological repair, cellular change, or a cure.

Do not install hidden instructions, undisclosed third-party activation, compulsory behavior, identity switching, or amnesia. A consciously chosen reusable cue should have a clear purpose, remain reviewable, and be retired if it becomes unwanted; saying “cancel” does not guarantee instant erasure.

End every waking session with ordinary identity, movement, orientation, judgment, and a direct statement that the session has ended.

## The subconscious will not answer

There may not be an answer. There may not be an answer that will help you. You may need to try asking a different question or you may just need to rest or finish your inner work for the day. If trust is actually a concern, you can try asking your subconscious “What would you need to know in order to trust me?” Some examples of answers might be “I need to know you will set boundaries,” “I need to know you will do what you said you will do,” “I need to know you won’t keep leaving,” or “I need to know you won’t think that knowing something means that you don’t need to do anything about it.”

## The inner child will not appear

If you can figure out who the guard is, talk to it. You can say to the guard “You don’t have to let me in, just tell me what I need to do to make it safe”. Even if there isn’t a child part that you can talk to, you can work with whatever you are aware of. You can work on your preferences or something that you’re curious about or something that you’re willing to try as a beginner etc. and see how it goes. Work on developing a sense of who you are and reparent yourself as best you can.

## You get anxious

Find out if the anxiety is bad enough that you want to decrease contact with it or if you can manage it and let it continue. If the anxiety is getting too bad, then you need to stop searching for content and help your body first, like the following:

-Where are your feet? Where are you sitting? Focus on your body, your breathing, and the room around you.

-Tell your little one and your guards (childhood protector parts) that you’re there for your little one, that you recognize that your guards are trying to protect you, and that you don’t need them to scream at you in order for you to know that something is wrong. Ask them if they need you to do something and if they do, ask them to show you one step at a time that you can do. If they don’t, then ask them to let your body calm down.

If you’re having chest pain, fainting, suicidal thoughts, psychosis-like symptoms, mania-like symptoms, severe dissociative symptoms, not being able to sleep, wanting to hurt yourself or somebody else, etc. then you need to get professional help. For more on handling anxiety, see the toolbox in Appendix D.

## You dissociate or feel unreal

Open your eyes, move around, feel something textured, name 5 things that you can see, and end the deeper practice for the day.

## You become grandiose

Go eat, go to sleep, do some chores, accept some humility, find somebody to disagree with you, etc.

What do you say to yourself in that moment? “If it’s really there, it’ll still be here after a good night’s sleep, some food, some time, and some subsequent humility, feedback, etc.”

## You become too analytical

You’re being too analytical. “What if you didn’t try to improve the practice?” Do the two-hand practice and say, “I don’t have to do this perfectly. I’m here for a relationship with something deeper than my surface mind.” “What can you do to make this a communion, not a performance?” Maybe it’s silence, resting, or doing something. Whatever it is, just do it.

## You become too blissful or diffuse

You’re being too blissful, diffuse, etc. Bring it down to a more specific level. “What’s the smallest, most specific thing your love is asking you to do next? A task, a boundary, a meal, some rest, a repair, a conversation?”

## You want the hidden truth immediately

Don’t pry.

“Show me the capacity before the content.”

## You keep daydreaming

You’re daydreaming. Sit up and open your eyes, and ask yourself, “What are you avoiding doing in the real world by doing this?”

## You get stuck in spiritual bypass

“Where has divine love not yet descended into the wound?”

Maybe you had a profound opening that showed you how much more love and freedom is possible. Let that matter, but don’t demand that the child receive it on command. Invite your little one in with you. They might go numb, or call BS on that. If so, they might just need to see if you’re actually humble enough, and grateful enough for them, to listen to their rejection and be patient. Maybe when you prayed it seemed like you were being abandoned and condemned. Maybe you felt betrayed by a spiritual teacher. It may be worth exploring these, but you might not want to do more of the same practice.

Sometimes it’s best to get some practical help while life still feels too small, and let yourself be curious about finding more happiness, without necessarily believing the explanation of how it’s going to work. Sometimes people have enough love and meaning to just live their lives, and trying to be spiritual doesn’t have to be another assignment.

## You get stuck in endless processing

You may have a real wound that you need to deal with and be caught in a checking loop at the same time. Consider asking yourself “Will processing this again actually help me feel, understand, decide, or do something differently? Or is this a form of checking to see if I can get the reassurance that something is finally okay?” Sometimes, whether you’re grieving or doing your practice, it’s okay to cover the same ground again and again.

The best thing to do, though, if you notice you’re in a checking loop, is allow the thought to be there without engaging with it and get on with what you have chosen to do. You don’t have to have a meeting with every thought that pops into your head before you start your life. You don’t have to complete a ritual before you start your life.

What would the Guide choose to do now?

## You keep using hypnosis but not changing life

You’re doing hypnosis over and over again without making changes in your life. Ask yourself, “What can I do to show my little one I’m for real?” Pick one thing to do that’s small enough to repeat, then go do it. A big gesture you later drop would only prove them right to doubt you, but a small thing you keep doing can change their mind.

# What not to do

DO NOT: View your inner experiences as commands or proof. They’re internal happenings that need to be reality tested at all times.

DO NOT: Engage in seeking “downloads” of information or make a hobby of demons/jinn/Unattached Burdens or the like. Stabilize your life first, and get help to do so if you are not reality testing well, or not functioning well in your life.

DO NOT: Participate in self-hypnosis while operating a car, bathing, swimming, operating any equipment, supervising children, or engaged in any activity which requires active attention.

DO NOT: Attempt to “force” memories, to use trance to “prove” something happened to you, or to skip medical attention for symptoms just because you can “force” them down using hypnosis.

DO NOT: Force yourself to ignore your body’s needs, such as eating when hungry, resting when tired, responding to pain, etc., or your emotions, such as letting yourself grieve when someone dies, feeling anger when it is appropriate, acknowledging your fears, etc., until you know more about what the signals are telling you.

DO NOT: “Extend” the duration of altered states indefinitely.

DO NOT: Allow various internal roles to get out of proportion (e.g., allow the Inner Child to run your adult life, the Protector to shut out all love, the Nurturer to disregard consequences, the Guide to ignore the needs of the Child). You are capable of analysis, as well as tenderness.

DO NOT: Utilize NLP techniques to force down guard parts, to give yourself certainty you do not have, to manipulate others, or to heal yourself when your body is telling you not to.

Don’t consider negative indicators (doubt, distraction, resistance, lack of response, saying “No”) as good signs that the induction is working.

Don’t rely upon the safety of your unconscious mind as the only safety factor in any session. Be sure you have set up clear limits, are in a safe place, and have a way to terminate the session and get out which has been tested and proven to work.

Don’t believe that normal physical sensations are indications of hidden trauma, illness, toxins in your body, possession, “attachments,” “transformations,” or information from your “subconscious mind.”

Don’t believe that anything is “healed” in your body (neurologically, cellularly, etc.) simply because it is “healed” in your imagination.

Don’t put hidden commands in yourself, nor allow anyone to do so. Even if you are consciously placing something in yourself which you wish to utilize later, be sure that you know exactly what you are doing and why you are doing it, and be sure that you can review it and CAN “take it out” if you decide that you no longer wish to use it. It may not be as easy as simply saying, “Take out.” Nor should you allow anyone to activate these commands in you without your knowledge, or use them to make you do something you don’t want to do, to make you into someone you don’t want to be, to give you amnesia, etc.
