# Part 13: the guide's claims and instructions (for the contradiction read, E310)

- Appendix B (NLP = Neuro-Linguistic Programming): not needed for the main path; useful for imagery, body states, rehearsal and sentences, as long as it helps you listen; a trap if it becomes a bag of tricks instead of love, truth, contact and consent.
- Four levels: sensory (the form of an inner experience), state (making useful states easier to reach), strategy (practicing a better response), language (pacing truth, opening awareness, presuming only safe capacity, careful cause-effect links, embedded invitations, de-hypnotic questions against vague, absolute, manipulative or grandiose language).
- The Milton model (sentence architecture): commands ("relax now", "trust me") don't work; indirect language goes around the part that wants to argue; start with something true, take the pressure off, presume only a tiny bit, invite one thing (bed-sensation example); then test it with the body.
- Pattern 1, pacing and leading: what's true (the sensation, the system's understandable reaction), while something else is equally true (the adult is here, breathing, listening). Truth, invite, truth, invite.
- Pattern 2, awareness verbs (notice, realize, sense, wonder, imagine, allow, discover...).
- Pattern 3, presuppositions: only presume choice of timing, ability, safety, being loved, the adult leading; never obedience.
- Pattern 4, bridge words (as, while, because, when, after, before, which means) link events without proving causation. (His words:) avoid commanding bridges ("Because I breathe, the trauma leaves"); prefer "As I breathe, my adult self can allow itself to become present enough to hear a bit of what my wounded inner child needs."
- Pattern 5, embedded invitations: nudges, not hidden commands ("And I can allow myself to experience...", "My little one doesn't have to trust me all at once..."); not ten in a row.
- Pattern 6, ethical double binds: two genuinely safe options (warmth or space), unlike a manipulative one.
- Pattern 7, conversational postulates ("Can I allow my subconscious to show me the next thing to do, without ... all the details of wounding?").
- Pattern 8, tag questions: oily fast; don't overdo.
- Pattern 9, nominalizations (healing, safety, trust...): vague, so the subconscious fills them in.
- Pattern 10, Meta Model questions (what specifically, according to whom, compared to what, always, never, how do you know, what would change your mind): Milton opens, Meta checks; ethics needs both (Milton when the body stiffens, Meta when you lose touch with the truth).
- Submodalities: work only with sensory qualities you actually notice (don't invent them); move an image back, soften edges, lower a voice's volume; you're not deleting the message; ask the guard first.
- Anchoring: pair a cue (hand on heart, fingers, breath, word, posture, smell, song) with a state you've really been in, while you clearly feel it; for re-entry a usable receptive state, not a peak; the shortcut section for getting back in; keep it different from the calm-but-alert cue and the synchronicity cue.
- The synchronicity wake-up cue: noticing a synchronicity means "wake up", not a message: stop, orient, go wider, reality-check, listen, do one loving thing; feel body and room; the noticing doesn't confirm the reading; widen; ask what's real vs. interpretation, what needs warmth, protection, action; from the widest grounded perspective feel your feelings; a negative state may be the inner child, a guard, grief, the body, or the situation; choose a loving action; be in whatever state is real. You don't identify with the state but still hear its message.
- Installing it: rehearse a harmless synchronicity, the "whoa", feel feet, body, breath, room; it's a bell, not confirmation; ask the Nurturer, Protector and Guide; rehearse grounding, a wider view, an action, waking up fully. Cues don't authorize decisions or block intuition's warnings. Like implementation intentions (Gollwitzer), though not researched for this.
- False positives: when absorbed or destabilized you may see synchronicities that aren't there; apophenia is linked with psychosis-spectrum traits (Blain; Grant); noticing patterns isn't pathology. If cues make things worse (compulsive looking, no sleep, anxiety, certainty that random events are messages, can't tell noticing from interpreting), deactivate: stop rehearsing, decide while awake; the association may not vanish at once.
- Reframing without lying (partly his words): not "it was good all along"; ask what else might be true, what the part is doing for me, how the Nurturer/Protector/Guide would reframe it (e.g. a vicious cycle of expectation).
- Perceptual positions: self, other, witness, universal heart; for accuracy, never to rationalize bad behavior.
- Future pacing: imagine scenarios where the old behavior would be triggered; choose small new behaviors with if-then plans.
- The Swish, softened: let the old pattern be there while holding the new one; feel and compare the futures.
- Parts integration: ask conflicting parts what they want, fear and need; find a third option without either dominating.
