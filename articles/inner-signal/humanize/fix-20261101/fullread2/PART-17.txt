## A response check, not a verdict about what it is

Once you’re stable enough to stay present, notice what helps without turning the response into a metaphysical test. Put a hand on the heart if that feels welcome, take a comfortable breath, and keep the room available.

I don’t have to decide what this is right now. I can notice whether warmth or distance helps, and keep my boundary either way. Nothing here requires my obedience.

If vulnerability appears—a knot in the throat, sadness, fear, or a sense of trying to protect something—you can bring in the Nurturer. If the experience remains hostile or becomes more frightening, stop engaging with it and return to the body and room.

Neither response establishes its origin. A protective part needn’t reveal its fear quickly; a comforting response doesn’t prove an interpretation either. The useful question is whether this way of responding helps you become clearer, steadier, and less hooked. Love and boundaries don’t require a verdict first.

## Do not fight from fear

The first impulse may be to fight, banish, hate, prove power, argue, panic, or identify completely with the threatened self. Some traditions use prayers, mantras, protections, banishing rituals, or exorcism forms, and some people find them useful. But fighting from fear can fasten attention more tightly to the thing you want gone.

Fear gives it center stage. Hatred gives it a hook. Pride gives it an opponent. Panic gives it the nervous system.

“I do not need to hate this. I do not need to merge with this. I do not need to obey this. I do not need to defeat this from fear. I return to loving awareness.”

## A bodily boundary: compassion present, door closed

When the presence feels hostile, cold, alien, parasitic, or boundary-violating, don’t offer soft sentimental love as though inviting it in for tea. Use love with a boundary.

Sit upright. Press your feet into the floor. Let the spine rise. Gently draw the shoulder blades back so the chest is open but not exposed. Feel the skin boundary of the torso. Feel that this body has a perimeter.

“I recognize that suffering may be here, and I wish all suffering to return to the space where it belongs. But the boundary of this skin belongs to my adult life. You do not have permission to enter this house.”

“Compassion is present. The door is closed.”

## If it might be psychosis or destabilization

A symbolic hostile-presence experience that settles with grounding and love is very different from a state that becomes persistent, paranoid, sleepless, dangerous, or impossible to reality-test.

If somebody believes they’re under attack for days, can’t sleep or function, hears commands, feels compelled to hurt themselves or anyone else, becomes manic, can’t communicate normally, or can’t distinguish symbolic experience from ordinary reality, stop treating the problem as hypnosis material. Get help.

If the practice makes you more loving, grounded, relational, honest, and functional, continue gently. If it makes you more paranoid, sleepless, grandiose, isolated, frightened, or unreal, stop deepening and get support.

## The cosmic-download humility test

Hostile presences, shadow forces, grandiose parts, and altered-state imagery don’t always attack through fear. Sometimes they seduce through importance: a cosmic secret, a special mission, a vast download, a feeling of being uniquely chosen, or a revelation that makes washing dishes seem beneath your new rank.

“Thank you for the imagery. I do not require a grand performance. If this information cannot become a simple, honest act of care tomorrow morning — washing the dishes, cleaning my room, keeping a promise, apologizing, resting, eating, or helping someone I love — it is not useful to my path.”

Guidance worth keeping should make ordinary life more beautiful, honest, relational, humble, reliable, and useful to other people.

## Don’t become an entity hobbyist

Demons, jinn, hostile presences, and “Unattached Burdens” all lead to the same practical training: can love remain present without surrendering the boundary, and can the mind become harder to hook? I’m not trying to turn anyone into an entity hobbyist.

# Appendix J: A practical induction library

Preview the sequence, choose one purpose, and decide whether you want to begin. Each family can be light, eyes-open, or replaced by no-trance contact. Notice the actual response—including no change—and let it determine the next move. Move, imagine, change the sensory channel, remain still, or stop. Use the complete waking return after every induction.

If vivid images don’t come, translate the metaphor into words, rhythm, spatial sense, touch, movement, or simple knowing. The mind doesn’t owe anybody a movie.

## 1. Stretch and release

Feel the support beneath you and the position of one hand or foot. Press gently, stretch slightly, and release—or imagine it. Notice ease, tension, warmth, breath, or no change. Repeat with no more than three areas, letting “release” guide the sequence. Which area or inner voice wants attention? What support is needed? Restore ordinary movement and return.

## 2. Supported floating

Notice where your weight is carried. Let support carry 1% more effort, as if floating without losing contact, or keep ordinary weight and pressure. Notice the response. You may deliberately alternate attention toward ordinary awareness on the inhale and support on the exhale; name this fractionation beforehand. What becomes easier to hear? Keep the support, regain chosen weight and firm contact, then return.

## 3. Rhythmic breath or pulse

Accompany a natural rhythm: breath, a comfortable hum, a finger tap, outside sound, or silent counting. No rhythm is also an option. Repeat “here, and here again,” noticing where attention lands between repetitions. Stop the deliberate rhythm and notice the first quiet beat, without trying to become blank. Is a word, feeling, need, or action clearer? Let ordinary rhythm resume and return.

## 4. Unwinding object

Notice a concern with several strands. Imagine yarn unwinding, bubbles settling, or knots placed visibly beside you. Leave the object unchanged or label it in words when that fits better. Count only strands you actually set down; nothing is erased. Which needs warmth, protection, or action? Keep one useful strand, give the others a visible holding place, and return.

## A response check, not a verdict about what it is

If, however, you feel like you’re stable enough to stay present, check in with yourself and see what’s best to do. Put your hand on your heart if that feels okay, breathe comfortably, and stay aware of the room.

Internalize the fact that you don’t have to know what it is. Find out if it helps to be warm towards it or to keep it at arm’s length, and keep your boundary either way. Keep in mind that you don’t have to do what it tells you to do.

If you feel vulnerable, if you feel tightness in your throat, if you feel sadness or fear or like you’re trying to protect something, call upon your Nurturer. If you don’t feel better, or if it continues being hostile towards you, stop trying to deal with it and return to your body and the room.

You don’t have to find out if it’s a guard immediately, and you may not be able to comfort it quickly. Just keep an eye on whether the way you’re responding to it helps you get clearer and steadier and less hooked.

## Do not fight from fear

Your first instinct may be to fight it, banish it, hate it, show it that you’re more powerful than it is, argue with it, panic, become your own threatened self in fear of it, etc. Some people may want to use traditional prayer or mantras or protection or banishing or exorcism techniques, and those may well work for them. But if you’re fighting it out of fear, it can backfire:

It becomes a center of attention if you fear it, and if you hate it you give it a hook in you. (If you’re proud you give it an opponent, and if you panic you give it your nervous system.)

So the next time it comes up you can just say, “I see that there is no reason to hate and revile what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to merge with or become what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to obey what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to try to defeat what I am experiencing out of fear. I choose to return my attention to loving awareness.”

## A bodily boundary: compassion present, door closed

You can also tell the presence that while you have compassion for it, the door is closed.

This is especially a good idea if the presence you’re experiencing seems even the slightest bit hostile, cold, alien, parasitic, etc. It’s not really helpful to approach it with the kind of soft, sentimental love that you’d use if you were inviting it to stay for tea, but it can be useful to approach it with love while also making it clear that it needs to respect your boundaries.

Sit up, make sure your feet are grounded on the floor, straighten your spine, pull your shoulder blades back a little, open your chest a little, feel the boundary of your torso’s skin, feel the perimeter of your body, and tell the presence, “While I have compassion for you and any suffering you may be experiencing, this suffering needs to go back where it came from. The boundary of my skin is the boundary of an adult now, and you’re not welcome to come inside the house anymore.”

## If it might be psychosis or destabilization

This is assuming that you are having a symbolic experience of “hostile presences” which responds to grounding and love. It may be that what you are experiencing is actually psychotic or a form of destabilization.

If you feel like you are under attack for multiple days at a time, if you are not able to sleep, if you are not able to function normally, if you are hearing voices telling you to do things, if you feel you need to hurt yourself or somebody else, if you feel you are going manic, if you feel you can’t communicate normally, if you feel you can’t distinguish between what you are experiencing symbolically and normal reality, get help immediately. It’s not something to deal with in hypnosis.

### The General Rule

The General Rule is: if you find that what you're doing is making you more loving, more grounded, more relational, more honest, more functional, then it's probably OK to keep doing it gently. But if what you're doing is making you more paranoid, sleepless, grandiose, isolated, scared, or more and more unreal, then DON'T GO DEEPER! GET HELP!

## The cosmic-download humility test

Presences, shadow forces, or grandiose parts don’t always attack through fear, either. They may also try to give you a sense that you’re special, try to “download cosmic info” to you, try to give you a special mission, make you feel doing the dishes is beneath you, etc. You can just say, “Thank you for the imagery, but I don’t need your grand performance. Unless the info you’re trying to download to me can help me do the dishes, clean my room, be more honest, be more helpful, be more loving, keep my promises, take care of my body by resting and eating etc, I don’t need it.”

Guidance worth keeping should make ordinary life more beautiful, honest, relational, humble, reliable, and useful to other people.

## Don’t become an entity hobbyist

Finally, some of you may be tempted to make this an area of hobbyism – to become interested in demons or jinn or hostile presences or Unattached Burdens or whatever. I just don’t want you to do that. They all lead to the same thing, which is how to maintain a presence of love without giving up your boundaries, while also making your mind harder to hook.

# Appendix J: A practical induction library

Preview the sequence. Work out in advance which one you want to do and what you want to use it for (pick just one) and whether or not you want to do it at all! Any of these families of induction can be done as light inductions, with eyes open, or you can just do them as a no-trance inner contact.

Once you start an induction, you will want to notice what’s going on (even if nothing changes) and work out what to do next: move, imagine something else, change the sensory channel you’re using, do nothing, or stop the induction sequence and do something else… Whatever you do, make sure you bring yourself fully back to waking at the end of an induction.

NB: if you can’t ‘see’ the images you’re trying to visualize, that’s fine, change them into words, rhythm, spatial relations, something to touch or move or just plain knowing…

## 1. Stretch and release

Work out what will be supporting you, and focus on the feeling of support. You can also focus on where one of your hands or feet is. Either actually, or in your imagination, stretch a little bit, either stretching that part of your body slightly or pressing gently down into the support, and then let go of the effort, just relax. See if there is any difference (it’s fine if there isn’t). Do this for no more than 3 places on your body. Let releasing guide you as you go through the sequence. Which bit of your body, or inner voice, needs this? What do you need to support it? Bring your body back to normal movement and return.

## 2. Supported floating

Work out what is supporting you and where your weight is. Imagine that the support is holding a tiny bit (1%) more of your effort. It’s like floating but you don’t have to lose contact, or you can just keep it at the same level, with just enough weight/pressure. See what happens. You can fractionate this if you want, perhaps noticing your normal awareness when you breathe in and the support when you breathe out. Tell yourself you are going to fractionate before you do it. What can you hear more easily now? Keep the support, come back to your normal weight and firm contact, and return.

## 3. Rhythmic breath or pulse

Use your breathing or a slight hum in your throat for a natural rhythm. You could also tap your finger, or follow a sound outside. You can even do it by counting to yourself (silently) or with no rhythm at all. Say “here, and here again” over and over, and notice what you pay attention to in between the phrases. Then stop saying it. Notice what you become aware of in the first beat – you don’t need to make your mind blank, just notice what comes up. Does it make any words, feelings, needs or actions more clear? Bring back your normal rhythm and return.

## 4. Unwinding object

Unwinding an object is good for when you have a few different strands to one thing you want to work on. Imagine an object unwinding – maybe some yarn, some bubbles, or some knots that you place beside you. Once you’ve placed them all, give yourself a minute to look at them all. You don’t have to change anything, but you can label them if you need to (just a word or two). How many did you put down? Which one do you need to work on, and what does it need – warmth, protection, action? Keep one strand in your attention and put the others somewhere you can see them and return.
