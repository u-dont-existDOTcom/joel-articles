# Part 1: the guide's claims and instructions (for the contradiction read, E310)

- Order of use: Quick start, one induction and the whole return, then "Let the next response guide you"; daily and weekly sections; appendices; sources in Appendix K.
- The method's steps, in order: gather attention; notice what is here; offer one fitting response; wait; come all the way back. Not much needs to happen in a session.
- Before the first session: read the induction and the full return; then put the screen away (you run the practice yourself).
- Follow whatever you notice (sensation, image, phrase, feeling, objection, silence); let it shape the next invite; vary invites (warm, boundary, direction, more time).
- Always complete the return to normal waking consciousness; afterwards note what happened and the real-world actions.
- If panicky, disoriented, more unreal or less functional: stop deepening, return to the room, get support (not the time to stay with hard feelings).
- Listening is the most important step; a dramatic trance isn't needed; results show in day-to-day life.
- Work with the subconscious as an ecology; often begin with the Higher Self (universal heart); separate into parts only when needed.
- "Say", "ask", "listen" are invitations; parts needn't answer in sentences or pictures.
- Hypnosis (APA Division 30): focused attention, reduced peripheral awareness, increased responsiveness to suggestion; here used as therapy.
- Mesmer discovered the modern technique in the 1770s; nightly sleep/waking trance states resemble hypnosis and can be used for therapy.
- Influence often bypasses the conscious mind (FTC dark patterns, OECD).
- Self-hypnosis trains your own mind from the inside.
- People can be manipulated through hypnotism (the V. story).
- Never let a strong state, charisma, sexual charge, spiritual claim or authority replace the adult part; legitimate influence survives questions, sleep, boundaries and time.
- Affirmations: don't affirm what you know is false (that's bullying your beliefs); his dad's "expect the very best, but be prepared to take what you get"; say "I'm feeling sad now, but I'm doing better and better" rather than "I am sad" (identifying keeps you stuck).
