# Part 5: the guide's claims and instructions (for the contradiction read, E310)

- Reparenting and self-hypnosis fit together, but learn inner-child work sober first, in your ordinary state (the one you live in), and get enough Nurturer and Protector in the room before going deep.
- In hypnosis your inner child may show up in any form (image, posture, voice, memory, emotion), any age, any vulnerable part; it needn't be a child; if none is there, work with a guard or whatever you find.
- Don't rush the repair: don't start a healing project right away; be with them; tell them they needn't explain, be healed today, or impress you; you want to know what would help them feel less alone.
- Needs can be anything: warmth, play, protection, mourning, anger, food, rest, boundaries, better relationships, less fantasizing, less overwork, a promise kept today.
- When a younger you, guard, feeling or signal comes up, take it slowly: A) prepare (privacy, support available; check how much grown-up you is in the room: can you pause, follow through? if you're basically the frightened kid, leave the deeper conversation for later and come back first); B) look before judging; C) ask what you can do / if you can come closer, even if you think you know (warmth, space, words, silence, distance); D) give what you can: one need, the smallest helpful thing, not a total fix; E) let it sink in (time, sleep); F) reconnect if they're willing, no expectation to merge, go away, forgive or become happy; G) stop and rest before care becomes a performance; reconnect later to see what helped.
- The goal: good as you (more supported and connected), not good as new. If parts work makes you feel more divided, use a signal, feeling, or an imagined younger you.
- Play is fine in reparenting (textures, drawing, exercise, a plushie with a voice) as long as reality testing holds (you're not a child, pet or doll; nobody owns you); play is part of how successful therapy is measured.
- Rest doesn't mean helplessness: limit decisions for a while, list what you may not get to, let the inner adult keep watch; you can still move, stop and come back.
- Non-dominant hand dialogue: if speaking or visualizing feels fake, write; the inner child or guard writes with the non-dominant hand, you answer with the dominant; example dialogue ("I don't trust you" / "I know ... I love you ... a promise I can keep today" / "what promise?" / eat, clear the desk, not text so-and-so). Only say "I love you" if you feel it; otherwise say you care.
- When "your voice" is everyone else's: if no private self could develop (controlling family, rigid religion, enmeshed community, early pressure to achieve), you may not know what you feel, want or believe until you hear others; "love yourself" is hard when you don't know who "yourself" is; do reparenting and finding yourself in parallel. For others the inner child is easy to feel.
- Signs: don't know what you want until others say; opinions shift with company; agree then later disagree; responsible for others' emotions at your expense; self-love feels like a show; guessing instead of feeling; can rebel but not yet choose.
- Explanation: childhood left little room to find what you like, want to try, who you are when nobody grades you; you were busy avoiding pain and keeping the bond.
- Taking a break from the people or group you get answers from can help; some come back on different terms, sometimes the break has to be long-term; you learn which; let quieter preferences surface, not just echo the group.
- Human being vs. human doing (his words): without feeling good enough as a being, you earn it by beauty, skill, smarts, status, spirituality; real gifts, but a bargain for belonging when failing feels like losing your inherent value.
- Questions: what do I like when nobody looks; believe without checking; want that's neither rebellion nor compliance; where do I buy belonging by betraying myself; what can I try without being good at it; with whom can I be myself; what do I feel in my body and make of it.
- Give your inner child things with no résumé value it needn't be good at (painting, dancing, gardening, a language, sport, volunteering, music); the inherited parent script demands instant skill (humiliating); the adult answers "We're finding out whether we enjoy this, not proving our worth"; keep going until the humiliation is gone.
