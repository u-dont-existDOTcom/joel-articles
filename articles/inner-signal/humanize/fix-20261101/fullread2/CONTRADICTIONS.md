## Checked and not contradictions

- Appendix A asks the subconscious for answers while Appendix B warns about presupposing: the prompts ask, they don't presume obedience.
- The shortcut's mudra (his dad relaxed with it while driving) and "never use, practice or test it while driving": the guide keeps the calm-but-alert cue separate from the cue for going inward, and says not to drive in a trance.
- Fractionation offered in Optional fractionation and listed as a way to take choice away in Soft language can still take away choice: the second is unannounced fractionation; the guide's is announced.
- "Start with whatever adult function you can connect to" and "most of us need enough Nurturer and Protector first, before the Guide": the same sentence gives both, as a default and its usual exception.
- Daydreaming in session (Stay awake: guidance, working through, or avoidance), in troubleshooting (You keep daydreaming: avoidance) and in Appendix E (helping or replacing life): the troubleshooting entry is for when it is the problem; Appendix E has the test.
- "You don't need to ask any parts for permission to stop" and "Ask yourself for permission: is it useful to work with this pattern today?" / "always ask the guard if it's ok to do this": permission to start or to change something, never needed to stop.
- Self-hypnosis worked better for him than a hypnotist (Why doing it myself worked better for me) and both have advantages (Appendix F): the first paragraph already names a skilled guide's advantages.
- The three "your functioning in ordinary life is the feedback" rules (When this needs more support, Appendix G, The General Rule) say the same thing.
