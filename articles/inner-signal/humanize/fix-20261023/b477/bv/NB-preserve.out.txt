The funny thing about that, though, is that the words seem to have survived pretty well. People transmitted them communally, and despite a lot of variants—and re-ordering and additions—the early collections that were kept separately all have a lot in common. So the problem isn't preserving the pieces of the puzzle. The problem is preserving the picture on the box.

There's also a practical problem. Time and again, as in DN 3 and MN 56, the Buddha gives his students certain teachings only after he sees that their minds are ready: pliable, rid of the hindrances, elated, and confident. It's then—and only then—that he gives them the teaching that causes them to gain the Dhamma-eye. What are people supposed to do when they have a map that tells them what he said at each stage?
