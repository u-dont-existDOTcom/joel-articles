# Quick start: do this first

To work on difficulties or cultivate positive qualities (love, curiosity, play, confidence, etc.), simply do these steps, in order: 1) Attention; 2) Notice; 3) Offer one fitting response; 4) Wait; 5) Come all the way back . Not much needs to happen in each session!

Prior to your first session, read through the induction and the full return in waking life, so you’re more familiar with it. Give yourself a chunk of time when you can be physically secure for the time it takes. Prior to each session, consider what one thing you’re going to be working on or cultivating during this session, and what you’re going to leave alone. Put this screen on the shelf and conduct the practice.

You'll follow whatever you notice - sensation, image, phrase, feeling, objection, silence - as you're invited. Vary the invites as needed to be responsive & warm, to create a boundary, to give direction or more time, etc. Complete the return. After, note what happened & any real-world actions.

If at any point you feel panicky, disoriented, more unreal, or less functional, this is not about bravely sticking with difficult feelings (that's another choice, made freely & while staying present). Stop deepening, return to the room & get appropriate support.

# What I mean by hypnosis

APA Division 30 commonly defines hypnosis as focused attention, reduced peripheral awareness, and increased responsiveness to suggestion. That’s basically true, but there’s a lot more than meets the eye. In fact, hypnosis is such a broad and common type of altered state that it’s hard to confine it to one use. But I’m using hypnosis here as a therapy.

And hypnosis as a therapy is as old as time itself. While the modern technique of it was discovered by Franz Anton Mesmer in the 1770s, shamans have been using dancing, music, and drugs to induce hypnosis for therapeutic purposes forever. We enter into trance states every night which resemble hypnosis as we go to sleep and as we wake up as well. [Those states can also be used for deep therapy](http://lucid.u-dont-exist.com/).

# Affirmations Done Right

[My dad](http://utopia.u-dont-exist.com/) always said: “Always, and in everything, expect the very best. But be prepared to take what you get. The best might come afterwards.” That’s a better affirmation than what most people think of as affirmation, because it’s not telling you something which you know to be false in the present. If you say “I am beautiful” but you know that’s not true, then you’re challenging your beliefs, and if you’re challenging your beliefs you should have some argument or reason why they are wrong, otherwise it’s bullying.

Another quote I use all the time from my dad… when someone asks me “How’s it going?” I say “Better & better!” This really confuses a lot of people. Some think it’s amazing that I’m always doing better. Some ask if I was sick or something. But it’s doing multiple jobs. On the one hand, it’s deflecting from the fake question because most people don’t actually want to hear how you are doing in detail anyway. On the other hand, more importantly, it’s reprogramming your mind to remove you from tunnel vision. You might be sad now, but if you say, “I am sad” then you’re subtly identifying with the sadness and it keeps you stuck. It’s best to say, “I’m feeling sad now, but I’m doing better and better, every day, in every way!” That’s more like a *mission statement* than a historical analysis. Although you can also re-evaluate your history as lessons to make that true.

Here I will be teaching you a real self-skill, so you don’t end up a consumer of more and more elaborate trance recordings.

# A useful map of the mind

Note: whenever I refer to the ‘subconscious’ I’m referring to all of the processes under the surface of your conscious awareness: perceptions, emotions, desires, avoidance tendencies, imagery, memories, body signals, habits, intuitions, automatic behaviours and so forth. You can work with all of these together as if it’s all one ‘mind’, or you can work with it as a set of parts, an ‘ecology’ if you like. There isn’t a ‘right’ or ‘wrong’ way to approach it. They’re all maps. Don’t form a priesthood around either one!

Working with parts: Precision. Working with Higher Self: Coherence. Doing both: Communion.

If you need to work with the ‘whole’, the subconscious as one:

a) ‘Invoke’ the subconscious in some way. Something simple like ‘Hello, wise subconscious, please help me understand…’ or whatever works for you.

If you need to work with the ‘ecology’ in the subconscious (child parts, protectors, ‘critic’, fantasy making parts, body memories, social part, spiritual part (God/source/best benefit of all beings part) ‘wiser’ part etc etc then:

a) It’s usually a good idea to start with unity. So ‘invoke’ your Higher Self: Hello my Higher Self/universal heart-mind/wise subconscious, please guide me…

b) Move into differentiation. “Which part is afraid? Which part wants to protect you? Which part wants to avoid it? Which part knows what to do? Which part is too young for this?” Etc.

c) Move back into unity again. “And all of these parts are part of the one life, and as the adult in charge of this life you can lead with love…” Etc.

## Sometimes start with the whole

Some people make themselves too fragmented by starting to work with parts. ‘Here’s a part! Here’s another one! I’ll get twelve of them in a room and give them all an office to hang around in!’ So it’s better to start from the Sacred.

Start like this: ‘I’m approaching this from the universal heart. I’m approaching this from my Higher Self. I’m approaching this from the part of me that knows it belongs to life, not any wound, or role, or family, or group, or identity, or ideology, or story. So can you please tell me what’s going to be true and kind and timely and stabilising and useful here?’ Etc.

It can be that you can’t even access your Higher Self, or the universal heart, or whatever works for you. (Btw, if it doesn’t work for you don’t use it! It’s no good adopting somebody else’s map.) In that case, start by just witnessing whatever is coming up. Notice the room, your body, the fact that you’re in a younger state etc. Being able to notice is enough adult presence for the first step.

Once you have the ability to notice, and to differentiate, you don’t need to keep rehearsing it. Just work on whatever it is you need to work on (often warming up and protecting yourself and knowing what to do etc). So even though it can be useful to talk about your Higher Self, it doesn’t mean you already have all the good inner parenting functions etc that you need. You usually need to work on developing them.

Differentiate only as you need to. “Is this my inner child? Or is this a protector? Is this my body? Is this fear? Is this intuition? Is this my social part? Is this divine guidance, or is this my interpretation of divine guidance?”

Always start from the heart, differentiate when you need to, then come back to the heart again.

# The inner child, the guards, and the three inner adults

The inner child won’t trust you because you said three kind sentences during one meditation. Trust grows when the present-day adult becomes reliable.

I organize that adult capacity into three functions: Nurturer, Protector, and Leader/Guru. They aren’t three literal beings. They’re three ways the present-day person reparents the inner child.

The child’s worth doesn’t depend on developing those capacities. Worth was never the missing skill. The child also brings play, tenderness, irreverence, creativity, and wonder. Those qualities belong inside the adult relationship, not just the wounds.

## When the adult role is not available yet

Some people can summon one of those adult functions. Others go blank, hear only the inherited critic, or feel like a child pretending to be grown. Telling them to “be the adult” assumes the capacity they are trying to build.

For that starting point, I use a provisional, author-developed bridge I call borrowed adulthood. It combines familiar practices—witnessing, modeling, supported action, and gradual transfer of responsibility—without claiming that the complete sequence has been validated as one treatment.

Witness, when needed. Notice what is present without demanding warmth, wisdom, or certainty. If you already distinguish the internal positions, move to the adult function you need.

Borrow one function. Choose a safe adult, mentor, older sibling, future self, spiritual figure, value, or written plan. Borrow only the warmth, boundary, or direction needed now.

Become the adult apprentice. Choose the exact words or action yourself and carry at least one small part.

Hand it back. Name what the borrowed figure modeled and what five percent you can now do without them.

The borrowed figure stays beside your judgment rather than replacing it. The fuller sequence is in my [Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/inner-child-self-love-tips-and-guided).

You don’t need a history full of trustworthy people to borrow care. Use a remembered person when that helps, or an imagined adult whose intention is to take good care of you. Ask, “What would that person say or do with my actual history, limitations, and options?” Begin with the least cruel response you can believe if warmth is unavailable. Once you find one usable sentence or action, try it rather than repeating the exercise in different words.

Borrowing can also help the position trying to be the adult: how would a decent adult hear distrust without retaliating, defending their goodness, collapsing, or leaving? Borrow that one response. You may still feel young while doing it; adult identity can develop after the behavior.

## Let the child speak without handing over the steering wheel

Letting a younger feeling-state speak doesn’t mean handing adult judgment to an outside caretaker or trying to become literally younger. Wonder, play, vivid sensation, vulnerability, honest need, or the wish to carry fewer decisions can all come forward while the internal adult remains present.

“A younger part of me may speak. The adult in me remains here to listen, evaluate, protect, and stop. No child-state, guide, recording, or outside person replaces adult judgment.”

That distinction lets you recover curiosity, spontaneity, play, sensory freshness, honest need, and quick emotional movement without pairing those gifts with obedience, helplessness, scolding, or weakened judgment.

## Love doesn’t have to wait for trust

When love is available, the younger state doesn’t have to believe in it before you can offer it. Trust can question your promises, remember failures of protection, and take its time. Love can remain present while that happens.

“Okay. I believe that you don’t believe me. I still love you. You don’t have to love me back. Tell me what feels unsafe.”

That isn’t a way to end the complaint. Listen for what is true in it and answer seriously. Hearing anger without retaliation, admitting a real failure, and repairing after an internal attack are evidence alongside meals, boundaries, appointments, and kept promises. Non-retaliation expresses love; it shouldn’t replace love with a colder performance of good behavior.

Notice what each position teaches the others. A vow followed by blame when it is doubted teaches that love depends on receiving it correctly. The blaming voice and the voice making the vow may be different positions, the same position, or a blend. Don’t decide from the person’s chronological age.

When a younger version of you becomes the defendant, ask what they understood then, what options they could see and tolerate, and what happened because of the choice. Harm can require responsibility and repair. Punishment cannot retroactively create the capacity that was missing.

## The two-hand practice

Place one hand at the center of the chest as a physical connection to love. Put the other at the solar plexus, just beneath the chest, where you can relate to the vulnerable child.

Send warmth from the heart toward the child. Notice whatever comes back—a sensation, image, feeling, resistance, or nothing—and answer with warmth again. Let it become a conversation rather than a performance.

Love above. Child below. Adult present.

## The Nurturer

The Nurturer brings warmth without trying to rush the feeling away, and takes pleasure in the child’s company: “I’m glad you’re here.” The child needs to be enjoyed, not only comforted when hurt.

“It’s okay to feel this.” “I’m here.” “You don’t have to do anything right now.” “You are not too much.” “You can be small here.” “You can be angry here.” “You can be sad here.” “I will stay.”

## The Protector

The Protector turns warmth into something the child can believe.

“I will set the boundary.” “I will leave the unsafe conversation.” “I will stop texting the person who destabilizes us.” “I will eat.” “I will sleep.” “I will keep a small promise.” “I will not leave the child alone with adult consequences.”

This is where inner-child practice often turns decorative. The voice says loving things while the adult sends the destabilizing message, skips the meal, avoids the task, and breaks the promise. Trust begins when you handle the consequence.

If warmth is unavailable, a protective act can begin the relationship. Make it small enough to carry out and review what actually happened. A missed action might reflect fear, exhaustion, missing skills, a practical barrier, or a poor plan. Completion without immediate relief isn’t automatically failure, and a tick on a list doesn’t prove the action helped.

## The Leader, Guide, or Guru

The Leader gives direction. You can call this function Leader, Guide, Guru, Wise One, Sage, Inner Teacher, Director, or Higher Adult. The label matters much less than the ability to orient the whole system toward a life.

Direction can ask for something difficult without turning life into perfect discipline and hidden needs. Sometimes the next adult move is asking for help, expressing a preference, or letting a safe person see an imperfection. Wanting closeness is legitimate; punishing someone for needing space is a different strategy. Find a way to meet the need without erasing either person.

“We are not only here to feel better.” “We are here to become whole.” “We are here to belong to life itself, not only to people who approve of us.” “We are here to serve truth, beauty, love, God, source, nature, universe, dharma, Tao, and the benefit of all beings.”

Begin with the adult function you can actually reach. Protection may come before warmth. Direction should help the child grow without erasing care, play, or support.

## Working with protectors

Use this parts language when you actually recognize a protective pattern or find it useful to explore. A sensation, distraction, or objection doesn’t establish its cause. Notice it first; hunger may need food, skepticism may be reasonable, and a sentence may simply not fit.

People assume inner-child work begins with the child. Very often, you meet the guard at the door first.

You sit down to connect and get numbness, cynicism, anger, sudden hunger, a phone urge, a sexual urge, planning, sleepiness, dissociation, or the thought, “This is stupid.” I wouldn’t call those failures. They’re often the protector making an appearance in its usual charming way.

One important voice is the *inherited parent*: the internalized caregiver voice formed from how the child was treated. This is different from the *inner adult*—the Nurturer, Protector, and Leader/Guru you deliberately build. The inherited parent may sound like a critic or warden—don’t cry, don’t be needy, don’t embarrass us, don’t become too independent—and can make a new loving voice feel fake before you’ve even begun.

“I notice you. You are trying to protect something. I want to understand. What are you afraid would happen if you stopped?”

Then wait. The answer may be: “You’ll lie to yourself again.” “This will become another performance.” “If you feel the pain, you’ll be destroyed.” “You’ll use spirituality to avoid reality.” “You’ll forgive too early.” “You’ll call fantasy guidance.” “You’ll abandon us as soon as the session ends.”

“That makes sense. You do not have to move yet. What would help you trust me 1% more?”

Usually the protector needs evidence more than another spiritual insight. Hold the boundary. Handle the task. Eat the meal. Don’t send the message. Keep the promise. Sleep. Contact the therapist. Acknowledge the debt. Write down the truth.

## Protection that knows when to stop scanning

The Protector is more than an inner voice saying no. You can practice it as a bodily sequence:

Orient: look around and feel the support beneath you.

Assess: ask what is known, what is uncertain, and what needs checking.

Distinguish: separate present danger from uncertainty, old alarm, or imagined danger.

Set one proportionate boundary: move, stop, delay, ask, leave, or get support.

Verify enough safety: not perfect certainty, just enough reality-based evidence for the next step.

Stand down and rest: protection includes ending the scan when continued vigilance is no longer helping.

A mature Protector can say, “I don’t know.” It can ask another human, delay interpretation, or choose a no-trance practice. I’m aiming for discernment that acts when needed and then quits scanning the room after the danger has passed.

## Inner child reparenting

I connect my [reparenting therapy](http://innerchild.u-dont-exist.com/) to self-hypnosis because I find they are a perfect match. The child may arrive as an age, image, posture, voice, memory, emotion, or plain vulnerability. Sometimes no child appears. Stay with a recognizable protector, or with the sensation, feeling, or preference that is actually present.

When the child does appear, resist the urge to begin a healing project immediately. Be with them first.

“Little one, I am here. You do not have to explain everything. You do not have to be healed today. You do not have to become impressive. I only want to know what would help you feel less alone.”

The need may be warmth, play, protection, grief, anger, food, rest, a boundary, a safer relationship, less fantasy, less overwork, or one adult promise kept today.

## Don’t rush the repair

When a younger state, protector, feeling, or bodily signal needs care, move patiently:

Prepare: create enough time, privacy, support, and adult capacity.

Inspect: notice what is present before deciding what it means.

Ask permission: “May I come closer, or would you prefer warmth, space, words, silence, or no contact?”

Respond to one need: do the smallest useful thing rather than attempting a total repair.

Wait: let the response settle. Some changes need quiet, sleep, or ordinary time.

Reconnect: invite contact only where it is welcome. No part has to merge, disappear, forgive, or become cheerful.

Rest: stop before care becomes another performance.

Reassess: return later and ask what actually helped.

The goal isn’t “good as new,” as though history should disappear. Aim for good as you: more supported and connected while still recognizably yourself. If parts language makes you feel more divided, use *signals*, *feelings*, *younger states*, or another metaphor that helps.

## Play and rest still need an adult present

Reparenting doesn’t have to become solemn. A younger state may respond to texture, drawing, movement, a plushie with a voice, or whatever feels interesting. Keep reality-testing intact. You’re recovering play, not becoming somebody’s child, pet, doll, or owned object.

Rest also doesn’t require helplessness. Reduce decisions for a defined period, put unfinished tasks somewhere visible, and let the internal adult keep watch. You remain able to move, stop, and return.

## Non-dominant hand dialogue

When speech or visualization feels fake, write. Let the non-dominant hand speak for the child or protector, awkward handwriting and all. Answer with the dominant hand as the adult.

Non-dominant hand: “I don’t trust you.”

Dominant hand: “I hear that you don’t trust me. I still love you. Tell me where I’ve let you down. I won’t demand trust; I’ll listen and keep one small promise today.”

Non-dominant hand: “Which promise?”

Dominant hand: “I will eat, clean the desk, and not text the person who makes us feel unsafe.”

# When “your voice” is everybody else’s voice

Sometimes the inner-child question lands nowhere because a private self had very little room to develop. In controlling families, rigid religions, enmeshed communities, or high-pressure achievement systems, performance can begin so early that a person doesn’t know what they feel, want, believe, or prefer until somebody else tells them first. Then “love yourself” produces a reasonable question: love whom? Reparenting and identity-building can proceed together. For some people the child-self is already easy to feel. For others, creating enough safety for preference, curiosity, and experimentation is part of how that self becomes easier to hear.

This may apply when:

You do not know what you want unless someone else says what they want first.

Your opinions shift dramatically depending on who is in the room.

You agree in the moment and later realize you did not agree.

You feel responsible for other people’s emotions to the point of self-erasure.

Self-love feels performative.

The question “What does my inner child feel?” produces guesses, not felt answers.

You can rebel, but you cannot yet choose.

A useful developmental explanation is that when childhood is organized around avoiding pain and preserving attachment, attention goes into reading danger, meeting expectations, and keeping the bond. That can leave less room for the ordinary experiments that answer, “What do I like? What do I want to try? Who am I when nobody is grading me?” This is a practical model, not a claim about one specific brain system.

Sometimes a temporary, intentional period of distance from the people or group supplying the answers helps quieter preferences surface. Use that distance only long enough to tell your own signal from the social echo; prolonged isolation would undermine the purpose.

The difference between a *human doer* and a *human being* helps here. When worth isn’t felt internally, it gets earned through beauty, performance, intellect, status, spirituality, or becoming indispensable to everybody else. Those can all be real gifts. They become belonging bargains when failure in the arena feels like losing yourself.

Useful questions:

“What do I like when no one is watching?” “What do I believe before I check whether others approve?” “What do I want that is neither rebellion nor compliance?” “Where am I buying belonging with self-betrayal?” “Which arena am I using to earn the right to belong?” “What would I try if nobody needed to be impressed?” “Which relationships let me remain myself?” “What am I noticing in my body, and what meaning am I giving it?”

Give the inner child a small experiment with no résumé value: painting, dancing, gardening, a language, a sport, volunteering, music, or anything else you’re allowed to be bad at. The inherited parent may demand immediate talent. The adult can answer, “We’re finding out whether we enjoy this, not proving our worth.” Give it enough repetitions to get past the humiliation of being a beginner.

For a child, belonging can feel like survival, so families, religions, romances, political tribes, spiritual groups, institutions, and online communities can reach astonishingly far into identity. Adulting means noticing where you buy belonging with self-betrayal, choosing your own ground, and learning that you can disappoint someone without disappearing. The subconscious contains a crowd of borrowed voices, which is why this belongs in a self-hypnosis guide.

“Where do I become less true in order to remain included?”

“What would belonging look like if I did not have to disappear?”

“Is this guidance, or is this a belonging program?” “Is this love, or is this fear of exclusion?” “Is this conscience, or is this conditioning?” “Is this devotion, or is this compliance?” “Is this rebellion, or is this freedom?”

Then wait for the body. It isn’t infallible, but it deserves a seat at the hearing.

# De-hypnosis: wake up inside the influence

One of the best side-effects of learning self-hypnosis is influence literacy: an increased awareness of all the ways your attention is being shaped in your day-to-day life. By a screen? By a specific ad? By a politician? By religious fear? By erotic imprinting? By charismatic authority? By pressure to belong? By shame? By a sense of urgency? By repetition? It’s all around you. Sometimes it’s benign – like lullabies or prayers or being taught by a good teacher or listening to a beautiful song or being in love. But you may want to ask yourself what effect it’s having on your ability to see clearly and make choices.

Manipulation often comes with any of the following; urgency, you are special, FOMO (fear of missing out), not be included, sexual charge, shame, confused, someone will rescue you, destiny, you should obey, secret knowledge, and an excessive sense of certainty about something way too quickly and you have a sense that one good question will break the spell.

First of all, if you sense you are being hypnotically influenced, stop. Don’t obey immediately, but don’t rebel automatically, just realise that you are being influenced.

Secondly, some of the ways that hypnotic language is used to manipulate are where you have consented to something you didn’t realise, or you being given only one choice, or something not yet agreed upon is embedded in a sentence. For example;

You will soon realise that I am right …. Or you may, in time, decide to admit that …. Or the only reason you do not agree is …. Or part of you already knows …. Or your higher self wants …. Or everyone who is awake knows …. Or the reason you do not believe that is true is because your ego does not want you to …

If you are unsure, you may like to ask yourself the following questions;

What am I being asked to agree to? What choice am I being given? What is being assumed that I may not agree with? Who benefits if I comply? What is the emotionally charged part of what is being said? What is not being said? If I say no, what will the consequences be? (Will I be punished, shamed, laughed at, not be included, be told I am not ‘awake’ etc?)

This is a good guide to help engage your ‘adult’ in the conversation. The more powerful the ‘state’ the more you will need to make use of these.

## Soft language can still take away choice

Be aware that even when using ‘permissive’ language, you can still take away choice. Be aware of;

[Indirect suggestion](https://nimja.com/stories/random/the_contradiction_of_subject_agency)s can by-pass the subject’s ‘skeptical guard’. They do this by not directly telling the subject what to do. This does not mean that indirect suggestions are not specific. It simply means that they are worded in a vague, conversational, suggestive manner.

1) Utilizing induction techniques while seemingly explaining something (even something like "anti hypnosis" can use the same mechanics of induction).

2) In any way making it so that whatever the subject says, shows that a suggestion has worked, when this may not be the case (this includes answering yes, no, noticing something, not noticing something, trying not to do something, not answering, etc etc etc)

3) Including phrases such as whether or not you notice etc etc

4) Including any concepts that may make a subject think they must obey, or take on a role, or that it is good/safe to please the guide or belong to someone etc etc.

5) Fractionating a subject without telling them that you are going to take them in and out of absorption a few times.

6) Giving a subject cues (words, gestures, blinks, sounds, listening a certain number of times etc) that you intend to use to make them do something at a later date, without informing them exactly what it is that they will be doing.

7) Including suggestions for amnesia, or making a subject think that something (dream, amnesia, imagery, body experiences etc) has happened when this may not necessarily be the case.

8) Not taking a subject out at the end of a recording, or leaving them with a role to play, with their judgement down, with their physical abilities down, with their minds blank, or in any other way different for an indefinite length of time.

In order for a subject to be making a REAL choice whilst in a hypnosis, there MUST be at least one way for a subject NOT to carry on with the hypnosis if they wish. This could be by making the hypnosis lighter, using a different method of hypnosis, being in full awareness whilst in a hypnosis, or being able to pause or stop the hypnosis. Just so you know, if you say no, you are quite entitled to mean NO, even if someone with a nice voice is asking you to do something! Also, just because you don't say anything, or don't know what to say, DOES NOT mean yes!

# Induction: gathering attention without disappearing

An induction gathers scattered attention until quieter material becomes easier to hear. You don’t have to surrender, play asleep, or obey anyone. When you’re sober, distracted, analytical, or guarded, an actual induction may help. When meditation, loveyhuasca, lucid dreaming, grief, music, prayer, fatigue, or another intense opening has already made you trancey, begin with orientation and containment instead of adding depth.

Aim for the amount of trance that suits the practice: focused, warm, receptive, embodied, and still able to stop. Depth is optional.

## What an induction is actually doing

Prepare the session, gather attention, deepen only when that helps, then let what you notice shape the words and the next response. Counting is one way in. Conversation, story, movement, repeated sound, and curiosity can also organize attention. Say so when you use them; elegance doesn’t excuse a covert induction.

For a simple, chosen target, you can test the suggestion before deepening. If the useful response is already happening, build from it. An induction helps you find a receptive way of listening; you don’t need to complete a ceremony before you’re allowed to respond.

## Please don’t put six metaphors in one trance

Choose one purpose, one starting anchor or image, and a way back. Decide whether warmth, a firm boundary, direction, or neutral witnessing would help. Floating, stairs, gardens, inner rooms, colored light, and child dialogue do not all need to report for duty at once. Switch consciously when the metaphor stops helping.

Imagery is optional. A signal may arrive through sensation, rhythm, movement, words, spatial sense, simple knowing, or no response at all. Keep the language in the world you chose: let a count mark something meaningful there, and reverse the journey when you finish. Borrow qualities such as warmth or steadiness without replacing your identity.

Pick one central verb, one sensory quality, one short refrain, and one transition phrase, then read the script aloud; long elegant sentences become soup during an induction.

## Before you begin

Read the induction and Come all the way back while fully awake. Choose the question or quality you want to explore, what you’re leaving alone today, and how much time you have. Love, play, confidence, creativity, and communion are worthwhile purposes; you don’t have to find a problem. Leave several minutes for returning and reflecting.

Choose a physically secure place without driving, water, machinery, or anyone needing your supervision. When intoxicated, acutely confused, or unable to keep yourself safe, deal with immediate support rather than exploratory hypnosis. The later Already open? practice is for orientation, not permission to intensify an unstable state.

Decide what kind of encouragement is welcome: spacious listening, warm company, or a firmer invitation to stay with a feeling. Preview eye closure, any repeated exit and re-entry, and the return. For a recording, read the script beforehand when possible. “Stop” ends the exercise; “less” reduces the demand; “quiet” stops the words. You can simply open your eyes and finish. No inner part has to approve your decision to stop.

When entry and return are unfamiliar, first rehearse them without a difficult subject. Once you know how to change direction, you don’t need to supervise every moment. Let a useful response become easier and less effortful. A no remains no; silence doesn’t authorize a narrator to add something you haven’t agreed to.

Let the next invitation grow from the response you got—not the response the script expected.[Utilization](https://catalog.erickson-foundation.org/page/ericksonian-hypnosis-therapy-techniques) means working with what actually happens: a thought, sound, movement, or objection may change where the practice goes. Notice what actually happens; no change doesn’t mean secret success. Keep safety and exit instructions plain. Appendix B teaches the richer language tools, and Let the next response guide you shows how to use them after the induction.

## Sober induction: 3–5 minutes

Read this slowly into a recorder, speak it aloud, or learn its movement and use silent words if that comes easily. Choose one anchor—a sound, bodily contact, or your two-hand practice—and an opening phrase. Keep using the same anchor where the words refer to it. Pause between paragraphs; don’t read the delivery directions aloud.

Let your eyes settle somewhere in the room, somewhere they can rest for a little while, and notice the difference between looking for something and letting what is already there come into view.

There is a surface beneath you. Notice where you meet it … the contact, the pressure, whatever you can actually feel. And while you notice that, a sound may reach you, or a breath may draw your attention, without needing you to arrange either one.

You can give this a little time.

A thought about the day may still be here. A thought about whether you’re doing this properly may be here too. There is room to notice a thought and return to the anchor you chose, without finishing the thought first.

And as you return, you may find one detail that holds your interest … something about the sound, or the contact, or that particular way of listening … and let attention stay with it long enough to become curious.

Perhaps you can give yourself the time you would give someone you care about who has begun to speak and hasn’t yet found the words. You can remain nearby, listening, without finishing their sentence for them.

Let your chosen phrase come to mind now. Say it as an invitation, and notice how it is received.

As you continue, your eyes can close, soften, or stay open. Let that be a choice you make. Listening may deepen while a sound from the room remains available in the background.

Count slowly from five to one. Give each number its own moment.

Five. Notice your chosen anchor again. Four. Let attention gather around what you find. Three. Leave a little space after the words. Two. Listen into that space, without preparing an answer. One. Stay here for a moment.

Bring your chosen concern lightly to mind, or remain with your wish for contact. Let it be present without rehearsing its whole story.

What am I noticing now?

Give the response time to take its own form. It might be a sensation, a few words, an image, a feeling, or an impulse to move. There may be nothing clear yet.

Leave room to notice, with words or without them.

## Already open? Contain before you deepen

Use this when loveyhuasca, meditation, lucid dreaming, grief, music, prayer, erotic energy, exhaustion, or intense beauty has already opened the state. Stabilize before making it stronger.

“The state is already open. I do not need to intensify it. I need to aim it, stabilize it, and keep it kind.”

Look around the room. Name where you are. Name the date, or at least the day. Feel your feet, hands, and spine. Press your thumbs gently but firmly against your index fingers. Focus on the physical friction of skin meeting skin.

“Only what can be integrated is welcome now. Anything too much can wait. The body may stay safe. The adult self remains present. The inner child does not have to carry this alone.”

Once you’re oriented, choose one intention:

“Please show me the next useful truth, symbol, feeling, question, or action — only in the amount I can live well with.”

## Stay awake when your mind likes to escape

Sit upright. Open the eyes. Feel the feet. Let the breath be natural.

Say: “I am not going away. I am coming more fully here.”

Look at one object. Feel the spine.

“Is this daydream guidance, emotional digestion, or avoidance?”

“If it is guidance, show me the action inside it. If it is avoidance, bring me gently into the present and show me what I am avoiding.”

If stillness makes you sleepy, diffuse, or escape-prone, [hypnosis can become more alert rather than more relaxed](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00029157.2018.1496318). Keep the eyes open and pair a small repeated hand movement with increasing clarity; stop the movement and notice what remains without trying to disappear inward.

“Eyes open. Press thumb and forefinger together gently. Release. Again — clearer, not sleepier. One more time. Now stop the deliberate movement. Notice the first second after the rhythm ends. Is there an echo in the fingers, the breath, the attention, or nowhere at all? Stay upright and work from exactly that level of alertness.”

## The two-hand induction

Place one hand on the heart. Place the other hand on the solar plexus.

Let the heart hand represent the Nurturer. Let the solar plexus hand represent the vulnerable child.

Breathe gently.

“Love above. Child below. Adult present.”

“I send love to the child. I receive the child’s signal. I send love again. I listen again.”

## Sleep induction

Sleep induction has a different job. In inner work, the adult returns, writes, and acts. At bedtime, analysis can remain off duty. Revelation can wait; the task is rest.

For sleep hypnosis, see [Nimja’s sleep playlist](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IqyqE8V2CJg&list=PLZhjR9_8QJZsWUmSS5KpiueoQuq0k-poT).

## Optional fractionation: practice coming back

Fractionation means returning toward ordinary awareness and then deciding whether to enter absorption again. Used openly, it teaches that trance has a door in both directions. Name the technique before using it; every answer cannot secretly mean “deeper.”

“Open the eyes and return toward ordinary awareness. Feel the room and decide whether to stop here. If you choose to continue, close or soften the eyes and notice what changes as attention gathers again. You may stop at either point.”

## Deepening, if useful

“If it is useful, attention may become more gathered. If it is not useful, I remain exactly as awake as I need to be.”

Choose one simple scene: a room in the heart, a garden, a temple, a fire with the Protector nearby, a clear lake, or a chair where the adult waits. One scene is plenty.

“Who or what needs to speak first?”

## Optional trance depth check

“As I wait, I give up the responsibility of making anything happen.

If it is useful, the wise subconscious may let one finger twitch, pulse, lift, warm, tingle, or become subtly noticeable when the inner meeting space is ready.

I will not force it. I will not fake it. I will simply wait with love and notice.”

A finger may move, a hand may warm, the breath may change, or nothing may happen. None proves truth; at most, something may be listening.

## A shortcut back into hypnosis

Learn a useful induction and the full return below before developing a shortcut. My dad brought two fingers together in a mudra like the OK sign to help himself relax, including while driving. That’s his experience, not a recommendation to enter trance at the wheel. Keep alert calming and inward hypnosis on different cues.

Choose a distinctive word, phrase, or optional gesture. During a familiar, welcome hypnotic state, pair it with that receptive way of listening—not peak emotional intensity, a child-state, or a traumatic scene. Give it the meaning you choose:

When I’ve set aside time, settled somewhere appropriate, and deliberately use my cue to begin, I can return to this familiar way of listening. I can change direction or finish.

Return fully. On a later suitable occasion, deliberately begin practice and try the cue. Give it time; gradually shorten the longer induction when the shortcut helps, and use the familiar route when it doesn’t. No fixed repetition count, instant depth, or particular sensation is required.

Check usefulness and selectivity: does it help when chosen, remain absent when unchosen, and leave changing direction and returning workable? It mustn’t become obedience to a narrator, notification, or overheard word. Faster entry doesn’t determine what material you can handle.

Never train, test, or use hypnotic re-entry while driving or doing anything requiring active attention. Open eyes and conditional wording are not safeguards.

To retire the cue, stop rehearsing it, remove it from recordings or active app instructions, and return to the familiar induction. State your decision while awake; a cancellation sentence does not prove the association vanished instantly. Persistent unwanted responses or disorientation need qualified help. Any retained cue is for a future, separately chosen session; today’s altered state ends with the return.

# Come all the way back

Reverse whatever you used to enter. If you floated, regain chosen weight. If you became small, return to ordinary scale. If you met a younger state or several parts, confirm present wholeness, body ownership, and adult judgment. If you traveled, come back by the same route. Keep only the qualities you deliberately chose.

“I bring back only what is useful now. The rest can remain safely held until there is more capacity. I return to the room, the body, the day, and one honest action. All instructions to deepen or remain in this session are finished. I keep only the ordinary-life intention or future-practice cue I consciously chose; neither keeps today’s altered state running. I am myself, in my body, with adult judgment fully available.”

One — feel the body’s contact and chosen weight. Two — let the breath return to its ordinary rhythm. Three — hear the room and name where you are. Four — move fingers, toes, shoulders, and eyes. Five — eyes open, present, ordinary, clear. The session has ended.

Name the day or date. Look left, right, near, and far. Move before standing, and stand only when steady. Check that you are actually oriented and alert; repeating the count is not proof of a complete return. Note only what actually appeared, and any action it calls for. Then do something normal.

If a recording stops unexpectedly: say, “The recording stopped and the session is over.” Open your eyes, name the location and day, press your feet or hands into a surface, move, look around, and use the five-step count. Don’t wait for the missing narrator to release you.

Sleep is separate. A sleep recording may end in sleep or quiet rest. A waking recording shouldn’t end by preserving a role, reduced judgment, impaired movement, blankness, an undefined reset word, or an invitation to remain altered indefinitely.

# The actual inner meeting

Note: If you have a tendency to day dream, dissociate or fall asleep, it is better to do this exercise sitting up and save lying down for when you want to go to sleep, get a very deep rest or incubate dreams. If you are sitting, keep your spine straight but not rigid like a soldier. Relax your jaw and stay somewhat connected to the room you are in.

“I am not leaving life; I am listening more deeply to life.

I invoke my Higher Self, universal heart-mind, wise subconscious mind, intelligence in my body, inner child(ren), inner adult(s), protector(s) and all that is of truth and love to help me know what I need to know in a form and at a level that will be useful, kind, stabilizing and real for me.”

Ask your conscious mind to listen.

This does not mean you have to stop thinking to listen. Just notice your thoughts, body sensations, pictures, doubts, emotions, etc. Don’t fight what comes up, but don’t go with it either. Just notice what stands out on its own as you hold the idea of what you are doing in the back of your mind.

Internally say, “Tell me what I need to know or ask me what you need to know and I’ll sit in silence for 5 minutes and listen.”

You may hear a phrase, feel a body sensation, notice a memory, see a color, be contacted by a child or protector, feel pain, warmth, complete silence, know that you need to do something, be grateful for something, feel you need to stop the listening or any number of things.

Listen to everything and believe nothing automatically.

# Let the next response guide you

Once you've read the induction and done the inner meeting, this will make more sense. Read through these, and then try doing it without looking at the screen.

Respond to each thing that comes up with an accurate notice and a small reasonable invitation (or act or taking more time etc). The 'thing' might be a sensation, image, gesture, feeling, words, or anything. The notice and invitation don't need to take all those forms, just notice and invite what happens.

## Finding your own words

An example notice might be, "I notice my chest tightening when I imagine asking for help". Don't add interpretation like "this must be the abandoned child part of me, or a protective mechanism" - just notice what you notice. An example invitation might be, "And as I notice that, I can take some time before deciding what it means, and notice if I want to say anything, or change it, or just stay like that".

Then just listen. You don't need fancy language - often it will be better to just ask what happens next, or whatever. If you notice a feeling developing, it might be best not to say anything while it's developing (if you can help it) but somedays you need to talk to understand, so just do that.

You can be pretty straightforward when that's the help you chose - "Let the explanation wait a moment. What do I feel as I say that?" can be a perfectly valid thing to say to yourself, and help you focus. It's ok to just be with an unpleasant feeling, you don't have to wait till it's a nice one before you know you're ok. As long as you're still present, and able to change if you want to, and happy to carry on, a sad feeling or tension or whatever doesn't mean it's not ok.

It's worth trying to separate the notice, the meaning, and your thoughts on the meaning, but in practice it will often be pretty fuzzy. When you can, it can be helpful to separate it out like that though - "I noticed... this seemed to mean... here's why that might be true / false" or something like that.

Is this a notice or meaning? "My stomach went tight" is a notice, "He's a complete bastard" is a meaning, even if you didn't think it in words. You can then go on to find out what he did, notice what it was that gave you the idea, and consider if there might be other explanations. It's also ok to use phrases that you didn't think in your head - if something comes up that you're analysing, it's still good to analyse, even if it's not in your head already. Nonverbal thoughts and early thoughts aren't necessarily any better or worse than other thoughts. Feelings are good to pay attention to, but it's still good to consider what they mean.

## Let the response change the invitation

Your shoulders tightening, or 'hmm, that feels kind of wrong', is a signal to investigate further, but 'No! That is Not Right!' is a signal to stop what you're doing and change it. If you don't feel like it fits, don't keep doing it. Just change it. You don't need any other reason.

"I notice the tightness, and I can just leave it like that. I wonder if that fits, or if I'd rather do something different?"

Sometimes it just isn't right - sometimes it's something you hadn't thought of. Neither is automatically a protector, and just being silent doesn't mean there's a process.

## Look at the situation too

If there is another person involved in the situation, it may be a good idea to look at what they are doing as well. Are they able to communicate in a mature fashion? Try and be specific in your description, and be willing to alter it if you find out that it was incorrect. Just because someone is disagreeing with you or trying to set boundaries around you does not mean they are in their emotionally immature parts. You don’t need to subject yourself to something that may be dangerous to determine if this is the case.

On the other hand, you may find that you have acted inappropriately and you feel guilty. Having feelings of guilt does not mean that you have done something wrong or that you have been manipulated. Have you done something that was wrong or that caused harm? If so, what can you do to fix it? On the other hand, did you give in to someone else just to keep them from being disappointed in you? What do you want to do or achieve the next time you interact with this person? Do you need them to understand what you are feeling/thinking? Do you just need to communicate information to them? Do you need to set boundaries with them? Do you need to end the interaction prematurely? You don’t have to make them understand before you can act.

## Change the amount of contact

When you start to contact it, if it feels too much, immediately start to widen your awareness to something else in the room, in your body, etc. Keep moving back and forth between the difficult thing and the easier thing. This is called pendulation and titration. Do as much or as little as you feel you can. You set the pace, you don’t have to do a certain number.

Ok, I think that is enough for now.

Feel free to go back and explore more if you wish to do so. If, at any point, you start to lose contact with the here and now, feel that things are becoming more and more unreal, or you feel that you are not able to switch to something else or stop, stop trying to explore inside of yourself and just do the entire “coming back” part. If you have trouble orienting yourself and it lasts for a prolonged period of time, please get some human help rather than trying a stronger “induction” back to the here and now.

If you feel an urge to move, turn, reach, push, or change your body’s position, feel free to either actually do something that will not cause your body pain or discomfort, or just imagine doing what you feel you need to do, and see what happens next. You do not need to “act out” the urge, feel that you need to move, shake, release, etc. If you do move and you feel that you are causing yourself pain or other symptoms that you are concerned about, stop moving.

## Offer the care that is actually wanted

If any “younger” parts come up, offer them the opportunity to spend time with you, or some space if they would rather. If one of them says “don’t come closer”, stay at the distance they choose. You don’t have to consider them literally “younger”, nor do you have to ask them why they are there or what happened to them. If you can identify any “protector” parts, talk to them and find out what they are concerned about. All of them are welcome to the “meeting”. You don’t have to get rid of any of them.

If you feel that it would be useful to bring in a “nurturer”, “protector”, “leader”, etc to help you either change the “tone” of the interaction, set boundaries, or help you figure out how to live your life in a better fashion, feel free to do so. You don’t have to talk to them, just use them to get whatever function you need that you aren’t able to do on your own. You only need to use the “Witness” if you need help getting started with a step. If you are already able to do a certain step, you don’t have to do it again.

Try some different ideas as to what might be helpful for this ‘inner part’ to feel better (being welcomed by you, visualising someone else, giving them the sort of words they would like to hear, you placing yourself in a different body position, you moving further away etc). Only try one idea at a time to see if it works. Just because an idea ‘should’ be a resource, that doesn’t mean that it will be.

It is possible that when you try and give the part what it wants, you might feel very upset. It may be that it never got what it needed at the time and now that you are giving it what it wants, it is processing the upset of not getting what it wanted before. If this happens, allow yourself to respond for as long as you want to do so, and for as long as you can ‘stay there’ (ie remain in the process). If you feel that the voice, distance, touch, image etc is too much, then change it to something that is less. Don’t think that you can’t ‘take’ the love.

Allow yourself to see where the metaphor takes you. Ask questions like ‘if the problem had an image or story, what would it be?’ or ‘what would need to happen to make things right?’ If it feels like you need to ‘do something’ to give yourself the resource that you feel you need (or to put a boundary in place, or to move away or to take some other action) then try ‘doing that’ for as far as it takes to be of use to you. Remember that whatever you find out, is likely to be metaphorical. It may be a good hypothesis for what you need to do but it is not a proven fact.

Alternatively, you may want to use the session to develop a capacity that you want to develop (ie love, play, courage, curiosity etc). You can also use it to rehearse something that you have decided to do.

## Finish without forcing a revelation

Ending the session . Don’t feel that you have to end the session with a ‘great revelation’! When you have done as much as you want to do, it is a good idea to find out if there is anything that you need to do to make sure that you can leave things where they are for the time being. This might include thanking parts for their help, or making sure that you do something ‘in real life’ (eat a good meal, put a boundary in place, get some rest, repair a relationship, keep a promise etc) or it may be that there is nothing to do!

Note : throughout the exercise, allow your ‘analytical’ part to make comments and/or tell you that it doesn’t like what you are doing and/or tell you to stop doing the exercise. Just tell it that you will check things out when you come back and make any commitments etc when you are back. You can stop whenever you want to. Remember that the images that you see can be meaningful without being actual memories of things that happened and just because you see an image, or feel a body release, that doesn’t mean that you are right about what is going on!

When you have finished, even if you stopped part way through, use the ‘ Come all the way back ‘ procedure to bring yourself back. When you are back, it is a good idea to write a few lines in a notebook about what you have done. Just jot down the essentials (what was there before you started, what you have done to change things, what you are unsure about, any phrases that were said, any body signals, any information that the ‘inner child’ said that it needed, anything that a ‘protector’ wanted you to know, any boundaries that you put in place, any ‘repairs’ that you did, anything that you learnt about what it is a mistake to try and force etc). You don’t need to write down the whole cast of characters, just what you actually noticed.

# The 15-minute daily practice

Notice there is no need to learn a second script! The process is the same. You are simply practicing for fifteen minutes or so (a convenient time, not a dose or a timetable). That is long enough to induce yourself (it only takes a few minutes), practice and let it unfold at its own pace (five minutes is plenty to see what happens when you simply sit in silence, listening, and you can interrupt yourself at any time to change direction if that feels better) and come out again (that takes a few minutes too).

You can also try other ways of inducing yourself such as becoming aware of 3 things you can see, 3 things you can hear, 3 things you can feel in your body; then 2 of each, 1 of each, counting from 5 to 1 and beginning to listen. Choose one way to induce yourself, you don't need to try all of them! (In fact, it might be counter-productive!)

Once you have practiced listening a few times with the recordings, you may want to shorten them to speed up your induction, or even practice without playing them back at all. On the other hand, if you like using the recordings, there is no reason to stop! Being able to guide yourself does not mean you have to! (NB. You may wish to use the re-entry cue to help you re-enter your process, this will also shorten your induction if you already know what to do.)

As you come out of the process ask yourself what you learned needed warmth, what needed to be protected, what you need to do differently to be more in accord with truth and love and life. Is there something you want to explore more fully or a capacity you want to build? And most importantly, what can you carry with you as you move forward from this? If you identified something you needed to do differently, make a plan with yourself to do that thing. The more specific you can be, the more likely you are to change: “If _________ (fill in the cue for the old pattern), then I will do ________ (fill in one small action that will begin to change the pattern).” Then check back later and see how you are doing – sleeping, relating, functioning, etc., not only how impressive the session felt

# The weekly deeper practice

It’s good to give this more time once or twice a week, when that’s useful. It doesn’t have to be very intense but it is good to start from Higher Self or at least a neutral witnessing place and follow what appears. It’s also good to bring in some adult function to make sure nothing is being bypassed and so that at the end of the exercise, when the experience is over, there is the possibility of coming out completely and doing whatever normal thing needs doing.

If possible, it’s good to do the normal thing immediately after the exercise, so that the subconscious sees that the adult heard and acted. Otherwise, the dishes won’t get done.

# Capacity before content

NOTE: If it is medically unsafe or you are in a place of panic, sleep deprivation, nausea, deep destabilization, mania, psychosis like symptoms, feeling overwhelmed by chemicals etc. then first focus on stabilizing and then look for what your subconscious needs you to know! If it takes you destabilizing to know what your subconscious knows, it is not meant for you to know directly at this time! Focus on asking for capacity over content!

Sometimes, it feels like your subconscious knows something that your conscious self is not prepared to know. It may be something related to your sexuality, grief, trauma, family issues, betrayal, shame, vocation, a relationship, illness fears, spirituality, addiction, identity, moral issues etc.

What you DON’T want to do is bully your subconscious into telling you what it knows by saying things to yourself like:

“What is it you won’t tell me? What are you hiding? Tell me NOW! I am telling you to tell me!”

This can lead to flooding, panic, false certainty, confabulation, dissociation etc. and can make your day SUCK! When you are flooded, it is much harder to access your adult capacities so focus on being able to regulate and getting support! Sometimes, it is better not to know what is not being said until your adult has the capacities to regulate, get support, be humble, etc. Just ask for what you need to know in a way that you CAN tolerate:

“What can you tell me, that will make my conscious self 5% better at knowing the thing you want to tell me?” “Can you tell me in symbolic form so I am not completely overwhelmed by the revelation?” “Can you tell me what I need to do to be safe, without actually telling me what it is”? “Do I need to know WHAT it is, or do I need to know that I need to get some support to have the capacity to know what it is, and who would be a good person to help me?” etc.

Instead, say to yourself something like this:

“Dear Wise Subconscious Mind, if there is something you know that my conscious self is not ready to know, please don’t break it open violently! Do what you can to make me able to know it in a way that is safe! Prepare me BEFORE you break it open! Ground me BEFORE you show me the memory! Support me BEFORE you make it intense! Give me one next action!” etc.

REMEMBER: Not everything in hypnotic imagery is literal, especially if it relates to trauma and memory. The mind makes it symbolic, squashes things together, distorts, dramatizes, pulls from cultural influences, fears etc. So, treat it like an image and get some help outside of yourself!

# Troubleshooting

## Nothing happens

Start smaller:

“Show me one body sensation.” “Show me one word.” “Show me whether today is for listening or resting.”

## It feels fake

First ask what feels fake: the wording, the image, the expectation, or the role you’re trying to perform. Not experiencing an inner voice or image isn’t, by itself, resistance or a missing adult capacity. Correct an explicitly unwanted invitation. If you recognize a protective part, you can talk to it:

“What are you protecting me from?” “Are you afraid I will lie to myself again?” “Are you afraid this is performance?”

The voice may be the inherited parent enforcing an old belonging rule. Don’t argue with it. Ask which rule it is enforcing and what ordinary evidence would make the new adult voice more believable.

The fake feeling can also mean that a needed adult capacity isn’t available yet. Stop demanding a convincing voice. If you can already observe what is happening, borrow the particular warmth, boundary, or direction you lack. A real or imagined caring figure can help. Try one believable sentence or action and see how it is received.

## The subconscious will not answer

There may be no answer, or the question may not help. You can change it, rest, or finish. If a concern about trust is actually present, ask:

“What do you need to know before you trust me?”

The reply may be extremely un-mystical: “Will you set the boundary?” “Will you keep the promise?” “Will you stop abandoning us?” “Will you stop substituting insight for action?”

## The inner child will not appear

If a guard is recognizable, speak to it; otherwise stay with the feeling or preference that is actually present.

“You do not have to let me in. Just tell me what would make the doorway safer.”

If no clear child-state appears, use the session to notice one preference, curiosity, or beginner experiment instead of forcing an image. Identity-building and reparenting can happen in the same practice.

## You get anxious

Notice whether the anxiety is manageable and you want to stay with it, or whether you need less contact. When it is escalating or overwhelming, stop searching for content and help the body first.

Feet. Chair. Breath. Room.

“Little one, I am here. Protector, thank you for warning me. You do not have to scream for me to listen. If there is real action needed, show me one step. If there is no action needed, help the body stand down gently.”

Get professional help when anxiety includes chest pain, fainting, suicidal thoughts, psychosis-like or mania-like symptoms, severe dissociation, inability to sleep, or urges to harm yourself or someone else. Appendix D has the longer anxiety toolbox.

## You dissociate or feel unreal

Open your eyes. Move. Touch something textured. Name five objects. End the deeper practice for the day.

## You become grandiose

Return to food, sleep, chores, humility, and somebody who will disagree with you.

“If this insight is real, it will survive sleep, food, time, humility, and feedback.”

## You become too analytical

When the practice turns into a technical performance, stop improving it. Put one hand on the heart and one on the solar plexus.

“I am not here to do this perfectly. I am here to be in relationship with what is deeper than my surface mind.”

“What would make this feel more like communion and less like performance?”

Silence may be the answer. Rest may be the answer. One honest action may be the answer. Take the answer that actually arrived.

## You become too blissful or diffuse

Ask the bliss to become specific.

“What task, boundary, meal, rest, repair, or conversation does love require next?”

## You want the hidden truth immediately

Don’t pry.

“Show me the capacity before the content.”

## You keep daydreaming

Sit up and open your eyes.

“What real action is this fantasy replacing?”

## You get stuck in spiritual bypass

“Where has divine love not yet descended into the wound?”

A genuine opening can show you a depth of love or freedom you hadn’t known was possible. Let that matter without demanding the child receive it on command. Prayer can also evoke abandonment or condemnation, or a teacher’s betrayal can damage something sacred. Ask what was lost and what needs questioning; more of the same practice isn’t automatically the answer.

Sometimes practical care helps while the available horizon of life still feels too small. Curiosity about deeper happiness is worth following without pretending to believe an explanation. Other people already have enough love and meaning for the life they want. Spirituality doesn’t need to become another assignment.

## You get stuck in endless processing

A real wound and a checking loop can coexist. Ask whether this round helps you feel, understand, decide, or respond differently—or whether you’re checking the same question for reassurance. Grief and practice can revisit familiar ground; novelty isn’t required every time.

When it is a checking loop, let the thought remain without arguing with it or chasing it away, and return to a chosen activity or relationship. You don’t have to hold an inner meeting every time a thought appears or complete a ritual before you’re allowed to live.

“What direction would the Leader choose now?”

## You keep using hypnosis but not changing life

“What would prove to the inner child that the adult is real?”

Then do the thing.

# What not to do

DO NOT: View your inner experiences as anything but internal happenings that need to be reality tested at all times.

DO NOT: Engage in seeking “downloads” of information or make a hobby of demons/jinn/Unattached Burdens or the like, until you have stabilized your life. Get help to do so, if you are not reality testing well, or not functioning well in your life.

DO NOT: Participate in self-hypnosis while operating a car, bathing, swimming, operating any equipment, supervising children, or engaged in any activity which requires active attention.

DO NOT: Attempt to “force” memories, to use trance to “prove” something happened to you, or to not get medical attention for symptoms, because you can “force” them down using hypnosis.

DO NOT: “Force” yourself to ignore your body’s needs, such as eating when hungry, resting when tired, responding to pain, etc., or your emotions, such as letting yourself grieve when someone dies, feeling anger when it is appropriate, acknowledging your fears, etc., until you know more about what the signals are telling you.

DO NOT: “Extend” the duration of altered states indefinitely or accept “hits” of intuition as “real” without reality testing them.

DO NOT: Allow various internal roles to get out of proportion (e.g., allow the “Inner Child” to run your adult life, the “Protector” to preclude all loving relationships, the “Nurturer” to disregard consequences to others, the “Leader” to ignore the needs of the “Child”). You are capable of analysis, as well as tenderness.

DO NOT: Utilize NLP techniques to “force” down “protectors,” to give yourself certainty you do not have, to manipulate others, or to “heal” yourself when your body is telling you not to.

Don’t consider negative indicators (doubt, distraction, resistance, lack of response, saying “No”) as good signs that the induction is working.

Don’t rely upon the “safety” of “your unconscious mind” as the only safety factor in any session. Be sure you have set up clear limits, are in a safe place, and have a way to terminate the session and “get out” which has been tested and proven to work.

Don’t believe that normal physical sensations are indications of hidden trauma, illness, toxins in your body, possession, “attachments,” “transformations,” or information from your “subconscious mind.”

Don’t believe that anything is “healed” in your body (neurologically, cellularly, etc.) simply because it is “healed” in your imagination.

Don’t put “hidden ‘commands’” in yourself, nor allow anyone to do so. Even if you are consciously placing something in yourself which you wish to utilize later, be sure that you know exactly what you are doing and why you are doing it, and be sure that you can review it and CAN “take it out” if you decide that you no longer wish to use it. It may not be as easy as simply saying, “Take out.” Nor should you allow anyone to activate these “commands” in you without your knowledge, or use them to make you do something you don’t want to do, to make you into someone you don’t want to be, to give you amnesia, etc.

DO bring yourself completely back at the end of every “waking” session, declaring that the session is over.

# When this needs more support than a guide can provide

Use self-hypnosis as a relationship with yourself. When the situation needs actual care, the guide shouldn’t become a substitute for it.

Get urgent help now for immediate danger, severe confusion, inability to keep yourself safe, or concerning physical symptoms such as chest pain or fainting. New or worsening suicidal thoughts, severe insomnia, mania-like or psychosis-like symptoms, or dissociation that disrupts functioning need prompt professional assessment, not a four-to-twelve-week practice schedule.

For ordinary learning, start with shorter sessions, warmth, a manageable subject, and a full return. Over a week or two, track one target—anxiety, daydreaming, relationship repair, an avoided task, sleep, emotional regulation, or intuitive accuracy. Worsening function or growing certainty about unverifiable memories calls for reassessment, not more intensity.

Seek suitable ongoing help for recurring panic, compulsive behavior, substance-related destabilization, or major relationship instability.

## Methods to explore with a trained person present

Use a trauma-informed clinician trained in the method, present with you, for deliberate trauma-focused regression, searching for unknown traumatic origins through an affect or somatic bridge, or work with substantial flashbacks or dissociative reactions. A bridge follows associations from a feeling or sensation; regression revisits recalled or imagined younger experience. Either can have different uses, including resourcing. Ask what is intended and how uncertainty and unexpected reactions will be handled. Hypnosis cannot prove a historical event, even with a clinician present.

Trauma history, tears, or wanted difficult feelings don’t automatically exclude self-practice. Choose the working range with attention to the actual response and available support. Some people need a narrow range; others much broader. Confidence alone doesn’t supply missing support.

Major trauma guidelines emphasize treatments such as trauma-focused CBT variants, CPT, PE, and EMDR depending on the person and guideline. Self-hypnosis and inner-child practice may support that process, but high-risk trauma work needs a larger container.

If the practice makes ordinary life more loving, honest, functional, relational, grounded, and precise, continue gently. If it makes ordinary life less stable, stop deepening and return to sleep, food, grounding, boundaries, and outside support.

# Appendix A: How to ask the subconscious a useful question

Your subconscious mind is NOT a large language model of AI. However, there is a bit of overlap in the way you can ask questions to both. So, if you know how to do prompt engineering for AI, this can be very useful to use it to help you be more present, honest, loving etc. On the other hand, you should NEVER use it to be more controlling.

To get as much precision as possible, you need to include the following in your prompt:

Role: Who or what are you talking to?

Context: Give some context so it knows what you’re talking about.

Intention: What do you want to change or accomplish?

Constraint: What do you need to do to make sure your asking is safe, true, kind etc.

Output: How do you want to receive the answer? As an image, phrase, sensation in your body, memory, action, question, symbol, silence, rest etc.

Evaluation: How are you going to evaluate the answer? How will you know if it’s true, kind, timely, stabilizing, specific, testable etc?

Iteration: Iterate by changing your prompt depending on what you get from reality as feedback.

Most importantly, you always need to make your prompt “safe” otherwise, the analytical part of your mind will “make up” an answer just so it fits with your prompt. I usually add the following to my prompt:

“If you need more time or the best and safest answer is silence, then I ask you to make my body go into a deeper state of comfortable rest. A peaceful silence would be a successful answer to my question.”

There is a very good chance there is no answer for your question because there is nothing happening. It might be that it’s not time yet, or that you need to go rest or do whatever you need to do.

Once you do that, here are the core prompts you can use:

## Core prompts

For getting clarity: “I ask you, my wise subconscious mind, to show me what is true, kind and timely. If what you want to show me is too intense for me to integrate, please transfer it to my conscious mind in a way I can integrate it, for instance as a symbol, a signal in my body, a next step I can take. Most importantly, show me first my capacity to be peaceful, and then, the content of my wounding.”

When you experience resistance: “What are you protecting me from? How can you trust me 1% more?”

When you want to ask your inner child something: “What do you want me to know? What do you need me to do today so that you are not alone in dealing with it?”

When you want to ask your adult Protector something: “What do you need me to do in the external world so that you can feel that you and the system are safe?”

When you want to ask your leader or guru self: “What direction is the most in alignment with truth, love, life and all beings?”

When you want to listen to your intuition: “I ask my intuition to give me a signal, my analytical mind to decipher it, my body to give me feedback as to whether my deciphering is correct and reality to give me feedback as to whether my deciphering is correct.”

When you want to know if a particular intuition you have is coming from your universal heart or from your programming (this one is longer and more complicated): “I ask my wise subconscious mind to help me see if the intuition or impulse I have about …is coming from my universal heart or from my family, my religion, marketing, erotic programming, my political tribe, my trauma response or an old deal I did as a child to belong. When you are anxious: “What are you protecting me from? How can you trust more my adult self?”

When you suspect there is something true that you don’t know: “If knowing the truth is too much for me, please don’t show me everything. Show me first my capacity to be peaceful, and then, the content of my wounding.”

Once you get an answer, you can ask: “What can I do in my ordinary life to prove that I heard correctly?”

The prompt will tell your subconscious what you want. The NLP can help you get it in a safer way, but that doesn’t make the answer automatically true.

# Appendix B: NLP without turning yourself into a sales seminar

You can practice the core method without learning one term of Neuro-Linguistic Programming. Read this appendix when you want more precision about imagery, bodily states, rehearsal, and sentence design. NLP is useful when it helps the system listen; it becomes a bag of tricks when technique replaces love, truth, contact, or consent.

## The four NLP layers

Sensory NLP changes the form of an inner experience: image, sound, distance, size, brightness, volume, posture, pressure.

State NLP makes useful states easier to access: warmth, adult presence, courage, steadiness, love, clarity.

Strategy NLP rehearses a better response: when this cue appears, the adult does this.

Language NLP shapes the sentence: pace what is already true, open awareness, presume only safe capacity, link cause and effect carefully, embed invitations, and use de-hypnotic questions when language becomes vague, absolute, manipulative, or grandiose.

## The Milton Model, minus the covert sales seminar

The Milton Model concerns the architecture of the sentence. Commands such as “Relax now,” “Stop being anxious,” or “Trust me” often make a part brace.

Part of the point of [indirect language](https://learn.nimja.com/techniques/hypnotic_language) is to get past that reflexive skeptical argument without having to overpower it. The destination can still be deliberate even when the route is subtle.

Begin with what is already true, lower the pressure, presume only a small safe capacity, and invite one movement:

“As the body notices the bed beneath it, it may become a little easier to stop holding the whole day alone.”

Pace reality. Open awareness. Validate function. Presuppose capacity. Bridge gently. Invite one small movement. Test with the body.

### Pattern 1: Pacing and leading — truth before suggestion

*Pacing and leading* means stating what is already true before inviting anything new.

“Anxiety is here. The body is activated. A protector is trying to help. And one breath at a time, the adult can start to listen.”

The simplest form is a Truth + Invite loop:

“It is completely true that [current sensation or emotion] is here. It makes sense that my system is reacting by [current protective behavior or thought]. And right alongside that truth, my adult self is here, breathing in this room, ready to listen to whatever comes up next.”

### Pattern 2: Awareness verbs

Awareness verbs point attention without yelling at it: *notice, realize, recognize, sense, feel, wonder, imagine, picture, listen, allow, discover, remember, consider*.

“Notice whether the answer comes as an image, a word, a pressure, a warmth, a silence, or a next action.”

### Pattern 3: Presuppositions

A presupposition hides an assumption inside a sentence. Use that power only to presume choice, timing, capacity, safety, love, and adult leadership. Don’t smuggle in obedience.

“Before any part has to change, it can first become more understood.”

### Pattern 4: Gentle cause-effect bridges

Words such as *as, while, because, when, after, before*, and *which means* connect one event to another. Use them carefully. A gentle voice doesn’t make a false causal claim true.

Too strong: “Because I breathe, the trauma leaves.” Better: “As I breathe, the adult self may become present enough to hear one small piece of what the wound needs.”

### Pattern 5: Embedded invitations

An embedded invitation places a small direction inside a larger sentence. It should invite a modest inner movement, not hide a demand.

“And somewhere inside this breath, I can let the adult self listen.”

“As I sit here, I may allow one small truth to become clear.”

“The child does not have to trust me all at once; it can simply notice I am still here.”

Use one when it helps. Ten embedded invitations in a row feel like somebody took a weekend certification.

### Pattern 6: Ethical double binds

A manipulative double bind traps every answer inside the speaker’s agenda. An ethical one offers two genuinely safe routes.

“Would this part prefer warmth first, or space first?”

### Pattern 7: Conversational postulates

These are questions whose ordinary conversational form also acts as an invitation.

“Can the system show me the next step without showing me the whole wound?”

### Pattern 8: Tag questions

Tag questions—*isn’t it, can’t it, doesn’t it*—can soften a sentence. They become oily very quickly, don’t they? Use them lightly.

“The body already knows something about resting, doesn’t it?”

### Pattern 9: Nominalizations

Nominalizations turn processes into nouns: *healing, safety, trust, integration, communion, clarity, rest*. Their vagueness can sometimes help because the subconscious supplies its own concrete meaning.

“A little more safety can arrive in whatever way the body understands.”

### Pattern 10: Meta Model questions — the de-hypnosis tool

The Milton Model opens possibility. The Meta Model asks exactly what somebody means. Ethical practice needs both.

“How specifically?” “According to whom?” “Compared to what?” “Always?” “Never?” “What exactly would happen?” “How do I know?” “What evidence would change my mind?”

Use Milton Model to soften into the body. Use Meta Model to return to truth.

## Sensory, state, and strategy tools

### Submodalities — editing the form, not denying the truth

Fear, craving, fantasy, belief, and memory may have sensory qualities: near or far, bright or dim, loud or quiet, moving or still, heavy or light, inside or outside the body. Work with qualities you actually notice. If none are apparent, skip this tool rather than inventing an image or voice.

Ask how the experience is represented. What changes when the image moves two feet back, the voice gets quieter, or the edge becomes less sharp?

“I am not deleting the signal. I am adjusting the volume so I can listen without being overwhelmed.”

Before moving one imaginary pixel, ask the guard whether that change is acceptable.

“Would it be okay if we moved this image back just a little, or lowered the volume slightly, purely so my adult self can stay present enough to hear what you need to tell me?”

### Anchoring — giving the body a doorway back

An anchor is a cue connected to a state: a hand on the heart, two fingers touching, a breath, a word, a posture, scent, or song. Find a real state you want easier access to, feel it, and pair the cue while it is clearly present with the phrase, “This is available.” For hypnotic re-entry, choose a usable receptive state rather than peak emotional intensity; follow “A shortcut back into hypnosis” in the main induction section. Keep alert calming and the synchronicity wake-up cue separate.

### Using synchronicity as your own wake-up bell

People notice a synchronicity and immediately ask what it means. Sometimes that opens insight; sometimes it starts compulsive pattern-hunting. I prefer using the moment of noticing as a wake-up bell. The coincidence doesn’t have to contain a message. It has already captured attention, which is enough.

The cue I give myself is:

“Whenever I notice something that feels like a synchronicity, I wake up inside the state I am in. I feel my body and the room. I remember that the coincidence is not proof of an interpretation. I let attention widen beyond the story my current mood is telling. I ask what is fact, what is interpretation, what needs warmth, what needs protection, and what needs action. I return to the highest perspective I can access without leaving the body, the facts, or ordinary human reality.”

I make room for whatever is genuinely available: objectivity, clarity, ease, peace, freedom, joy, and gratitude. I do not have to manufacture any of them.

Synchronicity noticed → pause → orient → widen → reality-test → listen → choose one loving action.

The cue interrupts identification without erasing the state’s message. A negative state may still contain an inner-child need, a Protector warning, grief, a bodily problem, or a real demand for action.

#### Installing the synchronicity cue

Imagine noticing an ordinary, harmless synchronicity.

Feel the moment when attention says, “Whoa.”

Use that opening to feel the feet, body, breath, and room.

Repeat: “The noticing is my wake-up bell, not proof of a message.”

Ask the Nurturer, Protector, and Leader what each sees.

Rehearse returning to a grounded, wider perspective.

End with one small action and the complete waking exit.

The cue remains self-authored, visible, reversible, and limited to orientation. It can’t authorize a decision, prove an intuition, suppress a warning, or let another person control the response.

Psychologically, this resembles an [implementation intention](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/sites/default/files/pictures/Gollwitzer_Implementation-intentions-1999.pdf): an advance “if X, then Y” plan that links a recognizable cue with a chosen response. Gollwitzer’s foundational paper supports the general mechanism. This particular use of synchronicity hasn’t been studied directly.

The same mechanism creates the risk: rehearsing synchronicity as a cue may make you scan for it. Pattern perception can drift toward false-positive meaning, especially during absorption or destabilization. Research links stronger apophenic pattern detection with false-positive judgments and psychosis-spectrum traits, although ordinary pattern noticing is not itself pathological. See [Blain and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32212749/) and [Grant and colleagues](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2014.00135/full).

Deactivate the cue if it increases compulsive scanning, sleeplessness, anxiety, certainty that random events are personally directed messages, or difficulty distinguishing observation from interpretation.

To retire it, stop rehearsing it and say while fully awake: “Synchronicity is no longer a cue. I release this association and return coincidence to ordinary uncertainty.” Remove it from the written cue list and stop rehearsing it. The statement expresses your decision; it does not prove the association has vanished immediately.

### Reframing — changing the meaning without lying

Dishonest reframing declares that the problem was secretly good all along. I prefer questions that widen meaning without lying:

“What else might be true?” “What is this part trying to do for me?” “What would the Nurturer, the Protector, and the Leader each say about this?”

### Perceptual positions — self, other, witness, universal heart

First: what do I feel from inside myself? Second: if I sit inside the other person, what might they be feeling or protecting? Third: if I watched this from across the room, what would I notice? Fourth: from the universal heart, what serves truth, love, boundaries, and the good of everyone involved?

Don’t use perceptual positions to excuse harm. Use them to escape the prison of one angle.

### Future pacing — let the future test the insight

Don’t end with “I understand.” Ask where life will test the understanding, picture the actual cue, and rehearse the smallest better response.

“If [cue], then I [small action].”

### The Swish, softened

Classic NLP uses the Swish to replace an old self-image with a new one. Done mechanically, it can feel like an inner eviction. Here, the old pattern stays visible, the new possibility stands beside it, and I notice the body’s response while comparing what each future would actually require.

### Parts integration, without pretending the conflict is solved

“What does one side want?” “What does the other side fear?” “What need sits under both?” “What third option honors both without letting either run the whole life?”

To talk myself out of a real signal.

## Running the whole NLP sequence

Induce or contain. Gather attention, or stabilize first if you are already wide open.

Ask permission. Is it useful to work with this image, feeling, or pattern today?

Represent the pattern. How does this show up — image, voice, sensation, posture, distance, size, color, movement?

Exit and act. Come back to the room. Do one ordinary thing.

# Appendix E: Is the daydream helping or replacing your life?

Sometimes daydreaming is something that your subconscious mind is doing to help you. It is simulating an idea or event to help you digest grief or teach you something. The very act of daydreaming may be helping you to access your creativity or allowing your subconscious to show you a potential future. Sometimes your subconcious will show you things that you may be afraid of. Sometimes it will show you what you want.

Other times daydreaming may be taking you away from your life in a detrimental way. It may be that you are escaping reality and are addicted to fantasies rather than the real life. You may be dissociating or obsessing about romantic partners or escaping from being an adult.

How do you know which it is? Simply ask yourself if the daydreaming is taking you closer to life, with more clarity, love and ability to take action or if it is taking you away from life, in a repetitive and craving way.

If it is helping you then simply ask yourself what the guidance is and how you can use it.

If it is taking you away from life then change your body position by sitting upright, open your eyes, feel your feet and ask your subconscious to help you. Simply say to yourself, “Dear subconscious thank you for showing me images. If what you are showing me is helpful then please also show me the helpfulness in it. If what you are showing me is a way of avoiding dealing with life then please show me in a gentle but structured way how to deal with life as it is right now.”

Then ask yourself what it is that you need to do in “real” life that you are avoiding by daydreaming.

You may need to send a text message or stop sending text messages. You may need to clean up your room, eat, sleep, deal with grief, create, apologize, leave, tell the truth, make a plan, get help, get back to a task that you were supposed to be doing, stop chasing love that is not available to you etc.

# Appendix F: Self-trust, outside guidance, and oracle-like states

One of the issues here is that people vary dramatically in how suggestible they are, both internally and externally, and different people vary in how suggestible they are in different states. ## When the subconscious speaks like an oracle

Your subconscious is amazing at picking up on things your conscious mind isn't getting. Whether it's anticipations, reading patterns, or just picking up on uncanny things, it's good to respect what your subconscious is picking up on. But just because your subconscious gives you an image or tells you to do something, that doesn't mean you need to treat it like an order.

Especially if it's a big thing, or something that can't be easily undone, slow it down to the scale of the thing. Look at it with your adult's eyes, and take it to council.

## The council — internal first, then outward

Ask your child, your adult, your conscious mind, your subconscious mind, your intuition, your analytical mind. But if it's bigger than all of those, ask outside of yourself, too. The bigger and more un-undoable something is, the more you want to reach outside.

Ask people who love you, people who are like you, people who have done what you are thinking of doing. Sometimes it is right to go it alone, but I would suggest making it the last step, after you've tried to get council.

On the other hand, it is possible to give your power away. Seeking counsel can quietly turn into abdicating to an authority figure. Another person doesn’t get automatic trust, or automatic dismissal.

Sometimes you need the support of your peers. In [Hearthwork](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling), the concept is that you give each other equal time, warmth, confidentiality, and acceptance of “no” as an answer, and you act for each other as Nurturer and Protector, but not as Guide. Then you reverse roles. Sometimes, with a question asked carefully and at the right time, your peers can help you hear your own Guide more clearly, but they don’t interpret what you experience.

## Forgiveness without bypass

We can not ‘force’ forgiveness on ourselves, it has to come from a place of understanding otherwise it can be bypassing. However, if we remain in the anger forever, we are allowing the injury to dictate how we live our lives in the present. So how do we hold all of this? We simply remember that it can all be true: the harm happened, the boundary still matters, the person is still accountable, and forgiveness may come later on its own.

A helpful understanding to come to, is that while their actions were harmful, in a sense, they could only make the moves they made, given their accumulated nature at the time (conditioning, history, pain, capacity, etc). This doesn’t negate that harm was caused, holding someone accountable, allowing for consequences, working on making different moves now, etc. It simply helps us come to a place where we aren’t expecting something from someone that they can’t give. It doesn’t make their limitations matter any less.

“You don’t have to forgive before you’re ready. I won’t use spirituality to silence your pain. And I won’t build our whole life around this wound forever.”

Some questions that may be helpful to ask when coming to a place of forgiveness, while still holding all of the above:

What actually happened? What was the impact? What boundaries need to be set? What shaped the other person(s) into someone capable of that harm? What is theirs? What is mine? What does accountability require? How can I let this go, without denying any of the above?

For self-forgiveness, name what you did, without taking responsibility for what wasn't yours. Let the remorse register the harm, or how it went against your values, without making yourself a permanent criminal.

Take responsibility, feel the remorse, then rectify if you can, and then let it go. Be specific about the action you took, and the impact it had, feel the remorse, do what you can to rectify or keep it from happening again, and then stop holding yourself hostage.

# Appendix H: Demons, jinn, hostile presences, and “Unattached Burdens”

I’m speaking from experience in this appendix.

During self-hypnosis, meditation, lucid dreaming, loveyhuasca, grief, prayer, plant-medicine-adjacent practice, or other altered states, people sometimes meet something that doesn’t feel like an ordinary emotion or younger part. It may appear as a demon, jinn, parasitic energy, dark intelligence, or terrifying archetype—a voice or image that feels intrusive, hateful, seductive, mocking, or simply alien.

I don’t need this guide to settle the metaphysics. Perhaps these are literal beings. Perhaps they are symbolic mind-forms, trauma in a frightening costume, dissociated energy, or ancestral and relational burdens. The practical question is:

“What response makes me safer, clearer, more loving, more grounded, and less hooked?”

## Start with the room, the body, and basic safety

When a demonic, jinn-like, hostile, or intrusive presence appears, don’t reward it with instant metaphysical certainty. Run the triage first: medical danger, environment, body, overload, and how open the mind has become. Open the eyes, feel the feet, name the room, lower stimulation, leave hostile company, and stop adding prompts. Don’t deepen, hunt for hidden truth, or debate ontology while destabilized.

“Only what can be integrated is welcome now. Anything too much can wait. The adult self remains present. The body may return to safety.”

Danger to self or others, psychosis-like fear, inability to sleep, extreme paranoia, chest pain, seizures, serious overheating, overdose concern, or loss of basic functioning means the self-hypnosis session is over. Get real-world help.

## A response check, not a verdict about what it is

Once you’re stable enough to stay present, notice what helps without turning the response into a metaphysical test. Put a hand on the heart if that feels welcome, take a comfortable breath, and keep the room available.

I don’t have to decide what this is right now. I can notice whether warmth or distance helps, and keep my boundary either way. Nothing here requires my obedience.

If vulnerability appears—a knot in the throat, sadness, fear, or a sense of trying to protect something—you can bring in the Nurturer. If the experience remains hostile or becomes more frightening, stop engaging with it and return to the body and room.

Neither response establishes its origin. A protective part needn’t reveal its fear quickly; a comforting response doesn’t prove an interpretation either. The useful question is whether this way of responding helps you become clearer, steadier, and less hooked. Love and boundaries don’t require a verdict first.

## Do not fight from fear

The first impulse may be to fight, banish, hate, prove power, argue, panic, or identify completely with the threatened self. Some traditions use prayers, mantras, protections, banishing rituals, or exorcism forms, and some people find them useful. But fighting from fear can fasten attention more tightly to the thing you want gone.

Fear gives it center stage. Hatred gives it a hook. Pride gives it an opponent. Panic gives it the nervous system.

“I do not need to hate this. I do not need to merge with this. I do not need to obey this. I do not need to defeat this from fear. I return to loving awareness.”

## A bodily boundary: compassion present, door closed

When the presence feels hostile, cold, alien, parasitic, or boundary-violating, don’t offer soft sentimental love as though inviting it in for tea. Use love with a boundary.

Sit upright. Press your feet into the floor. Let the spine rise. Gently draw the shoulder blades back so the chest is open but not exposed. Feel the skin boundary of the torso. Feel that this body has a perimeter.

“I recognize that suffering may be here, and I wish all suffering to return to the space where it belongs. But the boundary of this skin belongs to my adult life. You do not have permission to enter this house.”

“Compassion is present. The door is closed.”

## If it might be psychosis or destabilization

A symbolic hostile-presence experience that settles with grounding and love is very different from a state that becomes persistent, paranoid, sleepless, dangerous, or impossible to reality-test.

If somebody believes they’re under attack for days, can’t sleep or function, hears commands, feels compelled to hurt themselves or anyone else, becomes manic, can’t communicate normally, or can’t distinguish symbolic experience from ordinary reality, stop treating the problem as hypnosis material. Get help.

If the practice makes you more loving, grounded, relational, honest, and functional, continue gently. If it makes you more paranoid, sleepless, grandiose, isolated, frightened, or unreal, stop deepening and get support.

## The cosmic-download humility test

Hostile presences, shadow forces, grandiose parts, and altered-state imagery don’t always attack through fear. Sometimes they seduce through importance: a cosmic secret, a special mission, a vast download, a feeling of being uniquely chosen, or a revelation that makes washing dishes seem beneath your new rank.

“Thank you for the imagery. I do not require a grand performance. If this information cannot become a simple, honest act of care tomorrow morning — washing the dishes, cleaning my room, keeping a promise, apologizing, resting, eating, or helping someone I love — it is not useful to my path.”

Guidance worth keeping should make ordinary life more beautiful, honest, relational, humble, reliable, and useful to other people.

## Don’t become an entity hobbyist

Demons, jinn, hostile presences, and “Unattached Burdens” all lead to the same practical training: can love remain present without surrendering the boundary, and can the mind become harder to hook? I’m not trying to turn anyone into an entity hobbyist.

# Appendix J: A practical induction library

Practise! Work out in advance which one you want to do and what you want to use it for (pick just one) and whether or not you want to do it at all! Any of these ‘families’ of induction can be done as light inductions, with eyes open, or instead of trance you can just do them as a contact. Once you start an induction, you will want to notice what’s going on (even if nothing changes) and work out what to do next, move, imagine something else, change the sensory channel you’re using, do nothing, stop the induction sequence and do something else… . Whatever you do, make sure you bring yourself fully back to waking at the end of an induction.

NB: if you can’t ‘see’ the images you’re trying to visualise, that’s fine, change them into words, rhythm, spatial relations, something to touch or move or just plain ‘knowing’… .

## 1. Stretch and release

Stretch and Release: Work out what will be supporting you, focus on the feeling of support. You can also focus on where one of your hands or feet is. Either actually, or in your imagination, stretch a little bit, either stretching that part of your body slightly or pressing gently down into the support, and then let go of the effort, just relax. See if there is any difference, even if there isn’t. Do this for no more than 3 places on your body. Think of it as ‘releasing’ as you go through the sequence. Which bit of your body, or voice, needs this? What do you need to support it? Bring your body back to normal movement and return.

## 2. Supported floating

Supported Floating: Work out what is supporting you and where your weight is, imagine that the support is holding a tiny bit (1%) more of your effort. It’s like floating but you don’t have to lose contact, or just keep it at the same level, just enough weight/pressure. See what happens, you can fractionate this if you want, perhaps noticing your normal awareness when you breathe in and the support when you breathe out. Tell yourself you are going to do this before you do it. What can you hear more easily now? Bring yourself back to normal feeling of support and return.

## 3. Rhythmic breath or pulse

Rhythmic: Use your breathing or slightly hum in your throat to make a natural rhythm, or tap your finger or listen to sounds outside to make an artificial one. You can even do it by counting to yourself (silently) or with no rhythm at all. Tell yourself you are going to say “here, and here again” over and over, and notice what you pay attention to in between the phrases, then stop saying it. Notice what you become aware of in the first beat – you don’t need to make your mind blank, just notice what comes up. Does it make any words, feelings, needs or actions more clear? Bring back your normal rhythm and return.

## 4. Unwinding object

Unwinding Object: Good for when you have a few different strands to one thing you want to work on. Imagine an object unwinding – maybe some yarn, some bubbles, or some knots that you place beside you. Once you’ve placed them all, give yourself a minute to look at them all, you don’t have to change anything, but you can label them if you need to (just a word or two). How many did you put down? Which one do you need to work on, what does it need – warmth, protection, action? Keep one strand in your attention and put the others somewhere you can see them and return.

## 5. Sensory journey

Sensory Journey: Pay attention to the sounds in the room you’re in and how your body feels, then imagine yourself walking somewhere. Where do you want to go? Listen to the sounds of your footsteps and take in your surroundings as you walk, what do objects feel like if you touch them, what landmarks do you see? When you feel you have enough of your attention, imagine the scenery changing until you are in a place where you can talk to whoever you need to (or you can just cut to the chase, if you prefer). Pay attention to something without worrying about what it means. Allow yourself to be curious about what’s going to happen next, but don’t make it a “what are they going to do to you?” kind of thing. When you are ready to finish, imagine yourself walking back the way you came and close the space.

## 6. Gradual wave

Gradual wave: start with a very small neutral area, feel into it a little and then imagine a wave spreading out to a new area that is still ok. Sense into the new area (or continue using the old). See if there is anything different at each step, without claiming it is a medical repair. Does the body want any contact, space, words, other care? Let the mental image go, sense your body, sense your contact points, come back.

## 7. Care workshop

Care workshop: prepare the workshop (time, place, tools, boundaries, etc). Invite the signal/feeling/young part to come only as close as is welcome. Try to avoid any language that makes you not one person. Care for it as best you can, by asking what it wants and giving that if you can, or sensing into it and responding, or just being with it. Take breaks, get back in contact if that's ok, take more breaks, etc. Keep bringing in adult functions (or borrow someone else's function). Have the adult part of you run the show. Goal is to make it "good as you", not to erase history. Close the workspace, reassure yourself that you are one whole person, that you're an adult now, and come back.

## 8. Resource embodiment

Resource: pick a resource quality. Imagine yourself accessing 1% more of that quality (or just sense yourself). What is different? How is your body, voice, etc? Do this a few times to strengthen it. Come back, keeping the quality and one positive action that you would do with it, but leaving the "person" behind, saying "I am me".

## 9. No-trance inner contact

For the no trance inner contact, I would do: Pacing: notice you can see, it's (day of the week), you have feet, you can read, you can write, etc. Suggestion: in your mind, ask one clear question and wait, but don't focus too hard. Choice: you can answer the question, write an answer, write that you don't know, take a break, rephrase the question, ask someone else, stop doing this, etc. Autonomous: write whatever you get. Deepener: none. Handoff: have your adult self answer the question in whatever way is appropriate, preferably by writing or doing. Integration/exit: reality test the autonomous, do one small thing based on it, stop doing it.
