What I mean by hypnosis

P1APA Division 30 commonly defines hypnosis as focused attention, reduced peripheral awareness, and increased responsiveness to suggestion. That’s basically true, but there’s a lot more than meets the eye. In fact, hypnosis is such a broad and common type of altered state that it’s hard to confine it to one use. But I’m using hypnosis here as a therapy.

P2And hypnosis as a therapy is as old as time itself. While the modern technique of it was discovered by Franz Anton Mesmer in the 1770s, shamans have been using dancing, music, and drugs to induce hypnosis for therapeutic purposes forever. We enter into trance states every night which resemble hypnosis as we go to sleep and as we wake up as well. Those states can also be used for deep therapy.

Affirmations Done Right

P1My dad always said: “Always, and in everything, expect the very best. But be prepared to take what you get. The best might come afterwards.” That’s a better affirmation than what most people think of as affirmation, because it’s not telling you something which you know to be false in the present. If you say “I am beautiful” but you know that’s not true, then you’re challenging your beliefs, and if you’re challenging your beliefs you should have some argument or reason why they are wrong, otherwise it’s bullying.

P2Another quote I use all the time from my dad… when someone asks me “How’s it going?” I say “Better & better!” This really confuses a lot of people. Some think it’s amazing that I’m always doing better. Some ask if I was sick or something. But it’s doing multiple jobs. On the one hand, it’s deflecting from the fake question because most people don’t actually want to hear how you are doing in detail anyway. On the other hand, more importantly, it’s reprogramming your mind to remove you from tunnel vision. You might be sad now, but if you say, “I am sad” then you’re subtly identifying with the sadness and it keeps you stuck. It’s best to say, “I’m feeling sad now, but I’m doing better and better, every day, in every way!” That’s more like a mission statement than a historical analysis. Although you can also re-evaluate your history as lessons to make that true.

A useful map of the mind

P1Note: whenever I refer to the subconscious I’m referring to all of the processes under the surface of your conscious awareness: perceptions, emotions, desires, avoidance tendencies, imagery, memories, body signals, habits, intuitions, automatic behaviors and so forth. You can work with all of these together as if it’s all one mind, or you can work with it as a set of parts, an ecology if you like. There isn’t a right or wrong way to approach it. They’re all maps. Don’t form a priesthood around either one!

P2Working with parts: Precision. Working with Higher Self: Coherence. Doing both: Communion.

P3If you need to work with the whole, the subconscious as one:

P4a) Invoke the subconscious in some way. Something simple like ‘Hello, wise subconscious, please help me understand…’ or whatever works for you.

P5If you need to work with the ecology in the subconscious (child parts, guards, critic, fantasy making parts, body memories, social part, spiritual part (God/source/best benefit of all beings part) wiser part etc etc) then:

P6a) It’s usually a good idea to start with unity. So invoke your Higher Self: Hello my Higher Self/universal heart-mind/wise subconscious, please guide me…

P7b) Move into differentiation. “Which part is afraid? Which part wants to protect you? Which part wants to avoid it? Which part knows what to do? Which part is too young for this?” Etc.

P8c) Move back into unity again. “And all of these parts are part of the one life, and as the adult in charge of this life you can lead with love…” Etc.

Sometimes start with the whole

P1Some people make themselves too fragmented by starting to work with parts. ‘Here’s a part! Here’s another one! I’ll get twelve of them in a room and give them all an office to hang around in!’ So it’s better to start from the Sacred.

P3It can be that you can’t even access your Higher Self, or the universal heart, or whatever works for you. (Btw, if it doesn’t work for you don’t use it! It’s no good adopting somebody else’s map.) A shortcut back into hypnosis

P1Once you have learned a useful induction and how to come back down at the end, you can do the following….

P2Have an unusual word, phrase, and/ or motion (optional) that you will use to go into hypnosis again. Say the word and/ or phrase while doing the motion (if applicable). Be sure to do this when you are in hypnosis and in a good place. Don't do this when you are in a very emotional state, child state, or thinking about something that could be considered trauma. The idea is for you to be able to pick a good state to go into, and you can decide what that is.

P3Ex: My dad used to use a mudra (like putting two fingers together like an "OK" sign) when he needed to relax. He even did it when he was driving. Not that I recommend driving in a trance, don't do that! Make sure that your relaxation/ stay alert cue is not the same as your cue to go inward to hypnosis.

P4Give your cue the meaning you choose: “When I pick the time and place (an appropriate time and place) and say the word and/ or phrase to begin, I can go into this state that I picked and am familiar with. I can change direction, or stop, at any point.”

P5Now come all the way back up again, and at a later time, when it is appropriate, start using this. Over time, you can shorten the length of the longer induction, or use the longer induction if this doesn't seem to be enough for your needs. There is no need to have to do this a certain number of times, go to a certain depth, or even have a certain sensation when you do this.

P6Also, be sure that this is working for you. Be able to turn it on and off at will. Make sure it isn't there when you don't want it to be. Be able to change directions or come back up when you are in it. Don't use it in a way that makes you follow instructions that you are hearing from a narrator, notifications, or something that you overhear. Don't use this to go into hypnosis faster for things that you shouldn't be working on yet.

P7There is something you should never do while using this induction. And that's use it, practice it or test it while you're driving or doing something where you need to be actively paying attention. It's just not safe to be going into a trance where you are actively paying attention to what's going on inside of you when you need to be paying attention to what's going on outside of you. Even though your eyes can be open, even though you're using "if it would be useful for you to do so" type language, it's just not safe.

Come all the way back

P1If you have done anything to alter your state when entering the trance, make sure to reverse those things. If you have floated, make sure to gain your weight again (whatever weight you want of course). If you have shrunk, make sure to grow back to your regular size. If you have gone somewhere, make sure to go back the same way you went there. Etcetera.

P2Here is a generic bring-back that you can use: "When I come out of this session, I will only bring with me that which is useful to me in my everyday life right now. Anything else I will bring with me at a later date, when I am better able to integrate it. When I come out of this session, I will be coming back to the room I am in now, and to the day in which I am participating in this session now. All the instructions to carry me deeper into this session or keep me in this state are now finished. The only intention I will take with me into my everyday life, and the only cue I will use to take me into this state at a later date, will be the ones I consciously choose, and neither of them will keep me in this state. When I come out of this session I will be me, in my body and with my adult judgment."

On 5 open your eyes, back in the here and now, feeling normal and clear and the session is now over.

Take mental note of only what actually came up for you and any actions you need to take regarding it. Then go on and do something normal.

Do not fight from fear

P1Your first instinct may be to fight it, banish it, hate it, show it that you’re more powerful than it is, argue with it, panic, become your own threatened self in fear of it, etc. Some people may want to use traditional prayer or mantras or protection or banishing or exorcism techniques, and those may well work for them. But if you’re fighting it out of fear…

P2It becomes a center of attention if you fear it, and if you hate it you give it a hook in you. (If you’re proud you give it an opponent, and if you panic you give it your nervous system.)

P3So the next time it comes up you can just say, “I see that there is no reason to hate and revile what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to merge with or become what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to obey what I am experiencing. I see that there is no reason to try to defeat what I am experiencing out of fear. I choose to return my attention to loving awareness.”

A bodily boundary: compassion present, door closed

P1Second, feel free to tell the presence that while you have compassion for it, the door is closed.

P2This is especially a good idea if the presence you’re experiencing seems even the slightest bit hostile, cold, alien, parasitic, etc. It’s not really helpful to approach it with the kind of soft, sentimental love that you’d use if you were inviting it to stay for tea, but it can be useful to approach it with love while also making it clear that it needs to respect your boundaries.

P3Sit up, make sure your feet are grounded on the floor, straighten your spine, pull your shoulder blades back a little, open your chest a little, feel the boundary of your torso’s skin, feel the perimeter of your body, and tell the presence, “While I have compassion for you and any suffering you may be experiencing, this suffering needs to go back where it came from. The boundary of my skin is the boundary of an adult now, and you’re not welcome to come inside the house anymore.”

If it might be psychosis or destabilization

P1This is assuming that you are having a symbolic experience of “hostile presences” which responds to grounding and love. It may be that what you are experiencing is actually psychotic or a form of destabilization.

P2If you feel like you are under attack for multiple days at a time, if you are not able to sleep, if you are not able to function normally, if you are hearing voices telling you to do things, if you feel you need to hurt yourself or somebody else, if you feel you are going manic, if you feel you can’t communicate normally, if you feel you can’t distinguish between what you are experiencing symbolically and normal reality, get help immediately. It’s not something to deal with in hypnosis.

The General Rule

P1The General Rule is: if you find that what you're doing is making you more loving, more grounded, more relational, more honest, more functional, then it's probably OK to keep doing it gently. But if what you're doing is making you more paranoid, sleepless, grandiose, isolated, scared, or more and more unreal, then DON'T GO DEEPER! GET HELP!

The cosmic-download humility test

P1Finally, presences, shadow forces, or grandiose parts don’t always attack through fear. They may also try to give you a sense that you’re special, try to “download cosmic info” to you, try to give you a special mission, make you feel doing the dishes is beneath you, etc. You can just say, “Thank you for the imagery, but I don’t need your grand performance. Unless the info you’re trying to download to me can help me do the dishes, clean my room, be more honest, be more helpful, be more loving, keep my promises, take care of my body by resting and eating etc, I don’t need it.”

P2Guidance worth keeping should make ordinary life more beautiful, honest, relational, humble, reliable, and useful to other people.

Don’t become an entity hobbyist

P1Finally, some of you may be tempted to make this an area of hobbyism – to become interested in demons or jinn or hostile presences or Unattached Burdens or whatever. I just don’t want you to do that. They all lead to the same thing, which is how to maintain a presence of love without giving up your boundaries, while also making your mind harder to hook.

Appendix J: A practical induction library

P1Preview the sequence. Work out in advance which one you want to do and what you want to use it for (pick just one) and whether or not you want to do it at all! Any of these families of induction can be done as light inductions, with eyes open, or you can just do them as a no-trance inner contact.

P2Once you start an induction, you will want to notice what’s going on (even if nothing changes) and work out what to do next: move, imagine something else, change the sensory channel you’re using, do nothing, or stop the induction sequence and do something else… Whatever you do, make sure you bring yourself fully back to waking at the end of an induction.

P3NB: if you can’t ‘see’ the images you’re trying to visualize, that’s fine, change them into words, rhythm, spatial relations, something to touch or move or just plain knowing…

1. Stretch and release

P1Stretch and Release: Work out what will be supporting you, focus on the feeling of support. You can also focus on where one of your hands or feet is. Either actually, or in your imagination, stretch a little bit, either stretching that part of your body slightly or pressing gently down into the support, and then let go of the effort, just relax. See if there is any difference, even if there isn’t. Do this for no more than 3 places on your body. Let releasing guide you as you go through the sequence. Which bit of your body, or inner voice, needs this? What do you need to support it? Bring your body back to normal movement and return.

2. Supported floating

P1Supported Floating: Work out what is supporting you and where your weight is, imagine that the support is holding a tiny bit (1%) more of your effort. It’s like floating but you don’t have to lose contact, or you can just keep it at the same level, with just enough weight/pressure. See what happens, you can fractionate this if you want, perhaps noticing your normal awareness when you breathe in and the support when you breathe out. Tell yourself you are going to fractionate before you do it. What can you hear more easily now? Keep the support, come back to your normal weight and firm contact, and return.

3. Rhythmic breath or pulse

P1Rhythmic: Use your breathing or slightly hum in your throat to make a natural rhythm, or tap your finger or listen to sounds outside to make an artificial one. You can even do it by counting to yourself (silently) or with no rhythm at all. Tell yourself you are going to say “here, and here again” over and over, and notice what you pay attention to in between the phrases, then stop saying it. Notice what you become aware of in the first beat – you don’t need to make your mind blank, just notice what comes up. Does it make any words, feelings, needs or actions more clear? Bring back your normal rhythm and return.

4. Unwinding object

P1Unwinding Object: Good for when you have a few different strands to one thing you want to work on. Imagine an object unwinding – maybe some yarn, some bubbles, or some knots that you place beside you. Once you’ve placed them all, give yourself a minute to look at them all, you don’t have to change anything, but you can label them if you need to (just a word or two). How many did you put down? Which one do you need to work on, what does it need – warmth, protection, action? Keep one strand in your attention and put the others somewhere you can see them and return.

5. Sensory journey

P1Sensory Journey: Pay attention to the sounds in the room you’re in and how your body feels, then imagine yourself walking somewhere. Where do you want to go? Listen to the sounds of your footsteps and take in your surroundings as you walk, what do objects feel like if you touch them, what landmarks do you see? When you feel you have gathered enough of your attention, imagine the scenery changing until you are in a place where you can talk to whoever you need to (or you can just cut to the chase, if you prefer). Pay attention to something without worrying about what it means. Allow yourself to be curious about what’s going to happen next, but don’t make it a “what are they going to do to you?” kind of thing. When you are ready to finish, imagine yourself walking back the way you came and close the space.

6. Gradual wave

P1Gradual wave: start with a very small neutral area, feel into it a little and then imagine a wave of warmth, steadiness, or attention spreading out to a new area that is still ok. Sense into the new area (or continue using the old). See if there is anything different at each step. Does the body want any contact, space, words, other care? Let the mental image go, sense your body, sense your contact points, come back.

7. Care workshop

P1Care workshop: prepare the workshop (time, place, tools, boundaries, etc). Invite the signal/feeling/young part to come only as close as is welcome. Try to avoid any language that makes you feel you're not one person. Care for it as best you can, by asking what it wants and giving that if you can, or sensing into it and responding, or just being with it. Take breaks, get back in contact if that's ok, take more breaks, etc. Keep bringing in adult functions (or borrow someone else's function). Have the adult part of you run the show. Goal is to make it "good as you" rather than "good as new", not to erase history. Tell your little one, "That's enough for today. We're going back to the life we're building." Close the workspace, reassure yourself that you are one whole person, that you're an adult now, and come back.

8. Resource embodiment

P1Resource: pick a resource quality you understand (warmth, boundary, steadiness, curiosity, or dignity). Imagine yourself accessing 1% more of that quality (or, if you prefer, just observe it rather than embody it). What is different? How is your body, voice, etc? Do this a few times to strengthen it. Come back, keeping the quality and one positive action that you would do with it, but leaving the imagined you behind, saying "I am me".

9. No-trance inner contact

P1For the no trance inner contact, I would do: Pacing: notice you can see, it's (day of the week), you have feet, you can read, you can write, etc. Suggestion: in your mind, ask one clear question and wait, but don't focus too hard. Choice: you can answer the question, write an answer, write that you don't know, take a break, rephrase the question, ask someone else, stop doing this, etc. Autonomous signal: write whatever you get (a word, sensation, impulse, objection, or silence) without interpreting it as proof. Deepener: none (full alertness isn't a lesser version). Handoff: have your adult self answer the question in whatever way is appropriate, preferably by writing or doing. Integration/exit: reality test the signal and do one small thing based on it. Since you didn't go into trance, just close the exercise and carry on with your day.
