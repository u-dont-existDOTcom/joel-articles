Before you begin

Read this page, and the Come all the way back page, when you're awake.

Think about what you want to do. Are there things you don’t want to do today? How long do you want to spend on this? (You don't have to do this on a problem. You can do it on something you want to experience more of: love, play, confidence, creativity, communion, whatever.) Make sure you're leaving some time at the end to come back, and reflect on what you experienced.

Make sure you're in a safe place to do this. Don't do this if you're driving, in the water, operating machinery, or otherwise need to be attending to yourself or other people. If you are intoxicated, acutely confused, or not able to keep yourself safe, then get some immediate support instead of doing exploratory hypnosis. If you are already partially or fully opened, use the later 'Already open? Contain before you deepen' section to help orient yourself, but do not attempt to deepen yourself if you are not stable.

Decide to do it. How will you coax yourself to do this? Make it spacious? Make it warm and held? etc. Preview what you are going to do. Will you close your eyes? Will you come out and go back in again? What will you do at the end? If you are going to use a recording, read the script if you can. Remind yourself that if you say 'stop' at any time, the induction will end. If you say 'less', you will be asked to do less. If you say 'quiet', the talking will stop. You can also just open your eyes and stop, and then come all the way back anyway. You don't need to ask any parts for permission to stop, even if a part tells you that you do.

If you are new to this or haven't done it for a while, work on the coming in and coming out part first, don't bring in the hard stuff till later. You don't have to watch yourself all the time, but for the first time, make sure you can change state. Let yourself make it easier and easier to do this and notice that it takes less and less of you to do it. A no will stay a no. The narrator can't add stuff just because you don't say anything.

Let your response guide you as to what to do next regardless of what the script is expecting. Utilize. Use what you have to go to the next place, whatever it is. Learn how to notice what is there. Learn how to notice if there is no change, and that no change is not a positive change. For more on language you can use, see Appendix B, or keep reading below for information on using the tools after induction in Let the next response guide you. Either way, keep the instructions about how to stay safe and how to get out plain.
