It can be done in an office, but there are many other ways to hypnotise someone; through music, marketing, religious rituals, sexual chemistry, charismatic authority, social media, grief, fear, political messaging, sense of belonging, fatigue, love, trauma, beauty, etc.

There are a lot of ways to influence someone without them consciously engaging. Marketers know about these ways, as do interface designers. Take a look at the FTC’s report on “dark patterns” (trick or manipulative designs) or the OECD’s description of commercial patterns that steer, deceive, coerce, or manipulate someone into making a choice that may not be in their interest.

Conscious agreement, unconscious receptivity, emotional compliance, legal and ethical consent – can someone have their mind influenced without the conscious agreement of their adult self?
