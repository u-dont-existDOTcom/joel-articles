# Appendix K: Sources and further reading

The personal accounts and the way I arrange this practice are my own, especially if it involves adaptations like the one I'm doing here where I am combining body orientated work and hypnotic work. This is not a validated form of treatment at this time. These instructions are not necessarily needed during the practice, they're here for your perusal.

## Learning, entering, and returning

Learning to learn, how to get in and out

Healing the Divided Self , Maggie Phillips and Claire Frederick, (1995) pp 24-27, 45-50, 83-90, covers a lot of aspects of this post, such as interactive hypnosis, helping the subject learn how to learn by using other senses, learning self hypnosis, making sure the subject comes back at the end, etc. However, this is a professional clinical book, not a self help book, so be careful about trying to use the procedures used with the cases, etc in this book for solo work (the same applies to historical information it contains). Also, Adam Eason's work with Benjamin Parris in the International Society of Hypnosis, specifically his article about self hypnosis , should be a useful thing to read (especially the parts that talk about how to learn to learn when you're directing it yourself, and about whether you need another person to help you, and using recordings to help you, etc.)

## Indirect language and re-entry cues

For information about using indirect hypnotic language patterns, such as pacing, utilizing, metaphor, and associational cues, etc, see Healing the Divided Self , Phillips and Frederick, 1995, pp 53 - 57, 96 - 100, as well as nimja's tutorial on hypnotic language patterns , and his tutorial on triggers . The information on these techniques comes from these sources, not because they are necessarily validating the validity of some aspects of NLP, or because every example suits trauma work (nimja did not write or endorse this guide), but because I was adapting the language used for my own purposes, and I deliberately used a sequence that would be easy to use for re-entry.

## Body awareness and changing the amount of contact

NOTE: Some of the information in this post comes from In an Unspoken Voice , Peter Levine, 2010, especially ch 5 and 7, as well as ch 3 and 4 of Freedom from Pain , Levine and Maggie Phillips 2012, and ch 4 of Healing Trauma , by Levine. These sources talk about the key concepts of tracking what is happening in the body, resources, titration, pendulation, and the range of foci available when working, from sensation and imagery to behavior, emotions, and meaning, etc. This is not done from a position of "here is what you need to do to physiologically fix it", but here are some things you can try, etc.

## Inner-child care and adult capacities

For information about all of these things, see Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide . The practices in this post are designed to match with those in the guide, but include some elements of learning to get into self hypnosis, as well as other helpful language, and making sure you come back at the end, etc.

Some of the information in this post is taken from Healing the Divided Self , Phillips and Frederick, 1995, pp 148-153, 171-174, about renurturing work, and how it can be combined with somatic work, and how to develop capacities for nurturing, etc. For information about attunement, and how every child is different and needs to be responded to differently, see Trauma Through a Child's Eyes , Levine and Maggie Kline, especially ch 4, 5, 10, etc, as it talks about actual kids (and this guide is applying this to how to respond to younger feeling states within an adult). The sequence for doing this with borrowed adulthood and the arrangement of 3 functions of adulthood, etc, is not from these authors, however.

## Inner speech and putting experience into words

Not everybody has an inner voice , Nedergaard and Lupyan 2024, is not about self hypnosis adaptations, but about people with different amounts of inner speech. The questions in the quick start are not a validated way of seeing if you can do self hypnosis, but are simply there as a practical way of seeing for yourself how you are.

## Memory, support, and choosing a practitioner

Chapter 7 of Trauma and Memory , by Peter Levine, 2015, contains useful information on this. This US Veterans Administration website , contains useful general information about hypnosis, such as the fact that it can be very strong and should be carefuly used, and that it may result in false memories. This ASCH website , contains information about the usefulness of getting certified in hypnosis, and why it doesn't guarantee competence. Links are available for these and other useful groups in the evidence and specialist sections, but they point to their particular claims, not every use of hypnosis.
