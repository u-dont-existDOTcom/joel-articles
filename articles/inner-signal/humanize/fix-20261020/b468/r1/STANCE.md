# Stance check: b468 rewrite

Scope: four rewritten paragraphs. One is in "When 'your voice' is everybody else's voice" (human doer and human being). Three are in Appendix F, "Forgiveness without bypass": the opening paragraph, the quoted vow, and self-forgiveness. I checked every sentence that states a position or a claim against the whole published essay.

## Conflicts in the rewrite

### 1. A hedge is added to the bypass warning, and the warning's trigger changes

- Rewrite: "it has to come from a place of understanding otherwise it can be bypassing."
- Essay (Appendix F, "Forgiveness without bypass", the published version of this paragraph): "It grows from understanding, and pushing it too early creates bypass."
- What a reader would wrongly believe: that forgiveness forced early only *might* be a bypass, and that the danger is missing understanding rather than pushing it too soon.

### 2. A hedge is dropped from the warning about lasting anger

- Rewrite: "However, if we remain in the anger forever, we are allowing the injury to dictate how we live our lives in the present."
- Essay (Appendix F, "Forgiveness without bypass", same published paragraph): "Staying angry forever can also let the injury keep governing the present."
- What a reader would wrongly believe: that anger which never ends always means you're letting the injury run your life. The essay says only that it can.

## Contradictions in the published text

I found none in the four published paragraphs, either within themselves or against the rest of the essay.

Not counted: two passages come close, but I read both as ordinary speech, not contradictions.
- "When worth isn't felt internally, it gets earned through beauty, performance..." describes what people do, and the paragraph then calls it a bargain. So it doesn't take back "Worth was never the missing skill" (The inner child, the guards, and the three inner adults).
- "It grows from understanding" and "forgiveness may come later when your relationship to the event changes on its own" read together as: understanding helps, and the forgiveness itself shouldn't be forced.
