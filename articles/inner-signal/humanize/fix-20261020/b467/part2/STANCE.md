# Stance check: b467 part 2 (Appendix D breathing, Appendix F forgiveness)

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. Appendix D, breathing**
- Rewrite: "Then work on your reduced breathing / Buteyko’ breathing."
- Essay (Appendix D, opening): "Body first. Inner child second. Inquiry third. Action fourth." Also Appendix D, Anxiety script: "First, body. Feet, chair, breath, room."
- A reader would believe breathing is the step after the inner-child practice (the sentence follows "Inner child love"), which reverses the appendix's body-first order.

**2. Appendix D, breathing**
- Rewrite: "If you feel bad, dizzy, or panicky, or have medical issues, STOP!"
- Essay (Appendix D, Reduced breathing / Buteyko-style breathing): "Stop if dizzy, strained, panicky, or medically concerned."
- A reader would take any existing health condition as a reason to stop. The essay says to stop when you're medically concerned.

**3. Appendix F, forgiveness**
- Rewrite: "We can not ‘force’ forgiveness on ourselves, it has to come from a place of understanding otherwise it can be bypassing."
- Essay (Appendix F, Forgiveness without bypass): "It grows from understanding, and pushing it too early creates bypass."
- A reader would believe that forgiveness pushed ahead of understanding only sometimes turns into bypass. The essay says it does (a hedge was added).

**4. Appendix F, forgiveness**
- Rewrite: "However, if we remain in the anger forever, we are allowing the injury to dictate how we live our lives in the present."
- Essay (Appendix F, Forgiveness without bypass): "Staying angry forever can also let the injury keep governing the present."
- A reader would believe that lasting anger always means the angry person is choosing to let the injury run their life. The essay presents this as a risk (a hedge was dropped).

## Contradictions in the published text

None that a reasonable reader would see. Two near misses were not counted. "can help some people who overbreathe or panic" next to "Stop if dizzy, strained, panicky" reads as a list of reactions to the exercise, like a physio's "stop if it hurts" (the rewrite keeps the same pairing). And "the person remains accountable" is restated, not taken back, by the next paragraph ("so do accountability").

Conflicts in the rewrite: 4. Contradictions in the published text: 0.
