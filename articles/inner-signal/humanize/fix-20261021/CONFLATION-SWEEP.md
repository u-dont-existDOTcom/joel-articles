## Findings

### 1. B142 (the script runs on through B143–B144) · Working with protectors · type (c)

Sentence (B142): (“I notice you and what you are trying to do. I want to know more. Could I ask you what you are afraid would happen if you stopped protecting me by…..?)

And in B144: "Tell them that it makes sense that they want to protect you and that they don’t have to stop protecting you, ..."

**Who a reader could take it for:** the protector part (guard) introduced in B140, or the inherited parent from B141.

**Why:** the script names no addressee until B144 ("Now talk to the protector"). B141 sits between the guard and the script, and it ends "this voice may jump in to say that what you’re doing is “fake”." B143's "pretend again" echoes "fake". So a reader asks the inherited parent what it fears "if you stopped protecting me", then tells it that it doesn't "have to stop protecting you". That hands the critic/warden the protecting role and permission to carry on. The script was written for a guard: B143's fears include "you will forgive someone who shouldn’t be forgiven yet". The guide also handles the parent's voice differently in B379 (find the rule it enforces).

**Fix:** open B142 with four words: (To the protector part: “I notice you and what you are trying to do. ...
Alternative that changes no words: move B141 to after B145.
