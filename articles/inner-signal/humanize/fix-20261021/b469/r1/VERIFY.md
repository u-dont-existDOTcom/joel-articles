## Pair 1

HG18-count-feet. In scope: the count's step One and, from the stopped-recording paragraph, pressing your feet or hands into a surface (the rest of that paragraph is covered elsewhere).

- Step One, feel the body's contact: CHANGED, slightly. "Feel your body again" asks for general body awareness, and contact now comes only from pressing feet or hands into what's under you. Noticing the rest of the body's contact with its support (seat, back, bed) is no longer asked.
- Step One, feel the chosen weight: KEPT. "Feel your weight (of your choice)" still makes it a weight the reader chooses. (This reads "chosen weight" as the body's weight as the reader chooses to feel it. If the guide uses "chosen weight" for something the reader picked earlier, "your weight" shifts it to body weight.)
- Stopped recording, press your feet or hands into a surface: CHANGED, slightly. The action is kept, and a reader whose recording stopped still reaches it through the count. But "a surface" is now "whatever is under you", so a wall or a table in front of the reader no longer fits.

ADDED:
- Pressing is now part of step 1 of every count-up, not only after a recording stops unexpectedly. A reader would press their feet or hands into what's under them at the end of every session.

Not judged: the lead-in, steps 2 to 5 and the closing line, which have no published counterpart in this pair. One note: the paragraph says it uses "the five steps listed above". If that list still has the published step One, the list and this count now differ by the pressing.

## Pair 2

HG32-prose.

- When to use the checklist (a person, advertisement, ideology, spiritual teaching, romantic pull or inner voice pressing too hard): CHANGED, slightly. All six are kept, but the trigger is now "something pressing on you too hard" with the six as examples ("stuff like that"), so a reader would use it for any similar pressure.
- The self-talk, all KEPT: noticing influence is happening ("I'm being influenced"); not needing to obey it or attack it; slowing down; asking what is being presupposed ("what it's taking for granted"); asking who benefits; noticing the body's signals without assuming what they prove ("hold off on deciding what that proves"); asking what love, truth, humility and ordinary life would require.
- The four tests, all KEPT: truth survives questions; love survives boundaries; guidance survives sleep; what's for me won't require abandoning my adult self.
- Before acting, ask: KEPT as "before acting on it, look for" (its new open-ended framing is under ADDED).
- Questions KEPT: urgency; secrecy; shame; a threat of exclusion ("left out"); a promise of specialness; sexual charge; spiritual superiority; a demand for money, sex, loyalty, silence or obedience (all five); questioning treated as betrayal; resistance pathologized; ordinary life downgraded ("starts to look small next to it"); whether the adult self is still allowed to say no.
- A narrowing of choices: CHANGED, slightly. "Your choices keep getting narrower" asks for a continuing trend. A single narrowing, such as an ultimatum, fits the published question but less clearly the edited one.
- The child part seduced, scared or rescued: CHANGED, slightly. Seduced and scared are kept. Rescue is split off and hedged ("Being rescued can be part of it too"), so it reads as a possible or lesser sign rather than one of three equal ones.
- Several yes answers mean slow down; don't deepen trance; don't make an irreversible decision: KEPT.
- Return to the room, body, food, sleep, time and counsel: KEPT, all six.
- A good guide increases your adult self; a good guide can tolerate your no: KEPT.
- A bad guide replaces your adult self: CHANGED, slightly. "Tries to replace" makes it an attempt rather than what the bad guide does.
- A bad guide calls your no resistance, ego, fear, low vibration, trauma, sin, or proof that you need more of their guidance: CHANGED, slightly. All the labels are kept, but "or proof" became "and they'll say it proves", so a reader now expects a bad guide to do both rather than one of these.

ADDED:
- "You could say something like": the self-talk becomes an optional, approximate sample rather than the checklist's own words.
- "the usual signs, like ... that kind of thing": the questions become examples of familiar signs from an open list, so "several of these" counts against a looser set.
- "and so on" after the anchors and "you name it" after the labels: both lists now read as samples of a longer set.

VERDICT: CHANGED
