## The one that matters: the citation says the opposite

**Line 99.** "Chronic cycles lead to the accumulation of DeltaFosB, a protein switch that permanently alters gene
expression to cement the trauma circuitry ([Vialou, et al, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20473292/))."

That paper found the reverse. <cite index="4-1">It opens by saying little is known about the molecular mechanisms of resilience, the ability of some individuals to escape the deleterious effects of stress, and reports that ΔFosB mediates an essential mechanism of it.</cite> <cite index="2-1">Mice were put through ten consecutive days of social defeat and split into susceptible and resilient groups by social avoidance, and the resilient mice showed the greatest induction of ΔFosB in both the core and the shell of the nucleus accumbens.</cite> <cite index="1-1">ΔFosB induction was also required for fluoxetine to reverse the behavioural pathology that social defeat had caused, and it worked through induction of the GluR2 AMPA subunit, which decreased the responsiveness of nucleus accumbens neurons to glutamate.</cite> <cite index="47-1">And in the human arm: depressed people examined at autopsy had reduced ΔFosB in the nucleus accumbens.</cite> <cite index="5-1">NIMH's write-up puts it plainly — triggering deltaFosB in the reward hub is both necessary and sufficient for resilience, and post-mortem tissue from depressed patients held about half as much as controls.</cite>

So the sentence's source is evidence against the sentence. Two separate problems in it, as well: "permanently" is wrong
(below), and the paper is about depression after social defeat, not trauma circuitry.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4380559 · https://www.nimh.nih.gov/news/science-updates/2010/resilience-factor-low-in-depression-protects-mice-from-stress

## Which cells it is in decides which way it goes

This is why the direction can't be stated flat. <cite index="22-1">In both chronic restraint and chronic social defeat, ΔFosB is induced in both major subtypes of medium spiny neuron — those expressing mostly D1 and those expressing mostly D2, together about 95% of neurons in the region — with no induction in interneurons or non-neural cells; the modest induction in susceptible mice is primarily in D2-type neurons, while the more robust induction in resilient mice is specific to D1-type.</cite> <cite index="21-1">The follow-up work makes the same split causal from the other end: Fosb-targeted histone acetylation in D2 neurons or histone methylation in D1 neurons promotes a stress-susceptible, depressive-like phenotype, while the opposite pairings increase resilience.</cite> <cite index="26-1">Voluntary wheel running induces it too, and high levels in D1 neurons go with resilience while high levels in D2 neurons go with susceptibility.</cite>

"More ΔFosB = more armor" is therefore not a claim the literature supports in either direction on its own. Where it
accumulates is the whole finding.

Sources: https://www.nature.com/articles/npp201788 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4380559 ·
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6046059

## "Permanently" against "degraded by Week 8"

The article says both. Line 99 has it permanently altering gene expression; line 375 has it degraded and the hardware
reset by Week 8+. The literature is close to the timeline and not to "permanently".

<cite index="43-1">The 35–37 kDa isoforms accumulate under chronic exposure because of their extraordinarily long half-lives, and so persist in the neurons for at least several weeks after the stimulus stops.</cite> <cite index="14-1">ΔFOSB's half-life runs up to 8 days in vivo, against minutes to a couple of hours for the other AP1 proteins.</cite> <cite index="15-1">In cell culture it is about 10 hours, and phosphorylation is what stabilises it.</cite> <cite index="41-1">Its stability comes from two things: ΔFosB lacks the two degron domains that target full-length FosB and the other Fos proteins for rapid degradation, and it is phosphorylated at its N-terminus by casein kinase 2 and perhaps other kinases.</cite> <cite index="48-1">The longest persistence found: in sexually active rats, ΔFosB was still present in nucleus accumbens neurons at least 28 days after the behaviour stopped.</cite> <cite index="44-1">In the VTA after 14 days of cocaine it persisted almost 2 weeks.</cite>

"Several weeks" is the sourced phrase. Eight weeks is inside it but is the protocol's own number, not a measured one,
and nothing found measures a human de-armoring timeline at all.

## Dynorphin and ΔFosB pull against each other, not together

**Line 91** pairs them in the heading as the hardened circuit; **line 93** has dynorphin suppressing dopamine to make the
Void and **line 99** has ΔFosB cementing it. They are doing the same job in the article. In the sources one represses the
other.

<cite index="32-1">Zachariou 2006: ΔFosB in the nucleus accumbens, partly through the repression of dynorphin expression, mediates several major features of opiate addiction.</cite> <cite index="31-1">Overexpressing it in the accumbens increased sensitivity to morphine's rewarding effects and worsened physical dependence, while reducing sensitivity to morphine's analgesic effects and speeding analgesic tolerance — with dynorphin apparently the target through which it produced that phenotype.</cite>

Worth keeping straight, because the confusion is understandable: <cite index="39-1">the standard ΔFosB overexpression model targets it to the dynorphin-positive neurons of the nucleus accumbens and dorsal striatum.</cite> So ΔFosB sits *in* dynorphin neurons while turning dynorphin *down*. Co-location, not co-operation.

Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16415864/ · https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/an-essential-role-for-%CE%B4fosb-in-the-nucleus-accumbens-in-morphine--2/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2607320/table/tbl2

## The fix side: the HDAC step is real, its consequence isn't sourced

**Line 53** has the protocol acting as a metabolic solvent, an HDAC inhibitor, softening the neural concrete by
modulating glutamate and introducing ketones. **Line 97** has ketones and HDAC inhibitors remodelling the physical
structure.

The first half holds. <cite index="73-1">Shimazu 2013: D-β-hydroxybutyrate is an endogenous and specific inhibitor of class I HDACs; exogenous βOHB, fasting and calorie restriction all raised global histone acetylation in mouse tissues, and treated mice were substantially protected against oxidative stress.</cite> <cite index="72-1">It is structurally related to butyrate, a known class I and II HDAC inhibitor, and it works over a clinically relevant 1–2 mM range.</cite> So "ketones inhibit class I HDACs" is well supported.

What doesn't follow is that this softens armor. Two things cut against it.

First, ΔFosB *recruits* HDAC1 rather than being removed by it. <cite index="67-1">Renthal 2008: accumulated ΔFosB desensitises c-fos induction by recruiting HDAC1 to the c-fos promoter, which deacetylates the surrounding histones and attenuates the gene; knocking out HDAC1 locally in striatum abolished that desensitisation.</cite> <cite index="61-1">At a gene it activates instead, cdk5, it recruits histone acetyltransferases and SWI/SNF complexes, with HDACs absent from that promoter.</cite> Inhibiting HDACs releases one of its brakes; it doesn't degrade the protein.

Second, in the reward region the behavioural direction mostly runs the wrong way for this protocol. <cite index="81-1">Sodium butyrate pretreatment augmented morphine-induced locomotor sensitization and conditioned place preference, and mice given it developed enhanced locomotor responses to cocaine, ethanol and morphine.</cite> <cite index="84-1">Daily HDAC inhibitor infusions into the nucleus accumbens shell raised the break point under a progressive-ratio schedule — more motivation to work for cocaine.</cite> <cite index="88-1">Co-treating with a D1 agonist and sodium butyrate significantly enhanced cocaine-induced locomotor activity and place preference over either alone, while overexpressing HDAC in striatum attenuates the animal's response to cocaine.</cite>

But this is genuinely split, and the split is about the regimen, not the drug. <cite index="89-1">Prior HDAC inhibitor studies report conflicting effects on cocaine-elicited behaviour: several find that inhibition enhances the behavioural effects of cocaine or amphetamine, while more chronic regimens block psychostimulant-induced plasticity — and continuous infusion of the selective inhibitor MS-275 into the accumbens over a broad time course attenuated cocaine's behavioural effects and triggered other chromatin changes known to oppose cocaine-induced plasticity.</cite> <cite index="86-1">In another, butyrate's reward effects were transient, apparent only within 2 days of the last dose.</cite>

Two different outcome measures, two different answers, and the protocol's own use is continuous rather than
intermittent — which is the arm that went the protocol's way. That is as far as the evidence reaches: it does not reach
"softens the armor," and nothing found tests whether HDAC inhibition lowers ΔFosB.

## Question 9, for him

The article's spine is that ΔFosB accumulates as armor and the protocol dissolves it. The sources give a different
shape: in the nucleus accumbens, ΔFosB tracks with resilience and with antidepressant response, depressed humans have
less of it, and whether a rise is good or bad depends on whether it lands in D1 or D2 neurons. Three ways to go, and the
choice is his, not mine:

1. **Keep the frame, re-sourced.** Drop Vialou, keep ΔFosB as the hardening switch on the strength of the addiction
   literature (where accumulation does go with sensitisation and with more motivation for the drug), and say that the
   stress literature points the other way. Honest, and the protocol's steps are unaffected.
2. **Narrow it.** ΔFosB in the addiction arm of the story, out of the trauma arm. The Role-Self glue becomes dopamine
   and the structural changes, not ΔFosB.
3. **Flip the role.** Follow the stress literature: in trauma, too little ΔFosB in D1 neurons is the problem, and
   anything that raises it there is on the protocol's side. This reads interesting, but it inverts the de-armoring
   metaphor the whole article is built on, so it is the biggest change.

None of these touches a compound, a dose, a timing or a warning. The protocol runs the same either way; what changes is
what the article says is happening underneath it.
