## DFB-1: "a protein switch that permanently alters gene expression ... (Vialou, et al, 2010)" (A point that doesn't match its source)

*C. The "Hardened" Circuit: Dynorphin & DeltaFosB, P4*

> a protein switch that permanently alters gene expression to cement the trauma circuitry (Vialou, et al, 2010).

**What changes:** Two changes in one sentence: "permanently" goes, and the citation changes.

**As it would read:** The Hardening: Chronic cycles lead to the accumulation of DeltaFosB, a protein switch that alters gene expression and holds the circuitry in place for weeks after the cycles stop ([Nestler, 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2607320)).

**Why:** The paper cited here found close to the opposite. Vialou, et al, 2010 is about resilience: the mice that came through ten days of social defeat best were the ones with the most DeltaFosB in the nucleus accumbens, raising it there was both necessary and sufficient for that resilience, and fluoxetine needed it to work at all. In the human arm of the same paper, people who had been depressed had roughly half as much DeltaFosB in the accumbens as controls -- less, not more. ([The paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20473292/).) So it can't be the source for DeltaFosB cementing trauma circuitry. The accumulation claim itself does hold, from the addiction side, and that is what the new citation points to ([Nestler, 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2607320)). "Permanently" is separately wrong, and your own Week 8+ line already says so: the measured half-life is up to about 8 days in living animals, and the protein persists for at least several weeks after the stimulus stops -- the longest anything ran was 28 days ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8879420)).

**What stays:** The hardening, the accumulation, the protein-switch framing, and the sentence's place in section C.

**You're deciding:** Whether to keep the hardening claim with a citation that supports it and no "permanently", or to say something different about DeltaFosB altogether (question 9).

**My ask:** Use this wording. Whether the opposite finding goes into the article as well is question 9 -- that one changes what the section argues, so it's yours to call, not mine.

## DFB-2: Dynorphin and DeltaFosB read as partners here, and they aren't (Something the section is missing)

*C. The "Hardened" Circuit: Dynorphin & DeltaFosB, P1*

> The Problem: To protect itself from the Glutamate storm, the brain eventually releases Dynorphin, a chemical blanket that suppresses dopamine. This creates the "Void"--a state of apathy, grayness, and an inability to feel pleasure.

**What changes:** One sentence at the end of the paragraph.

**As it would read:** The Problem: To protect itself from the Glutamate storm, the brain eventually releases Dynorphin, a chemical blanket that suppresses dopamine. This creates the "Void"--a state of apathy, grayness, and an inability to feel pleasure. The two aren't one mechanism, though: DeltaFosB accumulates inside the dynorphin neurons themselves, and what it does there is turn dynorphin down, not up ([Zachariou, et al, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16415864/)).

**Why:** The heading puts Dynorphin and DeltaFosB together and the section has them doing the same job -- dynorphin making the Void, DeltaFosB cementing it. In the animal work they pull against each other on that exact point: DeltaFosB in the accumbens produces its opiate-addiction phenotype partly by repressing dynorphin expression ([Zachariou, et al, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16415864/)). What makes it easy to conflate is that the standard way of studying DeltaFosB puts it in the dynorphin-positive neurons, so the two are in the same cells -- in the same place, doing opposite things. A reader who takes them as allies will be wrong about why the Void lifts.

**What stays:** Everything you wrote about dynorphin, the chemical blanket, and the Void.

**You're deciding:** Whether section C tells the reader how dynorphin and DeltaFosB actually relate, or stays as it is.

**My ask:** Your call. It makes section C more accurate and slightly more crowded. If you'd rather keep the section clean, the heading and the Void story still work -- it's the implied teamwork that doesn't.
