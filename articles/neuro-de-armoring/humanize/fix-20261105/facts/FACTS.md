Adrenaline does push potassium into cells and lowers blood potassium (Brown 1983, https://go.drugbank.com/articles/A27114). **Corrected 2026-11-08 (card SC-106b, lesson neuro-2026-11-08j):** the human axons were one patient's arm and leg nerves at a blood level of 2.1; the brain keeps the potassium around its neurons within tight limits whatever the blood does (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27799072/), and in guinea-pig brain slices lowering potassium from 4 to 2 brought seizure-like bursts after a brief quieting (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11454323/), so neither direction holds for the brain. What holds for eating potassium is blood pressure (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23558164/) | F-11 |

## Checked and holds

- B68, phosphatidylserine: 400 mg of the PS complex blunted the cortisol response; 600 and 800 mg didn't (Hellhammer 2004, https://edoc.unibas.ch/8375), nor did 200 mg (Hellhammer 2014, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25081826/).
- B58, Color-Path over counting: in the trauma-film studies a visual-spatial task cut later flashbacks while counting backwards increased them (Holmes 2004, https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/507975). Color-Path itself hasn't been tested.
- B107, 4,700 mg of potassium: still the FDA label Daily Value (https://ific.org/?p=17530); since 2019 the National Academies' Adequate Intake is 3,400 mg for men and 2,600 mg for women (https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/sodium-and-potassium-dietary-reference-intake-values-updated-in-new-report). Either works as a target; on card F-11.
- B110 to B114, the potassium foods: about right per USDA (beet greens 1,309 mg a cup: https://getfoodfacts.com/high-in/potassium).
- B150, D-serine and kidneys: rat kidney damage, rare in human studies; the 30 mg/kg cap matches the dose of the PTSD pilot trial (about 2 g a day).
