The passage combines established neuroscience with speculative interpretations. The NMDA/excitotoxicity mechanism is real, and GluD receptors genuinely regulate synaptic function. But the proposed connections to anxiety, rage, hypervigilance, apathy, and a persistent neural “armor” state go considerably beyond the evidence.

There are also two important distinctions:
1. Excessive NMDA receptor activation is not equivalent to ordinary anxiety or heightened glutamate signaling.
2. GluD receptors are not simply adjustable switches that determine whether the brain is hyperactive or underactive. Their effects depend on the circuit and their interactions with other synaptic proteins.

2. What Johns Hopkins actually discovered
The underlying paper is Wang et al., 2025, Nature: Delta-type glutamate receptors are ligand-gated ion channels.

This is a significant molecular neuroscience discovery.

Previously, GluD receptors were understood primarily as organizers of synapses, and whether they functioned as conventional ion channels was controversial.

The researchers purified human GluD2 and demonstrated that:
- D-serine and GABA can activate GluD2 channels. This was unexpected, particularly for GABA, which is usually associated with inhibitory signaling.
- GluD2 can conduct ions through a ligand-gated channel.
- A mutation associated with cerebellar ataxia can produce abnormal spontaneous leak currents.
- Receptor structure helps explain how these channels open and how mutations alter their function. 
  
  Nature
This is stronger evidence than simply discovering that GluD receptors are associated with psychiatric disorders.

However, the experiments primarily examined purified receptor proteins, their structures, and their electrical behavior. They did not demonstrate that manipulating GluD activity treats anxiety, schizophrenia, or hypervigilance.
3. The most important correction: NMDA versus GluD

NMDA receptors

Participate in excitatory signaling, calcium-dependent plasticity, and learning. Excessive activation under pathological conditions can contribute to excitotoxicity.




GluD receptors

Help organize synapses and regulate their function. New evidence shows that GluD2 also possesses ligand-gated ion-channel activity, but its normal physiological regulation remains incompletely understood.



One especially important detail: GluD receptors are named after the glutamate receptor family, but glutamate itself is not their established activating ligand.

The Johns Hopkins experiments identified D-serine and GABA as activators of purified GluD2. This makes the description of GluD as simply another glutamate-controlled volume knob misleading.

4. What about the armor hypothesis?
Connecting this with our earlier ΔFosB discussion, there is a potentially interesting research hypothesis:

Chronic stress can alter glutamatergic signaling, receptor expression, synaptic plasticity, and the responsiveness of stress-related neural circuits. ΔFosB is one transcription factor involved in sustaining some of those adaptations.

GluD receptors might participate in these adaptations through their roles in synaptic organization and signaling.

But the proposed sequence:

Chronic stress → ΔFosB-mediated neural armor → GluD dial becomes stuck → persistent hypervigilance or apathy

has not been demonstrated.

It would be a mistake to present it as an established biological mechanism.
5. Suggested scientifically corrected wording
Glutamate, NMDA Receptors, and Neural Hyperexcitability
The mechanism: Glutamate is the brain's principal excitatory neurotransmitter. When glutamate signaling becomes pathologically excessive, NMDA receptors can contribute to excitotoxicity through excessive calcium entry, mitochondrial dysfunction, and oxidative damage. This can injure or kill neurons. However, ordinary anxiety, rage, and hypervigilance do not necessarily indicate excitotoxicity. These experiences can involve altered excitatory and inhibitory signaling without neuronal damage.

The signal regulators (GluD receptors): Research published in Nature in 2025 by Johns Hopkins scientists revealed that GluD2 receptors, previously known primarily for organizing synapses, can also function as ligand-gated ion channels activated by D-serine and GABA. Certain disease-associated mutations produce abnormal channel activity, suggesting potential targets for future neurological treatments.

The connection to persistent stress adaptations: Chronic stress can remodel synapses and alter the balance of excitation and inhibition. Transcription factors such as ΔFosB can help sustain some of these changes. GluD receptors may contribute to circuit-specific regulation, but there is currently insufficient evidence that they become persistently stuck in high- or low-activity states during psychological hypervigilance, emotional shutdown, or other proposed neural armor states.




Bottom line: The 2025 GluD discovery is legitimate and important. Its potential therapeutic applications are interesting, but the strongest current evidence is at the molecular level. The passage should distinguish established excitotoxicity, newly discovered GluD channel activity, and the still-hypothetical connection to chronic psychological stress adaptations.
