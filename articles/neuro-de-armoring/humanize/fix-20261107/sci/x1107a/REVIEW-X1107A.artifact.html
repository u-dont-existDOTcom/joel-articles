B. The "Overheating" CPU: Glutamate Excitotoxicity

P1The Problem: Glutamate is the brain's primary excitatory neurotransmitter. In states of stress, the brain floods with Glutamate. This is the "voltage spike"--the engine is redlining.

P2The Metabolic Red Line: We often assume we can 'think' our way out of trauma, but Dr. Bruce Perry's data proves that anatomy overrides intellect. You can often tell when you're dysregulated by noticing if your heart is beating faster or if your heart-rate variability declines. Part 2: Reducing Anxiety & Depression

P1Lower the Cortisol Floor (Phosphatidylserine)

P2Before ketones, before D-Serine, address cortisol directly--because cortisol is what sensitizes the brain to Glutamate and locks the armor in place (see the Isolation Trap above). The single most useful nutrient I've found for this is Phosphatidylserine (PS), a phospholipid that blunts the HPA-axis cortisol response to stress.

P3Who it's for: People running high or reactive cortisol--the "wired and tired," can't-downshift, waking-at-3-a.m. type. This includes many people with slow COMT (the Val158Met variant that clears catecholamines slowly, so stress hormones linger and you stay on high alert). One friend, Scott, with exactly this profile got more out of PS than anything else in this protocol. Be honest about the evidence, though: the cortisol-lowering effect is best documented in chronically stressed people; the slow-COMT angle is mechanism plus my own observation, not a controlled finding.

P5Food instead of capsules: One of the few supplements you can actually eat your way to. Atlantic mackerel (~480 mg/100g) and herring (~360 mg) are richest; tuna is lower (~194 mg). A 200g mackerel fillet ≈ 960 mg PS--above clinical doses. (Don't lean on tuna daily--mercury.)

If yours isn't actually elevated, lowering it pushes you down the other side of the curve, where the symptoms look almost identical to high cortisol. Watch for either signal:

P7Flat / numb / no drive--you've over-suppressed. Classic too-little-cortisol.

P8More wired, not less--racing thoughts, 3 a.m. waking, irritability, vivid or stressful dreams. Counterintuitive but real: when cortisol drops too far, the body leans on adrenaline to hold up blood pressure and alertness, and you feel more stressed, not less.

P9Either one means PS is the wrong tool for you right now. Don't raise the dose to push through--stop. If you want to know before you start, a 4-point salivary cortisol test tells you whether you're actually high.

P10The rule of thumb if you can't test: Wired, tired, can't switch off, 3 a.m. waking → probably high, PS is worth a trial. Numb, low-drive, flat → probably not high; PS will likely make it worse. Skip it or test first.

Agmatine vs PS vs Phosphatidylcholine

P1Do not confuse these three tools:

P2Phosphatidylserine (PS) is the cortisol-floor tool. Use it only for the high-cortisol "wired and tired" profile.

P3Phosphatidylcholine (PC) is a membrane/choline nutrient. It may support general cell-membrane and acetylcholine biology, but it is not a targeted cortisol-lowering or glutamate-braking intervention.

P4Agmatine is different from both. It is an optional glutamate/NO/imidazoline "voltage governor." It belongs in the Shield section, not as a replacement for PS or PC. Like PS, it can overcorrect the system: PS can push low-cortisol people into flatness; agmatine can push already-numb people into more emotional blunting.

Dopamine Receptor Burnout / DAWS Pattern: Uridine Monophosphate

P1Who this is for: Uridine isn't for everyone with depression. It could be helpful, though, for folks that show a "burned-out dopamine receptor" pattern, where everything feels gray and there's no desire for anything (anhedonia). These folks also find that dreams and psychedelic visuals are weak, so they need unusually high doses to feel anything, and as a result they tend to compulsively reach for ketamine/LSD/novelty just to get a hit. In the medical literature, this burnout pattern is talked about with regard to dopamine agonist withdrawal syndrome (DAWS). People with DAWS, after tapering off dopamine agonist drugs (like pramipexole or ropinirole), present with symptoms of anxiety, panic, dysphoria, depression, agitation, fatigue, autonomic symptoms, pain, and drug cravings. Scott's case looked like DAWS with dopamine receptor D2/D3 reward-system downregulation, which then got worse from frequent LSD microdosing and from trying to force dopamine hits with ketamine.

P2The tool: Uridine monophosphate (UMP). There's no study showing that "UMP cures DAWS", only more basic research on what uridine, choline, and DHA do in the brain. All three get carried in the blood to the brain, which uses them to make the fatty membranes at its synapses (circulating precursors for brain phosphatide / synaptic membrane synthesis). Wurtman's research group found that giving the three together can increase synaptic membrane components and synaptic proteins, and that in animals, adding UMP to DHA made the neurons grow more new connection points (DHA-induced dendritic spine formation). And in old rats, UMP in their food meant their neurons put out more dopamine when stimulated with potassium, and grew more nerve fibers. So it's plausible that it works on the synapses this way, but using it for DAWS in people means carefully extrapolating from those studies and from anecdotes like Scott's.

P3Scott added a standard dose of UMP, and within a day his ketamine/LSD cravings fell hard. He already had choline covered with eggs, and took fish oil for DHA/EPA. His psychedelic tolerance seemed to drop too. You can't prove from this whether UMP rebuilt his D3 receptors overnight, but it does suggest that in the right person, UMP can show you very clearly when the brain's system for rebuilding synaptic membranes and recovering dopamine receptors has been starved of building materials. Scott was having vivid dreams again too, with real childhood memories surfacing.

P4Do a trial with 150-300mg Uridine, in the morning with food. It shouldn't take more than 3 to 7 days to get an idea of whether or not it's beneficial. Keep your Omega-3's up, especially DHA and EPA. Make sure you have sufficient Choline - from eggs, Phosphatidylcholine, CDP-choline, etc, whichever works best for you. Don't start the uridine at the same time as starting some other major variable (like 5-HTP, Agmatine, D-serine, Keto diet, Iboga, Shrooms, LSD, Ketamine, etc), since the point is to see whether uridine itself changes the signal.

P5Side effects / stop signs: In practice, the most common problem is the "choline headache", or a spaced-out kind of brain fog. That isn't shown in the studies I've seen, but the likely reason is that UMP speeds up neuronal and synaptic membrane synthesis, which places a higher demand on choline than what people may normally get from diet alone. Other stop signs include insomnia, jitteriness, racing thoughts, irritability, emotional flatness, nausea, stomach cramps, or loose stools.

P6Vivid dreams and increased sensitivity to psychedelics are the hoped-for benefits of UMP. That means you should not take the old high doses of your psychedelic at first, as it might hit you way harder than you expected.

Serotonin Bottleneck Pattern: 5-HTP + Low-Dose P5P

P1Who this is for: 5-HTP is not for every depressed person either. It could help, though, with a low-serotonin-looking pattern, where it's hard to fall asleep and your mood is low, without much warmth to it. At night you get anxious and keep going over the same things. It makes more sense if there's also a genetic or clinical reason to suspect a bottleneck in serotonin synthesis, like with Scott, whose genetic data pointed to a TPH2 bottleneck, and 5-HTP helped his sleep and mood. 5-HTP skips over that bottleneck, because your body uses it to make serotonin directly, so people who respond to it can feel it quickly, although it's not as strong for sleep or mood as some hype it up to be (see this broad review of 5-HTP). I'd avoid casually experimenting with 5-HTP if you're agitated or manic, as it could sometimes push that further. And watch out for serotonin syndrome risk if you're on serotonergic meds, or using serotonergic psychedelics/entactogens.

P2Scott took 100 mg 5-HTP sublingually at night, with 10–20 mg P5P sublingually in the morning. The P5P isn't there as a stimulant, but as the active form of vitamin B6, which the enzyme that turns 5-HTP into serotonin (aromatic amino-acid decarboxylase) needs. He's genetically slow-COMT, making him sensitive to too much P5P.

P3If you're sensitive, start lower and do the trial with just 50mg 5-HTP at night for 2–3 nights, then go up to 100mg if 50mg was clearly helpful and not causing side effects. Don't do the trial at the same time as the other suggested regimen changes (like UMP, agmatine, D-serine, psychedelics, or prescription medication changes), since the point is to see whether the 5-HTP itself is doing anything. If it's the right tool, the first things you'll usually notice are your mood or sleep quality improves, you have less nighttime rumination, and your dreams become more vivid or memorable.

P4Side effects / stop signs: The most dangerous symptoms to watch out for are diagnostic of serotonin syndrome, which can be deadly if untreated: agitation + sweating + diarrhea + tremor/clonus + fever, confusion, fast heart rate, or unstable blood pressure. Other stop signs include less dangerous symptoms (some of which are part of serotonin syndrome, but not dangerous alone) like nausea, stomach discomfort, loose stools, headache, vivid or intense dreams, insomnia, sweating, tremor, anxiety, irritability, emotional agitation, or a too-bright / hypomanic feeling.

P5Serotonin syndrome usually comes from mixing 5-HTP casually with anything else that raises serotonin, like antidepressants or MAOIs. Reviews keep warning about the risk with 5-HTP, like this case report, where a young guy on sertraline (an antidepressant) took 5-HTP with a whole pile of weight-loss and energy supplements before a workout, and ended up with serotonin syndrome so bad that surgeons had to cut open both of his legs and his forearm to relieve the pressure! Luckily he recovered. If you're doing psychedelics, avoid 5-HTP on your psychedelic days, and be especially strict around ayahuasca's harmala MAOI chemistry and around iboga or ibogaine.
