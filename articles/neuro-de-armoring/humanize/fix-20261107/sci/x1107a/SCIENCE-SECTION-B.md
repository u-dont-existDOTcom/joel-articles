| Block | Claim in the article | What the sources say | Verdict | Card |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 35 | Under stress the brain floods with glutamate | Restraint stress raises glutamate outside cells in the prefrontal cortex, accumbens and hippocampus in rats; stress hormones raise glutamate release ([Popoli et al. 2011, review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3645314)) | holds (animal work) | none |
| 37 | Excitotoxicity is "literally vibrating cells to death" | Over-activated NMDA receptors let in so much calcium that the cell dies; it matters in ischemia, trauma and neurodegeneration ([Sattler & Tymianski 2001](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1385/MN:24:1-3:107)) | wrong | SB-37 |
| 37 | It "feels like anxiety, rage, or an itchy brain" | nothing ties those feelings to cells dying | unsupported: the feeling is more likely the over-excitement | SB-37 |
| 38 | Johns Hopkins found GluD is a "master volume knob" for the synapse's "hum" | GluD2 is a channel opened by D-serine and GABA, not glutamate ([Wang et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12520249/)); a year earlier two other labs found no sign of direct gating ([Itoh et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11295041)) | oversimplified, and still argued over | SB-38 |
| 38 | Armored state jams it "High" (ataxia, hyper-vigilance) or "Low" (apathy, schizotypy) | an ataxia mutation makes the channel leak; in schizophrenia GluDs are less active ([Johns Hopkins 2025](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/news/newsroom/news-releases/2025/09/scientists-find-potential-new-drug-target-for-psychiatric-and-neurological-disorders)); nothing tests armor, trauma or hyper-vigilance | a hypothesis: the Low side has an anchor, the High side's only known case is a movement disorder | SB-38 |
| 39 | "The specific drug form studied is D-cycloserine" | D-cycloserine (DCS) is a different drug acting on the same NMDA spot as D-serine ([Smits et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3747985)); the brain makes D-serine from L-serine ([Wolosker et al. 1999](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC23961)) | wrong | SB-39 |
| 39 | DCS "has evidence as an exposure-therapy augmenter" | 21 trials, 1,047 patients: a small boost ([Mataix-Cols et al. 2017](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2599177)) | holds, but small | SB-39 |
| 39 | Session quality matters: a bad session can be consolidated | DCS helped after sessions that ended in low fear and not after the others ([Smits et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3747985); [height phobia](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3636175)) | holds | SB-39 (cited) |
| 39 | D-serine itself works the same way | the trials I found all used DCS; none tested D-serine with exposure therapy | open; the article already says "possibly" | none |
| 40, 119 | NAC "clears the flood" / "mops up glutamate" | the swap raises glutamate outside the synapse, which trips mGluR2/3 and cuts glutamate release into it, in rats ([Moran et al. 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1413952)); at high concentrations NAC did the opposite through mGluR5 ([Kupchik et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3340445)) | the effect holds (in rats), the mechanism is backwards | SB-40 |
| 41 | Zinc and magnesium "physically block the receptor channel" | magnesium plugs the channel, a block voltage lifts ([Jalali-Yazdi et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6333211)); zinc mainly makes it open less, with a weaker plug ([Amico-Ruvio et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21504727/)) | half right | SB-41 |
| 42 | L-serine is what the brain makes D-serine from | yes ([Wolosker et al. 1999](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC23961)); oral L-serine raised brain D-serine only a little in rats ([Teramoto et al.](https://www.eurekaselect.com/article/43946)) | holds, as does "isn't very efficient" | SB-42 (cited) |
| 42 | L-serine "recalibrates the GluD Volume Dial" | no volume dial (block 38) | wrong | SB-42 |
| 42 | Leucine "tunes synaptic gain" and eases the itchy brain | no source found; in rats at least half of brain glutamate's nitrogen came from leucine ([2004 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14720211/)), and BCAAs compete with tryptophan and tyrosine to get into the brain ([National Academies](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK209312/)) | unsupported, and the evidence points the other way | SB-42 |
| 43, 44 | Agmatine as an NMDA/GluN2B brake; not with D-serine sessions | checked in the earlier agmatine pass (gate E344) | holds as written then | none |
| 148 | Take D-serine after PQQ and ketones "so your brain is fueled when the GluD dial is turned down" | D-serine opens GluD channels and switches on the NMDA spot: up, not down | wrong direction (the timing itself stays) | SB-148 |
