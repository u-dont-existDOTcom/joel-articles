The Signal Dial (GluD Receptors): So if NMDA is the spark, is anything else turning the signal up or down? Well according to a recent finding by Johns Hopkins researchers, GluD receptors, mainly known for helping build synapses, are also channels themselves, gates that let current into the cell, and what opens them is D-serine (one of the tools below) and GABA, the brain's main calming chemical, rather than glutamate (Wang, et al, 2025). Other researchers' tests the year before found no sign of that, so it's still being argued over (Itoh, et al, 2024). A mutation that causes cerebellar ataxia, a movement and balance disorder, leaves the channel leaking when it should be shut, and in schizophrenia GluDs seem to be less active than normal (Johns Hopkins, 2025). Whether an armored state jams it too, on "High" as hyper-vigilance or on "Low" as apathy and schizotypy (odd, schizophrenia-like thinking), nobody has tested, so that part is a hypothesis.
