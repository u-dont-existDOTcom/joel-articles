The Signal Dial (GluD Receptors): So if NMDA is the spark, is anything else turning the signal up or down? Well according to a recent finding by Johns Hopkins researchers, GluD receptors, until now known for helping build synapses, are channels in their own right, and what opens them is D-serine (one of the tools below) and GABA, the brain's calming chemical, rather than glutamate (Wang, et al, 2025). What has been measured in people so far all points one way: the channel sticks open and leaks, so the synapse never gets quiet. That is what the mutation behind one inherited ataxia does, and a gene variant that raises the risk of schizophrenia does the same (Allen, et al, 2023). So my guess for the armored state is that it jams the dial the same way, stuck part-way on, which is what hyper-vigilance would be at the level of one synapse: a floor the signal can't drop below. Nobody has tested that in trauma yet, and it can be tested, because a drug that shuts these leaking channels already works in the lab.
