The Mechanism: When glutamate floods in, it jams open the NMDA receptors, the doors glutamate opens on a brain cell, like a key stuck in the "On" position, and calcium pours in through them. Calcium that high kills the cell only in extremes, a stroke or a brain injury, and that killing is what "excitotoxicity" means (Sattler & Tymianski, 2001), so a storm doesn't mean your neurons are dying. What you feel comes from where the glutamate lands, and under stress it rises in the amygdala, the part that flags threats (Wilson, 2015), so an amygdala firing too hard hands you threat: anxiety, rage, the "itchy brain", and never joy. Block the NMDA receptors there and anxious behaviour drops in animals (Boyle, 2013).
