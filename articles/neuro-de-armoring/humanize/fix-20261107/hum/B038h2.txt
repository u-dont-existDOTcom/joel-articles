The Signal Dial (GluD Receptors): So if NMDA is the spark, what sets the level? Well, Johns Hopkins researchers recently found that GluD receptors, until now just the things that help build synapses, are channels themselves, and what opens them is D-serine (one of the tools below) and GABA, the chemical that quiets a cell down, not glutamate (Wang, et al, 2025). In people, what turns up is a leak. The mutation behind one inherited ataxia leaves the channel stuck open, and a gene variant that raises the risk of schizophrenia does too (Allen, et al, 2023), so the synapse never gets quiet. My guess is that an armored state jams it the same way, stuck part-way on, so you never come down to a baseline, which from the inside is hyper-vigilance. Nobody has tested that in trauma, so it stays a guess, though the lab already has a drug that shuts these leaking channels.
