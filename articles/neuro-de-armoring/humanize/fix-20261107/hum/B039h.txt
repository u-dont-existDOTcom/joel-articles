D-serine may help therapy rewrite old memories, since it switches on the spot on the NMDA receptor that this kind of learning runs through (Wolosker, et al, 1999), and it may open the GluD channels as well. The drug actually tested alongside exposure therapy, where you face the feared thing in small safe doses, is a different one, D-cycloserine (DCS), working on that same spot, and across 21 trials with over 1,000 patients it gave the therapy a small push (Mataix-Cols, et al, 2017). It doesn't erase fear, it strengthens whatever you learn in the session, which is why how the session ends matters so much: finish with the fear down and a sense of mastery and DCS helps that stick, and probably D-serine too, but finish overwhelmed and it can cement the fear instead (Smits, et al, 2013).
