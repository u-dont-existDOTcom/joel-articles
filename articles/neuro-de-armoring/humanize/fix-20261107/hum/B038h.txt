The Signal Dial (GluD Receptors): So if NMDA is the spark, what sets the level? Well, Johns Hopkins researchers recently found that GluD receptors, which everyone had taken for scaffolding that helps build synapses, are channels in their own right, and what opens them is D-serine (one of the tools below) and GABA, the calming chemical, not glutamate (Wang, et al, 2025). Everything measured in people so far points one way: the channel sticks open and leaks, so the synapse never gets quiet. That's what the mutation behind one inherited ataxia does, and a gene variant that raises the risk of schizophrenia does it too (Allen, et al, 2023). My guess is that an armored state jams the dial the same way, stuck part-way on, which is what hyper-vigilance would be inside a single synapse: a floor the signal can't drop below. Nobody has tested that in trauma, and someone can, because a drug that shuts these leaking channels already works in the lab.
