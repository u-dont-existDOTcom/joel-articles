The Problem: If you're feeling apathetic, gray, no desire for anything, you might be in a neurochemical "Void" state caused by Dynorphin & DeltaFosB. That's because the brain releases dynorphin to suppress dopamine in order to protect from trauma's Glutamate Storm.  And as we know, dopamine is the neurotransmitter for motivation (by showing us what is important to pay attention to). Even though dynorphin clears relatively quickly, allowing us the motivation to learn, DeltaFosB keeps cementing our neural connections, preventing that learning from really "clicking" as durable insights. And because we're still reacting in the same maladaptive ways (same fears, etc), our psychology then asks for more DeltaFosB to keep the armor in place, in a vicious cycle. (Nestler, 2008; Vialou, et al, 2010; Zhong, et al, 2023).
