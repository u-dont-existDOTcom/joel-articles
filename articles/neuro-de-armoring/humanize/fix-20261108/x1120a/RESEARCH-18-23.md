## A. Tabernanthe elliptica

### A1. "Iboga cousin with many iboga alkaloids"
SUPPORTS (weak, indirect)
- Taxonomy: T. elliptica is a recognized sister species. Kew-based monograph (Wageningen e-depot, https://edepot.wur.nl/468098, botanical monograph): "Two species are recognised, T. elliptica and T. iboga"; "hybridisation occurs between the two species." No alkaloid data.
- Wikiwand genus page (https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Tabernanthe, encyclopedia): lists T. elliptica (Congo-Brazzaville, Zaire, Angola) beside T. iboga. No alkaloid data.
AGAINST / GAPS
- PubMed search "Tabernanthe elliptica" returned 0 results (PubMed tool, run today). I found NO phytochemistry paper naming alkaloids in T. elliptica.
- Papers I opened that profile iboga products or plants mention only T. iboga (and Voacanga africana): ibogaine analysis of treatment-provider products, https://www.scielo.br/j/rpc/a/47C7mb4VG5Mff4r7PFvnwwf/?format=html&lang=en (study; found ibogaine, ibogaline, ibogamine, voacangine, iboluteine in T. iboga-derived products); same study summary at https://iceers.org/iboga-alkaloids-treatment-products; PMC6301038 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6301038, review: T. iboga root bark ibogaine 1.93% w/w; mentions only "T. manii" as a pharmaceutical preparation with 8 mg ibogaine per tablet). Springer chapter (https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-49712-9_2) names only T. iboga; full text paywalled.
- A 1985 PubMed paper that surfaced (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4094474) is about Tabernaemontana crassa, a different plant; not relevant.
UNCLEAR: which iboga alkaloids T. elliptica has, and in what amounts. The statement "many iboga alkaloids" is unsupported by any source I opened. (A 2025-era literature may exist behind paywalls or in chemistry journals not indexed by PubMed; I could not open it.)
Also note: Chacruna (https://chacruna.net/?p=104293, article): "not known how many species of the genus Tabernanthe there are, and which ones are distinct species and which ones are varieties." Also T. manii is named as a source by a small share of survey respondents; unclear if it is T. iboga's synonym or a separate taxon.

### A2. "Lower potency than regular iboga"
SUPPORTS
- Only the Substack (https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/iboga-guidance, blog, anonymous byline "u-dont-exist.com", dated Aug 2025, modified Oct 2026): elliptica is "bisexual/healer iboga," about "half the potency"; "they say not to flood dose with it as it causes headache." No source cited for the potency claim.
AGAINST: none directly; no measurement found anywhere.

Note too that even within T. iboga, ibogaine content in root bark is "highly variable" (0.6% to 11.2%, avg 6.2%, n=6) in the product study at the scielo link above, so "half the potency" would be hard to separate from normal variation.

### A3. Names "bisexual" / "healer" iboga
SUPPORTS
- Substack above: "bisexual/healer iboga (Tabernanthe elliptica)." This is the only page I found using the terms.
AGAINST

- Wikipedia T. iboga (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabernanthe_iboga), ICEERS basic info (https://www.iceers.org/iboga-basic-info/), Erowid article (https://www.erowid.org/plants/iboga/iboga_article1.shtml), Psymposia Bwiti interviews (https://psymposia.com/magazine/ibogaine-conversation-2-taking-iboga-with-the-people-of-gabon/), Untamed Science Bwiti healer interview (https://untamedscience.com/?p=8408): none mention elliptica, "bisexual," or "healer" iboga.
UNCLEAR: no vendor page, Bwiti practitioner, or forum found using these names. My searches (vendor and forum phrasing) returned none. I could not open DMT-Nexus or Bluelight iboga threads.

### A5. Heart safety
T. elliptica: no data found (PubMed: 0 hits; no paper, case report, or vendor page opened). State plainly: unknown.
Iboga/ibogaine in general (all opened via PubMed, abstracts read in full):
- Litjens & Brunt 2016, Clin Toxicol, review, https://doi.org/10.3109/15563650.2016.1138226 : hERG blockade "delays this repolarization, resulting in QT... prolongation and... arrhythmias and sudden cardiac arrest"; 27 fatalities reported; 8 case reports of ventricular tachyarrhythmia and QT prolongation "in individuals without any pre-existing cardiovascular condition"; "Noribogaine appears at least as harmful to cardiac functioning as ibogaine." Also notes that iboga extract, root scrapings and ibogaine HCl are all used and "risky."
- Brunt 2026, Addiction, review, https://doi.org/10.1111/add.70319 : "QTc prolongation and potentially fatal ventricular arrhythmias such as Torsades des Pointes... occur with therapeutic doses... and in individuals without pre-existing cardiac conditions"; CYP2D6 variability may raise risk.
- Schep et al. 2016, Drug Alcohol Depend, review, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drugalcdep.2016.07.005 : reports "ventricular arrhythmias and sudden and unexplained deaths."
- Sharma et al. 2026, J Psychopharmacol, scoping review, https://doi.org/10.1177/02698811261443674 : noribogaine 60-180 mg RCT showed "dose-dependent... QTc prolongation"; "Given ibogaine's cardiotoxicity and narrow therapeutic margin, clinical use cannot be recommended" without larger trials.
- ICEERS (https://www.iceers.org/iboga-basic-info/, advocacy/education site): ECG "the absolute minimum test required"; CYP2D6 inhibitors such as quinine and grapefruit worsen heart slowing.
Is the risk from the alkaloids themselves? Yes per these reviews: ibogaine and its metabolite noribogaine block hERG (mechanism in the alkaloid). Whether whole root bark carries the same risk is not settled in the papers I opened; Litjens & Brunt list root scrapings and extracts among risky forms. Substack claim "male iboga ... almost surely more cardiotoxic": no source supports it; it is the author's own guess ("almost surely").
Related (Stanford, Cherian et al. 2024 Nat Med, observational, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02705-w): reports "Ibogaine has been associated with instances of fatal cardiac arrhythmia, but coadministration of magnesium may mitigate this concern"; 30 male veterans; "no unexpected or serious adverse events." Open-label, no control group.

## B. "Male" and "female" iboga

### B1. What the terms mean
- Substack (https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/iboga-guidance, blog): three categories male, female, elliptica, sorted by effect/potency. Author concedes the real distinction is "ethnobotanical phenotype," "not technically the sex," and that it changes the alkaloid profile. Says female was "formerly called T. manii." Says "the Pygmies normally use female trees in ceremonies." No citations.
- No botanical source supports separate male/female plants: monograph (https://edepot.wur.nl/468098) does not use the terms; flowers described with both stamens and pistils; synonyms are listed (T. albiflora, T. pubescens), none "male/female."
- Bwiti sources I opened (Psymposia, https://psymposia.com/magazine/ibogaine-conversation-2-taking-iboga-with-the-people-of-gabon/, interviews): "male" and "female" there refer to initiation cults/rituals (Missoko men's, Mabandji women's), not plant types. Quote: "male initiation is obligatory while female initiation should only be performed if there's a problem." Dose and ritual structure differ by cult; the plant types are not distinguished.
- Untamed Science (https://untamedscience.com/?p=8408, interview): does not distinguish types; says alkaloid content "varies from plant to plant and batch to batch."
- Chacruna (https://chacruna.net/?p=104293) cites "a lack of understanding of the different iboga varieties."
Verdict: the plant-sex meaning is unsupported by botanical sources; vendor-marketing use is plausible but I opened no vendor page using it. Unclear.

### B2. Which is better for what
- Substack (same): male = physical health, hunting, "more stimulating and in your face"; female = "gentle and loving," spiritual use, microdosing; elliptica = clear-headed. It says male for addiction is "not clear at all." It says NOTHING about brain fog or numbness (page does not discuss them).
- So the writer's new claim (male for brain fog, female for numbness) and his old claim (female for numbness/fog, male for helplessness/relapse) have no support in any source I opened; they contradict each other and the Substack's own descriptions (Substack: female = gentle/loving; male = stimulating).
- No vendor, practitioner, or forum page found stating which is better for brain fog or numbness.

### B3. "Male iboga seems closest to what is mainly studied"
- What research used: Cherian 2024 (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02705-w): "magnesium-ibogaine," abstract says ibogaine "plant-derived" but names no species variant or male/female. Full text not opened (PMC page blocked by captcha), so the exact formulation is unconfirmed.
- Product study (scielo link above): products on the market are root bark, total alkaloid (TA), purified TA, and ibogaine HCl; no mention of male/female.
- Sharma 2026 scoping review (https://doi.org/10.1177/02698811261443674): RCTs used ibogaine (1800 mg single dose in one cocaine study) and noribogaine; no male/female distinction.
- No source connects "male" iboga to any research material. The claim is unsupported. Research mainly uses isolated ibogaine HCl or noribogaine, which is not plant-type specific.

## C. Agmatine with psychedelics / MDMA

### C1. "Increases strength of trip/roll while capping euphoria"
SUPPORTS: nothing directly found.
MIXED / AGAINST (forums, anecdotal)
- Bluelight thread (https://www.bluelight.org/community/threads/concerns-experiences-with-agmatine-ashwaghanda-and-mdma-5-mapb.911273/, forum, accessed through a summarizer): one poster took agmatine mid-roll and noticed "a noteworthy drop in intensity," unsure if it was agmatine or the comedown; another reports "absolutely zero influence on my MDMA rolls"; one says it helps "smoothing out any rough edges." Opposite of "increase strength." The OP also says others claim agmatine "may kill/diminish the experience."
- Erowid report ID 105962 (https://taz4.erowid.org/experiences/exp.php?ID=105962): the supplement stack there did NOT include agmatine (Vitamin C, ALA, magnesium, EGCG, ALCAR, melatonin); the poster felt euphoria "weakened" but afterglow strong. Not evidence about agmatine; I report it so it isn't mistaken for support.
UNCLEAR: no PsychonautWiki, DMT-Nexus, Shroomery or LongeCity page opened. Claim not verified.

### C2. Introspective benefits like iboga; agmatine + iboga "go well together"
SUPPORTS (weak)
- Substack: says headache from elliptica is "(which agmatine might fix)." That is speculation about headaches only, not an introspective-effect claim.
- EA Forum comment (https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/8Sed33q54kdhZ4M9m/get-out-of-hell-free-necklace/comment/KM6PGssxAtSd3Nzdq, forum comment): cites Thomas Ray linking agmatine's imidazoline affinity to the "magic of MDMA" and "anecdotal accounts" that agmatine plus 5-HT2A agonists gives "a subtle empathogenic effect." Relevant to MDMA, not iboga.
MECHANISTIC LINK (animal study): MacInnes & Handley 2002, Br J Pharmacol, rat drug discrimination, https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.bjp.0704579 : ibogaine substituted potently for the imidazoline I2 ligand 2-BFI; "Agmatine... substituted at one dose only"; authors conclude "Ibogaine exhibits a commonality in its subjective effects with those of I(2)-site ligands." This is the only agmatine-ibogaine paper I found (PubMed "agmatine ibogaine" returned 1 hit). It shows partial overlap in rats, not benefit in people.
AGAINST: no source reports a human agmatine+iboga combination, good or bad.

### C3. Studies of agmatine with MDMA, psilocybin, LSD, ketamine, ibogaine
- MDMA: PubMed searches "agmatine MDMA" and "agmatine methylenedioxymethamphetamine" returned 0 results. No agmatine-MDMA hyperthermia or neurotoxicity study found. (The MDMA hyperthermia papers I opened, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14726996/ and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18074122/, do not involve agmatine; they show warm environments worsen MDMA hyperthermia in rats.)
- Methamphetamine (closest relative): Thorn et al. 2016, Behav Pharmacol, rats, https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/agmatine-attenuates-the-discriminative-stimulus-and-hyperthermic-/ : "Agmatine attenuates the discriminative stimulus and hyperthermic effects of methamphetamine"; reduced methamphetamine's effect up to ~3.5-fold and blunted ~2 degC temperature rise. Direction: agmatine REDUCES stimulant effects in rats, which is against "increases strength." Not MDMA, not human.
- Ketamine/amphetamine: Kotagale et al. 2012, Pharmacol Biochem Behav, rodents, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pbb.2011.09.006 : agmatine "diminished amphetamine and ketamine hyperlocomotor activity."
- Psilocybin, LSD: no agmatine studies found (broad PubMed query returned unrelated results).
- Ibogaine: only MacInnes & Handley 2002 above.
- Mechanisms (Wikipedia Agmatine, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agmatine, encyclopedia): "blocks NMDA receptors"; binds alpha2-adrenergic and imidazoline sites; both inhibits and activates NOS depending on isoform; page does not mention MDMA, psychedelics or ibogaine.

### C4. Safety concerns raised
- No source I opened raises specific agmatine-psychedelic interaction warnings, and none shows safety. The absence is not evidence of safety.
- Agmatine has mild effects on heart rate and blood pressure per the Wikipedia page; relevant because ibogaine's main risk is cardiac, but no source links the two. Unclear.
- Bluelight posters report no problems, but these are a few anecdotes.
- Forum advice there is that antioxidants taken during a roll risk "nullifying the roll" (G_Chem), a separate point.
- General MDMA hyperthermia risk (rats): rises with ambient heat (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18074122/); human relevance as suggested by authors only.
