## SC-95: The two ketone strategies: what the choice decides, and what the salts do for the fog

*Fixing ATP Signaling if Cortisol Isn't High, P2*

**Now:**

> You must pick one of two ketone strategies, because the salts in one bring their own sodium while the diet in the other drains it. If you're in a crisis and need the fog gone fast, there's Strategy A, the "Crisis Mode". You drink BHB (beta-hydroxybutyrate) salts and keep eating a standard low-sugar diet.

**As it would read:**

> Your ketone strategy decides how you handle salt, since the salts bring their own sodium while the diet drains it. If you're in a crisis and need the fog to lift fast, there's Strategy A, the "Crisis Mode". You drink BHB (beta-hydroxybutyrate) salts and keep eating a standard low-sugar diet. In trials, ketone esters, salts and MCT oil improved thinking a little against a placebo, from a single dose as well as over two weeks or more (Bonnechère, et al, 2026).

**What changes:** Block 95 no longer says you must pick one strategy: it says your strategy decides how you handle salt. "Need the fog gone fast" becomes "need the fog to lift fast", and a sentence gives what trials found ketone supplements do for thinking.

**Why:** Reading the protocol as a whole, block 95 says you must pick one strategy, but the timeline starts you on the salts, moves you to the diet in Weeks 3 to 5, and still has you "Drink BHB" when foggy on the diet. Your post said the choice "determines your electrolyte protocol", and that's what holds: one after the other, the two don't clash, and the salts' sodium on the diet just goes toward what the diet drains. The Oslo PTSD study, which measured ketosis three times a day, gave both together ([Edwards, et al, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39588043/)). On the fog, in a 2026 meta-analysis of 29 trial protocols (1,117 people), ketone esters, salts and MCT oil improved thinking a little against a placebo, about as much after a single dose as over two weeks or more, and more at higher daily doses ([Bonnechère, et al, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42063954/)). Some found nothing, such as one in adults with type 2 diabetes ([Baranowski, et al, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39804761/)) and one in low oxygen ([Stalmans, et al, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40315254/)). So "gone" becomes "lift", with the size the trials found.

**What stays:** Strategy A, the "Crisis Mode", with the salts and a standard low-sugar diet; Strategy B below it; and the timeline as it is, starting on the salts, moving to the diet, the salts when foggy.

**You're deciding:** Whether block 95 says what the choice of strategy decides, instead of that you must pick one, and gives what the salts do for thinking at the size trials found.

**My advice:** Approve. I'm sure of what the trials found. A fresh reader would drop the trials sentence as not needed to choose; I'd keep it, since the promise that the fog lifts is made as science and the reader learns how much to expect. If you're in a crisis and need the fog to lift fast, there's Strategy A, the "Crisis Mode". You drink BHB (beta-hydroxybutyrate) salts and keep eating a standard low-sugar diet."
