## Sources

- [Edwards, et al, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39588043/) (doi 10.3389/fnut.2024.1406366): the Oslo PTSD
  study gave a keto diet and BHB salts together, with blood BHB measured three times a day.
- [Bonnechère, et al, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42063954/) (doi 10.3389/fnut.2026.1802531; abstract, and
  the full text's definitions read in PubMed Central): a meta-analysis of randomized trials of exogenous ketones (esters,
  salts, MCT) on cognition; 38 studies, 29 protocols and 1,117 people in the meta-analysis; SMD 0.29 (95% CI 0.16 to
  0.41) against placebo; no significant difference between a single dose ("Immediate") and use over more than 13 days
  ("Long term"), between formulations, or between healthy people and Alzheimer's disease; higher daily doses went with
  bigger gains.
- [Baranowski, et al, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39804761/) (doi 10.1152/ajpcell.00867.2024): ketone
  monoester, acute and 14 days, in adults with type 2 diabetes: no effect on cognition.
- [Stalmans, et al, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40315254/) (doi 10.1152/japplphysiol.00097.2025): ketone
  ester in acute hypoxia: no effect on cognitive function.
- Also read: a ketone monoester in older men gave a modest gain in attention and reaction time, not memory (PMID
  42263845, 2026); a ketone ester improved working memory within two hours in people with and without metabolic syndrome
  (PMID 40040390, 2025).
