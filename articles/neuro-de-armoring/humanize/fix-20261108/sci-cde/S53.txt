The Fix: If the brain is burning less sugar, we give it another fuel. Ketones are its backup fuel, and brains that take up less glucose (as in early Alzheimer's) still take up ketones normally (Cunnane, et al, 2016). PQQ made mouse cells build more mitochondria, the parts of the cell that burn fuel (Chowanadisai, et al, 2010); whether it does that in the human brain hasn't been shown.
