| block | claim | what the sources show | card |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | "less than 1/24th of the relational density our biology requires" | Perry's own figure is one-sixteenth: four mature people per young child then, one adult per four children now ([his 2023 piece](https://issuu.com/familyjusticegroup/docs/20230629_journal_final/s/27001732)); "1/24th" comes from a training summary of his work (F-4) | SC-24 |
| 47 | Ipser & Stein 2012 "showed that Prozac had zero effect on male combat veterans with calcified PTSD" | The no-better-than-placebo result is [Hertzberg et al. 2000](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/759904): 12 male Vietnam combat veterans, 12 weeks. The [Institute of Medicine (2008)](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/11955/chapter/15) found SSRIs looked ineffective in mostly male veterans with chronic PTSD; later work found veterans recruited outside the VA improved as much as civilians ([Bernardy and Friedman 2015](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/10.1007/s11920-015-0564-2)), and the [2023 VA/DoD guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38408360/) recommends paroxetine, sertraline and venlafaxine. Ipser & Stein 2012 is a review I could not open. Emotional blunting: 46% of people on antidepressants in [Goodwin et al. 2017](https://www.psych.ox.ac.uk/publications/702415) (which also says it can be the depression itself). "Calcified PTSD" isn't a term in the literature (F-5) | SC-47 |
| 48 | SSRIs treat "software" and "cannot penetrate the hardware"; ketones and HDAC inhibitors "remodel the physical structure of the brain" | A year of paroxetine grew the hippocampus 4.6% in PTSD ([Vermetten et al. 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3233762)). BHB inhibits class I HDACs in mice ([Shimazu et al. 2013](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4w889738)); HDAC inhibitors made extinction last in mice that couldn't keep it, and first small human studies point the same way ([Whittle and Singewald 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3961057)). Nothing shows ketones or HDAC inhibitors remodel the amygdala in people | SC-48 |
| 50 | "You cannot 'wait' for this to pass"; shrooms and iboga "stimulate GDNF, which physically rewires the brain" | DeltaFosB's half-life in the brain is up to about 8 days ([Robison and Nestler 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8879420)): it fades, slowly, and his own block 46 says the fears keep making more. Iboga raised GDNF in rats ([He et al. 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1193648)); psilocybin grew spines in mice that lasted a month ([Shao et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34228959/)), not GDNF (F-8). Block 182 says DeltaFosB has "degraded" by week 8: the same point from the other side | SC-50 |
| 52 | "Trauma causes cerebral insulin resistance--your neurons are swimming in glucose but cannot burn it" | Brain insulin resistance is reported after head injury ([PMC6114023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6114023/)), not psychological trauma. In PTSD, FDG-PET finds lower glucose use in the right anterior insula and nearby prefrontal and striatal areas, lower with worse symptoms ([Jeong et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6820768)) (F-9) | SC-52 |
| 53 | PQQ "boosts ATP"; ketones "bypass the broken glucose pathways"; "turns the lights back on immediately"; hot and cold showers "boost p131 and heat shock proteins" | PQQ made mouse liver cells build mitochondria ([Chowanadisai et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2804159)). Brains with low glucose uptake (MCI, early Alzheimer's) take up ketones normally ([Cunnane et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/molecular-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnmol.2016.00053/text)). Nothing shows "immediately". PI31 was raised by gene therapy in mice; nothing tests showers; heat-shock protein studies keep people in hot water 40 to 60 minutes (F-10, sources in FACTS.md). The shower routine in Part 3 goes to the coherence read | SC-53 |
| 56 | Tetris after a flashback: "The memory is restored, but the emotional 'sting' is permanently reduced" | Reminder plus about 20 minutes of Tetris within 6 hours of a car crash: fewer intrusive memories over the next week, not on other symptoms or at one month ([Iyadurai et al. 2018](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/63100)); 62% fewer ([press release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/641091)). Reminder plus Tetris a day later cut intrusions; neither alone did ([James et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26133572/)). What drops is how often it intrudes; "permanently" isn't shown | SC-56 |
| 57 | "Color-Path Connect" "is more effective than simple counting" | No study tests it. A verbal quiz game after a trauma film increased flashbacks; Tetris cut them ([Holmes et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21085661/)) | SC-57 |
| 61 | 10 to 20 minutes right after a trigger | Matches the studies (about 12 to 20 minutes, after a reminder) | none |
