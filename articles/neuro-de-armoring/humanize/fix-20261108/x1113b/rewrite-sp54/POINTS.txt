- Ketones are the brain's backup fuel, and brains that take up less glucose, as in early Alzheimer's, still take up ketones normally (Cunnane, et al, 2016).
- One man's Alzheimer's improved markedly on coconut oil, and later on a ketone drink (Newport, et al, 2015). Keep this sentence as it is if you can: "And one man's Alzheimer's improved markedly on coconut oil, and later on a ketone drink (Newport, et al, 2015)."
- In Parkinson's, a small study with no comparison group (five people, a month on a ketogenic diet) showed large improvement (VanItallie, et al, 2005).
- A bigger randomized trial against a low-fat diet: keto eased fatigue, daytime sleepiness and thinking problems more; movement improved about the same on both (Phillips, et al, 2018).
- PQQ made mouse cells build more mitochondria, the parts of a cell that burn fuel (Chowanadisai, et al, 2010); this hasn't been shown in the human brain.
