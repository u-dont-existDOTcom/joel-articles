The Fix: If the brain is burning less sugar, we give it another fuel. Ketones are its backup fuel, and brains that take up less glucose (as in early Alzheimer's) still take up ketones normally (Cunnane, et al, 2016). And one man's Alzheimer's improved markedly on coconut oil, and later on a ketone drink (Newport, et al, 2015). In Parkinson's too, one small study with no comparison group showed large improvement on a ketogenic diet (VanItallie, et al, 2005). In a bigger trial against a low-fat diet, the keto diet eased fatigue, sleepiness and thinking problems more, while movement improved about the same on both (Phillips, et al, 2018). PQQ made mouse cells build more mitochondria, the parts of the cell that burn fuel (Chowanadisai, et al, 2010); whether it does that in the human brain hasn't been shown.
