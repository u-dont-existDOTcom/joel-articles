## SC-DMSOc: DMSO: your two versions as one

*New item after 2. The Glutamate Shield (Anxiety):, P9*

**As it would read:**

> DMSO (99.9%): Smell it, as in the Fog table below. In rat brain cells in a dish, DMSO at 0.5 to 1.5% quickly turned down the signals glutamate sends through its NMDA and AMPA receptors, and the calcium they let in. It also kept glutamate from killing the cells (Lu & Mattson, 2001). Back in 1967, doctors in Lima injected it into the muscles of 42 psychiatric inpatients, and nine of them had severe anxiety or obsessive-compulsive disorder. Within days they grew calmer, and the acute cases went home sooner than 16 patients with similar diagnoses who got the usual treatments of the time, such as electroshock, insulin comas and tranquilizers (Ramírez & Luza, 1967). There was no placebo, though, and the patients they were compared with weren't picked at random.
>
> It is used for humans routinely in stem cell transplants and by many humans for all kinds of medical problems, from arthritis to brain injuries (Jacob & de la Torre, 2015). There is evidence (Mitrus, et al, 2018) that using too much DMSO (10% vs 5%) in stem cell transfusions increases mild side effects in adult humans. It hasn't been tested (that I know of, yet) specifically for PTSD, so for PTSD it rests on what people report, and many say it helps.
>
> Nebulized DMSO with heparin (a blood thinner) has been shown to cut the lung damage from toxic cotton smoke in sheep (Kimura, et al, 1988). In another study, the smoke killed all seven untreated sheep and four of the six given DMSO alone, while all seven given DMSO with heparin survived (Brown, et al, 1988). But it has shown some neurotoxic effects in rodent studies. In mice in their first month of life, even the smallest dose tested set off brain cell death, and the authors worried about the children who get it in transplants (Hanslick, et al, 2009). Translating mouse research to humans isn't straightforward.
>
> My experience and that of those I know and have read about, is that it has immense brain benefits, turning bad trips good instantly, giving relaxed alertness, more intelligence, brighter mood (especially when nebulized). When I nebulize it, within a minute, my brain feels crystal clean and super blissed out (much more bliss than other routes). But when used too close to bedtime or too much, it can reduce sleep, so it's best taken in the morning, and reduce the dose if you notice you're sleeping less. If it is killing my neurons, I'm pretty sure it's killing the trash ones that should be going out anyway. My dad used DMSO for decades and was healthy and mentally sharp into his old age.
