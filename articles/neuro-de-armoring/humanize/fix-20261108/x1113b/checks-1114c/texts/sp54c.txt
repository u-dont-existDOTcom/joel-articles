The Fix: If your brain is burning less sugar, give it another fuel. Ketones are the brain's backup fuel, and in early Alzheimer's, brains that take up less glucose still take up ketones normally (Cunnane, et al, 2016). And one man's Alzheimer's improved markedly on coconut oil, and later on a ketone drink (Newport, et al, 2015). An early Parkinson's study had only five people and no comparison group, but they improved a lot after a month on a ketogenic diet (VanItallie, et al, 2005). In a bigger randomized Parkinson's trial, people on keto got more relief from fatigue and daytime sleepiness than those on a low-fat diet. Their thinking problems eased more too. Movement improved about the same on both (Phillips, et al, 2018). PQQ made mouse cells build more mitochondria, the parts of a cell that burn fuel (Chowanadisai, et al, 2010), but that hasn't been shown in the human brain.
