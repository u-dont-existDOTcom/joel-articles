The Fix: What do you do if your brain is burning less sugar? You give it ketones, its backup fuel, since even brains that take up less glucose, like in early Alzheimer's, still take up ketones normally (Cunnane, et al, 2016). And one man's Alzheimer's improved markedly on coconut oil, and later on a ketone drink (Newport, et al, 2015). VanItallie, et al (2005) saw something like it in Parkinson's, where five people improved a lot after a month on a ketogenic diet, although no one was compared with them. Then a bigger trial put keto up against a low-fat diet, and the people on keto got more relief from their tiredness and thinking problems, while their movement improved about the same on both (Phillips, et al, 2018). PQQ is meant to help with the burning: in mouse cells, it built more mitochondria, the part of a cell that does the burning (Chowanadisai, et al, 2010), but I haven't seen a study of that in a human brain yet.
