NEEDS:
S1: To know in advance that feeling naked and exposed is an expected part of de-armoring, so when it comes they read it as the process working and don't stop or re-armor. The implied act (expect it, keep going) is not said.
S2: To recognise the specific thought they will have ("everyone can see I'm anxious") as a predicted thought and not a fact. The implied act: don't act on it (don't hide, leave, or apologise for looking anxious). It is also not said.
S3: This is the only sentence that carries the second half of what the paragraph is for, that others can't actually see their anxiety, and it carries it only through the one word "Illusion". The reader needs the plain statement (people see far less of what you feel than you think they do), which the sentence never makes. The name, the author and the book title are not what the reader needs; the fact behind them is.

UNNEEDED:
- none as whole sentences. But see S3: as written it gives the reader a label and a source, and leaves them to work out from the word "Illusion" that their assumption is wrong. A reader who doesn't unpack the term finishes the paragraph knowing they will feel seen and not knowing that they aren't. If the plain statement were added, the name and attribution could go with nothing missing.

NOTES FOR THE WRITER:
- The paragraph does the first half of its job (you will feel exposed) twice, in S1 and S2, and the second half (others can't see it) zero times in plain words. The missing sentence is the one that says so directly.
- S3 sense: "Dr. Nicholas Epley, Mindwise, calls this" reads as a citation dropped into a sentence; a reader who doesn't know Mindwise is a book can't parse the middle. "calls this" also reads as if Epley named it. From memory, not checked here (the brief said no searching): the term comes from Gilovich, Savitsky and Medvec (1998), and Epley's book discusses it. Worth checking before it stays as "calls".
- "this" in S3 points at S2's assumption, which is right, but S1's feeling of nakedness sits between the reader and that referent only loosely; once the plain statement is added the pointer is no longer a problem.
