NEEDS:
S1: To know that being depressed is not enough by itself to make 5-HTP a fit, so they go on to check the pattern before trying it. (The label "Who this is for:" is followed by who it is not for, which reads oddly, and "either" only makes sense if the section before said the same about another supplement.)
S2: The first half of the pattern to check themselves against: trouble falling asleep, and low mood with little warmth in it. This is the main thing the reader decides with.
S3: The second half of the pattern: anxiety at night and going over the same things. Needed, but it reads as a flat statement about the reader ("At night you get anxious") and not as part of the pattern in S2. The reader has to guess it is one more sign to check for.
S4: A second test of fit: is there a genetic or clinical reason to suspect a serotonin-synthesis bottleneck. The act it implies is to look at their own genetic data or clinical history for one (TPH2 is the example to look for). Scott shows what such a reason looks like and that it went with a response, so the clause earns its place, though "TPH2" is never explained. "It makes more sense" has no clear "it" after S3 (it means: trying 5-HTP).
S5: Two things. Why the bottleneck matters to fit (5-HTP comes after it, so the body can still make serotonin). And what to expect: a responder feels it quickly, and the effect on sleep and mood is modest. The act it implies and doesn't say: if you feel nothing after a short trial, you are probably not in this pattern, so stop and don't keep raising the dose. The sentence carries three points at once (mechanism, speed, hype) and the reader can lose the thread by the bracket.
S6: The first "who should not try it casually": people who are agitated or manic. The act it implies: don't try it on your own in that state (or with a history of mania), and ask a prescriber first. "Push that further" is clear enough.
S7: The second "who should not": people on serotonergic medication or using MDMA-type drugs. Needed, but "watch out for" doesn't say what to do. The act it implies: don't combine them, and check with a prescriber before adding 5-HTP. "Serotonin syndrome", "serotonergic meds" and "entactogens" are not explained, so a reader on an SSRI may not know the sentence is about them.
