NEEDS:
S1: Tells the reader what to drop: the idea that healing comes in an hour-long breakthrough. It sets up the contrast the paragraph is for. Two problems for the writer: the label "The Anti-Breakthrough (Micro-Dosing)" runs straight into "As Dr. Perry explains" with no break, so it reads as one garbled sentence; and "Micro-Dosing" is never explained (small doses of what? the reader may think of drugs). "Temporarily" implies an act the sentence doesn't say: until when, or until what changes, does the reader set the hour-long ideal aside?
S2: Gives the first half of the why: an hour of trauma work is more than this nervous system can take in, so it overwhelms instead of helping. The reader needs this point, but not in these words (see below). "Sensitized" and "armored" are used as if the reader already knows them.
S3: Gives the second half of the why, and the number the whole paragraph rests on: the stretch in which a dysregulated brain can take the work in is often only seconds long. The act it implies and doesn't say: so each dose of the work should last seconds, then stop. "Therapeutic window" and "dysregulated" need a plain gloss in the sentence, and "Dr. Perry's data" doesn't say what data.

UNNEEDED:
- S2: A sensitized, armored nervous system cannot metabolize sixty minutes of trauma processing; that is a flood, not a fix. — Listed for sense, not for content: the point is needed, the wording isn't. It mixes pictures for one thing: the nervous system wears armor, digests ("metabolize") an hour of "processing", and is then flooded. "Metabolize sixty minutes of trauma processing" doesn't make sense word by word (one doesn't digest minutes, or processing). "A flood, not a fix" is a slogan that tells the reader nothing the first half hadn't. The reader loses nothing if it is replaced by one plain statement: an hour of trauma work is more than a nervous system in this state can take, so it overwhelms instead of helping.

No sentence can go outright: with any of the three gone, the reader is missing either what to give up (S1), why an hour is too much (S2), or why seconds (S3). What is missing from the paragraph is the step from S3 to the protocol: it never says that the protocol therefore works in doses of a few seconds.
