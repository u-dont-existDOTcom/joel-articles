The Fix: If your brain is burning less sugar, give it ketones, which it can consume instead. Ketones work even when the brain isn't able to burn glucose efficiently, as in early Alzheimer's (Cunnane, et al, 2016). And one man's Alzheimer's improved markedly on coconut oil, and later on a ketone drink (Newport, et al, 2015). We've also got an early Parkinson's study with 5 people (no control group) who improved a lot after a month on a ketogenic diet (VanItallie, et al, 2005). In a bigger randomized Parkinson's trial, people on keto got more relief from fatigue, thinking difficulty, and daytime sleepiness than those on a low-fat diet. Movement improved about the same on both (Phillips, et al, 2018). And as for PQQ, it made mouse cells build more mitochondria, the parts of a cell that burn fuel (Chowanadisai, et al, 2010), but that hasn't been shown in the human brain.
