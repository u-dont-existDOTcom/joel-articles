The Mechanism: When glutamate floods into the neuron, it jams open the NMDA "doors" (receptors), which allows calcium to flood in. Calcium at such high levels caused by stress can injure the neurons. Possibly as a protective mechanism, those neurons have been observed (in rats) to pull back their complex interactions with other neurons for up to two weeks after a stressful event (Bonifacino, et al, 2023). It's theorized that chronic stress on neurons also makes them more vulnerable to other toxic insults (Conrad, 2008), which is kind of common sense. What does all that feel like? Although popular neuroscience doesn't communicate this well, neurotransmitters affect different brain regions differently. Under chronic stress, glutamate rises in the part of the amygdala that flags threats (Wilson, 2015), producing anxiety, rage, all that good stuff. Block the NMDA receptors there, and anxious behaviour drops in animals (Boyle, 2013).
