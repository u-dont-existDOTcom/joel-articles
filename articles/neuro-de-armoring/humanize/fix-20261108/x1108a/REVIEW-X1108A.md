## SC-37: Calcium injures; it isn't what swells the cell (A claim corrected)

*B. The "Overheating" CPU: Glutamate Excitotoxicity, P3*

**Now:**

> The Mechanism: When glutamate floods into the neuron, it jams open the NMDA "doors" (receptors), which allows calcium to flood in. Calcium at such high levels caused by stress can swell and injure the neurons. Possibly as a protective mechanism, those neurons have been observed (in rats) to pull back their complex interactions with other neurons for up to two weeks after a stressful event (Bonifacino, et al, 2023). It's theorized that chronic stress on neurons also makes them more vulnerable to other toxic insults (Conrad, 2008), which is kind of common sense. What does all that feel like? Although popular neuroscience doesn't communicate this well, neurotransmitters affect different brain regions differently. Under chronic stress, glutamate rises in the part of the amygdala that flags threats (Wilson, 2015), producing anxiety, rage, all that good stuff. Block the NMDA receptors there, and anxious behaviour drops in animals (Boyle, 2013).

**As it would read:**

> The Mechanism: When glutamate floods into the neuron, it jams open the NMDA "doors" (receptors), which allows calcium to flood in. Calcium at such high levels caused by stress can injure the neurons. Possibly as a protective mechanism, those neurons have been observed (in rats) to pull back their complex interactions with other neurons for up to two weeks after a stressful event (Bonifacino, et al, 2023). It's theorized that chronic stress on neurons also makes them more vulnerable to other toxic insults (Conrad, 2008), which is kind of common sense. What does all that feel like? Under chronic stress, glutamate rises in the part of the amygdala that flags threats (Wilson, 2015), producing anxiety, rage, all that good stuff. Block the NMDA receptors there, and anxious behaviour drops in animals (Boyle, 2013).

**What changes:** Two words out: "can swell and injure the neurons" becomes "can injure the neurons". Nothing else in your paragraph changes.

**Why:** The swelling seen when glutamate poisons neurons comes from sodium, chloride and water rushing in, within minutes; calcium is the slower part that does the killing, hours later ([Choi, 1987](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6568907); [Goldberg and Choi, 1993](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6576549)). Both are cultured neurons at toxic doses, nothing like stress levels, so "swell" says more than anything shows for stress, and it pins it on the wrong ion. Your version read [Human, High 137 and Medium 13](https://www.pangram.com/history/3630663f-3104-4ce7-86f7-462cbb26601d); with the two words out it reads [Human, High all 148 words](https://www.pangram.com/history/755ca0fd-b05f-4092-95b2-6e9da5daa36f).

**What stays:** Everything else as you wrote it: the jammed doors, Bonifacino, Conrad and "kind of common sense", the amygdala and Boyle.

**You're deciding:** Whether "swell" goes.

**My advice:** Approve.

## SC-38: NMDA is the main calcium door; AMPA is the spark (A claim corrected)

*B. The "Overheating" CPU: Glutamate Excitotoxicity, P4*

**Now:**

> The Secondary Signal Dial (GluD Receptors): While NMDA receptor opening is the main spark that starts up neuronal excitation, another calcium door was recently discovered in the GluD receptors. What opens them is D-serine (one of the tools below) and GABA, the neurotransmitter that usually quiets down communication, not glutamate (Wang, et al, 2025).

**As it would read:**

> The Secondary Signal Dial (GluD Receptors): While NMDA receptors are the main door calcium comes in through when glutamate fires, another door was recently discovered in the GluD receptors. What opens them is D-serine (one of the tools below) and GABA, the neurotransmitter that usually quiets down communication, not glutamate (Wang, et al, 2025).

**What changes:** Your first clause changes: "While NMDA receptor opening is the main spark that starts up neuronal excitation, another calcium door was recently discovered" becomes "While NMDA receptors are the main door calcium comes in through when glutamate fires, another door was recently discovered". Your second sentence stays word for word.

**Why:** Two things in the clause don't hold. The spark is the AMPA receptors: magnesium holds the NMDA door shut at rest, and only the jolt from AMPA frees it, after which NMDA is the main way calcium gets in ([Hansen, et al, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6080888)). That is the same magnesium plug the Lock paragraph is about, so "main spark" would contradict the guide a few lines down. And the GluD channel lets very little calcium through ([Schmid, et al, 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2700928)), so it is another door, not another calcium door; what makes it matter here is what opens it, D-serine and GABA ([Wang, et al, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12520249/)), which your second sentence says. Your version read [Human, High](https://www.pangram.com/history/e798ace7-cc92-45ff-973e-ceaf1f5ed2c4); the corrected one reads [Human, High](https://www.pangram.com/history/e3845c44-7bf0-4e7d-93a8-706b596c9c4b) too.

**What stays:** Your label, "recently discovered in the GluD receptors", and your whole second sentence.

**You're deciding:** Whether the first clause names NMDA as the calcium door rather than the spark, and drops "calcium" from GluD.

**My advice:** Approve.
