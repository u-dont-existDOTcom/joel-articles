## What holds

- **Stress injures neurons without killing them, and the sentence "Calcium at such high levels caused by stress can
  injure the neurons" is backed.** He is right, and I never meant to dispute it; the card's why read as if I did.
  - One footshock shortened and simplified prefrontal dendrites, measurable at 24 hours and lasting up to two weeks
    ([Bonifacino et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9938874)).
  - Chronic restraint stress retracts prefrontal dendrites; blocking NMDA receptors during the stress changes that
    remodeling ([Martin and Wellman 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3697127)); a review counts blockade of
    glucocorticoid, NMDA or D1 receptors as preventing it ([Wellman et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9483990)).
  - CRH, a stress hormone, at stress levels destroys dendritic spines through NMDA receptors and the calcium-activated
    enzyme calpain ([Andres et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3807024)).
- **The definition.** He is right here too: "excitotoxic" injury in the literature includes sublethal injury (beaded
  swelling of dendrites, spine loss) that the cell survives and recovers from
  ([Faddis et al. 1997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6573163); [Hasbani et al. 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6762381)).
  My notes on block 37 had used the narrow cell-death sense.

## Why "swell" still goes (he approved the cut)

- The swelling in excitotoxic injury is the beads along the dendrites, and it comes from sodium, chloride and water
  rushing in; it forms just as well in calcium-free solution (Hasbani et al. 1998, as
  [Ikegaya et al. 2001](https://cob.silverchair.com/jcs/article-pdf/114/22/4083/1466384/4083.pdf) report it). Calcium is
  what makes the damage last. So "calcium ... can swell" pins the swelling on the wrong ion.
- No stress study I found reports swelling; they report retraction and spine loss.
- My card's other reason, "cultured neurons at toxic doses, nothing like stress levels", was wrong as stated: the beads
  appear after brief sublethal doses too. It is struck here.
