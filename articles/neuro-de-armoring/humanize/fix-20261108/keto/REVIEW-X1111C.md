**As it would read:**

> Why don't these drugs work on calcified PTSD? Because even if they did work for mood by modulating serotonin transmission, PTSD is not a mood disorder. That's a bit like putting some Bengay on a broken leg. This protocol tries to get at the underpinnings of the problem. The ketone BHB blocks enzymes called HDACs (Shimazu, et al, 2013), and in mice that couldn't hold on to extinction learning (learning that a cue is now safe), an HDAC blocker made the new learning last (Whittle & Singewald, 2014). In people with PTSD, the first small study of a keto diet with BHB supplements found that both people who finished its four weeks improved, by 20 and 10 points on the standard 80-point PTSD checklist, which its authors call clinically meaningful (Edwards, et al, 2024). In two case reports, women whose PTSD hadn't responded to treatment were nearly or fully free of symptoms after months on the diet (Bellamy & Laurent, 2025; Laurent, 2026). No controlled trial of the diet has been done yet, and the one placebo-controlled trial of BHB supplements alone was too small to tell either way (Youssef, et al, 2022).

**Why:** You're right. My sentence was true of the HDAC route, but it reads as if ketosis had never been tried in people with PTSD, and it has. GPT's sources check out. In Oslo, of three people who started four weeks of a keto diet with BHB salts, the two who finished went from 70 to 50 and from 50 to 40 on the PCL-5, the standard 80-point PTSD checklist, which its authors call a reliable and clinically meaningful improvement ([Edwards, et al, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39588043/)). A woman whose PTSD three years of trauma-focused therapy hadn't notably improved went from 80, the checklist's maximum, to 0 by week 12 ([Bellamy & Laurent, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40626225/)), and a woman whose PTSD therapy and drugs hadn't helped went from 32 to 2 at 27 weeks ([Laurent, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41783812/)). This year's review of all of it finds only minimal reliable information from human studies so far ([Frontiers in Psychiatry, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42780737/)), and the studies registered have no comparison group: [a six-person one in Munich](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07415590) finished in August with no results posted yet. In chat I also said the one placebo-controlled trial of ketone salts ended with the two groups no different ([Youssef, et al, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36282607/)). That made a trial too small to tell sound like a negative. Its ketone group's scores fell (a median of 58.5 to 54) while the placebo group's didn't move, but with 21 people the difference between the groups wasn't significant, which its authors put down to the small size.
