Why don't these drugs work on calcified PTSD? Because even if they did work for mood by modulating serotonin transmission, PTSD is not a mood disorder. That's a bit like putting some Bengay on a broken leg. This protocol tries to get at the underpinnings of the problem. The ketone BHB blocks enzymes called HDACs ([Shimazu, et al, 2013](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4w889738)), and in mice that couldn't hold on to extinction learning (learning that a cue is now safe), an HDAC blocker made the new learning last ([Whittle & Singewald, 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3961057)). In people with PTSD, the first small study of a keto diet with BHB supplements found that both people who finished its four weeks improved, by 20 and 10 points on the standard 80-point PTSD checklist, which its authors call clinically meaningful ([Edwards, et al, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39588043/)). In two case reports, women whose PTSD hadn't responded to treatment were nearly or fully free of symptoms after months on the diet ([Bellamy & Laurent, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40626225/); [Laurent, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41783812/)). No controlled trial of the diet has been done yet, and the one placebo-controlled trial of BHB supplements alone was too small to tell either way ([Youssef, et al, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36282607/)).
