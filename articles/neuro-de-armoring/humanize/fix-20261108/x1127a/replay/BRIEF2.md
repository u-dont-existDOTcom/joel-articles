# Blind rewrite 2: the herbs warning, from lesson h with its limit (lesson m)

The second blind run, after the first ([RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md)) met rule 1 by saying the first sentence again. A fresh
Fable writer, told to open no file, got this brief: rule 1 restated with its limit (the half after "but" says something
new, never an earlier sentence again; the example from another article), rule 2 (one clarifying word), and my last try.

The brief's text, as given:

> 1. A "but" answers what it turns from, with something new. After a concession ("X is rare", "X is safe for most
> people"), the half after "but" says what it changes about the first half: what the comparison or the fact it brings in
> shows about it. It never says the first half, or an earlier sentence, again in other words. (From another article:
> after a sentence on magnesium and weak kidneys, "But they should be far more worried about the MDMA." stood alone; his
> "But they should be far more worried about taking the MDMA which depletes it" says what the MDMA has to do with the
> magnesium.)
> 2. Where a phrase leaves open something a reader could get wrong, add the one word that settles it. (From another
> article: "the evidence for it" became "the evidence for this med".)

The passage: my last try (W-LMA-his#299), with the same framing and the same words to keep as BRIEF.md.

The writer's rewrite:

P1:
I'm not advising lion's mane or ashwagandha, even though they're popular herbs for focus and stress, because some people report anhedonia (no joy or desire) and sexual dysfunction long after they stop taking them. Post-ashwa and post-lion's mane syndrome are rare, but it's the same kind of thing SSRIs and finasteride can do, so I believe the reports. If you're already on either herb, I'd quit it as soon as you notice any of those symptoms.
