# Blind rewrite: two warning paragraphs, from the lessons alone

You are rewriting two short paragraphs of a self-help article by Joel, a Substack writer (informal, first person, plain
words; "I" is Joel). The article is a protocol for adults with trauma that uses supplements, and these two paragraphs
sit among the warnings at its top. Each paragraph below is a draft that reads like a model wrote it. Rewrite each one
the way the lessons say. Keep every fact and every stop sign; add no fact, no example and no detail of your own; keep
the label that opens the first paragraph ("Lion's Mane & Ashwagandha:"). Two hyphens "--" are his dash, but see lesson 5.

## The lessons (from his fixes of other paragraphs)

1. **No sentence stands alone as an island.** A short sentence that only states a stance, only names something, or only
   adds one fact is joined to the sentence that gives its reason or uses it. A stance goes in one sentence with what it
   is held against and why. A sentence whose only job is to introduce a name is folded into the next point, with the
   name as that point's subject. (From another article: "Of course everyone does change over time as well. If our
   libidos later diverge, it's better to talk about what we'd do before either person is already hurt." became "Of
   course everyone does change over time as well, so that's one more reason to think about the relationship together
   before anyone gets their feelings hurt.")
2. **Say what a thing is in one plain word where it is first named** ("the ketone BHB", "the amino acid cystine"), so
   the reader can place it.
3. **One idea in one place.** A warning and the reason not to ignore it sit together, right after the thing they are
   about; the other items come after. An idea isn't split across the paragraph with other items between its halves.
4. **Say each thing once.** A state described two or three ways in a row is said once, the clearest way.
5. **Where a claim comes from is said plainly inside the sentence** (who reports it), with a caveat in brackets if it
   needs one. No aside set off by dashes inside a sentence that already carries its point.
6. **A warning is his own stance with its reason** ("I wouldn't ..., because ..."), not an order with a word of regret
   in front of it.
7. **Make the points fast.** A gloss is two or three words; no filler adverbs.

## Paragraph 1 (a new item among the warnings; it comes before the agmatine warning)

Lion's Mane & Ashwagandha: I'm not advising either of these. They're popular for focus and stress, but unfortunately some people report anhedonia (when nothing feels good anymore) and sexual dysfunction that hangs around even after they stop taking them--people call it post-ashwa or post-lion's mane syndrome. It's rare, but it's the same kind of thing SSRIs and finasteride can do, so it's something to keep in mind.

## Paragraph 2 (agmatine's stop signs, inside the agmatine warning)

Agmatine can leave you flat and numb--people do get that in practice, even if the human trials only turned up stomach problems--so stop it right away if you notice you're flatter or more depressed than before you started, things stop feeling good, or you just stop caring. Same if it winds you up instead (more irritable, can't sleep), or you get dizziness, palpitations, nausea, loose stools or stronger cravings. Unfortunately a few people say the numbness hung on for weeks after they quit, so don't push through it hoping it'll settle like side effects sometimes do.
