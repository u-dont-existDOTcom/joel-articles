## SC-DMSOb: DMSO in the Glutamate Shield, with your 1967 paper

*New item after 2. The Glutamate Shield (Anxiety):, P9*

**As it would read:**

> DMSO (99.9%): Smell it, as in the Fog table below. In rat brain cells in a dish, DMSO at 0.5 to 1.5% quickly turned down the signals glutamate sends through its NMDA and AMPA receptors, and the calcium they let in. It also kept glutamate from killing the cells (Lu & Mattson, 2001). Back in 1967, doctors in Lima injected it into the muscles of 42 psychiatric inpatients, and nine of them had severe anxiety or obsessive-compulsive disorder. Within days they grew calmer, and the acute cases went home sooner than 16 patients with similar diagnoses who got the usual treatments of the time, such as electroshock, insulin comas and tranquilizers (Ramírez & Luza, 1967). There was no placebo, though, and the patients they were compared with weren't picked at random. In a second dish study, DMSO at 0.5% and 1% killed rat brain cells by itself, and injections of it set off widespread cell death in young mice's brains (Hanslick, et al, 2009). So if you're pregnant, don't use it, and keep it away from children. Nobody has measured how much reaches your brain when you smell it. And no trial has tested it for PTSD, so for PTSD it rests on what people report, and many say it helps.
