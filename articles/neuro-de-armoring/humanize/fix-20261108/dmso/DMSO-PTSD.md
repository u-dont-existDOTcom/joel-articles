## His study, checked

- [Lu & Mattson, 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11421595/) (Exp Neurol; doi 10.1006/exnr.2001.7686; abstract):
  in cultured rat hippocampal neurons, DMSO at 0.5 to 1.5% (the strengths "to which neurons are typically exposed in
  experimental studies and in human patients") suppressed, "in a rapidly reversible manner", the currents and calcium
  influx set off by glutamate, NMDA and AMPA, and prevented excitotoxic death. It says what he says it does, in a dish.

## The other side

- [Hanslick, et al, 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19100327/) (Neurobiol Dis; doi 10.1016/j.nbd.2008.11.006):
  DMSO injected into mice from birth to 30 days old set off widespread cell death in the brain, at doses from 0.3 ml/kg;
  in cultured rat hippocampal neurons, DMSO at 0.5% and 1.0% killed neurons on its own.
- Rats exposed as infants (PMID 33838015, 2021): lasting changes in behavior and glia; cultured astrocytes (PMID
  25238609, 2014): mitochondria damaged; tau hyperphosphorylation in cells and, through hypothermia, in mice (PMID
  22768202, 2012).
- In people, given into a vein with frozen stem cells: rare seizures, strokes and encephalopathy (PMID 17353475, 2007;
  PMID 28121745, 2017).

## In people, other uses

- Its approved use is in the bladder, for interstitial cystitis; on the skin it gives quick, short pain relief; side
  effects are common but mostly mild and grow with strength (skin rash and itch, stomach upset by mouth, red cells
  breaking down when given into a vein); it carries other chemicals through the skin; its safety with other drugs isn't
  established ([Swanson, 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3916302/)).
- Into a vein after head injury, small uncontrolled series lowered pressure in the skull; that alone doesn't show lasting
  benefit ([Huang & Wang, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42939788/), a review); one series of six found it hard
  to give safely (fluid and salt problems) and cautioned against it (PMID 6462399, 1984).
- How much reaches the brain when it is smelled: no study found.

## His 1967 paper (sent 2026-11-13, 14:05 UTC), read in full

Ramírez E, Luza S. "Dimethyl sulfoxide in the treatment of mental patients." Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences
141 (1967): 655-667, doi 10.1111/j.1749-6632.1967.tb34937.x (his PDF, 13 pages; the PDF stays out of Git, as a
publisher's copy). My search above had said no human study of DMSO for anxiety turned up: it had searched "psychiatric",
and a 1967 title says "mental patients".

- **Who**: 42 inpatients in Lima: 25 with schizophrenia (14 acute, 11 chronic), four in mania, four with alcoholic
  psychoses, four with obsessive-compulsive neurosis and five with severe anxiety states.
- **What**: pharmaceutical-grade DMSO at 50% and 80% in saline, 5 ml vials, injected into the muscle; most began on one
  80% vial two or three times a day, up to five a day in the most disturbed, and one or two a day for the anxiety
  patients; agitated patients also had barbiturates in the first days. Earlier treatments were stopped at least a week
  before in the chronic patients.
- **Control**: "clinical control tests with placebo or with the blind and double-blind methods proved to be impossible"
  (DMSO's smell); the hospital stays of the 31 acute cases were compared with 16 acute patients with similar diagnoses on
  the usual treatment of the time (insulin coma, electroshock, phenothiazines, minor tranquilizers, barbiturates), not
  assigned at random.
- **Found**: agitation eased "from the very first few doses"; in the anxiety states, one or two vials a day "caused
  decrease in the anxiety symptoms", most of all in the obsessive-compulsive patients ("Patients said that they felt
  calm"); the acute cases' hospital stays were shorter than the comparison group's (the figure: about 15 against about 30
  days for the anxiety states); the effect showed "between the first and seventh days". Tolerated well: pain or heat at
  the injection site with the 80% vials, insomnia early on, some thirst, sweating and passing more water; laboratory
  tests normal.
- **Not**: smelled; not PTSD; no placebo, no blinding, no random assignment.
