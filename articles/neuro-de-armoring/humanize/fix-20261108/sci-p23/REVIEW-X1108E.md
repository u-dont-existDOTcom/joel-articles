> The Science: Your neurons have "brakes" called KCNQ channels that stop them from firing uncontrollably. These brakes run on Potassium ions. If you are low on potassium (common in high stress), your brakes fail. You can push the pedal (breathe), but the car won't stop.

**As it would read:**

> The Science: Eating more potassium brings high blood pressure down (Aburto, et al, 2013), which matters on Strategy A, since its salts bring their own sodium. If you have kidney disease or take a blood pressure drug that holds on to potassium, like an ACE inhibitor or an ARB, ask your doctor first, since potassium can build up in your blood (NIH, 2022).

On the mood trial you're right, and my card said more than the study found: I had read only its summary. In full, both test diets raised potassium about equally; the low-salt one lifted mood a little more, while the DASH-type diet didn't change overall mood significantly; more potassium in the urine went with less fatigue; and the authors name magnesium as another explanation, so potassium's own part in mood is open, not ruled out ([Torres, et al, 2008](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/dietary-electrolytes-are-related-to-mood/261729C7B3C7D3D2D8D5E1CDF74932FF); lesson neuro-2026-11-08k).
