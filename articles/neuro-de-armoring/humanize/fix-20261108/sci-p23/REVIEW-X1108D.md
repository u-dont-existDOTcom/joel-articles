As on the first card, "This saturates your cells, lowering your resting voltage ..." goes from block 115.

**Why:** GPT is right, and each fact it gives holds when checked: the study my card leaned on measured the arm and leg nerves of one patient whose blood potassium had fallen to 2.1 ([Kuwabara, et al, 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12210395/)); in guinea-pig brain slices, dropping potassium from 4 to 2 first quieted the cells, then set off seizure-like bursts ([Gorji, et al, 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11454323/)); broken KCNQ channels do make neurons fire too easily, as in one inherited epilepsy of newborns ([Biervert, et al, 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9430594/)); and the drop adrenaline brings is potassium moving into the cells, not a lack of it ([Brown, et al, 1983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6314140/)). What settles it is the point my card missed: your brain keeps the potassium around its neurons within tight limits, whatever your blood is doing ([Hladky & Barrand, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27799072/); in dogs, the fluid around the brain stayed the same while blood potassium changed, [Javaheri & Kazemi, 1981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7302394/)). So neither stress nor diet works the brakes through potassium. The trial I found on potassium-rich diets and mood doesn't show it either: both diets were high in potassium, and the one lower in salt did better ([Torres, et al, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18466657/)). What holds is blood pressure: across the trials, more potassium brought systolic pressure down by about 3.5 points, an effect seen in people whose pressure was high and not in others, and it goes with a lower risk of stroke ([Aburto, et al, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23558164/)). GPT's own version keeps the lab details, which a reader can't act on, and says the foods "support normal nerve function", which no source shows for eating more. My card stated one patient's nerves as what all nerve cells do and assumed the brain follows the blood (lesson neuro-2026-11-08j). A fresh reader found the sentence saying potassium won't calm the nerves unneeded once the brakes story is gone, so it's here, not in your article.
