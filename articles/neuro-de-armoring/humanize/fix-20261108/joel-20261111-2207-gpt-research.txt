Yes. I found studies and firsthand reports suggesting that increasing potassium intake can improve mood, fatigue, mental clarity, and possibly cognition. Some of the findings are quite interesting, particularly a 2025 depression case report and a 2019 pediatric study.

However, the evidence is inconsistent, and the key distinction is whether potassium itself produces the improvement or whether people benefit from correcting a deficiency, reducing sodium, or changing other aspects of their diet.
1. Most interesting human treatment evidence
2025: Depression and anxiety reportedly resolved after potassium supplementation
Uncontrolled case report
A woman with clinically assessed severe depression and mild anxiety began oral potassium chloride. Symptoms reportedly improved during the first week, disappeared by week four, and remained absent over nearly three years of reported follow-up.

Major limitations: She simultaneously stopped eating takeout food and took an extended paid leave from work. The report provided no measured potassium deficiency, control group, or comparable post-treatment depression scores. The author was acquainted with the patient and proposed the potassium-deficiency hypothesis.

2019: Potassium helped reported steroid-related psychiatric symptoms in children
Retrospective case series
Researchers reviewed 16 children with acute lymphoblastic leukemia who received potassium chloride for corticosteroid-associated mood and behavioral symptoms.

children reportedly improved (81%)

There was no untreated control group. This suggests a possible benefit in a specific medication-related condition, not an established response rate for ordinary anxiety or cognitive difficulties. Only the abstract was available for this review. 

pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov



2008: Randomized crossover dietary trial found improvements in mood

Participants reported less depression and tension, more vigor, and better overall mood on a low-sodium, high-potassium diet compared with a DASH-like diet.

The important detail from the full-text audit is that both experimental diets raised urinary potassium almost equally (approximately 35.4 versus 34.0 mmol/day relative to control), whereas sodium fell much more in the better-mood diet.

Higher urinary potassium was independently associated with less fatigue across observations, but the randomized comparison does not establish potassium as the cause. The paper also appears to misreport two explained-variance percentages: \(R^2=0.060\) means 6%, not 60%.

2. Studies measuring cognitive performance
Study	Result	Interpretation
2025 US, 2,555 older adults	Highest potassium-intake quartile had approximately 46% lower odds of poor processing-speed performance (adjusted OR 0.542).  pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov	Encouraging correlation, not proof of a supplement effect
2022 China, 4,213 adults	Higher potassium intake was associated with higher cognitive test scores. The highest quartile scored about 1.45 points higher on an adjusted 27-point test.  sciencedirect.com	Prospective observational evidence, not a dietary experiment
2018 US, 1,194 older adults	Potassium alone was not associated with cognitive decline over roughly seven years; the sodium-to-potassium ratio showed an association.  pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov	No independent potassium benefit identified
2014 Australia, 1,406 adults	Higher potassium intake was unexpectedly associated with greater risk of mild cognitive impairment over eight years.  pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov	Contradictory observational finding; does not establish harm
2025 randomized fruit trial, 40 adults	Eating more fruit for eight weeks increased potassium intake but did not consistently improve cognition.  pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov	A negative cognitive result from a multi-nutrient dietary intervention

There is another contradictory randomized finding worth mentioning: a 1991 trial involving 697 people with mild hypertension found more fatigue on a low-sodium, high-potassium diet than on the usual diet within its placebo-medication subgroup (34% versus 18%). That result also cannot separate potassium from sodium restriction or other dietary effects.

Other commenters reported dizziness after large amounts of coconut water, disturbed sleep after later-day potassium, or digestive intolerance. These are self-reports with different exposures and no objective cognitive testing.

One commenter described clearer thinking after using a combined sodium-potassium-magnesium drink alongside other supplements. Others reported that electrolytes did little or nothing for their brain fog. These are not potassium-only comparisons.


r/BrainFog — Potassium deficiency

One commenter who reported previously measured low potassium described improvement in tiredness and other symptoms after potassium-rich foods, while continuing to experience brain fog.


The community search was selective rather than exhaustive. Some discussions had hundreds of comments, and only a portion was accessible. The reports establish that these experiences occur, but not how often potassium works.
4. What these findings suggest
There are three plausible explanations that the evidence does not yet reliably distinguish:
1. Correcting inadequate potassium: People with low intake, increased losses, or actual potassium deficiency might experience better energy or mental functioning when the imbalance is corrected.
2. Indirect effects: Higher potassium intake may improve blood pressure or other physiological functions that affect long-term brain health.
3. A potassium-specific neurological effect: Extra potassium might affect mood or brain performance beyond restoring adequacy. This is the most interesting hypothesis, but the studies reviewed do not establish it.
The third explanation would require controlled supplementation trials keeping sodium, overall diet, and other important variables constant.

Safety matters: Potassium from ordinary food is generally well tolerated by people with normal kidney function. Concentrated potassium supplements and salt substitutes can cause dangerous hyperkalemia and heart rhythm disturbances, particularly with kidney disease or medications that retain potassium.

Conclusion: There is a credible research lead for potassium and mood, and favorable but conflicting associations with cognition. There is not yet convincing controlled evidence that increasing potassium alone enhances memory, focus, or general brain performance in potassium-replete adults.
