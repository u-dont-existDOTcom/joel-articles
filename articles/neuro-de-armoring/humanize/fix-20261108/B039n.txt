D-serine may help therapy update old memories, since it switches on a spot on the NMDA receptor that this kind of learning needs (Wolosker, et al, 1999). But the drug that's actually been tested alongside exposure therapy (facing the feared thing in small, safe doses) is a different one, D-cycloserine (DCS), which works on that same spot, and across 21 trials with over 1,000 patients it gave the therapy a small boost (Mataix-Cols, et al, 2017). So what does it actually do? It doesn't erase fear, it strengthens whatever you learn in the session, good or bad. End the session with the fear down and some sense of mastery, and DCS (and maybe D-serine) helps that stick; end it overwhelmed, and it can lock in the fear instead (Smits, et al, 2013).
