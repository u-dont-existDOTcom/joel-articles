You are reading one paragraph of an article for the person it is written for. Here is what the paragraph is for:

WHAT IT IS FOR: the reader who uses psychedelics learns what not to combine 5-HTP with, and to keep it away from trips, ayahuasca and iboga

HEADING: A Complete Guide to Bio-De-Armoring (the warnings at the top, before the protocol starts), the warning for people who use psychedelics

PARAGRAPH:
S1: Uridine & 5-HTP Warning for Psychedelic Users: These are not "trip enhancers," they're in this protocol for repair, and in practice they can change your brain's sensitivity quickly, so your usual dose might not act the way you expect.
S2: 5-HTP is the one to be careful with, because your body makes serotonin out of it, and serotonin syndrome (which can be deadly, see the 5-HTP section) usually comes from mixing it with something else that raises serotonin.
S3: So do not combine it with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, linezolid, lithium, tramadol, methadone, fentanyl, DXM, MDMA, St. John's wort, SAM-e, L-tryptophan, or other serotonin-raising stacks.
S4: Rule: do not take 5-HTP on the same day as a psychedelic trip, and do not use it anywhere near ayahuasca/harmalas or iboga (microdoses of iboga included), since ayahuasca's MAO-A inhibition can amplify the serotonin load, and ibogaine and noribogaine interact with serotonin transport on top of iboga's already serious cardiac and neurological risk.

For each sentence, answer in one line: what does the reader need this sentence for, given what the paragraph is for?
Then list the sentences the reader doesn't need.

A sentence the reader doesn't need is one they could lose and still do what the paragraph is for, with nothing
missing. These are not reasons to keep a sentence: it is true; it has a source; it is interesting; it is a careful
hypothesis or a testable guess; it explains a disease or a case the reader doesn't have; it qualifies another claim the
reader didn't need either; it was already in the text. Read also for sense: a sentence whose words don't make sense
together, or that mixes pictures (a dial, a door, a floor and a leak for one thing), goes on the list too, with why.

Before you list a sentence for giving the reader nothing to do, ask what it means they should do. A caution, a risk
or a difference between people often implies an act the sentence doesn't say ("Sensitivity varies" means: start
with less): that sentence is needed, and its NEEDS line says the act it implies, so the writer can add it.
