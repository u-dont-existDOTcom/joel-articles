# Blind rewrite: two passages, from the lessons alone

You are rewriting two short passages of a self-help article by Joel, a Substack writer (informal, first person, plain
words; "I" is Joel). The article is a protocol for adults with trauma and addiction; both passages sit in its section
"Psychology is the Software; Biology is the Hardware.", which argues that the brain's chemistry has to be worked on
alongside the psychology. Each passage below is a draft that reads partly like a model wrote it. Rewrite each one the
way the lessons say, changing as little as they call for. Keep every fact and every instruction; add no fact, no example
and no detail of your own. Keep these as they are: the citation "(Perry, 2023)", "Perry finds", the "Void" in quote
marks, the label "The Anti-Breakthrough (Micro-Dosing):", and the figures (one-sixteenth, 30 seconds, twenty times a
day), though a figure may be written in digits.

## The lessons (from his fixes of other passages)

1. **A second verb after "and" that a reader could hang from an order before it gets its own "who you" (or "that
   you").** After an order, "and" plus a verb can read as a second order instead of the second half of the clause it
   belongs to. (Invented example: "So pick a friend you trust and call every week" can read as "pick ... and call every
   week"; "So pick a friend you trust and who you call every week" can't.)
2. **A word readers know in one sense, used here for something else, is tied to the sense they know.** Don't claim the
   word with "which is what I mean by X here": say how the new use connects to the one readers bring ("another kind of
   X", "the same idea applied to ..."). (Invented example: "Then you go a day without your phone, which is what I mean by
   fasting here." reads as a redefinition; "..., which is fasting too, from screens instead of food" connects them.)
3. **A practice nobody does exactly is said as the rough figure it is.** A length of time or a number of times a day for
   a habit or exercise the reader will only do roughly gets "something like", "about" or "a few", with the same number.
   Never a dose, an amount of a substance, a limit, a timing a warning rests on, or a figure from a study: those stay
   exact. (Invented example: "Walk for 15 minutes after each meal." becomes "I'd walk something like 15 minutes after
   each meal."; "Take 200mg with food." stays as it is.)
4. **A count is written as a digit** ("4 adults", "20 times"), the way he writes it; it's a cheap fix that never stands
   in for the three above.
5. **When two paragraphs are rewritten together, every sentence of both is open to change**, the end of the first as
   much as the second, since a reader takes them in together.

## Passage 1 (one paragraph)

For context only (don't rewrite it), the paragraph before it ends: "As Dr. Bruce Perry explains, our brains evolved in
hunter-gatherer bands with a ratio of 4 developmentally mature adults to 1 child. Today, our "enriched" environments
offer 1 immature adult to 4 children."

That works out to about one-sixteenth of the grown-ups per child that our brains evolved with (Perry, 2023). So the emptiness you feel (the "Void") is real, since it's what that missing company feels like. Once you've de-armored, going back to a lonely life will tend to feel worse than it did with the armor on. So gather a tribe of people you're close to and see most days, or you'll eventually put the armor back on just to get through the loneliness.

## Passage 2 (two paragraphs)

For context only, the paragraph before them says the protocol softens the brainstem's "concrete" with glutamate
modulation and ketones "so we can dig under the house and pour a new foundation, and eventually work on the psychology
upstairs."

The Anti-Breakthrough (Micro-Dosing): A brain stuck in alarm can often take in healing work for only a few seconds at a time, Perry finds. So for now we have to give up on the hour-long breakthrough that therapy aims for, since an hour spent working on the trauma itself overwhelms an armored nervous system more than it heals it. Instead, this protocol works in doses of a few seconds, which is what I mean by micro-dosing here.

In practice, I'd do 30 seconds of slow breathing, twenty times a day.
