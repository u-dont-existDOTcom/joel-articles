A Glutamate Storm looks like Anxiety, Rage, an “Itchy” Brain, Racing Thoughts, or Flashbacks. The brain is firing too hot, so you need to cool the system and jam the reconsolidation.

The Perry-Sequence (Rhythmic First, Visual Second)

Anatomy overrides intellect, so if your heart rate is elevated or your heart-rate-variability (HRV) is reduced significantly, your “Reasoning Brain” is offline.

Step 1: Rhythmic Regulation (Brainstem). Do 5 minutes of rhythmic, repetitive sensory input, like walking, drumming on your thighs, or rhythmic “Therapeutic Sighing”.

Step 2: Check Your Baseline. Once your heart rate is within normal range, your cerebral cortex is receptive again. (Phosphatidylserine, PS, from Part 2, works on your daily baseline: it lowers how often and how hard these storms hit, so it won’t help in the moment.)
