5-HTP is for the low-serotonin-looking pattern, not for every depressed person: poor sleep initiation, low mood, low warmth, night anxiety, repetitive rumination, and a genetic or clinical reason to suspect a bottleneck in serotonin synthesis. Scott’s genetic data suggested a TPH2 bottleneck, and 5-HTP helped his sleep and mood. But if you’re already agitated, manic, on serotonergic meds, or using serotonergic psychedelics/entactogens, this is not a casual experiment.

5-HTP bypasses the tryptophan-to-5-HTP step and gives the body a more direct serotonin precursor. A broad review of 5-HTP discusses its role in serotonin biology and its use in mood, anxiety, sleep, obesity, myoclonus, and serotonin-toxicity contexts. The evidence for depression and sleep is mixed, and weaker than people online make it sound, but the mechanism is real enough that responders can feel it quickly.

Scott’s version is 100 mg 5-HTP sublingually at night, with 10–20 mg P5P sublingually in the morning. The P5P is there as the active B6 cofactor for aromatic amino-acid decarboxylase, and it isn’t meant as a stimulant. He’s slow-COMT and sensitive to too much P5P, so the low dose matters, and more is not better.

To trial it, start lower if you’re sensitive: 50 mg 5-HTP at night for 2–3 nights, then 100 mg only if the lower amount is clearly helpful and not activating. P5P is optional, 10–20 mg in the morning, especially if 5-HTP feels like it “almost works” but isn’t converting cleanly. Do not combine the trial with UMP, agmatine, D-serine, psychedelics, or medication changes. When it’s the right tool, the first signal is usually in sleep quality, dream tone, mood warmth, or less rumination at night.

Side effects and stop signs: nausea, stomach discomfort, loose stools, headache, vivid or intense dreams, insomnia, sweating, tremor, anxiety, irritability, emotional agitation, or a too-bright / hypomanic feeling. Stop immediately if you get agitation + sweating + diarrhea + tremor/clonus + fever, confusion, fast heart rate, or unstable blood pressure, because that pattern can indicate serotonin toxicity, which can become dangerous.

The hard interaction rule is that 5-HTP should not be mixed casually with serotonin-raising drugs or supplements. Case literature and clinical reviews warn about serotonin syndrome risk when 5-HTP is combined with antidepressants or MAOIs, and serotonin syndrome is most often a multi-drug / stack problem (see the dietary supplement interaction case report and the general serotonin-syndrome overview from MedlinePlus). For this readership, that means: avoid 5-HTP on psychedelic days, and be especially strict around ayahuasca / harmala MAOI chemistry and iboga / ibogaine.
