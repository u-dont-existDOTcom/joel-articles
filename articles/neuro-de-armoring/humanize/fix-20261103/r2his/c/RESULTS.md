- AI (High, 428 words): Dopamine Receptor Burnout / DAWS Pattern: Uridine Monophosphate Who this is for: This is not for everyone with depression, only for a specific phenotype, the “burned-out dopamine receptor” pattern (gray anhedonia, weak dreams, weak psychedelic visuals, needing unusually high doses to feel anything, compulsive reaching for ketamine/LSD/novelty just to get a hit), and especially for people with a history of dopamine agonist withdrawal from drugs like pramipexole or ropinirole. The medical literature calls this dopamine agonist withdrawal syndrome (DAWS), and describes what happens after dopamine-agonist tapering (anxiety, panic, dysphoria, depression, agitation, fatigue, autonomic symptoms, pain, and drug cravings). In Scott’s case it looked like DAWS with D2/D3 reward-system downregulation, made worse by frequent LSD microdosing and ketamine attempts to force dopamine hits. The tool: Uridine monophosphate (UMP). There’s no study showing that “UMP cures DAWS,” so the best evidence is the more basic fact that uridine, choline, and DHA are circulating precursors for brain phosphatide / synaptic membrane synthesis. Wurtman’s group has shown that DHA + uridine + choline can increase synaptic membrane components and synaptic proteins, that DHA-induced dendritic spine formation is enhanced by UMP in animals, and that dietary UMP increased potassium-evoked dopamine release and neurite outgrowth in aged rats, so the mechanism is plausible, but the DAWS use is still a cautious human extrapolation plus anecdote. Scott’s signal: Scott already had choline from eggs and DHA/EPA from fish oil, and after adding standard-dose UMP he got a striking response within a day: vivid dreams returned, real childhood memories came back in dreams, psychedelic tolerance seemed to drop, and ketamine/LSD craving fell hard. That at least suggests that, in the right person, UMP can be a very clear signal that the synaptic-membrane / dopamine-receptor-recovery axis was starved, whether or not it rebuilt any D3 receptors overnight. Side effects / stop signs: The most common problem is the “choline headache” or spaced-out brain fog, since if UMP accelerates membrane synthesis while choline availability is low, the system can feel under-supplied. Other stop signs include insomnia, jitteriness, racing thoughts, irritability, emotional flatness, nausea, stomach cramps, or loose stools, and if it makes you wired, numb, or weirdly irritable, stop taking it, and don’t push through. Psychedelic caution: If UMP makes dreams vivid or suddenly lowers psychedelic tolerance, treat that as a safety signal. Don’t take UMP on psychedelic days, and if you use psychedelics later, assume your previous dose may be too strong and that chasing the old visual threshold can overshoot.
