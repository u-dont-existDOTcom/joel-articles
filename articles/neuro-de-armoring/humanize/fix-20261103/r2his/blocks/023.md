In [*Adult children of emotionally immature parents*](https://www.amazon.com/Adult-Children-Emotionally-Immature-Parents/dp/B01F2L1HLM/ref=sr_1_1?crid=2JLWO51H6BXO1&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.WNWQbN2kbLNCbYq_W-fodAEeQ_UlnBi9Ju6cPln8M1TPg-RV-3-nRjn0Qj-b2kgxvix3vglikzPoFdr6m1bV4ZrTYX1qWRDNS0Dbk17dV3oK-KP3CXCX7QstfIkQGtiiLMJyKwT9N66J8RW_F6c7YoH8oGEsjPnyij0gNk5i-nnTMPGQa4VFhVtocMt46Fax3GwdCYOwIduJYQPc1LjZSgr9sS7b1bTey37wCvpdEnc.YYpzi2NWlm3O9D3YaGlV-1PGakmscQwxvmqh3imaiA8&dib_tag=se&keywords=children+of+emotionally+immature+parents&qid=1766678988&s=books&sprefix=children+of+emotionally+immature+parents%2Cstripbooks%2C395&sr=1-1), psychologist Lindsay Gibson explains that children in that situation survive by creating a “Role-Self,” a defensive personality that’s designed to please other people and manage the parent’s emotions. But we can’t just blame our immature parents for this Role Self, since it really is true what they say, that “It takes a village to raise a child,” and the modern large society with only one or two people caring for the children is vastly insufficient. As Dr. Bruce Perry explains, our brains evolved in hunter-gatherer bands with a ratio of 4 developmentally mature adults to 1 child, while today our “enriched” environments offer 1 immature adult to 4 children.
