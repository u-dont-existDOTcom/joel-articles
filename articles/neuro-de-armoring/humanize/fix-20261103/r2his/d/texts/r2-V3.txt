The Modern “Role-Self” vs. The Ancient “True-Self”

In Adult children of emotionally immature parents, psychologist Lindsay Gibson explains that children in that situation survive by creating a “Role-Self,” a defensive personality that’s designed to please other people and manage the parent’s emotions. But we can’t just blame our immature parents for this Role Self, since it really is true what they say, that “It takes a village to raise a child,” and the modern large society with only one or two people caring for the children is vastly insufficient. As Dr. Bruce Perry explains, our brains evolved in hunter-gatherer bands with a ratio of 4 developmentally mature adults to 1 child, while today our “enriched” environments offer 1 immature adult to 4 children.

We’re operating on less than 1/24th of the relational density our biology requires, so the “Void” you feel is real, and once you have de-armored, you cannot simply return to a lonely life. You must aggressively engineer a “High-Density” social environment (A Tribe), or the brain will eventually reach for the armor again just to survive the cold.

As we grow up, our “Role-Self” becomes calcified by Dopamine and DeltaFosB until it’s a prison, and your addiction is often just the fuel required to keep the “Role-Self” running. This protocol dissolves the chemical glue holding that false persona together, which allows your “True-Self,” the person you were before the trauma, to finally re-emerge.

The Anti-Breakthrough (Micro-Dosing) Dr. Perry says we need to give up on the “hour-long breakthrough” for a while, which is the ideal a lot of therapy is built around.
