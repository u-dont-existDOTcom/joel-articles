Uridine & 5-HTP Warning for Psychedelic Users: Don’t think of these as “trip enhancers.” They’re repair-oriented, and they can change how sensitive your brain is pretty quickly. 5-HTP is a direct serotonin precursor, which makes it serotonergic, so do not combine it with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, linezolid, lithium, tramadol, methadone, fentanyl, DXM, MDMA, St. John’s wort, SAM-e, L-tryptophan, or any other serotonin-raising stack without clinician review. So as a rule, do not take 5-HTP on the same day as psychedelics, and do not use it anywhere near ayahuasca/harmalas or iboga unless someone has medically reviewed the whole medicine plan. Uridine isn’t serotonergic, but it can still change your dream vividness, visual sensitivity, craving, and psychedelic tolerance, so do not take UMP on the same day as psychedelics either. And if your sensitivity changes, assume your old psychedelic dose may now be too high.

This is for a specific “burned-out dopamine receptor” pattern, where everything feels gray (anhedonia), dreams and psychedelic visuals are weak, it takes unusually high doses to feel anything, and you keep compulsively reaching for ketamine/LSD/novelty just to get a hit, especially if you have a history of dopamine agonist withdrawal from drugs like pramipexole or ropinirole. In the medical literature it’s called dopamine agonist withdrawal syndrome (DAWS), and they describe anxiety, panic, dysphoria, depression, agitation, fatigue, autonomic symptoms, pain, and drug cravings after people taper off dopamine agonists. Scott’s case looked like DAWS with D2/D3 reward-system downregulation, which then got worse from frequent LSD microdosing and from trying to force dopamine hits with ketamine. That’s a pretty specific phenotype, and I’m not saying everyone needs dopamine. The tool: Uridine monophosphate (UMP). There isn’t a study that shows “UMP cures DAWS,” so the evidence we have is more basic than that. Uridine, choline, and DHA are circulating precursors the brain uses for phosphatide / synaptic membrane synthesis, and Wurtman’s group has shown that DHA + uridine + choline can increase synaptic membrane components and synaptic proteins, that DHA-induced dendritic spine formation is enhanced by UMP in animals, and that dietary UMP increased potassium-evoked dopamine release and neurite outgrowth in aged rats. So the mechanism is plausible, but using it for DAWS is still a cautious human extrapolation plus anecdote. Scott’s signal: Scott was already getting choline from eggs and DHA/EPA from fish oil, and when he added a standard dose of UMP he got a striking response within a day. His vivid dreams came back, real childhood memories showed up in his dreams, his psychedelic tolerance seemed to drop, and his ketamine/LSD cravings fell hard. We can’t say from this that UMP rebuilt his D3 receptors overnight, but it does suggest that in the right person, UMP can be a very clear signal that the synaptic-membrane / dopamine-receptor-recovery axis was starved. Side effects / stop signs: The most common problem is what’s called the “choline headache,” or a spaced-out brain fog, which can happen if UMP speeds up membrane synthesis while your choline availability is low, so the system can feel under-supplied. Other stop signs include insomnia, jitteriness, racing thoughts, irritability, emotional flatness, nausea, stomach cramps, or loose stools. If it makes you wired, numb, or weirdly irritable, stop taking it, and do not push through it. Psychedelic caution: If UMP makes dreams vivid, or suddenly lowers psychedelic tolerance, take that as a safety signal and do not take UMP on psychedelic days. If you use psychedelics later on, assume your previous dose may be too strong now, and that trying to get back to the old visual threshold can overshoot.
