Optional Ready-Made Gut Stacks

A good stack should cover these 3 categories:

  1. First, support the Microbiome with a well-tolerated probiotic or probiotic system.

  2. Then keep the probiotics well-fed with a good prebiotic, and take something that helps short-chain fatty acid production, especially the main one, butyrate, or take butyrate itself.

  3. Other nutrients will help support barrier repair, such as glutamine, zinc carnosine, colostrum, mucosal-support herbs, or similar compounds.

You’ll also find “gut cleanse” stacks, built around oregano oil, wormwood, black walnut, berberine or other antimicrobial herbs. They kill bugs, like the herbal antibiotics in Phase 3, so they’re only for when changing your diet hasn’t cleared your symptoms. And even then I’d mostly skip them, because H2O2 works better without the side effects. The exception is berberine for H. pylori: take it on its own, not in a cleanse stack (see Parasites, above).

Start any of these supplements or stacks at a low dose and build up slowly as your gut gets used to it. It’s quite common for the prebiotic part especially to give you gas at first, since gas is what your bacteria make when they ferment the fiber or resistant starch, and a new probiotic can bloat you or upset your stomach for a while.

Some severe symptoms warrant seeing a doctor rather than considering this DIY approach at the moment. Take it seriously if you notice blood in your stool (if it’s blood from upper digestive tract, it could be smelly, black & tarry, not red like from the lower digestive tract). Other serious signs to note: losing weight fast, severe pain, a fever or dehydration. And if you’re pregnant or breastfeeding, skip berberine, which can give a newborn jaundice, and ask your doctor about any other herb or supplement first. If your immune system is weak, some probiotics could be risky, since they’re live bacteria that need to be kept in check to prevent overgrowth. If you have an IBD flare, cancer or another serious illness, the standard disclaimer applies: work out what to take with your doctor instead of putting a stack together yourself.
