Mitochondrial and cellular energy starvation is what you get when there's not enough of the fuels each type of cell in your gut prefers (glutamine and butyrate). Your gut barrier needs a lot of energy, to keep its tight junctions intact. If the cells lining your small intestine (called enterocytes) or the colonocytes lining your large intestine don't get that energy, the barrier loosens up. Then lipopolysaccharides (LPS, which come from the walls of bacteria) and other endotoxins leak through those (not-so-tight)-junction gaps into your bloodstream.

L-Glutamine (watch Dr. Rhonda vid) is the main fuel for your enterocytes, and for your immune cells when they're activated. Most food has to be digested before your cells can use it, and for protein that means being broken down into amino acids. But glutamine already is an amino acid, so your enterocytes can take it in as soon as it reaches them. Their mitochondria (the part of a cell that makes its energy) turn it into alpha-ketoglutarate (a-KG). And a-KG is one of the acids in the citric acid cycle itself, the loop mitochondria make that energy with. So that's why consuming pure glutamine gives energy to those cells faster than eating food, on top of what they get from their meals. They then use their extra energy to repair the gut lining by pulling the tight junctions together, and that keeps LPS from getting through. Glutamine also provides nitrogen to help build new cells.

Take 5g a day as a baseline, to keep your enterocytes fed. The 5g also counters the breakdown of the gut lining that hard or long training causes, like CrossFit or intense cardio. While you train, your body sends blood away from your gut to your working muscles, so your enterocytes get less oxygen and fuel. If your immune system is dealing with an acute exposure, if your whole system is under a lot of stress, or if you have active IBD (Crohn's or colitis), raise it gradually to 10-30g a day.

CAUTION: a lot of cancers feed on glutamine too, to fuel their growth. Your enterocytes take in a lot of it as soon as it reaches them, but not all of it, and the rest gets into your blood, where a cancer can get to it. They can only take in so much at a time, so the bigger the dose, the more of it gets into your blood. So if you have an active cancer, check with your oncologist before you take the 5g, and especially before you go up to the 10-30g.
