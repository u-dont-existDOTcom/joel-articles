## Optional Ready-Made Gut Stacks

I do not think there is a universal supplement stack that heals everyone’s gut. The correct order still matters: fix digestion mechanics first, reset diet and motility, then only escalate into antimicrobial or anti-parasite protocols if symptoms persist.

That said, some people want a ready-made starting point rather than buying every ingredient separately. For a general “repair and rebuild” approach, I would look for a stack that covers three categories:

- Microbiome support: a well-tolerated probiotic or probiotic system - Prebiotic / butyrate support: something that helps short-chain fatty acid production, especially butyrate - Barrier repair: nutrients that support the gut lining, such as glutamine, zinc carnosine, colostrum, mucosal-support herbs, or similar compounds

The best general commercial stack I have seen so far is Microbiome Labs Total Gut Restoration, because it combines probiotic, prebiotic, and mucosal-support components. For a more leaky-gut-specific stack, Dr. Ruscio’s Leaky Gut Support Kit is a reasonable option. For a single gut-lining repair formula, Designs for Health GI Revive is one of the more complete products, but it should be understood as a repair product, not a complete gut protocol by itself.

I would not recommend general “gut cleanse” stacks built around oregano oil, wormwood, black walnut, berberine, or other antimicrobial herbs as a universal starting point. Those belong later in the decision tree, when there is a stronger reason to suspect overgrowth, parasites, H. pylori, or another specific problem.

Start low, especially with prebiotics, resistant starch, butyrate-support products, and probiotics. People with severe pain, GI bleeding, fever, dehydration, rapid weight loss, active IBD flares, cancer, immune suppression, pregnancy, or complex medical conditions should work with a qualified clinician instead of self-stacking supplements.

## Optional Ready-Made Gut Stacks

I don't think there's one supplement stack (a set of supplements you take together) that heals everyone's gut, and no stack can do the phases above for you. So do those first, in order, and only go after bugs and parasites if your symptoms are still there after you've changed your diet. Then a stack can help with the rebuilding in Phase 5, once you're clear of infection and it's time to feed the good bacteria again.

A good stack should cover these 3 categories:

1. First, support the Microbiome with a well-tolerated probiotic or probiotic system.

2. Then keep the probiotics well-fed with a good prebiotic, and take butyrate itself or something that helps short-chain fatty acid production, especially the main one, butyrate.

3. Other nutrients will help support barrier repair, such as glutamine, zinc carnosine, colostrum, mucosal-support herbs, or similar compounds.

Gemini suggested these kits to me, but so far they're the best ones I've seen, tho I haven't used them myself, so check the reviews. Microbiome Labs Total Gut Restoration covers all three categories, with a probiotic, a prebiotic and a product for the gut lining in one kit, so it's the best general one. Dr. Ruscio's Leaky Gut Support Kit has a probiotic and two gut lining products, one of them glutamine, but no prebiotic, so it's more for when leaky gut is your main problem. Finally, Designs for Health GI Revive is only gut-lining nutrients and herbs, so you'd still need a probiotic and a prebiotic with it.

You'll also find "gut cleanse" stacks, built around oregano oil, wormwood, black walnut, berberine or other antimicrobial herbs. They kill bugs, like the herbal antibiotics in Phase 3, so they're only for when changing your diet hasn't cleared your symptoms. And even then I'd mostly skip them, because H2O2 works better without the side effects. The exception is berberine for H. pylori, the stomach bacteria that's usually behind heartburn you only get on an empty stomach: take it on its own, not in a cleanse stack (see Parasites, above).

Start any of these supplements or stacks at a low dose and build up slowly as your gut gets used to it. It's quite common for the prebiotic part especially to give you gas at first, since gas is what your bacteria make when they ferment the fiber or resistant starch, and a new probiotic can bloat you or upset your stomach for a while.

Some severe symptoms warrant seeing a doctor rather than considering this DIY approach at the moment. Take it seriously if you notice blood in your stool (if it's blood from upper digestive tract, it could be smelly, black & tarry, not red like from lower GI). Other serious signs to note: If you're losing weight fast, or if you have severe pain, a fever or dehydration. And if you're pregnant, some herbs and supplements, like berberine, aren't safe. If your immune system is weak, some probiotics could be risky, since they're live bacteria that need to be kept in check to prevent overgrowth. If you have an IBD flare, cancer or another serious illness, the standard disclaimer applies: work out what to take with your doctor instead of putting a stack together yourself.
