Malic and citric acid (which make green apples and tart cherries sour) are made and used partway round that same cycle, also called the TCA or Krebs cycle, so in theory they're a quick energy source for your enterocytes too. But there's no good evidence they add much fuel on top of what glutamine gives. The better reason to eat them is iron: they help keep the iron in a meal dissolved, so more of it gets absorbed. In one study, adding 1 g of pure malic or citric acid to a rice meal raised how much of its iron people absorbed two to three times, and the vegetables whose iron was well absorbed had a good amount of these acids in them (Gillooly et al., 1983).
