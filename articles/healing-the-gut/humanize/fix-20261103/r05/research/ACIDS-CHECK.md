# The TCA intermediates' point, checked (2026-11-04)

Joel's card F4-3, 11:32 UTC: "i just now asked gemini, it said glutamine is really what matters for the fuel source and actually even tho those acids are a quick energy for the cells, the main reason to take those in addition would be to boost stomach acid and mineral absorption. if you agree you can re-do the para so it focuses on glutamine for energy and breiefly explains the use of those acids". Each claim was checked against a source I opened (gate E344, the neuro lane's: warnings are checked before they are rewritten; the same goes for the points they rest on).

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Glutamine is the main fuel for enterocytes | Holds. The article already says so, in Joel's words. | (the glutamine item) |
| Dietary malic and citric acid are a quick fuel for the gut lining's cells | Possible in principle, since both are intermediates of the citric acid cycle, but I found no study showing it. A 2024 review of α-ketoglutarate (another TCA intermediate) and the gut epithelium reports barrier benefits, almost all of them in animals and cell cultures, and doesn't mention dietary malate or citrate as fuel. | [Bravo Iniguez, Du & Zhu 2024, *Advances in Nutrition*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11016550/) |
| They help mineral absorption | Holds for iron. In parous Indian women, 1 g of citric acid added to a rice meal raised non-haem iron absorption from 0.028 to 0.085 (geometric means), and 1 g of L-malic acid from 0.048 to 0.095. Calcium citrate malate is well absorbed, but that is a supplement salt, not eating the acids with a meal, so the paragraph claims iron only. | [Gillooly et al. 1983, *British Journal of Nutrition* 49:331](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/effects-of-organic-acids-phytates-and-polyphenols-on-the-absorption-of-iron-from-vegetables/14A62BC9249CEDBF072AED98BCBA70BB); [Smith et al. 1987, *Calcif Tissue Int* 41:351](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF02556676) |
| They boost stomach acid | Not supported. I found no study showing that eating an acid makes the stomach secrete more of its own. The feedback runs the other way: acid in the stomach holds back the release of gastrin, the hormone that drives acid secretion (in this study, less so in duodenal ulcer patients than in controls). So the paragraph leaves it out. | [Jensen et al., *Gut* 28:206](https://gut.bmj.com/content/28/2/206) |

Where it goes: the acids stay where they were, under the fuels, now as a short paragraph that says what they are, why they come up next to glutamine, that they add little as fuel, and their better use (iron). Because they are no longer one of the gut's fuels, "TCA intermediates" comes out of the fuels list in the opening paragraph and in Phase 5's step 4. The larger argument is unchanged: the gut lining's cells need their fuels, glutamine and butyrate.
