Thus, we have to target the problem from various angles--cleansing out pathogens, repopulating good bacteria, addressing vitamin (especially thiamine), mineral, and enzyme deficiencies, and changing what we eat. It's certainly a diet worth trying, but it can lead to deficiencies over time, especially with regard to vitamins C, E, fiber, magnesium, potassium, calcium, iodine and other trace minerals, and bacteria needed to process plant foods (which already are likely missing in people who need this diet), thus making the carnivore diet sort of hard to get off.

- Leaky gut (Phase 5): a widespread inflammatory condition often intersecting with other categories of illness such as Crohn's Disease or IBS, plant food intolerance, and what many alternative health practitioners consider as a root cause of most diseases. It's associated with increased zonulin levels clinically. Zonulin is the molecule released to signal your intestinal tight junctions to open. Too much opening time leads to allowing stuff to leak out into your blood stream which then burdens your immune system and detoxification systems. Game-changing treatments:

- Liposomal first-milking colostrum (esp. fermented per Matt Nedin's protocol with bifidobacterium, or SIBO yogurt bacteria). Matt from EndSickness.org finds that this helps especially to mop up mycotoxins in the gut. Activated charcoal can also be helpful, altho don't take that with things you want to actually absorb.

- Fermented veggie juices of turmeric, red cabbage, celery, beet greens (not just beet root).

- Zinc carnosine, the form of zinc which sticks to your intestinal lining.

- Beta-glucans, in yeast extract.

- Resistant starch to feed the good bacteria in your gut.

- Ozonated zeolite (or ZeoCharge): I find regular zeolite to be ineffective, because the pores on it are clogged up, so it can't adsorb excess zonulin, metals, and other toxins.

- Mitochondrial and cellular energy starvation is what you get when there's not enough of the fuels each type of cell in your gut prefers (glutamine and butyrate). Your gut barrier needs a lot of energy, to keep its tight junctions intact. If the cells lining your small intestine (called enterocytes) or the colonocytes lining your large intestine don't get that energy, the barrier loosens up. Then lipopolysaccharides (LPS, which come from the walls of bacteria) and other endotoxins leak through those (not-so-tight)-junction gaps into your bloodstream.

- L-Glutamine (watch Dr. Rhonda vid) is the main fuel for your enterocytes, and for your immune cells when they're activated. It doesn't have to go through the complicated digestive steps other nutrients do. Inside the mitochondria, it gets turned straight into alpha-ketoglutarate, or α-KG. The α-KG goes into the citric acid cycle, which is how the mitochondria make their energy. So that's why consuming pure glutamine gives energy to those cells faster than eating food. They then use their extra energy to repair the gut lining by pulling the tight junctions together, and that keeps LPS from getting through. Glutamine also provides nitrogen to help build new cells.

- Take 5g a day as a baseline. The point of that baseline is to counter the breakdown of the gut lining that high-intensity or high-volume training causes, like CrossFit or intense cardio. If your immune system is dealing with an acute exposure, if your whole system is under a lot of stress, or if you have active IBD (Crohn's or colitis), raise it gradually to 10-30g a day.

- CAUTION: cancer cells that multiply really fast can grab glutamine too, to fuel their growth. So if you have an active colon or liver cancer, don't raise the dose that high. The liver is on that list because the blood from your gut goes straight there, through the portal vein, so a big dose of glutamine reaches your liver before anywhere else.

- The colonocytes lining your large intestine are fueled by short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) like butyrate, while your small intestine runs on glutamine. Your gut bacteria make SCFAs when they ferment raw resistant starch, and you get SCFAs directly from your home-brewed SIBO yogurt too. Besides feeding the colonocytes, SCFAs lock down the barrier in your lower gut, so dangerous substances can't escape from gut to blood, and your body can turn down the inflammatory signals it would otherwise need to contain the damage.

- Some people will tell you to take malic and citric acid (which make green apples and tart cherries sour) as an extra quick energy source for your enterocytes. And they do supply quick energy, because they are made and used by that mitochondrial energy cycle, also called the TCA or Krebs cycle. But glutamine is still the main thing your enterocytes need, because it helps build new cells in addition to giving the needed energy. Those acids do help you absorb iron, even two or three times better according to one study where people ate rice with 1g pure malic acid or citric acid mixed in (Gillooly et al., 1983). That's probably because the acids help keep the iron dissolved so the gut can take it up. So something sour with an iron-rich meal is worth having anyway.

- IBS (Phase 4): While there are many causes and types of irritable bowel syndrome, psychedelics are one promising new avenue of treatment. However, it's always best to find the root cause of the problem, which requires investigation. A functional medicine gastroenterologist might help.

- Low stomach acid, enzymes, and thick bile (Phase 1): which in turn are often due to poor diet, stress, infections. T.C. Hale has a lot of videos on healing digestion issues, which in his experience as a health coach, are often due to these root causes. He's a bit controversial on some points such as his urine pH testing for whether your body is frozen in a building-up or a breaking-down state (anabolic or catabolic), but he does have some good suggestions he explains in his book.
