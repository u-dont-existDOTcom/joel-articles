## Concise Recipe for Gut Healing:

I have consolidated the top 9 most effective gut healing books into a customized smart Gemini Pro Gut Health Gem. You can ask any questions in that Gem to consult the following 9 experts at once and get far better advice than you'd get from any one of them! If you have any other books you think would be better, lmk in comments!

NOTE: this gut gem does not include some of my research in this article, which is newer, especially from folks like Matt from EndSickness.org who doesn't have a comprehensive text for Gemini to read. So the phases below are what the Gem will tell you, from the nine books (apart from my H2O2 swap in Phase 3). Where my notes in the causes above change or add to a phase, I point back to them under "My notes" in that phase.

### Text Summary of the Protocol

This protocol is meant to be done in phases. Don't skip ahead, since you only move on to the next phase if the one you're on doesn't clear up your symptoms.

#### Phase 1: The Pre-Flight Safety Check

Before you throw supplements or antimicrobials at your gut, the system mechanics that are foundational to gut function have to work. This part is from T.C. Hale (Kick Your Fat in the Nuts) and Stanley Rosenberg (Accessing the Healing Power of the Vagus Nerve).

Are your pupils dilated all the time? Are you missing your gag reflex? Then according to Hale, you're stuck in fight or flight mode (which in medical lingo is called "sympathetic dominance"), and digestion is offline. Rosenberg's fix is his Basic Exercise (eye movements) or the Salamander technique (which looks like some kind of yoga, see a video demo here). Both of those help to mechanically release the vagus nerve so your body can switch into rest and digest mode.

Now let's have a look at your bile function. Do you burp extra (not just like one little burp) after fatty meals? Do your stools float (like it's hard to flush), or look like clay? Then your bile is stagnant (not flowing well), and Hale says if you take antimicrobials now, you'll get a herx reaction: the dying microbes release toxins you can't detoxify. So thin the bile first with Beet Flow (an extract of beet greens) or phosphatidylcholine, before starting on any pathogen killing protocol.

- My notes: for stress and sleep see "Severe stress" above, and for stomach acid and bile see "Low stomach acid".

#### Phase 2: The Reset

This is Dr. Michael Ruscio (Healthy Gut, Healthy You) and Dr. Mark Pimentel (The Microbiome Connection): calm the inflammation down and starve the overgrowths, no harsh chemicals.

Diet-wise, go Paleo-Low FODMAP (Ruscio) or Low Fermentation Eating (Pimentel). Either way you stop feeding the bacteria in your small intestine.

And no snacking! Pimentel wants meals 4-5 hours apart, strictly, so the Migrating Motor Complex (MMC) gets to sweep your gut clean in between.

- My notes: see "Poor diet now" and "Plant foods", above.

#### Phase 3: The Purge

- If diet didn't clear your symptoms, you likely have an active overgrowth (SIBO/SIFO). This one is from Dr. William Davis (Super Gut) and Dr. Michael Ruscio.

1. AVOID most herbal antibiotics, even though Davis recommends specific herbal combinations like Candibactin AR/BR or FC Cidal/Dysbiocide for 4 weeks.

2. H2O2 actually works better than the herbal antibiotics according to me and orthomolecular drs. My BFF Pooyan tried candibactin actually and it nearly killed him. The H2O2 worked a lot better without side effects. There is one herbal antibiotic that works really well tho for H. pylori: Berberine. If you have an acute infection with something that H2O2 and berberine don't clear quickly, consider escalating to secnidazole treatment for rapid cure, and paromomycin post-treatment for relapse prevention, with medical supervision.

3. BIND the toxins, especially the metals and parasite die-off toxins, using Zholy3O.

4. At the same time, take Ruscio's Triple Probiotic Support, a blend of Lacto/Bifido, S. boulardii, and Soil-Based organisms. That's to maintain diversity and prevent opportunistic infections (like C. diff) during the kill phase.

5. And if all that failed, the overgrowth is too severe or the gut lining is too damaged for solids, so it's time for Ruscio's "Nuclear" Option, the Elemental Diet. That's a 2-3 week liquid-only diet (predigested nutrients), which starves the bacteria completely while still nourishing you.

- My notes: the H2O2 swap above is mine, not the books'. For berberine and H. pylori see Parasites, and for SIBO yogurt see SIBO, above.

#### Phase 4: The Investigation

- Benjamin Brown (Digestive Health Solution) is the only source for this phase. If even the "Nuclear Option" failed, you can cross the microbiome off the list. The problem is likely an organ that isn't working right, or environmental toxicity or your brain's false danger signals (those two are Phase 6, below).

1. So check your organs (Brown). Is it Exocrine Pancreatic Insufficiency? You'd test for that with Stool Elastase, and treat it with high-dose enzymes. Or is it Bile Acid Diarrhea? That test is SeHCAT, and the treatment is bile binders.

- My notes: two things the books don't cover, old impaction and colon hydrotherapy (Years of accumulated shit, above) and psychedelics for IBS (IBS, above).

#### Phase 5: Restoration & Maintenance (Let's Play Gardener!)

Once you're clear of infection, you have to rebuild a diverse ecosystem to prevent relapse. Dr. William Davis is back for that, along with Dr. Will Bulsiewicz (Fiber Fueled).

1. Start by re-seeding with Davis's high-dose "SIBO Yogurt", fermented for 36 hours. It contains L. reuteri, to heal the lining and boost oxytocin, and L. gasseri, to produce bacteriocins that kill SIBO.

2. Then feed the new healthy bacteria. Bulsiewicz says to slowly titrate plant diversity back into your diet, aiming for 30+ different plants per week. Does anyone count their plants?

3. Continue Pimentel's strict meal spacing from Phase 2 (4+ hours), forever, to keep the MMC cleaning wave active.

4. And fuel the mitochondria: see the fuels under Leaky gut, above (glutamine and butyrate).

- My notes: see "Leaky gut" above for colostrum, zinc carnosine, BPC-157, ozonated zeolite and the fuels, and "Sitting" above for movement and yoga.

#### Phase 6: Refractory Cases

- The last phase is from Neil Nathan (Toxic) and Annie Hopper (Wired for Healing). It's for people who are "sensitive to everything," or who relapse right away even though they've stuck to the protocol perfectly.

1. Check your environment (Nathan). Could it be mold/mycotoxins, or Lyme? Get assessed for those, and if it comes back positive, use specific binders (Cholestyramine, Charcoal, Clay).

2. Then check the brain. If you react to everything, Hopper says the issue is Limbic System Impairment, and you'd use "Brain Retraining" (DNRS) to stop the brain from firing false "danger" signals to the gut.

- My notes: Matt's note on mopping up mycotoxins with fermented colostrum is under Leaky gut, above.

## Optional Ready-Made Gut Stacks

I don't think there's one supplement stack (a set of supplements you take together) that heals everyone's gut, and no stack can do the phases above for you. So do those first, in order, and only go after bugs and parasites if your symptoms are still there after you've changed your diet. Then a stack can help with the rebuilding in Phase 5, once you're clear of infection and it's time to feed the good bacteria again.

A good stack should cover these 3 categories:

1. First, support the Microbiome with a well-tolerated probiotic or probiotic system.

2. Then keep the probiotics well-fed with a good prebiotic, and take something that helps short-chain fatty acid production, especially the main one, butyrate, or take butyrate itself.

3. Other nutrients will help support barrier repair, such as glutamine, zinc carnosine, colostrum, mucosal-support herbs, or similar compounds.

Gemini suggested these kits to me, but so far they're the best ones I've seen, tho I haven't used them myself, so check the reviews. Microbiome Labs Total Gut Restoration covers all three categories, with a probiotic, a prebiotic and a product for the gut lining in one kit, so it's the best general one. Dr. Ruscio's Leaky Gut Support Kit has a probiotic and two gut lining products, one of them glutamine, but no prebiotic, so it's more for when leaky gut is your main problem. Finally, Designs for Health GI Revive is only gut-lining nutrients and herbs, so you'd still need a probiotic and a prebiotic with it.

You'll also find "gut cleanse" stacks, built around oregano oil, wormwood, black walnut, berberine or other antimicrobial herbs. They kill bugs, like the herbal antibiotics in Phase 3, so they're only for when changing your diet hasn't cleared your symptoms. And even then I'd mostly skip them, because H2O2 works better without the side effects. The exception is berberine for H. pylori, the stomach bacteria that's usually behind heartburn you only get on an empty stomach: take it on its own, not in a cleanse stack (see Parasites, above).

Start any of these supplements or stacks at a low dose and build up slowly as your gut gets used to it. It's quite common for the prebiotic part especially to give you gas at first, since gas is what your bacteria make when they ferment the fiber or resistant starch, and a new probiotic can bloat you or upset your stomach for a while.

Some severe symptoms warrant seeing a doctor rather than considering this DIY approach at the moment. Take it seriously if you notice blood in your stool (if it's blood from upper digestive tract, it could be smelly, black & tarry, not red like from lower GI). Other serious signs to note: losing weight fast, severe pain, a fever or dehydration. And if you're pregnant, some herbs and supplements, like berberine, aren't safe. If your immune system is weak, some probiotics could be risky, since they're live bacteria that need to be kept in check to prevent overgrowth. If you have an IBD flare, cancer or another serious illness, the standard disclaimer applies: work out what to take with your doctor instead of putting a stack together yourself.
