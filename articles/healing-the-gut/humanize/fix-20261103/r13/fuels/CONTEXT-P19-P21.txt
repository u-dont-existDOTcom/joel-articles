Mitochondrial and cellular energy starvation is what you get when there's not enough of the fuels each type of cell in your gut prefers (glutamine and butyrate). Your gut barrier needs a lot of energy, to keep its tight junctions intact. If the cells lining your small intestine (called enterocytes) or the colonocytes lining your large intestine don't get that energy, the barrier loosens up. Then lipopolysaccharides (LPS, which come from the walls of bacteria) and other endotoxins leak through those (not-so-tight)-junction gaps into your bloodstream.

L-Glutamine (watch Dr. Rhonda vid) is the main fuel for your enterocytes, and for your immune cells when they're activated. Most food has to be digested before your cells can use it, and for protein that means being broken down into amino acids. But glutamine already is an amino acid, so your enterocytes can take it in as soon as it reaches them. Their mitochondria (the part of a cell that makes its energy) turn it into alpha-ketoglutarate (a-KG). And a-KG is one of the acids in the citric acid cycle itself, the loop mitochondria make that energy with. So that's why consuming pure glutamine gives energy to those cells faster than eating food. They then use their extra energy to repair the gut lining by pulling the tight junctions together, and that keeps LPS from getting through. Glutamine also provides nitrogen to help build new cells.

Take 5g a day as a baseline. The point of that baseline is to counter the breakdown of the gut lining that high-intensity or high-volume training causes, like CrossFit or intense cardio. If your immune system is dealing with an acute exposure, if your whole system is under a lot of stress, or if you have active IBD (Crohn's or colitis), raise it gradually to 10-30g a day.
