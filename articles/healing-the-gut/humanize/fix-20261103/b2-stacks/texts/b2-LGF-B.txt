Mitochondrial & cellular energy starvation (glutamine, butyrate, TCA intermediates): your gut barrier needs a lot of energy to keep the tight junctions intact, and it needs it right away, right there in the gut lining. If your enterocytes (small intestine) or colonocytes (large intestine) go without the fuels their mitochondria prefer, the barrier physically falls apart, which lets lipopolysaccharides (LPS) and endotoxins leak into your bloodstream.
L-Glutamine (watch Dr. Rhonda vid) is the main fuel for enterocytes, and for activated immune cells too. It skips the complex digestive steps and turns directly into alpha-ketoglutarate (α-KG) inside the mitochondria, where it powers the citric acid cycle. That immediate energy hit to the cells is what drives the repair of the gut mucosal barrier, and it prevents LPS from leaking through. Take 5g a day as a baseline to counter the gut mucosal breakdown you get from high-intensity or high-volume training (like CrossFit or intense cardio). During acute immune exposure or high systemic stress, work up to 10-30g a day. Same if you're dealing with active IBD (Crohn's/Colitis).
CAUTION: fast-multiplying cancer cells can also hijack glutamine to fuel their rapid replication. So if you have an active colon or liver malignancy right now, avoid the high-dose glutamine titrations (the liver sees it first, since the portal vein delivers it there).
Butyrate & SCFAs: the small intestine runs on glutamine, while the large intestine relies on short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) like butyrate, as I mentioned under diverticulosis. You can get these from raw resistant starch fermentation or your home-brewed SIBO yogurt, and they fuel your colonocytes and seal up the lower gut barrier. They also systematically suppress inflammatory signals.
TCA cycle intermediates (malic & citric acid): organic acids like malate and citrate feed directly into the exact same mitochondrial energy pathways as glutamine. You can take organic malic acid, or eat clean sour foods like tart cherries or green apples. Either way your gut epithelial cells get a rapid, alternative fuel, which speeds up mucosal healing from a different biochemical angle.
