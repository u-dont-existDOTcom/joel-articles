Mitochondrial fuels (glutamine, butyrate, TCA intermediates): your gut barrier needs a lot of energy, right there in the gut, to keep the tight junctions tight. If the cells lining your small intestine (enterocytes) or large intestine (colonocytes) don't get the fuels their mitochondria prefer, the barrier physically falls apart and lets lipopolysaccharides (LPS) and endotoxins leak into your bloodstream.
L-Glutamine (watch Dr. Rhonda vid) is the main fuel for enterocytes, and for activated immune cells. It skips the complicated digestive steps and gets turned straight into alpha-ketoglutarate (α-KG) inside the mitochondria, which powers the citric acid cycle. That quick energy hit drives the repair of the gut lining and stops LPS from getting through.
5g a day is the baseline, to counter the breakdown of the gut lining you get from high-intensity or high-volume training like CrossFit or intense cardio. Work up to 10-30g a day during acute immune exposure or high systemic stress, or if you have active IBD (Crohn's/Colitis).
But fast-growing cancer cells can grab glutamine too, to fuel their growth, so don't work up to the high doses if you have an active colon or liver cancer (the liver sees it first, since it comes through the portal vein).
Butyrate and SCFAs: the small intestine runs on glutamine, but the large intestine runs on short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) like butyrate. You get those from fermenting raw resistant starch, or from your home-brewed SIBO yogurt, and they fuel the colonocytes, lock down the lower gut barrier and suppress inflammatory signals.
TCA cycle intermediates (malic and citric acid): malate and citrate feed into the same mitochondrial energy pathways as glutamine. So clean sour stuff like organic malic acid, tart cherries or green apples is another quick fuel for the cells lining your gut, and speeds up healing of the lining from a different biochemical angle.
