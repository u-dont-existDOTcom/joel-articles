Fuels for the gut lining (glutamine, butyrate, TCA intermediates): the cells lining your gut need a lot of energy to keep the tight junctions closed, and without their fuels the barrier breaks down and lets LPS and endotoxins into your blood.
L-Glutamine (watch Dr. Rhonda vid) is the main fuel for the small intestine's cells. 5g a day is a good baseline, especially if you do hard training like CrossFit or intense cardio, and you can go up to 10-30g during acute immune exposure, high stress, or active IBD (Crohn's/Colitis). But skip the high doses if you have active colon or liver cancer, since cancer cells can use glutamine too.
Butyrate and the other short-chain fatty acids are the fuel for your colon's cells. You get them from fermenting raw resistant starch, or from your SIBO yogurt.
Malic and citric acid (organic malic acid, tart cherries, green apples) feed the same energy pathways as glutamine, so they're another fuel for healing the lining.
