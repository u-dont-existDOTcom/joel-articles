- Mitochondrial and cellular energy starvation: If you do CrossFit, that training breaks down your gut lining. Your gut barrier needs a huge amount of energy to keep its tight junctions intact, and the cells need that energy immediately, in the gut itself. The cells lining your small intestine are called enterocytes; the ones in your large intestine are colonocytes. When their mitochondria don't get their preferred fuels, the barrier falls apart, letting lipopolysaccharides (LPS, from bacterial cell walls) and other endotoxins leak into your bloodstream.

  - L-Glutamine ([watch Dr. Rhonda vid](https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=5DVUEKrekYw&ra=m)): Glutamine gives your gut lining the immediate energy that drives its repair and stops LPS from getting through. It's by far the best fuel for those small intestine cells, and for your immune cells when they're activated. It skips the complicated digestive steps. Inside the mitochondria, it converts directly into alpha-ketoglutarate (α-KG), which powers the citric acid cycle, the process mitochondria use to make energy.

    - Take 5g a day as a baseline to counter the breakdown of the gut lining from high-intensity or high-volume training (like CrossFit or intense cardio). Raise it gradually to 10–30g a day during an acute immune exposure or when your whole system is under a lot of stress. For active IBD (Crohn's or colitis), use that same gradual increase.

    - CAUTION: Cancer cells that multiply very fast can also use glutamine to fuel that growth. Don't raise it to the high doses if you have active colon or liver cancer. The liver gets the glutamine first, through the portal vein.

  - Your colon cells rely on butyrate and other short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) for fuel. These keep the lower gut barrier sealed, and they also keep suppressing inflammatory signals. You get them when your gut bacteria ferment raw resistant starch, or from your home-brewed SIBO yogurt.

  - Malic and citric acid (TCA cycle intermediates): Organic acids like malate and citrate go straight into the same mitochondrial energy pathways as glutamine. The cells lining your gut get a quick alternative fuel, and supplying those pathways with these acids speeds up healing of the lining through another biochemical route. Use clean, sour things such as organic malic acid. Tart cherries and green apples are food options too.
