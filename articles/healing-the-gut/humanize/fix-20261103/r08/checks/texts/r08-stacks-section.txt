A stack is a set of supplements you take together, and none of them replaces the phases above. I don't think there's one stack that heals everyone's gut, because guts go wrong for different reasons, and no kit can release a stuck vagus nerve or get your bile flowing. So whatever you take, keep to the order of the phases: get your digestion working mechanically first (Phase 1), then reset your diet and your meal spacing (Phase 2), and only go after bugs and parasites (Phase 3) if you still have symptoms after that. A stack is for the rebuilding in Phase 5, once you're clear of infection and it's time to feed the good bacteria again.
That said, some people want a ready-made stack rather than buying every ingredient for it separately. So, if I were to recommend a general "repair and rebuild" approach, it would be a stack that covers three categories:
First, support the Microbiome with a well-tolerated probiotic or probiotic system.
Then keep the probiotics well-fed with a good prebiotic, and take butyrate itself or something that helps short-chain fatty acid production, especially the main one, butyrate.
Other nutrients will help support barrier repair, such as glutamine, zinc carnosine, colostrum, mucosal-support herbs, or similar compounds.
Gemini suggested three kits to me, but so far they're the best ones I've seen, tho I haven't used them myself, so check the reviews. Microbiome Labs Total Gut Restoration covers all three categories, with a probiotic, a prebiotic and a product for the gut lining in one kit, so it's the best general one. Dr. Ruscio's Leaky Gut Support Kit has a probiotic and two lining products, one of them glutamine, but no prebiotic, so it's more for when leaky gut is your main problem. And Designs for Health GI Revive is only the third category, a powder of gut-lining nutrients and herbs, so you'd still need a probiotic and a prebiotic with it.
You'll also find "gut cleanse" stacks, built around oregano oil, wormwood, black walnut, berberine or other antimicrobial herbs. They kill bugs, like the herbal antibiotics in Phase 3, so they're only for when changing your diet hasn't cleared your symptoms. And even then I'd mostly skip them, because H2O2 works better without the side effects. The exception is berberine for H. pylori, the stomach bacteria that's usually behind heartburn you only get on an empty stomach: take it on its own, not in a cleanse stack (see Parasites, above).
Whichever kit you get, start each part at a low dose and build up as your gut gets used to it. That goes most for the prebiotic (resistant starch is one too), since your gut bacteria make gas when they ferment it, and for the probiotic, which can bloat you or upset your stomach at first as well.
Bleeding from your gut or fast weight loss can mean something more serious that supplements won't fix, so see a doctor before you start. So can bad pain or a fever, and so can getting dehydrated. If you're pregnant or your immune system is weak, work out what's safe with your doctor instead of putting a stack together yourself, since berberine isn't safe in pregnancy, and probiotics are live bacteria that a weak immune system can't always keep in check. That goes for an IBD flare too, and for cancer (see the glutamine caution above) or another serious illness.
