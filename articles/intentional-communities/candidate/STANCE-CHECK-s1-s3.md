# Stance check: sections 1–3 rewrite vs. the whole published essay

**1. Twin Oaks: the labor-credit system becomes a political system**
- Rewrite: "It was more like a tiny hippie town, but with a highly structured political system that didn't seem intuitive for hippies."
- Essay: "It felt more like a tiny hippie town with an unusually complicated labor-credit system." (Section 1, "The New Age May Dawn Suddenly"; the later visiting advice echoes it with "what labor system irritates you," in "What to Do This Month" under "Practice Comes Before Property")
- A reader would wrongly believe: Joel's point about Twin Oaks concerned its governance (and that it didn't suit hippies), when the essay's point is its unusually complicated labor-credit system.

**2. Joel snoring through meetings**
- Rewrite: "(Some people, including me, solved this problem by just going to sleep in the meeting and openly snoring.)"
- Essay: "Sometimes people solved that conflict by snoring through the meeting." (Section 1, "The New Age May Dawn Suddenly")
- A reader would wrongly believe: Joel himself fell asleep and snored in community meetings, which the essay never says.

**3. Freeloading: consequences and confrontation become know-how**
- Rewrite: "It requires knowing what to do if someone is leeching off the group and how to deal with it. It requires developing healthier forms of relationships."
- Essay: "Communal ownership therefore needs named stewardship, visible duties, consequences for chronic freeloading, and enough relational capacity to confront the problem before resentment becomes the real government." (Section 2, "Communes Are Hot Again," under "The Freeloader Problem")
- A reader would wrongly believe: Joel asks only that a group have a plan for freeloaders and generally healthier relationships, rather than insisting that chronic freeloading carry consequences and be confronted before resentment takes over.

**4. Learned helplessness becomes acted helplessness**
- Rewrite: "No, taking away the idea of a boss does not make everyone less passive, selfish, steal less, or stop trying to act helpless."
- Essay: "A community cannot run on the assumption that removing bosses removes passivity, selfishness, theft, or learned helplessness." (Section 2, "Communes Are Hot Again," under "The Freeloader Problem")
- A reader would wrongly believe: Joel thinks helpless members are putting on an act, where the essay names a learned condition.

**5. The meeting agenda as the place where hurt wins justice**
- Rewrite: "There’s no shared way to talk about the hurt itself, but the meeting agenda is the one place the group lets you push for something and win, so the angry communard goes there, trying to get some justice."
- Essay: "Jealousy and conflict should be addressed while they still belong to the people involved—before they acquire minutes, procedural objections, and a place near the bottom of an agenda nobody will finish." ("The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together," under "2. Relational pl/ork"); and "Eventually politics begins carrying emotional material that nobody knows how to name. People argue about schedules, committees, noise, food, or policy while the real subject sits nearby with its arms crossed." (Section 3, "Why Communities Keep Dying in the Same Two Ways," under "The secular failure")
- A reader would wrongly believe: Joel explains politicized hurt as members seeking justice in the one forum where they can win, whereas the essay offers no such mechanism and pictures such conflicts stalling at the bottom of agendas while the real subject goes unnamed.
