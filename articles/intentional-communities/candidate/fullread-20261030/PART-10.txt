# Appendix: Watch These, Then Read These

## Films

- ***Where’s Utopia?*** (1988) is a documentary by my father, made with Jerry Mintz. - ***A Place Called Chiapas*** (1998) is about the early days of the Zapatistas. - ***American Commune*** (2013) is about The Farm, seen through the eyes of two sisters who grew up there. Why this one? For this article, the view of the grown children is essential. - ***Commune*** (2005) is about Black Bear Ranch, and what radical freedom did to the relationships there. - ***A New We*** takes you on a tour of a bunch of ecovillages in Europe. How many different arrangements can hide under that one label? A lot, it turns out! - ***Within Reach*** follows a couple as they bike 6,500 miles, visiting a hundred or so communities. - ***Seeking the Good Life in America*** compares Twin Oaks, Acorn and Light Morning. - ***Voices of Cohousing*** shows the partial-community option, where you get high neighborliness and more private autonomy. - ***Inside the Bruderhof*** is about a high-commitment religious community, including the autonomy it requires members to give up. Oh, and the Bruderhof has a pretty extensive YouTube channel too. - ***Living in the Future: Lammas*** is about what happens when community-building meets land use and planning law.

## Books and Papers, in Reading Order

### For Child-Rearing

- ***The Continuum Concept*** by Jean Liedloff (1975) is a foundational natural-child-rearing book, based on her time with the Ye’kuana. Is it settled anthropology? No, it’s provocative observation. - ***Hunt, Gather, Parent*** by Michaeleen Doucleff (2021) is a practical, accessible book about kids taking part in family life. It’s also about autonomy, and spreading out the work of caring for them.

### Practical Community-Building Guides

- Where do you start with the nuts and bolts? ***Creating a Life Together*** by Diana Leafe Christian, for membership, money, land and agreements. - Want a guide to intentional community that covers the whole system? There’s ***Building Belonging*** by Yana Ludwig.

### Governance Manuals

- ***Many Voices One Song*** by Rau and Koch-Gonzalez is a practical manual for doing sociocracy. - ***Building United Judgment*** by Schaub and colleagues is about consensus decision-making. - Ludwig and Gimnig’s ***The Cooperative Culture Handbook*** has a bunch of exercises for being more cooperative, dealing with conflict and building group culture.

### What Helps Communities Last—and Whether Longevity Is Enough

- ***Communities That Abide*** by Dmitry Orlov makes an uncomfortable argument: the communities that last a long time often rely on a religious ideology which makes members feel alienated from the wider society. Basically, the more cultish, the more durable, since cults are designed to capture people.

It makes one wonder, is longevity alone a meaningful measure, when a community may keep going without its members flourishing? That’s the question in “Measuring Success in Intentional Communities”, a paper by Rubin, Willis and Ludwig. - ***Commitment and Community*** by Rosabeth Moss Kanter is a classic. It compares a bunch of nineteenth-century communes, and the practices that built commitment in them. - ***Alienation and Charisma*** by Benjamin Zablocki is field research on communes, and on the role charisma plays in them. - Jo Freeman’s “The Tyranny of Structurelessness” (1970) is the classic essay on hidden hierarchy. Can it be harder to challenge than explicit authority? She argues it can.

### For the Zapatistas

- ***The Fire and the Word*** by Gloria Muñoz Ramírez and Subcomandante Marcos is about how the Zapatista movement came about. - Want it straight from the Zapatistas? Their own archive is Enlace Zapatista, and look especially for their 2023 explanations of the new autonomy.
