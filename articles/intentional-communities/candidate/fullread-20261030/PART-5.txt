# Why Communities Keep Dying in the Same Two Ways

The communes I knew tended to fail inwardly in one of two directions.

# The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together

image 7

Communities tend to solve whichever part most interests the founders. The political people write bylaws. The spiritual people meditate. The therapists process. The psychedelic people discover that everyone is God and forget to decide whose name goes on the deed.

I divide the model into four parts. Yes, four sounds suspiciously tidy for something involving jealousy, children, land, money, God, and iboga. Bear with me. I’ve published detailed guides for the first three—free, with no funnel and no retreat to buy. Most of this article is about the fourth.

Food, housing, and logistics are not enough reason to bind whole lives together. Shared inner practice gives the difficulty meaning: we are not merely maintaining property together, but helping one another heal and become less governed by fear. An outward escuelita and federation purpose adds another reason to keep going—the community exists not only to protect the people already inside it, but to help other people build.

## All is not well in paradise

The warmth has hard limits here. One noteworthy example is the plight of LGBTQ+ people here, who cross them automatically.

Sénégal passed a law in March 2026 that increased the penalty for “consensual same-sex intimacy” to 5 to 10 years in prison, and it also made public advocacy for homosexuality a crime. Homosexuals were already facing arrests, family rejection, harassment, assault and mob violence, so ironically, some say they’re actually safer once they’re arrested, because then at least the mob can’t get at them. And also ironically, LGBTQ+ people are using this prejudice in their favor to get refugee status and live in rich countries.

Being in community isn’t automatically liberating. It all depends on who is allowed to belong without pretending.

One other shadow side of the otherwise forgiving social culture here is that people may go to great lengths to avoid confrontation. Who stole the sheep? We all know, but nobody wants to cause a stink by bringing it up. 🙉

Where I live, neighbors steal our moringa pods every morning. Every morning! They could plant a moringa tree in their own yard. Apparently maintaining this relationship with ours is more entertaining. 😂

Everybody here has sheep (even me), and they are super cute. It can feel like you’re living in another world, with the music, the animals, the joking, the praying, and just being around people all the time.

Of course, the old communal ways here are changing. Young people move to the city, get a job, get a phone, eat imported food, become part of the wider world, etc. (some of the men even make the dangerous journey by sea to Europe). So what about the families? The ones that used to share almost everything are now more reliant on cash and individual opportunities.

## Lessons from the Black Tax

Then there’s the so-called “Black Tax,” a strange legacy of the old communal system: if you’ve got any money at all, you’re expected to share it with your parents, your siblings, your cousins, to pay school fees, medical bills, funerals, etc. I understand the instinct. In the ancestral way of doing things nobody made it thru life alone, and the people who fed and raised you just naturally expected you to take care of the whole community with whatever you got from life as you matured. That’s how the whole organism survived (it wasn’t charity).

Double click to interact with video

But these days, the pressure to give back can be soul destroying. People have a lot less of the communal life that held the obligation up (the shared land, work, childcare, food and daily belonging), but they still carry it. So if one of them makes it into the cash economy, it’s really hard to build any independence or long-term stability, because they may be expected to carry everybody. And the relatives stay dependent too, since the payments aren’t in something that builds on itself, like compost for soil. Injecting cash into non-cash economies might feel good, but like candy, it can also undermine the health of the local relationships and sustainability.

# How-To Manual

## The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together

![Diagram of a house. The roof is Community. Communities tend to focus on the aspect of their project that was most interesting to the original founders, for example political types getting super into writing by-laws, or spiritual types just meditating all the time, or therapists processing each other, or people who like psychedelics discovering they’re all God together and never getting to the point of deciding who will put their name on the deed.

I’ve broken the model I propose down into the 4 parts shown in the above diagram. Note, Pl/ork means play + work. Calling therapy “inner work” forgets the inner child, who also wants to play. (Forgive the neologism. I hope pl/ork isn’t too corny. 🌽): 1) inner pl/ork (reparenting), 2) relational pl/ork (peer counseling especially), 3) medicine pl/ork (like loveyhuasca or iboga), and 4) the practical logistical foundation which is most of this article, since I have guides separately on the other parts. Obviously the details are complicated, and jealousy, children, money, land, God, and iboga don’t fit neatly into four boxes, but let’s go with it.

### 1. Inner pl/ork

Probably the most obvious type of pl/ork is reparenting. Here’s an example: you teach your inner child geography, while s/he teaches you the world is a cat!

![Meme: a world map with a cat’s outline traced around the continents, captioned “World is just a cat playing with Australia”.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!7tAF!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F66730244-8446-4321-83f0-f353d46a8e1e_770x844.jpeg)

Inner-child reparenting means learning to identify as the best holistic inner adult, which is comprised of the three inner adult roles: Nurturer, Protector, and Guide. The newly developed adult reparents their newly recognized inner child. The child receives all that love, protection, and guidance that you give it, and as a result, your little one gains more freedom to remain alive inside you, which brings back the joy of life that many adults have forgotten about.

And this reparenting journey is built on somatic regulation first. I.e., listening to the body’s feelings before making a decision with the mind. And that actually symbolizes the adult/child relationship to me in some way as well.

### 2. Relational pl/ork

Hearthwork distributes counseling among peers: equal time, attention without interpretation or fixing, and then you switch.

The idea is that the capacity to be there for people when they need help isn’t located in one professional or guru, or the founder of the group that everyone emotionally depends on, but in the community as a whole.

But since we’re merging peer therapy with community, we also need to make sure that the person caring about you and listening to you shouldn’t translate that information into administration over your life, the way a professional therapist might diagnose you as this or that and then suddenly you’re ineligible for certain careers. Although obviously that information can come out regardless when people are in close quarters.

Another thing is knowing which problems are beyond what peers can handle and need outside or expert help.

### 3. Medicine pl/ork

Community can reach new heights by carefully incorporating serious plant medicines with peer guidance rather than exalting any one person as a special shaman. I’ll go more in detail on this in “The Medicine Part” section below. Just because taking part in the ceremonies is optional doesn’t mean people don’t have to work with the other parts.

### 4. The container

This is the part about membership, land, resources, governance, children, leaving, etc.

The first three parts are the inside of the container, and this container is what gives those a stable place to manifest. It’s the practical logistical foundation that gets built once the architects can work well together.
