Communes Are Hot Again 🔥
Suddenly, the longing to escape loneliness, which inspired my father’s movie, is everywhere again. Back in 2023, ironically, just the year after Covid lockdowns ended, the U.S. Surgeon General even put out an advisory, saying roughly half of American adults experience loneliness, and it treated social connection as a **public-health** issue.
It’s obvious that a lot of people can’t tolerate modern life in isolation anymore (the UN’s rules for prisoners literally ban more than 15 days of it). Folks may disagree about politics, food, God, sex, or whether shoes are oppressive, but they agree that something about the current arrangement is starving them.
What’s starving them is anomie. We evolved to live in small groups where everybody knows, loves, and trusts everybody. In a large society, nobody knows anybody well enough or for long enough to tell who can be trusted—even your next-door neighbor is often a total stranger. That’s how large societies end up run by psychopaths. (Yes, I’m always going on about this.)
Check out the Google Trends for “ecovillage” and “intentional community”. After years of decline, interest climbed sharply in the mid-2020s.
![Google Trends chart of worldwide search interest from 2004 to now: highest in 2004, sinking to a low, flat line through the 2010s, then shooting back up in 2025.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!y0li!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fae1e4fd0-563f-4c2a-8228-7a549a4fc4d7_1211x373.png)
Apart from the Covid lockdowns just prior, AI became serious around this time, and it started changing people’s jobs and relationships, and how they see the future. Suddenly, forming a village sounded like a good backup plan, not just a hippie utopian whim.
It’s poppin’ on Instagram too! 🔥 Chantress Seba—my favorite light-language love influencer—posted an application for people interested in starting a commune. I applied, although I don’t think she’s coming to Africa, where I’m at. When I saw the post, many thousands of people appeared to be interested. That was encouraging, but imagine Chantress Seba the next morning opening her inbox to a zillion excited applications to co-found her dream village. 😅
Later, when another influencer with a big following floated the commune idea, the comment section started to become a recruitment board, where people were apparently forming joint operations together right in there.
