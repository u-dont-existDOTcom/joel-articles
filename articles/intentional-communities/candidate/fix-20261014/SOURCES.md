# Sources for the facts added on 2026-10-14

Each fix that explains a name at its first mention (Joel's rule of 2026-10-09) says only what these sources say.

- **The Island Pond raid** (section 8): on 22 June 1984 Vermont authorities raided the Northeast Kingdom Community Church in Island Pond (known today as the Twelve Tribes) and took 112 children into custody amid allegations of child abuse; Judge Frank Mahady, noting the state's inability to name individual cases, refused to let the state hold the children for examination and ruled the raid unconstitutional, and the children went home that day.
  - VTDigger, "40 years later, Island Pond has little interest in revisiting its historic raid" (2024): https://vtdigger.org/2024/06/21/40-years-later-island-pond-has-little-interest-in-revisiting-its-historic-raid/ (the raid "wasn't based on any specific claims involving any specific individuals"; "Hours after the raid began, Mahady ruled it unconstitutional")
  - Nicole Bazilchuk's article on the raid: https://www.nasw.org/users/nbazilchuk/Articles/islandpond.htm ("Ninety state troopers and 50 social workers took 112 children into custody amid allegations of child abuse"; Mahady "looked at the state's evidence, including its inability to name individual cases of child abuse, and ruled the raid unconstitutional"; "The children went home")
  - The Christian Science Monitor, News in Brief, 26 June 1984: https://www.csmonitor.com/1984/0626/062634.html (the judge "refused to allow the state to hold the children for physical examination"; "After questioning, the children were returned to their parents")
- **The Ye'kuana** (section 8): a Cariban-speaking rainforest people of the Caura and Orinoco regions of Venezuela (and of Roraima in Brazil); Jean Liedloff met them on a diamond-hunting expedition to Venezuela and went back to live with them, and *The Continuum Concept* (1975) is based on her time living with them.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Liedloff
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ye%EA%9E%8Ckuana
- **ZEGG and Tamera** (section 7): ZEGG (Zentrum für experimentelle Gesellschaftsgestaltung, "Center for Experimental Cultural Design") was founded in 1991 on the edge of Bad Belzig, about 80 km south-west of Berlin, and works on "innovative approaches to love and sexuality"; Dieter Duhm, Sabine Lichtenfels and Rainer Ehrenpreis co-founded Tamera in south-western Portugal in 1995.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZEGG_(community)
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieter_Duhm
- **Caracoles** (section 9): "caracoles" means snails (or seashells); since 2003 the Zapatista autonomous municipalities were coordinated through caracoles as community centers, Oventik among them; in 2023 the Zapatistas dissolved that structure.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapatista_territories
- **The technosphere** (section 6): "all of the technological objects and systems manufactured and created by humans", a concept credited to the geologist Peter Haff, the historian of science Jürgen Renn and others.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthroposphere
