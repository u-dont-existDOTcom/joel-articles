# Stance check 4: last paragraph of section 10 (wordings A and G)

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**Wording G, first sentence: narrows a position stated in the paragraph just before it**

- Rewrite: "That's solidarity running into each community's claim on its own stores."
- Essay (section 10, "Federation Without Building Another State", the paragraph just before this one): "Solidarity is not unlimited joint liability—or an unlimited claim on another community's labor, stores, land, or tools."
- A reader would come away believing the essay limits solidarity only where stored goods are concerned, and would miss labor, land, tools, and joint liability for another community's debts.

The narrowing is mild. G's next sentence ("a collision of two principles like that") still lets the record-keeping rule cover any collision, so only the description of the tension gets narrower. The rule itself doesn't.

Wording A, and the sentences both wordings share: no conflicts found.

## Contradictions in the published text

None found. The published paragraph ("And when two principles collide, don't preserve only the final rule…") doesn't take back any of its own sentences. It fits section 4's "answers remain fluid and organic instead of freezing into doctrine", because in it rules change and cases get rerun. Keeping case records doesn't clash with section 10's warning that federation records cost labor, or with its "Federation Without Building Another State" heading, and section 13 also accepts that "autonomous institutions can review local power without becoming the state." None of Joel's newer stated positions says anything about how communities record hard cases, so none of them conflicts with this paragraph.

Conflicts in the rewrite: 1 (wording G; wording A has 0). Contradictions in the published text: 0.
