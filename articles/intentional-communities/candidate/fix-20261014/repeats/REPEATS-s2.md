## 2. Contradictions

**1. B246–B247 (and B250) / B307–B308: decide case by case, or set it in advance?** (the clearest)
- B247: "It depends on so many factors that I can't decide any of this ahead of time."
- B308: "So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular: what conduct triggers immediate protective action"
- The pull: B246–B247 say that when someone gets removed, and who gets an exception, can't be decided ahead of time and is up to the people involved. B307–B308 say to decide in advance what conduct triggers protective action and how removal works. B181 and B293 also say to settle things beforehand. The two could be reconciled if the process is fixed in advance and the outcome decided case by case, but the article never says so. B250 extends the case-by-case stance to contact with outsiders.
- Better placed: B307–B308, in the section about removal, which gives the steps.

**2. B7 / B53 and B263–B269: come to heal, or heal first?** (moderate)
- B7: "people would live in the healing communities I’m proposing, to heal and to learn the relational and practical methods"
- B53: "A place where people who've done the healing first can join without pretending they're fully finished"
- B269: "A founding group shouldn’t pretend it can give you those."
- The pull: B7 presents the communities as places people come to heal. B53 and B263–B269 say people should already have done the healing, and that a founding group can't provide treatment. Two things partly bridge this: B53's not fully finished, and B266's note that larger groups have more spare capacity. But the article never says which kind of healing B7 means.
- Better placed: B53 and B269 carry the article's actual position. B7 is the opening statement that raises the question.

**3. B239 / B305: do visitors get terms?** (mild)
- B239: "Friends who come to visit don't get written terms, though. That's not how anything works"
- B305: "Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters, and interns do not need full political membership to have rights."
- The pull: B239 says visiting friends get only unwritten terms. B305 says visitors need clear terms, a way to be heard, and review whenever a decision affects their housing, safety or similar matters. B239 describes the informal stage before a community formalizes, B305 describes a formal path to membership, and clear terms are not necessarily written ones. Still, the article doesn't connect the two passages.
- Better placed: B305, in the membership section.

**4. B142–B143 / B160: medical choices protected, or shared vaccination values required?** (mild)
- B143: "No member should have to prove their ideological loyalty when they're scared or sick."
- B160: "it's one of the values I would absolutely need to share with people in a community, if I was going to raise children there."
- The pull: B142–B143 protect members' choices to accept or refuse different levels of care, with no loyalty tests. B160 makes shared vaccination values a condition for raising children in a community. B143 is about adults' own care, and B160 is about children and about choosing which community to join. But the article doesn't say how a parent who disagrees about vaccination would be treated.
- Better placed: B160 states the condition directly. B143 leaves it open.
