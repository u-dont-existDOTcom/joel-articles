# Stance check: section 8 rewrite, "The Grown Children Get the Final Review"

I checked each rewrite sentence that states a position, wish, rule, assessment, or claim about how things work against the whole published essay. Two sentences change a stated position. Every other position-bearing sentence is consistent with the essay.

## 1. Vaccination: a values-fit test becomes a demand that others agree with Joel (strengthens; Joel's own criterion becomes a requirement on other members)

**Rewrite:** "it's something I would absolutely need people in a community to agree with me on, if I was going to raise children there."

**Essay, section 8 ("The Grown Children Get the Final Review"):** "It is one of the values that would determine whether I could raise children inside a community at all." The same paragraph sets the shared bar: "communities need enough agreement on children’s health that parents can actually trust one another." Later in section 8 ("Discuss Child-Rearing Values…"): "plus enough room for parents to remain parents." Section 6 ("Which Connections to the Technosphere Do You Keep?") expects a community to decide "how to treat somebody who chooses a different level of medical involvement."

**Wrong takeaway:** Joel requires other members to adopt his own vaccination stance, rather than naming one value that decides where he could raise children and asking for enough agreement for parents to trust one another.

The next rewrite sentence adds to this impression: "I'm not even considering what would work for everyone" replaces "I’m not trying to design a village for everyone," and reads as indifference to other families, where the essay only admits the village won't suit everyone.

## 2. Fear and abuse prevention: a "can" claim becomes categorical (hedge dropped)

**Rewrite:** "Fear like that won't actually prevent abuse, but it can destroy that gift."

**Essay, section 8, "Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection":** "Fear can destroy that gift without actually preventing abuse."

**Wrong takeaway:** Joel asserts as fact that wariness toward unrelated adults never prevents abuse, where the essay claims only that fear can destroy the gift while failing to prevent abuse.
