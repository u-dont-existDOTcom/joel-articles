# Stance check: section 8 rewrite ("The Grown Children Get the Final Review") against the whole published essay (d1)

**1.**
- Rewrite: "I'm not trying to create a village for all people, just for a community that shares enough in common…"
- Essay: "I’m not currently recruiting anyone or founding a community." (Communes Are Hot Again, under "The Freeloader Problem"); also "I’m in Sénégal and expect to buy land, but I’m not announcing a commune." (Practice Comes Before Property, under "What to Do This Month"). The published version of this line says only "I’m not trying to design a village for everyone" (The Grown Children Get the Final Review).
- A reader would wrongly believe: Joel is actively trying to create a village and has already decided who it is for, though the essay says he is not founding one.

**2.**
- Rewrite: "just for a community that shares enough in common to trust each other with the health of their children"
- Essay: "communities need enough agreement on children’s health that parents can actually trust one another" (The Grown Children Get the Final Review); see also "The vaccination policy arrives two years later carrying documents." (Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View)
- A reader would wrongly believe: the bar is general like-mindedness for Joel's own village, not specific agreement on children's health that communities in general need before parents can trust one another.

**3.**
- Rewrite: "If all the children stay, how would anyone know whether they'd come back if they were free to leave?"
- Essay: "A group can have high retention because people love it—or because leaving costs too much." (Founderism: The Four Questions); the published line here is "A community whose children never leave cannot know whether they would freely return." (The Door Must Open Outward at Adulthood)
- A reader would wrongly believe: when every child stays, the children are presumably not free to leave, whereas the essay says staying alone can't tell love apart from the cost of leaving.

**4.**
- Rewrite: "and who would notice and act on any kind of problem that might come up"
- Essay: "many adults who know them, enjoy them, notice changes, and will act when something is wrong"; also children's territory lets them "resolve ordinary disputes, and experience some freedom from constant adult management" (both Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection)
- A reader would wrongly believe: the adults worth having step in on any problem a child has, rather than noticing changes in the child and acting when something is wrong, which cuts against children settling ordinary disputes themselves.

Conflicts found: 4
