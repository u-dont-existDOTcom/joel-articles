The realistic standard isn't unlimited freedom. It's just not making it artificially impossible for people to leave. Kids who grow up without ever using money especially need a decent education in how the money world operates, and what to do when they have to deal with it: jobs, rent, contracts, banks, scams, ID, getting around, and how to ask for help.

They also need a good set of transferable skills and education, good relationships with people outside the community, access to their own records and documents, and at least one plausible next step outside the community (school, an apprenticeship, work, family, another community). It would be great if the community could also provide resources to smooth the transition, like temporary housing, travel expenses, tools or a little money, but even if it can't, it can at least not make things worse by punishing people for leaving, or by holding back knowledge or records a young adult would need to function outside the community. No upbringing can provide perfect freedom, and the point isn't to erase path dependence. It's to not turn belonging into a trap.
