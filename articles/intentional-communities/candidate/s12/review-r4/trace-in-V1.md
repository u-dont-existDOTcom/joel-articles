# Section 12 pair (V1): published paragraphs and rewrite

Section 12 is "The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn't For". This pair is its end: a lead-in, a list of four items set as paragraphs, and a closing paragraph. Earlier in the section the author defines "free resource" (borrowed from Re-evaluation Counseling) as the attention, steadiness, skill and practical capacity a person has left over for others once they've dealt with enough of their own immediate material, and "need" as the help a person needs at a given time.

## P16pub+L1pub–L4pub+P17pub → P16f+L1–L4+P17v1

CONTEXT BEFORE: How to use the framing of “I don’t have the capacity to…” without it becoming a respectable way to exclude disabled people, poor people, people who aren’t very articulate or don’t have the language of therapy at their fingertips, people who are simply not convenient – I don’t know. It’s not going to be solved by pretending it’s unlimited; that’s going to require actually making concrete criteria, allowing for outside review, allowing for re-visiting a “not now” decision, and all of that. And even then, the power is dangerous.

PUBLISHED:

This community may not be right for you *right now* if:

You are fleeing an active crisis and mainly need safety, treatment, housing, or legal help. A founding group should not pretend it can replace those.

You want the feeling of village life without responsibility for governance, maintenance, conflict, and care.

You are unwilling to examine your behavior when jealousy or conflict appears.

Your current need greatly exceeds your available resource. That says nothing final about your worth. It describes timing. The first step may be building support and capacity where you already are.

A humane membership process should be able to say “later,” “in another role,” or “with more support” instead of converting every limit into rejection.

REWRITE:

This community may not be a good fit for you *right now* if:

You’re running from some crisis right now and mainly need a place to land, get treatment, housing, legal aid, etc. (A founding group shouldn’t pretend it can give you those.)

You want the feeling of village life but don’t want to take on governance, maintenance, conflict, care, etc.

You’re not willing to look at yourself when jealousy or conflict comes up.

You have a need that far outstrips your free resource right now. (This doesn’t mean you’re bad or doomed, it’s about timing; it may mean you need to take a first step of finding support where you are so you can build up your resources.)

(There should be ways of not flat-out rejecting people who fall into these categories, like saying “later,” or “in another role,” or “with more support.”)

CONTEXT AFTER: Land can wait. The first community is the small group considering whether to build one.
