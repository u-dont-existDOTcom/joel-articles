Section 12 is "The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn't For". The rewrite keeps the published order. In the published section the list after "This community may not be right for you right now if:" is four separate items; the rewrite folds the lead-in and the first item into one paragraph and starts the others with "Or if", "The same goes if" and "And if". The published P8 ("Everyone carries both, and the ratio changes.") and P9 (the author's own story) are unchanged and not traced here.

## P12pub → P12a

CONTEXT BEFORE: A group can only take on as much need as its members have free resource to meet. Eight people with two in a long crisis may end up spending most of their attention on emergency care. Eighty people can often spread that same need across lots of relationships and practical roles.

PUBLISHED:

The ability to welcome somebody is therefore partly a resource question rather than a measure of compassion. Saying yes beyond capacity can injure the existing members and the person who was promised support.

REWRITE:

So whether a group can welcome somebody is partly a question of resources, not a measure of how compassionate it is. Saying yes beyond what the group can carry can hurt the people already there, and the person who was promised support.

CONTEXT AFTER: It’s a tough paradox. Small communities need new members the most, and they can handle disruption the least. Eight people still have to grow food, fix equipment, keep the books, look after the kids, and keep the water system working. The well doesn’t care how beautiful the new member’s heart is.
