# Section pairs (r6): published paragraph and rewrite

The section is "The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For", from an essay about founding intentional communities. The rewrite keeps the published order.

## P13pub → P13p2t

CONTEXT BEFORE: The ability to welcome somebody is therefore partly a resource question rather than a measure of compassion. Saying yes beyond capacity can injure the existing members and the person who was promised support.

PUBLISHED:

The difficult paradox is that small communities need members most and can absorb disruption least. Eight people still need food grown, equipment repaired, accounts kept, children cared for, and the water system working. The well does not become more understanding because the new member has a beautiful heart.

REWRITE:

There is a difficult paradox to consider. The need for new members in a small community is greater, but the ability of that community to tolerate the disruption of new members is less. With only eight people in the community, someone still has to grow the food and fix whatever breaks. Somebody has to balance the books and watch the kids. And the water system has to keep working. The water well doesn’t care how beautiful someone’s heart is.

CONTEXT AFTER: So early members usually need more available resource than acute need, plus practical skills and a willingness to learn.

So early on, members usually need to have more available resource than acute need, and some practical skills to contribute. They usually need to be willing to learn, too.

CONTEXT AFTER: I still don’t know how to keep this language of capacity from becoming a respectable way to exclude people who are disabled, poor, less articulate, unfamiliar with therapeutic language, or simply inconvenient. Pretending capacity is unlimited won’t solve that. Any real process would need concrete criteria, outside review, and a way to revisit a “not now” decision. Even then, the power remains dangerous.
