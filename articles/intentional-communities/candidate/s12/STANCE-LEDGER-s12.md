## Preservation units (one per inseparable meaning)

- P1: U1 the article asks people to talk about hard personal things, all eight named (jealousy, God, death, childhood, money, sex, psychedelics, what happens when somebody wants to leave). U2 many intelligent adults would rather talk about something practical, for hours (the solar panels quip: keep its point).
- P2: U3 he understands why. U4 modern life trains us to perform competence and hide whatever complicates it. U5 screens let us escape discomfort instantly. U6 institutions reward narrow skills, then act surprised that nobody can sit through a conflict without appointing a subcommittee or blocking somebody (quip: keep both halves).
- P3: U7 the question isn't whether someone is "good enough". U8 the practical question has three parts: attention available for others, willingness to examine their own reactions, and whether the group can support them without being consumed.
- P4: U9 four kinds of people: years of practice; lucky in childhood; surviving an emergency, needing care more than responsibility; most of us move between these over a lifetime.
- P5: U10 the community shares some (not all) of the functions society gives therapists; peer counseling (the Hearthwork link) spreads the capacity to listen, accompany and help people process ordinary emotional material.
- P6: U11 that doesn't make the community infinitely absorbent.
- P7: U12 "free resource" is borrowed from Re-evaluation Counseling (link kept): attention, steadiness, skill and practical capacity a person has after dealing with enough of their own immediate material. U13 "need" is what currently requires support.
- P8: U14 everyone carries both, and the ratio changes.
- P9: U15 his own need has exceeded his free resource many times. "Good Lord." U16 the facts, exactly: during one stretch his first wife, psychotic, tried to kill him because she believed he was her sociopathic father; then she took his son (link kept); later she tried to kill his mother, while her pimp stalked his sister's family until they moved to Canada. No fact changed, added or softened.
- P10: U17 he could go on; the arithmetic is visible; he had very little left for anybody else. U18 when he says someone may need to stabilize and do inner pl/ork before helping found a community, he isn't speaking from a mountaintop.
- P11: U19 a group can absorb only as much need as its members have free resource to meet. U20 eight people with two in sustained crisis may spend most of their attention on emergency care; eighty can often spread the same need across many relationships and practical roles (numbers exact; "may", "often" kept).
- P12: U21 so whether a group can welcome someone is partly a question of resources, not a measure of compassion ("partly" and "therefore" kept). U22 saying yes beyond capacity can hurt the existing members and the person promised support.
- P13: U23 the paradox: small communities need members most and can absorb disruption least. U24 eight people still need the food grown, equipment repaired, accounts kept, children cared for, the water system working. U25 the well doesn't care how good the new member's heart is (quip: keep its point).
- P14: U26 so early members usually need more available resource than acute need, plus practical skills and a willingness to learn ("usually").
- P15: U27 he still doesn't know how to keep capacity language from becoming a respectable way to exclude people who are disabled, poor, less articulate, unfamiliar with therapeutic language, or simply inconvenient (five groups). U28 pretending capacity is unlimited won't solve it. U29 a real process would need concrete criteria, outside review, and a way to revisit a "not now" decision. U30 even then the power remains dangerous.
- P16 and L1 to L4: U31 the community may not be right for you right now ("right now" kept) if: (a) you're fleeing an active crisis and mainly need safety, treatment, housing or legal help, which a founding group shouldn't pretend to replace; (b) you want the feeling of village life without responsibility for governance, maintenance, conflict and care; (c) you won't examine your behavior when jealousy or conflict appears; (d) your current need greatly exceeds your available resource, which says nothing final about your worth, describes timing, and the first step may be building support and capacity where you already are.
- P17: U32 a humane membership process should be able to say "later", "in another role" or "with more support" instead of turning every limit into rejection.

Logical relations are units too: P11's "may" and "often"; P12's "therefore" and "partly ... rather than"; P13's "paradox" (need most, absorb least); P15's "Even then"; the list's "right now" and "if".
