# Section 12 pairs (U2): published paragraph and rewrite

Section 12 is "The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn't For". The rewrite keeps the published order and paragraphs: one rewrite paragraph for each published one. The published list after "This community may not be right for you *right now* if:" is four items set as paragraphs, and so is the rewrite's. Text in square brackets in the context is a chart that sits between P4 and P5.

## P13pub → P13o1

CONTEXT BEFORE: So it’s partly a problem of resources, not compassion -- how many people can a group take in without hurting the existing members or the people they are taking in?

PUBLISHED:

The difficult paradox is that small communities need members most and can absorb disruption least. Eight people still need food grown, equipment repaired, accounts kept, children cared for, and the water system working. The well does not become more understanding because the new member has a beautiful heart.

REWRITE:

There is a difficult paradox to consider. The need for new members in a small community is greater, but the ability of that community to tolerate the disruption of new members is less. Whether there are eight people in the community or eighty, someone still has to grow the food, fix the cars, balance the books, watch the kids, and maintain the water system. The water well doesn’t care how beautiful someone’s heart is.

CONTEXT AFTER: So early on, members usually need to have more resource than acute need, and some skill to contribute, and a willingness to learn.

CONTEXT BEFORE: How to use the framing of “I don’t have the capacity to…” without it becoming a respectable way to exclude disabled people, poor people, people who aren’t very articulate or don’t have the language of therapy at their fingertips, people who are simply not convenient – I don’t know. PUBLISHED:

This community may not be right for you *right now* if:

You are fleeing an active crisis and mainly need safety, treatment, housing, or legal help. A founding group should not pretend it can replace those.

You want the feeling of village life without responsibility for governance, maintenance, conflict, and care.

You are unwilling to examine your behavior when jealousy or conflict appears.

Your current need greatly exceeds your available resource. That says nothing final about your worth. It describes timing. The first step may be building support and capacity where you already are.

REWRITE:

This community may not be a good fit for you *right now* if:

You’re running from some crisis right now and mainly need a place to land, get treatment, housing, legal aid, etc. (A founding group shouldn’t pretend it can give you those.)

You want all of the benefits of village life but don’t want to take on governance, maintenance, conflict, care, etc.

You’re not willing to look at yourself when jealousy or conflict comes up.

You have a need that far outstrips your ability to bring in a resource for it right now. (This doesn’t mean you’re bad or doomed, it’s about timing; it may mean you need to take a first step of finding support where you are so you can build up your resources.)

CONTEXT AFTER: (There should be ways of not flat-out rejecting people who fall into these categories, like saying “later,” or “in another role,” or “with more support.”)
