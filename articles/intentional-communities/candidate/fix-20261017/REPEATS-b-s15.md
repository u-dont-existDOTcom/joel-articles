# Repeats check b-s15

Scope: every block of "What This Looks Like From Sénégal" (B314-B343) was checked against every other block there and against the rest of the article. Only items that involve at least one Sénégal block are listed. Sénégal sub-parts: opening (B315-B323, before the first sub-heading), All is not well in paradise (B324-B333), Lessons from the Black Tax (B334-B343).

## 1. Repeats, in article order

**1. B83, B219, B338: a moneyless community still needs a small cash budget for outside demands.**
- B83 (The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together, 4. The container): "a community that is nearly entirely moneyless, but has a small collective cash boundary on the outside"
- B219 (From One Community to a Movement, Federated Communities Make a Movement): "If the legal or economic environment requires money, the federation also needs to have a cash budget for dealing with it."
- B338 (Sénégal, Lessons from the Black Tax): "Life inside the commune can go without cash, but you'll still need money for taxes, hospital bills, visas"
- Later instances: B219 is one conditional sentence and adds only the federation level. B338 adds a longer list of what the cash is for, the question of who handles it, and the risk of a status hierarchy ("how to stop access to outside money from creating a new status hierarchy"). The core claim is the same, and a reader would feel told it again.
- Better placed: B83, because it sits inside the staged-money argument. B338 has the richer detail but arrives as if it were a new point.

**2. B318, B321, B323: spirituality shows up on public walls.**
- B318 (Sénégal, opening): "It's in the music, in the pictures everywhere, in the murals, in the names, in the conversations."
- B321 (Sénégal, opening): "And elsewhere there are murals of Sufi leaders."
- B323 (Sénégal, opening, image caption): "Graffiti in Dakar, Sénégal: Tolerance! Openness! Union! Happiness! Love!" This is the same wording B321 has already quoted from the wall.
- Later instances: B321 adds the actual graffiti text and the contrast with the walls the author grew up around, so it earns its place, though its mention of murals restates B318. B323 adds nothing; it repeats the quoted graffiti two blocks later.
- Better placed: B321 carries the point best. The caption is the redundant instance.

**3. B318, B319, B330: daily life there feels like another world (prayer, music, jokes).**
- B318 (Sénégal, opening): "Sénégal is unlike anywhere else I've lived." and "Five times a day the prayers pour out into the streets."
- B319 (Sénégal, opening): "It's the most joking culture I've ever been in"
- B330 (Sénégal, All is not well in paradise): "It can feel like you're living in another world, with the music, the animals, the joking, the praying"
- Later instance: B330 re-lists the prayers, music and joking from the opening and restates "unlike anywhere else". The only new items are the sheep and being around people all the time. A reader would feel told again.
- Better placed: B318-B319, which are specific and come first. B330 follows the turn away from paradise and reads as a return to the opening mood.

**4. B320, B333, B341: communal life is the inherited default there, and it is wearing away.**
- B320 (Sénégal, opening): "so much is taken care of by the extended family and the local memory"
- B333 (Sénégal, All is not well in paradise): "the old communal ways here are changing"
- B341 (Sénégal, Lessons from the Black Tax): "A lot of Africans start out with a communal life they inherited, and watch parts of it fall apart."
- Later instance: B341 squeezes B320 and B333 into one sentence. What it adds is the contrast with Westerners and the point about urban Africans feeling alienated from both city and tradition. The first sentence is a recap.
- Better placed: B320 and B333, which each carry their half in the natural place. B341 works as the summary.

**5. B325, B327, B343: the warmth is real but has hard limits; Sénégal is not a paradise.**
- B325 (Sénégal, All is not well in paradise): "some magical place that's kept its communal paradise" and "The warmth has hard limits here"
- B327 (same part): "spiritually alive, generous, funny and super communal, while still becoming cruel to those who break its social categories"
- B343 (Sénégal, Lessons from the Black Tax, closing practical note): "Of course that generosity lives right alongside watchfulness, social conservatism, bureaucracy and all the usual complications of a real country."
- Later instance: B343 adds only "watchfulness" and "bureaucracy". The claim is the one B325 and B327 already made at length, so a reader would feel told again.
- Better placed: B325 and B327, which give the full statement in the natural place. B343 is the redundant instance.

**6. B340 (one block, Sénégal, Lessons from the Black Tax): the Zapatista arrangement does not settle the problem.**
- "That doesn't make the pull between leaving and belonging go away"
- "I think it's not a full solution either."
- Later sentence: it adds only the author's own verdict. The limitation was already stated.
- Better placed: the first sentence, which also says what the arrangement gains (the pull can now be named and negotiated).

## 2. Contradictions

None that a reasonable reader would see. Pairs checked and set aside:
