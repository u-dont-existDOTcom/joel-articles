# Repeats check, sample s15

Scope: every block in "What This Looks Like From Sénégal" (B315 to B344) checked against every other block there and against the rest of the article. Only repeats that involve at least one block from that section are listed. "Sénégal" below means that section; its two sub-sections are "All is not well in paradise" and "Lessons from the Black Tax".

Counts: 9 repeats, 0 contradictions, 3 callbacks listed separately.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

1. Blocks B83, B219, B339. Point: even a nearly moneyless group has to keep a small shared cash pot for outside demands like taxes.
   - B83 (The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together > 4. The container): "a community that is nearly entirely moneyless, but has a small collective cash boundary on the outside"
   - B219 (From One Community to a Movement > Federated Communities Make a Movement): "If the legal or economic environment requires money, the federation also needs to have a cash budget for dealing with it."
   - B339 (Sénégal > Lessons from the Black Tax): "Life inside the commune can go without cash, but you'll still need money for taxes, hospital bills, visas"
   - Later instances: B219 adds only the federation level. B339 mostly restates B83, but adds the list of costs (hospital bills, visas, school fees) and the question of who handles it ("Who's that going to be?"). A reader would feel told the taxes-and-cash point again, with the who-handles-it question as the new part.
   - Better placed: B83. It sits in the three-stage money path and gives the reason (taxes on land) and the end point.

2. Blocks B84, B339, B340. Point: access to outside money gives standing or power, even in a moneyless group, so it has to be guarded.
   - B84 (The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together > 4. The container): "such power can be wielded: access to food storage, housing, tools, land, medicine, transportation, and the outside world’s currency"
   - B339 (Sénégal > Lessons from the Black Tax): "how to stop access to outside money from creating a new status hierarchy"
   - B340 (Sénégal > Lessons from the Black Tax): "buy their way out of everyday communal work, or to buy a greater say in the political system"
   - Later instances: B340 adds the concrete ways (buying out of labor, buying a political voice), ties it to the first stage, and adds the worry that outside jobs drain the people and skills the group needs. B339's clause comes one block earlier and says the same thing in summary, so a reader gets it twice in a row. B84 is the general version.
   - Better placed: B340. It is specific and tied to the first stage; B339's clause adds nothing B340 doesn't.

3. Blocks B319, B320, B331. Point: the daily feel of Sénégal, with constant prayer, joking, music and company.
   - B319 (Sénégal): "Five times a day the prayers pour out into the streets."
   - B320 (Sénégal): "People are outside laughing for hours at a time."
   - B331 (Sénégal > All is not well in paradise): "with the music, the animals, the joking, the praying, and just being around people all the time"
   - Later instance: B331 re-lists the items from B319 and B320. It adds only the animals (the sheep) and the "another world" feeling. A reader would feel told the same thing again, as a return to the charm after the harder material.
   - Better placed: B319 and B320. They come first, are specific, and sit where the place is introduced.

4. Blocks B319, B322. Point: spirituality shows up in the pictures on Sénégal's walls.
   - B319 (Sénégal): "in the pictures everywhere, in the murals, in the names, in the conversations"
   - B322 (Sénégal): "And elsewhere there are murals of Sufi leaders."
   - Later instance: B322 adds the graffiti message and the contrast with the walls where the author grew up. The murals claim itself is the same, three blocks later. This one is mild.
   - Better placed: B322. It is a concrete example with images; B319 is the general claim.

5. Blocks B322, B324. Point: the graffiti message appears in the text and again in the caption.
   - B322 (Sénégal): "Tolerance! Openness! Union! Happiness! Love!"
   - B324 (Sénégal): "Graffiti in Dakar, Sénégal: Tolerance! Openness! Union! Happiness! Love!"
   - Later instance: adds nothing, since Dakar is already named in B322. The same five words sit two blocks apart with the image between. Many readers will take the caption as a label, so this is mild.
   - Better placed: B322, which carries the author's reaction. The caption only labels the image.

6. Blocks B326, B328, B344. Point: Sénégal's warmth and hospitality come with hard limits and complications.
   - B326 (Sénégal > All is not well in paradise): "The warmth has hard limits here, and LGBTQ+ people can pay a brutal price for crossing them."
   - B328 (Sénégal > All is not well in paradise): "while still becoming cruel to those who break its social categories"
   - B344 (Sénégal > Lessons from the Black Tax): "that generosity lives right alongside watchfulness, social conservatism, bureaucracy and all the usual complications of a real country"
   - Later instances: B326 poses the question and B328 answers it, which is a distinct step. B344 is the extra restatement. It adds only "watchfulness" and "bureaucracy", so a reader would feel told the same thing again, briefly.
   - Better placed: B326 to B328, which have the evidence and the full treatment. B344 sits naturally beside the Teranga claim but adds nothing new.

7. Blocks B327, B328. Point: communal belonging does harm when it makes people hide or pretend.
   - B327 (Sénégal > All is not well in paradise): "ignore the people who have to hide who they are to survive it, or leave"
   - B328 (Sénégal > All is not well in paradise): "it all depends on whether you're allowed to belong without pretending"
   - Later instance: B328 adds the general principle that community isn't necessarily liberating for everyone. The hide-or-pretend claim itself is the same, in the next block.
   - Better placed: B328. It states the principle after the evidence; B327's sentence is the author's stance and overlaps with it.

8. Blocks B334, B338, B342. Point: the inherited communal life is breaking down as people enter the cash economy.
   - B334 (Sénégal > All is not well in paradise): "the old communal ways here are changing"
   - B338 (Sénégal > Lessons from the Black Tax): "People have a lot less of the communal life that held the obligation up"
   - B342 (Sénégal > Lessons from the Black Tax): "A lot of Africans start out with a communal life they inherited, and watch parts of it fall apart."
   - Later instances: B338 adds the Black Tax mechanism (the obligation continues without the support behind it), which is a distinct step. B342 adds the contrast with Westerners and the point about urban Africans feeling alienated from both city and tradition. Its first two sentences recap the starting point from B318 and B321 and the erosion from B334, so a reader would feel a light recap.
   - Better placed: B334 for the claim. It comes first and gives the concrete causes (the city, phones, imported food, migration). B342's new value is the Western contrast.

9. Blocks B343, B363. Point: visiting teaches what books can't. This is a faint echo, since one is about African villages and the other about intentional communities.
   - B343 (Sénégal > Lessons from the Black Tax): "knowledge in the living villages that no book can give you"
   - B363 (Practice Comes Before Property > What to Do This Month): "No manifesto is going to tell you any of that!"
   - Later instance: B363 adds the specific things a visit shows (togetherness, privacy, labor system). B343 adds visiting manners. A reader would probably feel only a faint echo.
   - Better placed: B363. It names what a visit teaches and sits in the advice section; B343's line is an aside.

Callbacks that rely on something sections back (not counted as repeats):
- B340 (Sénégal > Lessons from the Black Tax): "In the first stage, where people working outside should contribute an agreed share". It relies on the money stages in The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together > 4. The container (B81: "Members might have outside jobs, and they bring an agreed share of that income in."). A reader needs B81 and B82 to know what "first stage" and "common purse" mean.
- B342 (Sénégal > Lessons from the Black Tax): "Westerners often start out isolated and try to rebuild a tribe." It relies on the loneliness premise in Communes Are Hot Again > The Freeloader Problem (B39: "They just know they are tired and alone and want to change it.").
- B341 (Sénégal > Lessons from the Black Tax): "Some Zapatista communities used to try to stop their young people from leaving". It assumes the Zapatistas introduced in The Best Model I've Seen: Zapatistas (B191 to B212).

## 2. Contradictions

None that a reasonable reader would see. Pairs I looked at and did not count:
- B321 against B334 and B338. The extended family "takes care of" land, childcare and food, against "a lot less of the communal life". B334 opens with "Of course, the old communal ways here are changing", which separates villages from the cash economy.
- B318 against B343. "A strange Western phrase" against "a global issue, far from some Western lifestyle niche". B342 bridges them: the phrase is Western, the problem is not.
- B340 against B341. The author's own stage-one "agreed share" against calling the Zapatista version of outside work with defined obligations "not a full solution either". B340 presents stage one as only a first stage.

## 3. The five repeats a reader would most notice

Entries 2, 3, 1, 6 and 5, in that order. Entry 2 repeats in adjacent blocks, and entry 3 re-lists nearly the same items. Entry 1 shows up three times in separate sections, and entry 6 is a closing caveat after the "not a paradise" material was already made. Entry 5 is the same words twice, two blocks apart.
