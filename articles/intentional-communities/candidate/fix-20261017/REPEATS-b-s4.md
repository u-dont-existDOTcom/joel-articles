# Repeats check, run b-s4

Scope: every repeat or contradiction involving at least one block of "The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together" (B54 to B88: intro, "Founded on Freedom", "1. Inner pl/ork", "2. Relational pl/ork", "3. Medicine pl/ork", "4. The container"). Quotes are copied from the article. Counts: 11 repeats, 2 mild contradictions, 5 callbacks.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

1. B3 and B82. Same point: the father's 17 Bruderhof years prove common-purse living has been done.
   - B3 (The New Age May Dawn Suddenly): "So when I talk later about moving beyond internal money, I’m not proposing something nobody has ever lived."
   - B82 (4. The container): "and my father lived there for 17 years." Also "So a common purse isn’t just a theory."
   - Later adds Twin Oaks and other income-sharing examples, but the father fact and the "it has been lived" point are told again.
   - B82 sits better: it is beside the stage it supports.

2. B47 and B58. Same point: governance ends up carrying unspoken personal feeling.
   - B47 (Why Communities Keep Dying, The secular failure): "politics will become a way of carrying emotional material that people can’t name"
   - B58 (The Four Parts, intro): "Governance can end up slyly incorporating personal tensions like jealousy."
   - Later adds only jealousy as the example; a reader feels told again.
   - B47 is better: it gives the mechanism, in the natural place.

3. B53, B72 and B259. Same point: help does not sit in one guru or professional but in the whole community.
   - B53 (The spiritual failure): "a way to work in depth that isn’t run by a guru"
   - B72 (2. Relational pl/ork): "isn’t located in one professional or guru, or the founder of the group"
   - B259 (The Math of Absorption): "some of the functions that are outsourced to therapists in modern society are distributed throughout the community"
   - B72 adds the peer-counseling practice. B259 adds nothing to the point, only sets up the capacity limit; a reader feels told again.
   - B72 is better: peer counseling is defined there.

4. B56, B85 and B96. Same point: spiritual or peak-experience enthusiasm skips the practical decisions.
   - B56 (The Four Parts, intro): "discovering they’re all God together and never getting to the point of deciding who will put their name on the deed"
   - B85 (4. The container): "rare to see someone come back from an ecstatic vision with news of how to revamp the community’s policies on provisional members"
   - B96 (The Medicine Part): "the insight doesn't do the practical work for it"
   - B85 adds the list of neglected topics. B96 adds people who collect ceremonies but cannot apologize. Told three times in about 40 blocks.
   - B85 is better: concrete, under the heading that lists those topics. B56 is the right opener for the general pattern.

5. B63 and B283. Same point: what began as an experiment hardens into doctrine.
   - B63 (Founded on Freedom): "When agreements get frozen, religious communities tend to call the frozen version doctrine."
   - B283 (Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View): "think the experiment ended in 1978, and everything that came out of it is doctrine now"
   - Later turns it into a question for visitors, but the claim is told again, as a joke.
   - B63 is better: it names the mechanism and the secular "process" parallel.

6. B76 and B109 (also B111). Same point: peer-guided medicine, no special shaman.
   - B76 (3. Medicine pl/ork): "rather than exalting any one person as a special shaman"
   - B109 (My Ceremony Principles): "No special shamans here."
   - B111 (My Ceremony Principles): "placing one charismatic person above everyone else’s direct experience"
   - Later adds a lot (reasons, lineage disagreement, informal-shaman worry). B76 is a one-line overview, so low impact.
   - B109 is better: the full treatment, in the principles section.

7. B77, B114 and B119. Same point: peer-led ceremonies need set safety structure, with the same list.
   - B77 (3. Medicine pl/ork): "This doesn’t mean that a peer-led ceremony should be done without structure." Also "screening participants, making sure the sitters are sober, having a plan for dealing with emergencies"
   - B114 (My Ceremony Principles): "Just because it's a peer-led ceremony doesn't mean it's ok to be casually irresponsible."
   - B119 (My Ceremony Principles): "screening for drug interactions, being disciplined about the dose, and having a plan for dealing with emergencies"
   - B114 adds that spread-out authority needs more competence, plus psychiatric screening and medication review. B119 adds nutrition and dose. B77 adds nothing the later two lack; a reader feels told again.
   - B114 is better: fullest, and in the ceremony-rules section.

8. B80 to B83 and B347. Same point: move toward money-free in stages, not at once.
   - B81 (4. The container): "First there will be no money exchanged between members for basic community functions"
   - B347 (Practice Comes Before Property): "Same with money: do it in stages."
   - Later adds readiness tests (the group provides reliably first; cut outside money by shown capacity). Told again, with that addition.
   - B80 to B83 is the natural place for the stages. B347 carries the conditions.

9. B83 and B338. Same point: a nearly money-free community still keeps a shared cash boundary for outside costs.
   - B83 (4. The container): "a small collective cash boundary on the outside"
   - B338 (Lessons from the Black Tax): "Life inside the commune can go without cash, but you'll still need money for taxes"
   - Later adds a longer needs list, who handles the cash, and the status-hierarchy risk.
   - B338 carries it better; B83 is the shorter first mention.

10. B85, B86 and B286. Same point: one person controlling the land warps the community.
    - B85 (4. The container): "how to prevent one founder from having control over the land"
    - B86 (4. The container, next block): "one person owns the land or has the final say about what will happen on it"
    - B286 (Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): "If the deed is in the name of the founder, then he or she is really a landlord with a philosophy."
    - B85 and B86 say it in neighboring blocks. B286 adds the landlord image, the two-classes example and the legal-entity question.
    - B286 is better: fullest, in the land section.

11. B88, B345 and B352. Same point: skipped relational work comes due after land is bought.
    - B88 (4. The container): "at high stress, usually after the group has invested land, labor, or other resources"
    - B345 (Practice Comes Before Property): "Most people try and do community in the wrong order."
    - B352 (If You Can’t Move): "People sometimes buy forty acres and then bring all their unprocessed stuff with them to a more scenic location."
    - B345 adds the prescribed order. B352 adds the goats joke and pivots to starting where you are; its point is told again.
    - B345 is better: the full statement, in the ordering section.

## 2. Contradictions (both mild)

1. B80 (4. The container): "money is antagonistic to the spirit of community" against B347 (Practice Comes Before Property): "rather than because "money is impure!"" A reader may take B80 as the purity view B347 mocks. Nothing between them says that B80 gives the goal and B347 the timing.

2. B59 (The Four Parts, intro): "A community needs a shared sacred inner practice" against B61 (Founded on Freedom): "Each of us guides our own practice". Shared versus own is not reconciled. B61's next clause (practices weaving the community together) only partly does.
