# Stance check: section 11 rewrite (a2)

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. The reporting right in the majority-proof floor is narrowed to harm done to yourself.**

- Rewrite: "the ability to report being harmed without going through the friends of the accused person"
- Published floor (Founderism: The Four Questions → How My Own Model Could Become a Cult): "a way to report harm without asking the accused person's friends for permission"
- Elsewhere (The Grown Children Get the Final Review → Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection): "Children benefit from many adults who know them, enjoy them, notice changes, and will act when something is wrong."
- A reader would believe the floor guarantees a route only for reporting harm done to you, not harm you see done to a child or another member.

No other sentence in the rewrite changes a position stated anywhere in the essay. The two contradictions below came into the rewrite unchanged from the published text, so they are listed there instead of here.

## Contradictions in the published text

**1. The "boundary" says the principles never give the community power, but the essay's medicine prerequisite exists to filter other people.**

- Section 11 (How My Own Model Could Become a Cult): "Medicine prerequisites can create gatekeepers with power over people at their most open." The next paragraph: "the principles are commitments I choose for my own practice, never powers the community gains over me."
- The Medicine Part → My Ceremony Principles: "The medicine stays behind the other pl/ork." … "This prerequisite also filters out experience collectors."
- A prerequisite that filters out other people is a power over them, not a commitment for one's own practice. The section names the gatekeeper risk, then answers it with a boundary that the essay's own ceremony rule doesn't fit. The safeguards after it cover abstaining, admission and disagreement. None of them says who decides whether someone has met the prerequisite.
- Rewrite: still has it ("when things like that are required as pre-reqs for people to get medicine", then "These aren't principles to give the community power over me").

**2. Members can decline to share, but the forming-group test requires the old material to be found before the group agrees.**

- Section 11 (How My Own Model Could Become a Cult): "Members can decline to share without producing a therapeutic explanation for the refusal."
- Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community: "Find the jealousy, humiliation, fear, or old material involved. Reach an agreement only after the emotional pressure has changed enough"
- As written, the test can't be finished until the old material behind the conflict has been found. A member who uses the right to decline leaves that step undone, and the group stays "still a circle of promising strangers". Their privacy then works as resistance, which is the failure section 11 warns about ("ends by treating privacy as resistance").
- Rewrite: still has it ("Any member can choose not to share material, and they don't need to provide a therapeutic rationale for not sharing.").

Not counted as a contradiction: section 11 says "Every practice in this article can be turned against the people using it" and lists "Willing to be seen", but no other part of the essay introduces that practice. The rewrite still has it ("Even something couched in words like 'willing to be seen'").

Conflicts in the rewrite: 1. Contradictions in the published text: 2.
