# Section 11 stance check (rewrite a4)

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1.** Rewrite: "the six months of peer counseling before it is a rule the community holds for its ceremonies, not one of the principles above"
Essay, "My Ceremony Principles" (in "The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn't There"): "The medicine stays behind the other pl/ork. Reparenting, somatic capacity, and peer counseling come first."
A reader would wrongly believe that the medicine prerequisite is six months of peer counseling alone and that it sits outside Joel's principles. The essay puts reparenting, somatic capacity and peer counseling first, and lists the rule among his own ceremony principles.

No other sentence outside Joel's own paragraph changes a published position. His paragraph is covered in the last part.

## Contradictions in the published text

**1. The boundary vs. the medicine prerequisite.** Section 11 grants that "Medicine prerequisites can create gatekeepers with power over people at their most open." It then says "the principles are commitments I choose for my own practice, never powers the community gains over me." But "My Ceremony Principles" makes medicine-first one of Joel's principles and says "This prerequisite also filters out experience collectors." That is a principle the community applies to other people. **Rewrite: fixed.** It reclassifies the prerequisite as "a rule the community holds", though in doing so it narrows the rule (see conflict 1).

**2. The boundary vs. membership screening.** The same boundary runs against two other sections. "The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn't For" says "This community may not be right for you right now if: ... You are unwilling to examine your behavior when jealousy or conflict appears." "From Visitor to Member—Slowly" asks "Have they begun the relevant practices?" Willingness to practice becomes an entry condition that the community applies. **Rewrite: still has it.** Only the medicine rule is carved out, and "These aren't principles to give the community power over me" stands unqualified.

**3. One sign, or several?** Section 11's daily signs include "disagreement is treated as failure", followed by "None of these proves abuse by itself. Several together deserve attention." The "How My Own Model Could Become a Cult" subsection then says "When a community begins diagnosing dissent instead of responding to it, leave." Diagnosing dissent is a way of treating disagreement as failure, yet on its own it means leave. **Rewrite: addressed.** It adds "it's different from the other signs: once your disagreement gets diagnosed, you can't bring up any of the others either." The published essay never gives that reason.

## Questions on the author's own paragraphs

**1. What applies before critical mass?** You write "there's no formal community and no formal rules until people decide to come together to make them," with people "just living together like friends." Three sections you haven't rewritten set a different order:

- "Practice Comes Before Property": "Practice together. Process a real conflict. Clarify values. Test membership. Decide ownership and exit. Then consider land."
- "Resources, Land, and Exit Terms": "Write the exit terms while departure is still hypothetical."
- The same section: "If the founder's name is on the deed, you have a landlord with a philosophy."

Until critical mass, people would be living on land you own with no exit terms or membership process. "Communes Are Hot Again" also says "Every warning in this article applies to me too." Is natural formation an exception to the essay's order? If it isn't, which of these rules cover people who live there before you formalize?

**2. Who decides who stays until then?** You write "if I'm the only one there and I don't know who else will be there that I could trust." The "How My Own Model Could Become a Cult" subsection, which isn't one of your rewrites, says "No individual or permanent clique controls admission." Before formalizing, you as owner would decide alone whom to trust enough to let stay. Does that rule only start at critical mass?

**3. Would joining a group end it?** You write "If I could find a group of people and join them right now, this problem wouldn't exist." Section 11 opens: "many methods in this article are mine. I wrote the materials. That means I can easily become the person who decides..." If the group you joined used Hearthwork or pl/ork, wouldn't that authorship risk come with you? Or does "this problem" mean only being the landowner that others gather around?
