# Stance check: section 11 rewrite ("Founderism: The Four Questions")

I checked every sentence in the rewrite that states a position against the whole published essay. Nine sentences conflict with it. The rest keep the published meaning.

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1.** Rewrite: "Bad results are blamed on lack of good implementation."
Essay: "may look bad when starved of the assets it actually needs" (Experiment Before You Divorce). The published sign reads "every bad result is blamed on poor implementation."
A reader would wrongly believe that blaming any bad result on implementation is a cult sign. The essay itself argues that some failures come from an unfair test, and the published warning is about blaming every bad result that way.

**2.** Rewrite: "Increasing “insider” knowledge. Decreasing public accountability."
Essay: "insider knowledge grows while public accountability shrinks" (published s11).
A reader would wrongly believe that a community's internal knowledge growing is a warning sign by itself, and that it counts on its own toward "several". The essay flags the gap between the two trends.

**3.** Rewrite: "when things like that are required as pre-reqs ... to get medicine, it puts a lot of power in the hands of the gatekeepers"
Essay: "The medicine stays behind the other pl/ork. Reparenting, somatic capacity, and peer counseling come first." (My Ceremony Principles). The published s11 reads: "Medicine prerequisites can create gatekeepers".
A reader would wrongly believe that Joel's own prerequisite rule always hands power to gatekeepers, as if he were arguing against it. The essay only says it carries a risk to guard against. "Things like that" also merges it with the peer-surveillance risk, which the essay lists separately.

**4.** Rewrite: "they are simply a set of principles I am attempting to live up to."
Essay: "authority stays with the practitioner" (One principle runs through all of them). The published s11 reads: "commitments I choose for my own practice".
A reader would wrongly believe that the principles are ideals Joel is striving to meet, and may be failing to meet. The essay calls them commitments he chooses for his own practice.

**5.** Rewrite: "The right to let people into the group is not vested in any individual or permanent clique."
Essay: "the person everybody consults, trusts, and gradually stops questioning even though nobody gave them the title." (My Ceremony Principles). The published s11 reads: "No individual or permanent clique controls admission."
A reader would wrongly believe that a formal rule about who holds the admission right is enough. The essay's concern is who actually controls admission, whether or not they hold a title.

**6.** Rewrite: "And disagreement gets answered as disagreement."
Essay: "Disagreement has to be answered as disagreement." (published s11, How My Own Model Could Become a Cult).
A reader would wrongly take this as an assurance that Joel's model answers dissent on its merits. The essay states it as an obligation, and that subsection's premise is that his own model could fail it.

**7.** Rewrite: "the right to report harm without asking the accused person's friends for permission, the right to leave."
Essay: "so exit is not a paper right that works only when the community has spare money." (Resources, Land, and Exit Terms). Also: "At least one trusted adult, advocate, or reporting route should be reachable without going through community leadership." (Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection). The published floor reads: "a way to report harm ... and a usable way to leave."
A reader would wrongly believe that a formal right to leave and a formal right to report are enough to meet the floor. The essay requires an exit that works in practice and a reporting route that actually exists.

**8.** Rewrite: "There are some basics that the majority should not be allowed to vote away from any member of a community."
Essay: "autonomy is not permission to erase non-waivable individual rights" (When Somebody Has to Go). The published s11 reads: "a floor the majority cannot vote away".
A reader would wrongly believe that the floor is a policy a community ought to adopt and might not. The essay treats it as rights that no vote can remove.

**9.** Rewrite: "then there's no way to override the founder, no matter how many circles the community holds."
Essay: "The important thing is a competent layer that is not reviewing its own conduct" (When Somebody Has to Go). The published s11 reads: "the community cannot meaningfully override the founder".
A reader would wrongly believe that such a founder cannot be overridden by anyone. The essay says only the community's own process fails, and it points to outside review for exactly that case.

## Contradictions in the published text

**A. The "simple boundary" vs. the medicine gate.** Section 11 says "the principles are commitments I choose for my own practice, never powers the community gains over me." One paragraph earlier it admits that "Medicine prerequisites can create gatekeepers with power over people at their most open." Its own checklist of power also asks who "decides access to children or medicine." My Ceremony Principles turns the prerequisite into a community filter: "This prerequisite also filters out experience collectors." From Visitor to Member also screens applicants on "Have they begun the relevant practices?" The boundary is offered as the answer to all four risks just listed. Yet by the essay's own design, one of those risks is a power the community holds. **The rewrite still has it** ("These aren't things that give the community power over me"). Its "These" now points directly at the pre-reqs named in the previous sentence.

**B. Two thresholds for one behaviour.** The daily signs include "disagreement is treated as failure", followed by "None of these proves abuse by itself. Several together deserve attention." The subsection then says "When a community begins diagnosing dissent instead of responding to it, leave". Diagnosing dissent as a wound is one way of treating disagreement as failure. So one passage asks for attention once several signs appear together, and the other says to leave at the first instance. **The rewrite still has it**, and makes it sharper ("leave. Better yet, leave before it even gets to the point...").

Conflicts in the rewrite: 9. Contradictions in the published text: 2.
