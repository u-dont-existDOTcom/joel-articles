# Stance check: section 11, "Founderism: The Four Questions" (reviewer a5)

## Conflicts in the rewrite

None found. I checked every sentence the paraphrase produced that states a position, rule or claim against the whole essay. That covers the opening ("In every community there was somebody who took the initiative…") through "And ask them about me, if people end up living on land I buy", plus "Every practice in this article can be turned against the people using it". Each one keeps the published claim, its hedges and its scope. The differences are only in wording.

## Contradictions in the published text

**1. Section 11 says the practices "never" give the community power, but the essay uses them as gates.**
- Section 11: "the principles are commitments I choose for my own practice, never powers the community gains over me."
- My Ceremony Principles: "Reparenting, somatic capacity, and peer counseling come first." and "This prerequisite also filters out experience collectors."
- From Visitor to Member—Slowly: "Have they begun the relevant practices?"
- Practice is the gate to medicine and part of the membership test, so it is a power the community holds over members. One paragraph earlier, section 11 itself names "Medicine prerequisites" as a gate.
- Rewrite: no longer has it. Your paragraph now says "Well, it is gatekeeping."

**2. Section 11 says disagreement must be answered as disagreement, but the essay says the stated subject often isn't the real one.**
- Section 11: "Disagreement has to be answered as disagreement."
- Why Communities Keep Dying in the Same Two Ways (The secular failure): "politics begins carrying emotional material that nobody knows how to name" and "the real subject sits nearby with its arms crossed."
- The essay says secular communities fail because policy fights carry hurt that nobody names, and the model exists to name it. Section 11 makes answering disagreement as disagreement a rule.
- Rewrite: no longer has it. Your paragraph says "Sometimes it really is their wounded ego projection, and that should be acknowledged," and also calls overusing that label a failure.

Not counted, because it is a dangling reference rather than a reversal: section 11 names "Willing to be seen" as one of "every practice in this article", but the essay never states such a practice. The rewrite drops it.

## Questions on the author's own paragraphs

**1. Natural formation ("While founderism is a problem in every community…")**
You write: "there's no formal community and no formal rules until people decide to come together to make them."
Your reason (there is nobody yet to make rules with) explains why governance waits. But two sections you haven't rewritten ask for terms earlier:
- Resources, Land, and Exit Terms: "Write the exit terms while departure is still hypothetical."
- From Visitor to Member—Slowly: "Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters, and interns do not need full political membership to have rights". They need "clear terms, a way to be heard, and review."

Question: before the critical-mass point, do the people already living on your land get clear terms and written exit terms, or does that also wait for formalization?

**2. The privacy balance ("'Nothing suppressed, everything processed' can become…")**
You write: "privacy also can be resistance to the communal spirit, so there has to be a dynamic, living, always re-assessed balance"
The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together: "Nothing suppressed, everything processed; authority stays with the practitioner; the community provides the container"
Question: who strikes that balance? If the group can judge a member's privacy to be resistance, does authority over what that member discloses still stay with the practitioner?

**3. Not belonging ("It should go without saying…")**
You write: "if a member is avoiding the therapy process and becoming hard to deal with, they will naturally not belong at a certain point"
Two sections you haven't rewritten set a higher bar:
- When Somebody Has to Go: "If somebody poses a serious or generalized danger, repeatedly violates essential boundaries, or makes ordinary shared life impossible, ejection can be the right answer." The same section says "Decide the process before the name."
- The Math of Absorption warns that capacity language can become "a respectable way to exclude people who are … simply inconvenient."

Question: is "hard to deal with" meant as a ground for removal below that bar? And does "naturally" mean the person chooses to leave, or that the community decides through the process it set before anyone's name was attached?

**4. Outside relationships ("There are some basic human rights…")**
You write: "maintain relationships with others outside the community, as long as those relationships don't interfere with the community as a whole"
Your boyfriend example is about who lives on site, but the condition as written covers every outside relationship. Two sections you haven't rewritten protect some of these without any condition:
- Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection: "At least one trusted adult, advocate, or reporting route should be reachable without going through community leadership."
- When Somebody Has to Go protects "family contact" during a separation.

Question: does the "don't interfere" condition reach a child's outside adults, a reporting route, or family contact during a separation? A community facing a complaint could easily call any of those interfering.

Conflicts in the rewrite: 0 (plus 4 questions on the author's own paragraphs); contradictions in the published text: 2.
