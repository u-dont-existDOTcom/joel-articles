# Logic audit d1-B: "Jealousy Can Capture Monogamy or Free Love", published vs R

Logical relations only. Each table lists relations whose wording differs. SAME: relation kept. CHANGED: a reader would now believe something the published text doesn't say. AMBIGUOUS: two readings, one of which departs from the published text.

## P1 "Any community that lasts…"

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency | "will be reorganized by attraction and pair-bonding" | "will … reorganize itself due to attraction and pair-bonding" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) The community gets reorganized because of attraction (= published, where the forces act on the community). (2) The community, as an actor, deliberately restructures itself in response. |
| Time | — | "eventually" | SAME: "that lasts" already makes it a matter of time. |
| Degree, modality, time | "becomes the community's problem"; "because it changes"; "share … friends the next morning" | "a problem for the entire community"; "because it will change"; "the next morning" now attached to the kitchen only | SAME: communal in both; generic present and predictive "will" make one claim; the shared daily life holds either way. |

## P2 "The usual argument…"

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Causation | "close quarters magnify jealousy" | "in close quarters, jealousy gets magnified" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) Close quarters cause the magnifying (= published). (2) Close quarters are only where it happens; the cause goes unnamed. |
| Scope | "and jealousy can capture either system" (unrestricted) | "in close quarters, jealousy gets magnified, and it can capture either system" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) Jealousy in general can capture either system (= published and heading). (2) "In close quarters" and "it" (the magnified jealousy) limit capture to close-quarters settings. |
| Other | "usual argument—monogamy versus free love—starts one level too late"; "My Romance Guide gives the longer version" | "generally goes straight to 'should people be monogamous or free-love?'"; "as I discuss in my Romance Guide" | SAME |

## P3 "Rigid monogamy…"

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency | "Rigid monogamy can turn fear and possession into moral law"; "Free love without inner pl/ork can distribute…" | "If people are doing rigid monogamy, they can turn … into a moral imperative"; "they can just end up with a larger spreadsheet…" | CHANGED: the actor moves from the relationship form to the people practicing it. A reader takes it as a point about individual conduct and a personal imperative; the published says the form itself can make fear into binding moral law. |
| Comparison | "the same fear" | "their fear" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) The fear just named under monogamy, so one fear drives both systems (= published). (2) Free-lovers' own fear, with no claim that it is the same. |
| Who compares what | "comparison" | "comparing themselves" | CHANGED (minor): narrowed to self-comparison; the published "comparison" leaves open what is compared (oneself, partners, lovers). |
| Negation, sets, "just" | "doesn't release A, B, C, or D"; "another person"; "can distribute" | "doesn't release A, or stop people from B, C, or D"; "other people"; "can just end up" | SAME: none of the four in both; generic in both; "just" (merely) matches the published irony. |

## P4 "I don't think a community…"

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Degree of permission | "revise them" | "change those agreements as they wish" | CHANGED: revision becomes unlimited, at-will discretion; the published says only that agreements can be revised. |
| Who decides | "Couples and other relationship configurations … revise" (the unit) | "People … They can change … as they wish" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) The parties change their agreement together. (2) Any one person may change a shared agreement as they personally wish, i.e., unilaterally. |
| Other | "a community should choose the form for everyone"; "expose old material" | "up to the community to decide for everyone what form"; "bring up old material" | SAME: same decider and negation; the material pre-exists in both. |

## P5 "Whatever forms people choose…"

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modality | "should not depend on" (stated as principle) | "I do think that … should not be contingent on" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) Emphatic conviction, set against P4's "I don't think" (≈ published). (2) The author's opinion, weaker than the published flat rule. |
| Which agreements | "remaining inside one romantic agreement" | "staying in some agreement with a person they're romantically involved with" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) The romantic arrangement itself (= published). (2) Any agreement with a partner, such as shared housing or childcare, a wider protection than published. |

## P6 "ZEGG and Tamera…"

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence | "they demonstrate that a group can acknowledge erotic life" | "they are an example of how a community can acknowledge the erotic aspects of life" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) Proof that it can be done (= published). (2) A model of how to do it, which pulls against "not … commandments". |
| "rather than" → "not necessary"; negation over "because" | "can acknowledge erotic life rather than pretending twenty adults have become neutral colleagues because the bylaws say so" | "It is not necessary for 20 adults to pretend that they've become neutral colleagues just because the by-laws say so." | CHANGED: the published puts acknowledgment in place of the pretense. The rewrite says only that the pretense isn't required, and "not … just because" denies only that bylaws are reason enough. A reader may take the pretense as optional, or as justified on other grounds. |
| Who pretends; evidence scope | "a group … pretending", inside "they demonstrate that" | "for 20 adults to pretend", a separate sentence | AMBIGUOUS. (1) Still the group's stance, which ZEGG/Tamera show can be dropped (≈ published). (2) The author's own reassurance to individuals, not something the example shows. |
| Negation scope | "aren't commandments for everybody" | "are not a set of commandments that everyone must follow" | SAME |

## P7 "The non-negotiable…"

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modality scope | "has to be speakable, felt in the body" (only "speakable" is about ability; "felt" is required) | "needs to be able to be talked about and felt in the body" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) It must be felt (= published). (2) "Able to" covers "felt": it need only be possible to feel it. |
| Whose rules | "converted into punishment or policy" | "used to punish, or make rules" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) Group policy (= published). (2) Any rule-making, including rules partners set each other, which P4 leaves to them. |
| Other | "cannot become the hidden governor"; "released rather than converted"; — | "not be allowed to become"; "let go of gradually, and not used"; added "This doesn't mean it can't come up." | SAME: a prohibition in both; "and not" equals "rather than" here; the added sentence matches the published implication that jealousy will be present to speak of and feel. |

## P8 "The group can offer support…"

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quantifier | "can offer support and enforce basic boundaries without choosing…" | "There are some ways the group can help, and some basic boundaries the group can enforce, without determining…" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) The group can help and enforce the basic boundaries without dictating relationships (= published). (2) Only some of the basic boundaries can be enforced that way; the rest would mean dictating relationships or lie beyond the group. |
| What stays open | "People will still disagree, which is more realistic than declaring the matter solved" | "there will still be disagreements. That's more realistic than saying it's all been resolved" | AMBIGUOUS. (1) The relationship question stays open (= published "the matter"). (2) Conflicts between people won't all be settled, a claim about disputes, so the refusal to settle the monogamy/free-love question can drop out. |
| Other | "still"; "offer support"; "everyone's relationship structure" | "Even so … still"; "help"; "what type of relationships people have" | SAME: concession kept; the group still decides no one's structure. |
