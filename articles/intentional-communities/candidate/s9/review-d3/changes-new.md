## P12 → P12d

CONTEXT BEFORE: P11 (rewrite, already traced): This is also where I have to rein in my enthusiasm. Zapatista autonomy grew out of Indigenous communities with long histories. They share language and family ties. They've fought land struggles together, and defended themselves together for decades. A new ecovillage can't copy the diagram everyone can see and get the trust underneath it right away.

PUBLISHED P12:
The Zapatistas themselves have warned people not to import or export Zapatismo as a ready-made model. The useful thing is not their diagram. It is how they learn: build, ask, discover where practice fails, and change the structure.

REWRITE P12d:
One thing the Zapatistas have been clear about is that people shouldn't import or export Zapatismo as a ready-made model. The useful thing isn't their diagram, though. It's how they learn: they build things and ask questions, and when they find out where the practice isn't working, they change the structure.

CONTEXT AFTER: P13 (not settled yet; published): They are also not proof of my complete economic path from unpriced internal necessities, to a common purse, to eliminating outside money. Zapatista communities use collective work to support schools, clinics, autonomous government, and resistance; they also sell products, trade, and receive outside support. What they demonstrate is that major necessities and public functions can be taken out of ordinary market purchase and governed collectively. Outside help can build capability, but it can also create gatekeepers and uneven dependence when the specialist, donor, or organization remains indispensable.
