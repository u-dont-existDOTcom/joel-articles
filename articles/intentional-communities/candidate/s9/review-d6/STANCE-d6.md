# Stance check: section 9 rewrite (Zapatistas)

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. P15, last sentence**
- Rewrite: "It's for teaching enough of the social and material system that some people can build it somewhere else."
- Essay: "I want communities that can help one another, move people and knowledge around, and still remain different enough to learn from one another." (Federation Without Building Another State, under From One Community to a Movement). Also: "Allow parallel implementations. Fork the method." (Founderism: The Four Questions)
- The published sentence ends "some can build elsewhere". The added "it" points back to the host community's system.
- A reader would wrongly believe: an escuelita exists so graduates rebuild the same system elsewhere, rather than found their own variants.

**2. P15, first sentence (smaller shift)**
- Rewrite: "success gets measured by how many people come instead of how many daughter communities get started."
- Essay: "and, if it is meant to spread, do later projects actually emerge and last?" (Founderism: The Four Questions)
- A reader would wrongly believe: the number of foundings is the yardstick, rather than whether daughter communities last and how their members and children "actually did over time" (section 9's own test).

## Contradictions in the published text

**1. A borrowed replication ambition vs. the Zapatistas' warning (tension)**
- Section 9: "The Zapatistas themselves have warned people not to import or export Zapatismo as a ready-made model."
- Section 9: "I'm borrowing a pedagogy and a replication ambition"
- The published text borrows an ambition to replicate from people it says warn against exporting their model. It never says where that ambition fits with the warning.
- Rewrite: fixed. Your P12 holds both sides ("Despite actually trying to train people in their model"). Conflict 1 above leans back toward copying.

**2. Which Zapatista body reviews local power (tension)**
- Section 9: after 2023, "the coordinating levels are explicitly described as dependent on the communities rather than superior to them."
- When Somebody Has to Go (under Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community): "The Zapatistas are an important correction here: autonomous institutions can review local power without becoming the state."
- The later passage asks for "a competent layer that is not reviewing its own conduct" and names the Zapatistas as the example. Section 9 says they made their upper layer subordinate to the communities. The essay never says which Zapatista body does that reviewing now.
- Rewrite: still has it, in a sharper form (see question 2).

## Questions on the author's own paragraphs

**1. P9 and P11 vs. P8 (unchanged).** P8 presents the step from municipalities to the Good Government Boards as practice-first design: experience "generated another structure rather than being forced to fit the original plan." Your P9 says power moving away from "the original idea" to "higher levels... for practical reasons" was the problem, and P11 calls it "degeneration." Were the Boards part of that drift? If so, the same structure appears as the principle working (P8) and as the degeneration that was reversed (P11).

**2. P9 and P11 vs. When Somebody Has to Go.** That section cites the Zapatistas to show "autonomous institutions can review local power without becoming the state." Your P9 and P11 present power gathering at higher levels as degeneration, reversed by moving authority to each community. After 2023, which Zapatista body does the reviewing that section credits them with? Or does that section describe the pre-2023 Juntas?

Conflicts in the rewrite: 2 (plus 2 questions on your own paragraphs). Contradictions in the published text: 2, both tensions rather than flat contradictions.
