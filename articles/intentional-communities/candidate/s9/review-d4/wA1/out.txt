In 2023, after a long internal review, the Zapatistas announced that they were dissolving the Rebel Zapatista Autonomous Municipalities and the Good Government Juntas. In the new arrangement, they moved authority toward Local Autonomous Governments in each community. Those local bodies can coordinate through regional collectives, but it's spelled out that the coordinating levels depend on the communities, instead of being above them.

Most institutions keep adding layers and offices as they get older, but the Zapatistas reviewed theirs and pushed authority downward. They didn't issue a leadership book and open a certification program 😂 They changed the system they were actually living in instead.

This is also where I need to rein in my enthusiasm. A new ecovillage can't copy the visible diagram and instantly get the trust that's underneath it. Zapatista autonomy grew from Indigenous communities with long histories and shared land struggles. People there have language and kinship in common, and they've defended themselves together for decades.

The Zapatistas themselves have warned people not to import or export Zapatismo as a ready-made model. Instead of their diagram, the useful thing is how they learn. They build, and they ask. They find out where the practice fails and change the structure.

They're also not proof of my complete economic path, from unpriced internal necessities all the way to eliminating outside money, with a common purse in between. Zapatista communities use collective work to support schools and clinics, and they put it toward their autonomous government and their resistance. They also sell products and trade. They get outside support as well. But they do show that major necessities and public functions can be taken out of ordinary market purchase and governed collectively. Outside help can build capability, but it can also create gatekeepers and uneven dependence when the specialist or donor remains indispensable. The same can happen when an organization stays indispensable.
