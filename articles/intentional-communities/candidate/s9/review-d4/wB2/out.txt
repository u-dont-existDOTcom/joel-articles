I'm borrowing a pedagogy and a replication ambition from the Zapatistas, but I'm not claiming they already proved my whole model. In my research, I also tried to trace what happened after the Escuelita. I found alumni networks. I found people carrying practices home, and later organizing. The Zapatistas were already influencing people internationally before the Escuelita, so if a group resembles them later, that alone isn't proof it came out of the Escuelita. I was looking for a clear chain from an Escuelita student to a newly founded communal institution, and then for evidence about how that institution's members and children actually did over time. I didn't find that.

Sociocracy has some similar forms, and it comes from a very different history. It uses circles and consent. Authority is distributed, and there are links between levels. The resemblance gives builders another serious system to study, instead of inventing governance during their first dispute. It doesn't prove there's a universal formula, though.
