# Stance check: §4 rewrite ("The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together")

Section numbers follow the essay's top-level headings (§4 is the section under review). Findings are listed in the order they appear in the rewrite.

**1. Control of shared resources**
- Rewrite: "Those who have positions with more control of shared resources will automatically be affected by that psychologically (power corrupts, as they say)."
- Essay: "control of shared resources can become psychology" (§4); "Communal ownership therefore needs named stewardship, visible duties, consequences for chronic freeloading" (§2, Communes Are Hot Again → The Freeloader Problem).
- Wrong takeaway: Joel holds that looking after shared resources always corrupts the person doing it. The essay says it "can", and it calls for named stewards.

**2. Medicine and god complexes**
- Rewrite: "Those who know medicine tend to have another type of god complex since they may make life or death decisions for people routinely."
- Essay: "medicine can create status" (§4); "prevents an informal shaman from emerging anyway—the person everybody consults, trusts, and gradually stops questioning" (§5, The Medicine Part → My Ceremony Principles).
- Wrong takeaway: Joel thinks people who know medicine tend toward god complexes because they hold life-or-death power. The essay says only that medicine can create status, and that the status comes from other people deferring to an informal shaman.

**3. Name of the third inner adult**
- Rewrite: "…the three inner adult roles: Nurturer, Protector, and Guide."
- Essay: "the three inner adults: Nurturer, Protector, and Guru/Leader" (§4, 1. Inner pl/ork).
- Wrong takeaway: Joel's reparenting model calls its third inner adult "Guide". The essay names it "Guru/Leader".

**4. Somatic regulation as the foundation**
- Rewrite: "And it's built on somatic regulation first. Listening to the body's feelings before making a decision with the mind."
- Essay: "Then there's somatic regulation, because the mind can make a magnificent decision while the nervous system quietly files an appeal" (§4); "Start the inner pl/ork. Read the free reparenting guide and Hearthwork manual, or begin with another serious practice you already trust" (§16, Practice Comes Before Property → What to Do This Month).
- Wrong takeaway: Joel builds reparenting on somatic work first and defines that work as checking with the body before deciding. In the essay, somatic regulation is an extra practice for a nervous system that pushes back on decisions, and beginners start with the reparenting guide and Hearthwork.

**5. First-person view the essay doesn't support**
- Rewrite: "And that actually symbolizes the adult/child relationship to me in some way as well."
- Essay: no passage supports this. The published paragraph says only that "the nervous system quietly files an appeal" (§4).
- Wrong takeaway: Joel sees listening to the body before deciding as a symbol of the inner adult/child relationship. The essay never says this.

**6. The suttas**
- Rewrite: "Most are treasure, while some are obviously late additions (sometimes to the point of real absurdity)."
- Essay: "Most are treasure. Some seem like late additions." (§4, 1. Inner pl/ork)
- Wrong takeaway: Joel states outright that some suttas are late and absurd. The essay hedges ("seem like") and never calls any of them absurd.

**7. Peer care and administrative power**
- Rewrite: "Giving someone the job of taking care of people emotionally and giving them administrative power is just creating a new hierarchy."
- Essay: "should not automatically gain power over my membership, work, housing… Peer care becomes another hierarchy the moment emotional access turns into administrative authority" (§4, 2. Relational pl/ork); "The danger begins when the same person can define the problem, control the evidence, choose the remedy, and make disagreement or exit costly" (§3, Why Communities Keep Dying in the Same Two Ways).
- Wrong takeaway: anyone who counsels peers must never also hold administrative power, and emotional care is one person's job. The essay says only that the access a listener gets must not turn into authority.

**8. How far a community can go without money**
- Rewrite: "it may be impractical in some locations because of government requirements like taxes on land."
- Essay: "Governments may make it literally impossible through land taxes and other required payments" (§4); "the boundary problem a money-free commune has to solve. Internal life can be cashless while taxes… still arrive denominated in money" (§15, What This Looks Like From Sénégal → Lessons from the Black Tax).
- Wrong takeaway: going without money in outside dealings is blocked only in some places, and there it is just impractical. The essay says governments may make it impossible, and it treats outside cash demands as a problem any money-free commune has to solve.

**9. What visions can decide**
- Rewrite: "And if they do, it's often still something that needs a sober thinking-through."
- Essay: "the vision can't determine a departing member's share… or decide what happens when a child reports something serious" (§4); "Decide the response process before anyone's face is attached to it. Immediate safety comes first; investigation must be competent and fair" (§8, The Grown Children Get the Final Review → Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection).
- Wrong takeaway: a vision can sometimes settle a departing member's share, who controls the land, or a child's serious report without careful sober review. The essay says a vision can't decide these things.
