# Stance check: section 4 rewrite (v7) vs. the whole published essay

**1. Hedge hardened: resource control "automatically" corrupts**
- Rewrite: "Those who have positions with more control of shared resources will automatically be affected by that psychologically (power corrupts, as they say)."
- Essay, section 3 "Why Communities Keep Dying in the Same Two Ways": "A farmer or business manager may carry much of the economy. The danger begins when the same person can define the problem…" Published section 4 hedges: "control of shared resources can become psychology."
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel holds that resource control inevitably corrupts whoever has it, when the essay says it can, and places the danger in roles that concentrate and become hard to challenge.

**2. Unsupported assessment: people who know medicine have god complexes**
- Rewrite: "Those who know medicine tend to have another type of god complex since they may make life or death decisions for people routinely."
- Essay, "My Ceremony Principles" (in The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn’t There): "I don’t yet know how a peer-led medicine culture prevents an informal shaman from emerging … the person everybody consults, trusts, and gradually stops questioning". Published section 4: "medicine can create status."
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel sees medical knowledge itself as breeding god complexes, when the essay says medicine can create status through others' trust and deference, and admits he doesn't know how to prevent it.

**3. Unsupported fact: his reparenting model renamed**
- Rewrite: "…learning to identify as the best inner adult, which is comprised of the three inner adult roles: Nurturer, Protector, and Guide."
- Essay, published section 4 "Inner pl/ork": "identifying as the inner adult—or the three inner adults: Nurturer, Protector, and Guru/Leader"
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel's model centers one idealized "best" adult whose third role is "Guide," rather than three inner adults including a Guru/Leader.

**4. Unsupported sequence: somatic regulation made the foundation**
- Rewrite: "And it's built on somatic regulation first."
- Essay, "What to Do This Month" (in Practice Comes Before Property): "Start the inner pl/ork. Read the free reparenting guide and Hearthwork manual, or begin with another serious practice you already trust."
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel's inner pl/ork must start from somatic regulation, when the essay starts readers with reparenting and Hearthwork, or any trusted practice, and section 4 adds somatic regulation as a further component ("Then there’s somatic regulation").

**5. Unsupported first-person view**
- Rewrite: "And that actually symbolizes the adult/child relationship to me in some way as well."
- Essay, published section 4 "Inner pl/ork": "Then there’s somatic regulation, because the mind can make a magnificent decision while the nervous system quietly files an appeal." No passage ties body and mind to inner adult and child.
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel personally reads body-before-mind as a symbol of the inner adult/child relationship.

**6. Hedge dropped, ridicule added: the suttas**
- Rewrite: "Most are treasure, while some are obviously late additions (sometimes to the point of real absurdity)."
- Essay, published section 4 "Inner pl/ork": "Most are treasure. Some seem like late additions."
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel declares it obvious that some suttas are late additions and mocks some as absurd; the essay's judgment is tentative and carries no ridicule.

**7. Rule turned into an option: the stage-one income share**
- Rewrite: "Members might have outside jobs and bring their income in (some agreed share of it)."
- Essay, "Lessons from the Black Tax" (in What This Looks Like From Sénégal): "During the first stage, an outside earner should contribute an agreed share, but money cannot buy exemption from ordinary communal work"
- Reader would wrongly believe: in stage one, contributing a share of outside earnings is something members may or may not do, when the essay requires it of anyone earning outside.

**8. Obstacle softened and narrowed: full moneylessness**
- Rewrite: "it may be impractical in some locations because of government requirements like taxes on land."
- Essay, published section 4 "The container": "Governments may make it literally impossible through land taxes and other required payments." Also "Lessons from the Black Tax": "Internal life can be cashless while taxes, hospitals, visas, school fees … still arrive denominated in money."
- Reader would wrongly believe: only some places block a fully moneyless community, so it is reachable elsewhere, when the essay calls it "the furthest goal" that governments may make literally impossible, which is why it settles on a cash boundary.

**9. Softened toward reversal: visions as a source of policy**
- Rewrite: "It's rare to see someone come back from an ecstatic vision with news of how to revamp the community’s policies…" and "And if they do, it's often still something that needs a sober thinking-through."
- Essay, The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn’t There: "insight does not wash dishes, revise agreements, or calm a frightened child." Also "Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection": "Decide the response process before anyone’s face is attached to it."
- Reader would wrongly believe: visions sometimes deliver usable policy, even on a child's serious report, that may not need sober review, when the essay says insight does not revise agreements and child-report responses are set in advance.
