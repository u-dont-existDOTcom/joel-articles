# Logic audit v60-A: P2 and P26

Scope: P2 and P26 only; the other 31 paragraphs were audited separately. Numbering follows the brief (every block that isn't a heading or an image), and the two files align: P2 = "The movement is neither smooth nor inevitable…" / "That said, it's not a smooth ride…"; P26 = "Integration is ordinary life…" / "Integration is everyday life…". Pronoun and noun reference are included. Only relations whose wording differs are listed.

## P2

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Link to P1 | none | "That said," | SAME. Marks the concession the published order already implies: fast, but not smooth. |
| Subject reference | "The movement" (P1: "have moved from taboo toward regulated use") | "it" | SAME. "a smooth ride" picks up P1's "They've gone … really fast"; the only other candidate, "regulated use", gives the same claim. |
| Negation scope | "neither smooth nor inevitable" | "not a smooth ride or an inevitability" | SAME. "not … or" denies both. |
| Agent and acts ("they") | the FDA's letter "declining to approve … and asking for further evidence" | "where they did not approve it and requested more evidence" | SAME. Only the FDA approves, so "they" = the FDA; both acts kept, joined by "and". |
| Object ("it") | "the submitted MDMA-assisted therapy application" | "it" (antecedent "the MDMA-assisted therapy application") | SAME. Still one specific application; "requested more evidence" keeps the refusal provisional. Reading "it" as the therapy itself changes nothing material. |
| Time | "In 2024, … issued" | "issued … to the … application in 2024" | SAME. "in 2024" attaches to "issued"; "the application in 2024" is a strained parse. |
| Link FDA → Portugal | none | "And" | SAME. Portugal stays a second caveat under the first sentence. |
| Frequency; who says it | "is often described lazily" (describers unnamed) | "many people will lazily say" | SAME. Habitual "will", not a prediction; "often" counts occasions and "many" counts speakers, and both say the claim is common. |
| Quoted "they" | Portugal, subject of "having 'legalized everything'" | "they've legalized everything there" | SAME. "they … there" = Portugal's government. |
| Correction | "; it actually decriminalized" | "- but that's false," | SAME. "actually … while trafficking remains criminal" already entails that the description is false. |
| Second "they" | "it" (Portugal) | "they've only decriminalized" | SAME. The nearest plural is "many people", but only a government decriminalizes; it resolves to Portugal, as in the quote. |
| "only" (added) | "it actually decriminalized possession of small amounts for personal use" | "they've only decriminalized possessing small quantities for personal use" | AMBIGUOUS. (a) Decriminalized rather than legalized: the published contrast. (b) Nothing but small-quantity personal possession was decriminalized, so any other act (buying, for instance) stays criminal. The published sentence names one act decriminalized and one still criminal and says nothing about any other. |
| Possession and trafficking | "while trafficking remains criminal" | new sentence: "It's still a crime to traffic drugs." | SAME for contrast and time ("remains" = "still"). |
| "drugs" | implicit object of "trafficking": whatever "everything" and "small amounts" cover | "drugs" (bare plural) | SAME. Drugs in general, the same set as "everything" and "small quantities"; not narrowed to MDMA or psilocybin. |
| Where trafficking is a crime | Portugal: the "while" clause sits inside the Portugal sentence | freestanding sentence, no place named | AMBIGUOUS. (a) In Portugal, read through "still" after "only decriminalized" (likelier; = published). (b) A general closing statement: despite the regulated systems in P1, trafficking drugs is still a crime anywhere. The published text says this only of Portugal. |

## P26

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genericity; who is affected | "Ceremony opens something." | "The ceremony opens something up in you." | SAME. Generic or the reader's own ceremony; the published list ("the person's ability…") already places the change in the person. |
| Mood | "The following weeks reveal whether anything changed" (statement) | "Look at your life in the weeks after the ceremony and see how things have changed" (instruction) | CHANGED. A statement of where change becomes visible turns into an instruction. A reader now takes P26 as prescribing a review step; the published paragraph states a criterion and prescribes nothing. |
| Who checks | no checker named; the person in third person: "the person's ability to tolerate frustration" | "Look at your life … your relationships … how well you can deal with frustration" | CHANGED. The participant becomes the judge of their own change. The published paragraph names no judge and describes the person from outside, which leaves others as possible observers, such as the people who in P9 "can also notice what happens during the following months". |
| Open question | "whether anything changed" | "how things have changed, if at all" | SAME. "how" presupposes change, but "if at all" cancels it; "nothing changed" stays a live outcome, and "at all" matches "anything". |
| Time | "The following weeks" | "in the weeks after the ceremony" | SAME, whether the phrase attaches to "Look" or to "your life". |
| List; "and" | "—in relationships, daily behavior, sleep, decisions, and the person's ability…" (where change might show) | "Look at your relationships, your behavior, your sleeping habits, your decision-making, and how well you can…" (what to examine) | SAME. Same enumeration; "and" lists places to check, not a demand that all of them change. Read under the previous sentence's "how things have changed", the list still asks about change, not current level. |
| Ability; degree | "ability to tolerate frustration" | "how well you can deal with frustration" | SAME. "can" plus "how well" = the ability and its degree. |
| Scope of "without" | "tolerate frustration without declaring a new spiritual emergency" | "deal with frustration without declaring a new spiritual emergency" | SAME. "without" still attaches to handling frustration; attaching it to "Look at" is a strained parse. |

## CHANGED and AMBIGUOUS

1. P2 — "it actually decriminalized possession of small amounts for personal use" → "they've only decriminalized possessing small quantities for personal use" — AMBIGUOUS: under reading (b) a reader believes nothing but small-quantity personal possession was decriminalized.
2. P2 — "while trafficking remains criminal" (inside the Portugal sentence) → "It's still a crime to traffic drugs." (freestanding) — AMBIGUOUS: under reading (b) a reader believes trafficking drugs is still a crime in general, the P1 jurisdictions included, which the published text says only of Portugal.
3. P26 — "The following weeks reveal whether anything changed" → "Look at your life in the weeks after the ceremony and see how things have changed, if at all" — CHANGED: a reader believes the paragraph prescribes a self-review step.
4. P26 — "the person's ability to tolerate frustration" (third person, no checker named) → "Look at … how well you can deal with frustration" — CHANGED: a reader believes the participant is the one who judges whether anything changed.
