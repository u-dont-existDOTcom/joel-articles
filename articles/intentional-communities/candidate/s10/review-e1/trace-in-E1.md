# Section 10 pairs: published paragraph and rewrite

The rewrite merges published P3 and P4 into one paragraph (P34m); that pair compares both published paragraphs with it.

## P1 → P1a

CONTEXT BEFORE: [Sociocracy](https://www.sociocracyforall.org/sociocracy/) has a very different history, yet some similar features have emerged there as well: circles, decision-making by consent, distributed authority, and linking between the levels. Does this prove there's a universal formula? No. But it means that people building institutions of this sort have another serious system to study, instead of inventing their governance during their first dispute.

PUBLISHED P1:
Experimenting with a different rule does not have to mean everybody buys new land immediately. Try the different rule inside the parent group; then as a semi-autonomous project; then, if it needs more independence, as a nearby seed that still shares selected tools, childcare, business, or friendships. Full fission should be one step on a gradient, not the admission price for experimentation.

REWRITE P1a:
Experimenting with a different rule doesn't have to mean everybody goes out and buys new land right away. Try it inside the parent group first, then as a semi-autonomous project, and then, if it needs more independence, as a seed group nearby that still shares some tools, childcare, business or friendships. Splitting off completely should be just one step along that gradient, and nobody should have to do it just to be allowed to experiment.

CONTEXT AFTER: Some experiments cannot be tested cheaply. A different land system, population scale, or package of mutually supporting rules may look bad when starved of the assets it actually needs. “We tried it in one room for three months” is not always a fair test.

REWRITE P34m:
If these communities are supposed to spread, somebody eventually has to solve the boring problem of how they stay connected without turning into a super-commune (that's essentially just another state, with a nicer logo). I'd like them to connect, help each other and move people and knowledge around, but also to stay distinct enough that they can learn from one another. At the federation level, that outward purpose gets concrete: spreading healing practices, training people, seeding new projects, and keeping any one comfortable enclave from thinking it's the whole movement.

CONTEXT AFTER: The Federation of Egalitarian Communities says it is currently rebuilding, which is a useful warning. Federation work is work: delegates, records, compliance, resource coordination, meetings, succession, and any dispute function it actually accepts all consume labor and material capacity before local crises have eaten both. Where the surrounding legal or economic system requires money, the federation also needs an external cash budget. But that work does not all need to live in one body. Bilateral friendships, local clusters, and function-specific institutions can coexist with a general federation. The FEC’s PEACH health pool, for example, is currently governed independently from FEC delegates.
