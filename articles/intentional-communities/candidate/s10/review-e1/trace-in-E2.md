# Section 10 pairs: published paragraph and rewrite (second half)

## P5 → P5b1

CONTEXT BEFORE: If these communities are supposed to spread, eventually somebody has to solve the boring problem of how they remain connected without building another state. I don’t want one super-commune with a nicer logo. I want communities that can help one another, move people and knowledge around, and still remain different enough to learn from one another.

At federation scale, that outward purpose becomes concrete: spread healing practices, train people, seed new projects, and keep any one comfortable enclave from mistaking itself for the whole movement.

PUBLISHED P5:
The Federation of Egalitarian Communities says it is currently rebuilding, which is a useful warning. Federation work is work: delegates, records, compliance, resource coordination, meetings, succession, and any dispute function it actually accepts all consume labor and material capacity before local crises have eaten both. Where the surrounding legal or economic system requires money, the federation also needs an external cash budget. But that work does not all need to live in one body. Bilateral friendships, local clusters, and function-specific institutions can coexist with a general federation. The FEC’s PEACH health pool, for example, is currently governed independently from FEC delegates.

REWRITE P5b1:
The Federation of Egalitarian Communities says it's rebuilding, and that's a good warning. It takes work to run a federation. Delegates. Records. Compliance. Coordinating resources. Holding meetings. Succession. Whatever dispute function it actually takes on. These things all take up labor and material capacity before any local crisis has eaten into them. If the legal or economic environment requires it, the federation also needs to have a budget of outside money. It might also be good to know that the work of federation doesn't have to be done by a single entity. Two communities might want to be closely linked as friends, and that doesn't stop both of them from also being part of a general federation. There can also be local clusters, or single-function institutions like the FEC's PEACH health pool, which isn't currently governed by the FEC delegates.

CONTEXT AFTER: Cooperation also shouldn’t quietly become assimilation. The FEC currently distinguishes full members, communities moving toward membership, and Friends that may simply remain friends. People need a similarly practical freedom between communities. If someone can theoretically move but housing, status, health coverage, records, labor accounting, travel costs, taxes, and transition disputes fall into cracks, the freedom is decorative. Often the answer is not harmonizing every internal system; it’s a thin interface that says who is responsible for what while somebody crosses between them.

REWRITE P6h:
One key to this whole thing is that cooperation shouldn't quietly turn into merging. The FEC has full members and communities working toward full membership, and Friends who may just want to stay friends. Also, people need a practical freedom to move between communities, and that takes work: housing, status, health coverage, records, labor accounting, travel costs, taxes, and disputes over the transition all have to be set up, or the freedom to move is just decorative. Fortunately, that interface can often be thin, saying who's responsible for what while somebody crosses between them, and communities don't have to have the same internal systems for it to work.

CONTEXT AFTER: Exchange and obligations need the same honesty even if nobody uses money internally. A gift of food, pooled medicine, a loan of a vehicle or tool, reciprocal labor, emergency housing, a shared building project, and a visit meant mainly for training or relationship-building are different events. Some create a future obligation; some do not. A federation can keep purpose-specific records without pricing everything or forcing every transfer into parity. If cash is used at the outside interface, grants, loans, guarantees, and shared-enterprise commitments need the same separation. Solidarity is not unlimited joint liability—or an unlimited claim on another community’s labor, stores, land, or tools.

REWRITE P7b1:
Even if nobody uses money internally, communities still need an honest way to keep track of exchanges and obligations. A gift of food, pooled medicine, a vehicle or a tool on loan, trades of labor, emergency housing, a shared building project, a visit that's mainly for training or relationship building: they're all different events, and some of them create future obligations while others don't. The federation can keep track of these for each purpose, without having to put a value on them or require every exchange to be "even". If cash is used for transactions with the outside world, the same goes for grants and loans. Guarantees and shared enterprise commitments should be tracked separately too. Solidarity doesn't make every community liable for whatever the others owe, and it doesn't give anyone an unlimited claim on another community's labor and stores, or its land and tools.
