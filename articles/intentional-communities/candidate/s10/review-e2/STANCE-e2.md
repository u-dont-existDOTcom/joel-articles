# Stance check: section 10 rewrite ("From One Community to a Movement")

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. Hard cases now have to be recorded only when a new rule is written**

- Rewrite: "When you create a rule to deal with a collision of two principles, don't only store the rule."
- Essay, *From One Community to a Movement › Federation Without Building Another State*: "And when two principles collide, don’t preserve only the final rule. Keep the hard case"
- A reader would wrongly believe a case file is needed only when a collision produces a new rule. Collisions settled under an existing rule would go unrecorded, so they couldn't be rerun when that rule changes ("When the rule changes, rerun the case").

No other sentence in the rewrite changes a position the essay states, in section 10 or anywhere else.

## Contradictions in the published text

None found. Published section 10 doesn't take back any of its own claims, and it doesn't contradict the rest of the essay. These are the closest tensions I checked and didn't count:

- "“We tried it in one room for three months” is not always a fair test." (section 10) against "Rent a meeting space, borrow a room, or use somebody’s home. Discover the group’s conflict capacity before hiring a surveyor." (*Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community*). The room is where a group tests its conflict capacity. The section 10 caveat covers land systems, population scale and packages of rules that need real assets. The rewrite keeps both passages.
- "If someone can theoretically move but housing, status, … travel costs, taxes, and transition disputes fall into cracks, the freedom is decorative." (section 10) against "If the community can offer temporary housing, travel help … great. If it cannot, it can still avoid punishing departure" (*The Door Must Open Outward at Adulthood*). Something falls into the cracks when nobody has been made responsible for it. Section 10's remedy is an interface that names who is responsible, and it doesn't require the community to pay. The rewrite keeps the same standard: it says "all have to be set up" and then "that interface can often be thin, saying who's responsible for what."
- "Full fission should be one step on a gradient, not the admission price for experimentation." against "Some experiments cannot be tested cheaply." (both section 10). The second sentence limits the first to experiments that can be tried fairly at small scale, without withdrawing it. The rewrite keeps both.

Conflicts in the rewrite: 1. Contradictions in the published text: 0.
