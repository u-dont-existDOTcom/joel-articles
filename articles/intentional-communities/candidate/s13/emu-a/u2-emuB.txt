The problem is that people select communities for the same reason they select a vacation rental: the land is gorgeous, there’s a lovely pond there, and all the people seemed really nice when you visited.

Sure, the pond will be great for your first date, but it’s the vaccination policy you need in your hand when it comes time…

When you go to visit a community, have a list of your values ready to go. If you are unsure of your values in a given area, note that too. Some important areas to consider are: children, money, property ownership, romantic relationships, medical care, food, spirituality, technology, privacy, conflict resolution, leaving the community, etc.

Also consider your values on sexuality and gender. Would the community be open to hosting LGBTQ people? Should kids be taught to question their sexual identities at an early age? What will the community do if one of their kids comes out as gay or trans? If they haven’t addressed these topics yet, they probably haven’t finished working out their values on freedom or on how to raise kids, either.

Being willing to experiment is a totally valid value, but make sure you say so! The person next to you may not share it.

Finally, compare and contrast your values with the group’s. Be sure to note any red flags you see in how the group deals with conflict, makes decisions, allows people to leave, and handles land ownership.
