The problem was that the Farm grew too quickly; a lot of people needed this and there weren’t a lot of filters. But the openness to let people in was a beautiful and spiritual thing!

Anyhow, the point of a filtering process is to protect the members of the group; it’s not good for anyone to join a group that can’t deliver on its promises.

Correspond - have they read the relevant stuff, understand the purpose of the group, tried some practices?

Short visit - come for a week or two. Anyone can be charming for two weeks. Seriously, if someone can’t even pull that off, congrats, you can rule them out! 😅

Long visit - it takes a while for problems to show up. Some professional fakers can pull it off for a few months.

Provisional membership - whatever you want this to mean

Full membership - rights to land, shared resources, etc., whatever your founding documents say.

At each stage you want to evaluate people on actual behavior and not vibes, no matter how spiritual or therapeutic someone seems. It takes at least half a year to get a big enough sample to have any idea if someone is really a good person. If you’re going to let someone do something dangerous or have access to shared resources (especially kids, medical info, cars, etc.) you want to be sure they’ve shown they can handle that specific thing, preferably with references, and preferably gradually.

Before letting someone be a provisional member you should probably do at least one group decision, one work/shared resource thing, and process the conflict that comes up afterward. This will simulate what it’s like to live together without anyone having to sell their house to move in.

Note that this can create a servant class if you have a lot of people hanging around who aren’t members: visitors, volunteers, workers, renters, interns, etc. If you’re making a decision that affects them (especially their pay or access to shared resources) you need to treat it like a decision that affects members.
