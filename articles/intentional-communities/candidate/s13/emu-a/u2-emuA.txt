The problem is that people select communities the same way they pick out vacation rentals. They like the land, love the pond and found everyone they talked with to be warm and inviting.

The pond is kind of like going on a first date with someone; it's great until you have to start talking about whether or not you'll be getting each other vaccinated before moving in together (metaphorically speaking, of course).

Before you visit any community write out your own core values about: kids, money, land, love, health care, food, spirituality, technology, privacy, conflict resolution and leaving.

Also include sexuality and gender. Would you be comfortable having LGBTQ people in your community? Would you want children to be taught to question their sexuality at an early age? What if one of your children turned out to be gay or transgender? If the group you're talking to hasn't talked about this stuff they probably haven't talked about leaving or what values to teach children either.

It's totally okay to have "experimentation" as one of your values, you just need to declare it since the person next to you may not share that value.

Now compare your values to what you see when you visit a group. Also ask about conflict, decision making, leaving and land ownership just in case it wasn't obvious in your visit.
