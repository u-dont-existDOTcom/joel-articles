## Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community P2+3 → P2

CONTEXT BEFORE: Don't worry about getting land yet. If you've got a small handful of people who want to make a community but aren't sure yet, you are already a community, even if it's still an untested one. It sounds cheesy but it's true.

PUBLISHED:

Before ownership and major resources mix, process one real conflict all the way through.

Don’t smooth it over with a vision statement or settle for a truce nobody believes. Name what happened. Give each person time. Find the jealousy, humiliation, fear, or old material involved. Reach an agreement only after the emotional pressure has changed enough that the agreement means something.

REWRITE:

Don't even think about combining ownership or other big resources until you have gone through at least one real conflict together and really dealt with it. Not 'dealt with it' by writing a vision statement and shaking hands, or by calling a truce nobody believes, like "Let's all just agree to leave each other alone". But really, truly dealt with it. That's the real relational pl/ork of each of you naming what happened, allowing each of you to take the time you need, finding whatever is there (jealousy, humiliation, fear, old material, whatever), and dealing with it until the emotional pressure has changed enough that an agreement actually means something. Until then, what's it worth?

CONTEXT AFTER: Until that’s happened, the group is still a circle of promising strangers, and I wouldn’t ask that circle to take on a mortgage yet. In the meantime there’s plenty to do! Meet in rented or borrowed space, or at someone’s house, and find out how much conflict the group can handle before anyone hires a surveyor.

## Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View P4 → P3

CONTEXT BEFORE: When you go to visit a community, have a list ready of what you really believe about children, money, property ownership, romance, medicine, food, spiritual practice, technology, privacy, conflict and leaving. Where are you still unsure? Mark those places too.

PUBLISHED:

Add sexuality and gender to that list. Could an LGBTQ+ member live openly, form relationships, and remain fully protected by the community? Should children be taught that they should question their sexual identities at an early age? What happens if one of the children grows up gay or trans? A group that hasn’t answered those questions hasn’t finished discussing either freedom or child-rearing values.

REWRITE:

Throw sexuality and gender onto that list. Yes, it keeps getting longer. Could an LGBTQ+ member live openly there? Date and/or be in a relationship? Would the community keep them fully protected? Should children be taught to question their sexual identities at an early age? Should one of the children grow up to be gay or transgender, what happens then? Until a group has answered these questions, its values on freedom and child-rearing are incomplete.

CONTEXT AFTER: Willing to experiment? That's a real value too! Make sure that your list declares whether or not you value experimenting, because the folks next to you may think experimenting ended in 1978 and anything that came from it is doctrine now.

Then compare your values with the group’s actual behavior, not only its public language. Ask how a recent conflict was handled. Ask who can block a decision. Ask what happened to the last person who left. Ask who owns the land. The view will still be there after the uncomfortable conversation.

REWRITE:

Once you’ve got a good sense of what is important to you, compare this to how the group actually functions (not just how they talk about it in public). How was a recent conflict handled? Who can block decisions? How did it go for the last person who left the group? Who owns the land? It’ll be uncomfortable to have these conversations, but the view won’t go away after you’ve had them.

CONTEXT AFTER: Who has the title to the land? It matters so much! Whoever has it has a massive amount of power over the community. If the deed is in the founder's name, then they're really a landlord just with a philosophy. If three members out of twelve own everything, then there's effectively two classes of members even if the literature says everyone's equal.
