CONTEXT BEFORE: Full membership: rights to the land and shared resources, a voice, and long-term obligations, whatever your founding agreements say.

PUBLISHED:

At each stage, assess concrete conduct rather than spiritual vocabulary or therapeutic fluency. Vibes are easy, but so is mistaking conformity for safety. Six months gives you a larger sample; it does not validate a “good person” detector. For high-consequence roles involving children, shared resources, medicine, vehicles, or private records, use conduct-specific evidence, references where appropriate, staged access, and an independent way to challenge the decision.

REWRITE:

At each stage, judge people by their conduct, however spiritual or therapeutic they sound. It’s easy to go by vibes, and just as easy to mistake conformity for safety. Six months gives you a bigger sample, but it won’t tell you who’s a good person.

If someone’s going to have a role with real consequences, around children, shared resources, medicine, vehicles or private records, you want evidence of how they handle that specific thing. That means references where they make sense and access given in stages, plus an independent way to challenge the decision.

CONTEXT AFTER: Before anyone becomes a provisional member, do more than hang out together. Make one real group decision, do one piece of work or one shared-resource task together, and process one small conflict afterward. A trial run like that won’t show you anyone’s soul, but it will show you whether your way of coordinating works before anybody sells a house.

## From Visitor to Member—Slowly P10 → P11

CONTEXT BEFORE: Before anyone becomes a provisional member, do more than hang out together. Make one real group decision, do one piece of work or one shared-resource task together, and process one small conflict afterward. A trial run like that won’t show you anyone’s soul, but it will show you whether your way of coordinating works before anybody sells a house.

PUBLISHED:

Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters, and interns do not need full political membership to have rights. If a decision materially affects their compensation or access to shared resources, schedule, safety, housing, records, or discipline, they need clear terms, a way to be heard, and review. Egalitarianism that stops at the membership boundary can recreate a servant class between communes.

REWRITE:

Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters and interns don’t need full political membership to have rights. If a decision really affects their pay or their access to shared resources, they need clear terms and a way to be heard, and the decision should be open to review. The same goes for decisions about their schedule, safety or housing, and their records or discipline. Equality that stops at the membership line can bring back a servant class between communes.
