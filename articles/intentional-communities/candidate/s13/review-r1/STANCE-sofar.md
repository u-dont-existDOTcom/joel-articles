# Stance check: section 13 rewrite ("Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community")

I checked every position, rule and claim in the rewrite against the whole published essay and Joel's stated positions. Sentences that passed aren't listed.

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. A single trial run now gives a sure answer**
- Rewrite: "A trial run like that won't show you anyone's soul, but it will show you whether your way of coordinating works"
- Essay, section 10 ("From One Community to a Movement"): "'We tried it in one room for three months' isn't always a fair test." Section 13 (published), on the visits just before: "time gives you more evidence, not certainty". The published sentence said the simulation "can reveal" this, and the rewrite drops that hedge.
- A reader would wrongly believe: one decision, one task and one small conflict reliably show whether the group's coordination works.

**2. Full-membership rights now most naturally read as overriding the founding agreements**
- Rewrite: "Full membership: rights to the land and shared resources, a voice, and long-term obligations, whatever your founding agreements say."
- Essay, section 13 (published): "Land and shared-resource rights, voice, and long-term obligations follow the founding agreements." Joel, 2026-10-14: "membership rights do depend on following the community's therapy practices as a whole".
- "X, whatever Y says" normally means "X, regardless of Y", and the rewrite uses it that way earlier ("whatever the welcome packet says about everyone being equal").
- A reader would wrongly believe: full members get land rights, a voice and obligations no matter what the agreements say.

**3. Visitors are owed explicit terms and a review (kept from the published text; see C1)**
- Rewrite: "they need clear terms and a way to be heard, and the decision should be open to review." and "The same goes for decisions about their schedule, safety or housing"
- Essay, section 11 ("Founderism: The Four Questions"): "Friends who come to visit don't get written terms, though. That's not how anything works" and "There are implied terms, like be respectful and be honest". Joel, 2026-10-11, on his land before critical mass: "i decide who stays, i decide who leaves, i decide everything".
- A reader would wrongly believe: a friend visiting before any community is formalized is owed explicit terms and a reviewable decision about whether they can stay.

## Contradictions in the published text

**C1. Visitors' terms: section 13 against section 11 and Joel's stated positions**
- Section 13: "If a decision materially affects their ... housing, records, or discipline, they need clear terms, a way to be heard, and review."
- Section 11: "Friends who come to visit don't get written terms, though. That's not how anything works". Joel, 2026-10-11: "nobody gives clear written terms to visiting friends" and "i decide who stays, i decide who leaves".
- One section gives visitors clear terms, a hearing and review; the other gives visiting friends implied terms only, and Joel says the landowner decides who stays. Section 13 never says its process applies only once a community is formalized. The same gap reaches its "The recruiter should not control the decision", while Joel says that before critical mass he takes on partners himself ("I accept their partnership").
- Rewrite still has it: yes, in the visitor sentences and in "The decision shouldn't be up to the person who recruited them."

None. "So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular" agrees with section 8's "Decide on the response process ahead of time, before anyone's face is attached to it", and no passage he hasn't rewritten conflicts with it.
