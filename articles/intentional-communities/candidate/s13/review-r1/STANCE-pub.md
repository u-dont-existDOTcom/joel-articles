# Stance check: section 13 rewrite against the published essay

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. Every party's side treated as old wounds**
- Rewrite: "You find out what old material is getting touched for each of them and where the jealousy/humiliation/fear is"
- Essay, section 11 (Founderism), "How My Own Model Could Become a Cult": "'Your resistance is your wound talking' is one of the most efficient sentences ever invented for avoiding an argument." Published section 13 had "Find the jealousy, humiliation, fear, or old material involved": whichever of these is present, not something in each person.
- A reader would believe each party's side of any conflict is old material for the group to find. That claims more than the published "or ... involved" and comes close to the wound-diagnosis that section 11 warns against.

**2. Full membership rights cut loose from the founding agreements**
- Rewrite: "Full membership: rights to the land and shared resources, a voice, and long-term obligations, whatever your founding agreements say."
- Essay, section 13 (published): "Full membership. Land and shared-resource rights, voice, and long-term obligations follow the founding agreements." Joel, 2026-10-14: "membership rights do depend on following the community's therapy practices as a whole".
- Read like the rewrite's own "whatever the welcome packet says" (meaning "regardless of"), it tells a reader that full members hold these rights regardless of the agreements.

**3. Hedge dropped on what a trial run proves**
- Rewrite: "it will show you whether your way of coordinating works before anybody sells a house"
- Essay, section 10 (From One Community to a Movement), "Experiment Before You Divorce": "'We tried it in one room for three months' is not always a fair test." Published section 13 said a trial "can reveal" this, close to its "time gives you more evidence, not certainty."
- A reader would believe that one decision, one task and one small conflict reliably prove the group's coordination works.

**4. Visitors owed terms, a hearing and review (conflicts with Joel's stated positions)**
- Rewrite, said of "Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters and interns": "they need clear terms and a way to be heard, and the decision should be open to review" and "The same goes for decisions about their schedule, safety or housing". This matches the published text (see the second part).
- Joel, 2026-10-11 23:12: "nobody gives clear written terms to visiting friends". Implied terms are enough for them; internships and probation get written ones. Joel, 2026-10-11 18:35: "before critical mass, my rules apply ... i decide who stays, i decide who leaves".
- A reader would believe a visiting friend is owed explicit terms, a hearing and a review before being asked to leave.

**5. Lower bar for ejection**
- Rewrite: "keeps violating important boundaries, or has become impossible to live with, and then ejecting them can be the right answer"
- Essay, section 13 (published): "repeatedly violates essential boundaries". The same section calls removal "one of the easiest powers to abuse."
- A reader would believe that repeatedly breaking any important boundary, a house rule for example, can justify ejection, where the published text required essential ones.

## Contradictions in the published text

**Visitors' rights against Joel's stated positions. The rewrite still has it.**
- Published section 13: "Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters, and interns do not need full political membership to have rights." Then: "they need clear terms, a way to be heard, and review."
- Joel (2026-10-11) gives visiting friends implied terms rather than "clear written terms" and keeps written terms for internships and probation. Before critical mass, he decides himself who stays and who leaves. The published rule names visitors first and gives them a hearing and review over housing decisions. It makes no exception for friends or for the time before critical mass.
- The rewrite keeps it (item 4 above).

I found no other place where section 13 takes back its own words or contradicts another section. Section 13 leaves it to the community to decide "what calls for outside help", so I did not count it.
