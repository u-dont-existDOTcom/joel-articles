## Resources, Land, and Exit Terms P4 → P3+4

CONTEXT BEFORE: Some kind of legal form has to be chosen before the land is bought, whether that’s a cooperative, trust, association, company, or whatever fits your jurisdiction. Then you need to decide voting rights and access/use rights for shared resources, and whether contributions get counted. Work out inheritance and outside debt, how improvements get handled, and what happens when somebody leaves.

PUBLISHED:

Then make the material system legible. A money-free community still has scarce things and bottlenecks. Members should be able to know what the community owns or holds in common, what is reserved, what obligations exist outside the community, who can allocate major resources, and where conflicts of interest lie. Large transfers of land, equipment, stores, or outside cash should not depend on one person’s memory or permission. Define exit rights in whatever unit makes sense—cash if the community uses it, but also tools, transport, temporary housing, stored goods, or continued use rights—so exit is not a paper right that works only when the community has spare money.

REWRITE:

Even a money-free community has scarce things and bottlenecks, so the material side has to be visible to everyone. Members ought to be able to see what the community owns or holds in common and what’s been set aside, and what it owes to anyone outside. They should know who can hand out major resources, and where the conflicts of interest are. Big transfers of land, equipment, stored supplies or outside cash shouldn’t depend on one person’s memory or say-so.

People also need to be able to leave with something, in whatever form makes sense. That might be cash, if the community uses it, or it might be tools, transport, temporary housing, stored goods or the right to keep using something. Otherwise the right to leave is a paper right that only works when there happens to be spare money.

CONTEXT AFTER: Good people leave for good reasons, like a dying parent, love or work. Health and exhaustion count too, and so does a change of mind. If leaving means giving up everything they put in, staying becomes coercive.

REWRITE:

Good people leave for good reasons, like a dying parent, love or work. Health and exhaustion count too, and so does a change of mind. If leaving means giving up everything they put in, staying becomes coercive.

CONTEXT AFTER: That’s why exit terms are best written while nobody is leaving and it’s all still hypothetical. It’s the community version of a prenup that you work out while planning the wedding. Nobody finds that romantic, but it protects people. And it’s much easier to do before anybody is furious.
