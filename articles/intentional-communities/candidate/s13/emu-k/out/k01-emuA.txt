Don’t pick a community like you would pick a vacation rental. “Wow! Look at all this beautiful land! And there’s a pond! There’s a sunset in the pond!! Who needs a beach?! And these people seem really nice, let’s get in here!” “But…wait…you have a vaccination policy?!? And you gotta have some paperwork to get it?!?!”

Bring your values with you when you go to visit a community. Make a list of what you think is important for children, money, owning things, romance, medicine, food, spiritual practice, technology, privacy, conflict, leaving the community, etc. It’s a long list, I know. Put a bunch of question marks next to it.

Also think about sexuality and gender. Are LGBTQ people welcome in your community? Can they date? Be in relationships? Be protected? Should children question their sexual identities? What if a kid in the community comes out as gay or transgender? Until you answer these questions, you don’t have your values on freedom and on raising children complete. Yep. Both of them.

Are you willing to experiment? That’s a value too! Put it in your list of values whether or not you are willing to experiment. The people you live next to might think that the experiment ended in 1978 and everything that’s come out of it is doctrine now.

Finally, when you go visit a group, don’t just listen to what they say. Look at how they operate. How have they handled a conflict recently? Who is able to block decisions? What happened to the last person who left the community? (And who owns the land?) Is that how you want to live? Yeah, it’s a little uncomfortable to talk about these things, but the view isn’t going anywhere!
