# Repeats and contradictions: Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community

I checked every block of that section (B273–B304) against every other block in it and against the rest of the article. This report lists only repeats and contradictions that involve at least one block there. Repeats are numbered in article order of their first instance. Entries marked (mild) are ones a reader may not register.

Section names used below:

- Forming Group = Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community. "Intro" means B274–B277, before its first subheading. Subsections: Values = Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View; Resources = Resources, Land, and Exit Terms; Visitor to Member = From Visitor to Member—Slowly; Has to Go = When Somebody Has to Go.
- Four Parts = The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together (subsections Founded on Freedom; 4. The container)
- Medicine = The Medicine Part - Yes, I'm Naming It (subsection After the Psychedelic Calling)
- Grown Children = The Grown Children Get the Final Review (subsections Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection; The Door Must Open Outward at Adulthood)
- Zapatistas = The Best Model I’ve Seen: Zapatistas
- Movement = From One Community to a Movement (subsection Federated Communities Make a Movement)
- Founderism = Founderism: The Four Questions (subsection How My Own Model Could Become a Cult, shortened to Cult)
- Absorption = The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For
- Dissolution = Dissolution and Exit

## 1. Repeats

### Repeat 1. Past experiments harden into doctrine (mild)

Blocks: B63, B282

- B63 (Four Parts › Founded on Freedom): “When agreements get frozen, religious communities tend to call the frozen version doctrine.”
- B282 (Forming Group › Values): “the folks next to you may think experimenting ended in 1978 and anything that came from it is doctrine now.”
- Shared point: what a group once tried gets frozen into doctrine.
- Later instance: it adds a new instruction (put your stance on experimenting on your values list). The doctrine claim comes back only as the reason for it, so the echo is light.
- Better: B63. The claim stands on its own there; in B282 it only backs a different instruction.

### Repeat 2. Settle in advance what someone who leaves gets

- B84 (Four Parts › The container): “What happens when someone wants to leave?”
- B86 (Four Parts › The container): “any more than medicine makes up for the lack of a written exit agreement.”
- B286 (Forming Group › Resources): “and what happens when somebody leaves.”
- B287 (Forming Group › Resources): “It is also important to specify what rights people have to resources when they leave the community.”
- B288 (Forming Group › Resources): “You should write down the exit terms while no one is leaving yet”

- Shared point: decide, in writing, what a departing member gets.
- Later instances: B84 and B86 announce it. In Resources it then comes three blocks running. B287's sentence repeats B286's item and adds nothing; the rest of B287 adds the forms the support can take. B288 adds when to write the terms (before anyone is leaving) and why. B346 is a recap.
- Better: B288. It has the instruction, the timing and the reason together.
- Related: repeats 9, 11 and 22 are other exit points made in the same blocks.

### Repeat 3. A moneyless community still has scarce things, and someone controls them

Blocks: B84, B221, B287

- B84 (Four Parts › The container): “Even in a moneyless community, someone can control what's scarce. Who decides how these things are distributed?”
- B221 (Movement › Federated Communities Make a Movement): “Even if nobody uses money internally, communities still need the same honesty about exchanges and obligations.”
- B287 (Forming Group › Resources): “Visibility is important even in a money-free community. There are always scarce things and bottlenecks.” and “Who has authority over distribution of major resources?”
- Shared point: without money, scarcity and control over it remain, so they must be visible.
- Later instances: B221 applies it between communities, which is new. B287 repeats B84's claim and question in nearly the same words, so a reader who remembers B84 would feel told again. Its additions are specific checks: know what is held, set aside and owed; name conflicts of interest; large transfers can't rest on one person.
- Better: B287. It turns B84's questions into checks, in the subsection about resources.

## 2. Contradictions

### C1. Visitors: unwritten terms, or clear terms? (mild)

Blocks: B239, B299

- B239 (Founderism): “Friends who come to visit don't get written terms, though.” and “There are implied terms, like be respectful and be honest, and those shouldn't need to be in writing.”
- B299 (Forming Group › Visitor to Member): “Do visitors, volunteers, workers, renters and interns at a commune need some rights” and “they need clear terms and a way to be heard”
- The pull: B239 says visiting friends get only implied terms. B299 lists visitors first among people who need clear terms, a hearing and review when decisions affect their housing, safety and so on.
- A reading that removes the clash (not stated in the text): B239 describes the informal stage on the author's own land, and B299 an established commune. Also, clear isn't the same as written, and B299 applies only when a decision really affects them.
- Better: B299 holds for an established commune, the article's main case; B239 fits only the pre-community stage.

### C2. Removal: decided case by case, or settled in advance?

Blocks: B247, B302

- B247 (Founderism › Cult): “It depends on so many factors that I can't decide any of this ahead of time. It's up to the people involved.”
- B302 (Forming Group › Has to Go): “So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular. What conduct might constitute cause for immediate action to protect people?”
- The pull: B247 says whether to put someone out, or make an exception, can't be decided ahead. B302 says to settle the removal process ahead, including which conduct counts.
- A reading that removes the clash (not stated in the text): B247 is the author declining to rule on outcomes for every community, and B302 asks each community to set its own process. The article doesn't draw that line, and deciding which conduct counts comes close to what B247 says can't be done ahead.
- Better: B302. It is in the section about removal and gives usable steps.

### C3. Already a community, or still strangers?

Blocks: B273, B274, B276

- B273 (heading of Forming Group): “Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community”
- B274 (Forming Group, intro): “you are already a community, even if it's still an untested one.”
- B276 (Forming Group, intro): “Until that’s happened, the group is still a circle of promising strangers”
- The pull: two blocks apart, the same untested group is called a community and then strangers.
- What softens it: untested (B274) and promising (B276) point the same way. But strangers reads as the opposite of the heading's claim, and the text doesn't connect the two.
- Better: B274. It carries the section's stated position; B276's strangers is the outlier.

### C4. What comes first: the vision, or practicing together? (mild)

Blocks: B274, B289, B346

- B274 (Forming Group, intro): “If you've got a small handful of people who want to make a community but aren't sure yet”
- B289 (Forming Group › Resources): “First you get the vision, then you get the members, then you pool the major resources, and lastly you get the land.”
- B346 (Practice): “The better order is less cinematic. Practice together. Process a real conflict. Clarify values. Test membership.”
- The pull: B289 gives Christian's order, vision before members, as the order to follow. But the section starts from a group of unsure people (B274), and the closing's better order puts practicing together and a real conflict before clarifying values. The Zapatistas section also argues for practice before theory (B201: “build the practice first and develop the theory afterward.”).
- What agrees: all of them put land last. Only the first step differs, and the article doesn't say which to follow or why the order changed.
- Better: B346. It matches the section's own sequence (B274–B276) and the Zapatista principle; B289's first step is the odd one out.

## 3. The five repeats a reader would most notice

1. Repeat 17, land comes last. It appears nine times, and the whole closing section restates it.
2. Repeat 24, time gives evidence, not certainty. It appears three times in five blocks.
3. Repeat 19, a real conflict before property. B276 restates B275 in the next block, and it returns in B298, B346 and B360.
4. Repeat 4, whoever holds the land title holds the power. B283 asks “Who owns the land?” and the next subsection opens by asking it again as “very important”, after B86 and B231 already made the point.
5. Repeat 6, taking people in faster than the group can carry them. B291 restates B264 and B269 from the section just before, and B126 from the medicine section.
