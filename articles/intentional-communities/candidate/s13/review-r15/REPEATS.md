# Repeats check: "Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community"

Scope: every repeat or contradiction below involves at least one block from the section "Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community" (B273 to B304). Each instance names its section in square brackets, with the subsection after a › where there is one.

"Forming Group" in brackets is short for that section. Its blocks sit under these headings:
- B274 to B277: directly under the section heading
- B279 to B283: Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View
- B285 to B289: Resources, Land, and Exit Terms
- B291 to B299: From Visitor to Member—Slowly
- B301 to B304: When Somebody Has to Go

Quotes are copied exactly from the article, including its mix of straight and curly apostrophes.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

### 1. Frozen agreements turn into doctrine
- B63 [The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together › Founded on Freedom]: "When agreements get frozen, religious communities tend to call the frozen version doctrine."
- B282 [Forming Group › Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View]: "the folks next to you may think experimenting ended in 1978 and anything that came from it is doctrine now"
- Shared point: what started as an experiment or an agreement hardens into doctrine.
- Does the later one add anything? Partly. B282 adds an instruction (put your stance on experimenting on your values list), but the reason it gives is B63's point again. A mild echo.
- Better carrier: B63. It states the claim and pairs it with the secular version ("the process"). B282 only needs it as a reason.

### 2. A community without money still has scarcity and power
- B84 [The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together › 4. The container]: "Even in a moneyless community, someone can control what's scarce."
- B221 [From One Community to a Movement › Federated Communities Make a Movement]: "Even if nobody uses money internally, communities still need the same honesty about exchanges and obligations."
- B287 [Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms]: "Even if you're a money-free community, you'll still have scarce resources and bottlenecks."
- Shared point: dropping money doesn't remove scarce goods, power over them, or the need to keep track of them.
- Does the later one add anything? B287's opening sentence is B84 again in nearly the same words, so a reader would feel told twice. What follows it in B287 is new: make holdings, set-asides, obligations and authority visible. B221 applies the idea between communities, which is also new.
- Better carrier: B84. It names the scarce goods and asks who decides. B287 only needs it as the lead-in to its visibility rule.

### 3. Inner or spiritual strength doesn't keep the practical base running
- B85 [The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together › 4. The container]: "It's rare to see someone come back from an ecstatic vision with news of how to revamp the community’s policies"
- B96 [The Medicine Part - Yes, I'm Naming It]: "the insight doesn't do the practical work for it. Sometimes the dishes need doing"
- B265 [The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For]: "The water well doesn’t care how beautiful the new member’s heart is."
- B277 [Forming Group]: "a group could be great on an emotional level but be dead in the water because the well fails" / "It's boring stuff, I know."
- Shared point: emotional or spiritual quality can't replace dull practical work. The water still has to run.
- Does the later one add anything? B277 adds a budgeting checklist (water, food stores, how long the cash lasts, critical skills). But its closing line brings back B265's well a dozen blocks later, and "boring stuff" repeats B85's "less exciting topics". A reader would feel told again about the well.
- Better carrier: B265 for the well line, because it is sharper and tied to the eight-person example. B277's own contribution is the checklist and the one-person point (see item 16).

### 4. Taking people in faster than the group can support them
- B86 [The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together › 4. The container]: "when members come on board faster than the group can support them, all the peer counseling in the world won't make up for it"
- B126 [The Medicine Part - Yes, I'm Naming It › After the Psychedelic Calling]: "A group that begins in collective spiritual euphoria can recruit too quickly and promise too much"
- B264 [The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For]: "how many people can a group take in without hurting the existing members or the people they are taking in?"
- B269 [The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For]: "A founding group shouldn’t pretend it can give you those."
- B291 [Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly]: "Being open to everybody felt so spiritual! The practical burden piled up anyway." / "A membership process is there to protect both the people who are already there and the newcomers."
- Shared point: spiritual openness leads groups to admit and promise more than they can carry, and that hurts both the members and the newcomers.
- Does the later one add anything? B291 adds The Farm as a concrete case, and B126 had promised that the membership section would deal with this. But "protect both the people who are already there and the newcomers" is B264's point again, and "felt so spiritual" retells B126's euphoric recruiting. A reader would feel told again.
- Better carrier: B291. The Farm example makes it concrete, and it sits in the membership section that B126 sends readers to.

### 5. Whoever holds title to the land holds the power
- B86 [The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together › 4. The container]: "the mess that is created when one person owns the land or has the final say about what will happen on it"
- B231 [Founderism: The Four Questions]: "Whose name is on the deed?"
- B283 [Forming Group › Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View]: "Who owns the land?"
- B285 [Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms]: "If the deed is in the name of the founder, then he or she is really a landlord with a philosophy."
- B345 [Practice Comes Before Property]: "ownership concentrated because somebody had the money"
- Shared point: land held in one person's name, or a few people's names, is real power over everyone else.
- Does the later one add anything? B285 adds the partial case (three names out of twelve makes two classes of members) and the "landlord with a philosophy" line. Otherwise a reader who has met B86 and B231 would feel told again. B283 and B345 restate it in a phrase each.
- Better carrier: B285. It is the passage about land, it has the sharpest line, and it is the only one with the two-classes case.

### 6. Beautiful land doesn't fix what actually breaks communities
- B86 [The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together › 4. The container]: "But no amount of gorgeous land will make up for the mess"
- B279 [Forming Group › Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View]: "Have you noticed people often choose their community in the same way they would choose a vacation rental?"
- B352 [Practice Comes Before Property › If You Can’t Move]: "People sometimes buy forty acres and then import every kilogram of their unprocessed material to a more scenic location."
- Shared point: falling for the land or the view doesn't solve the problem underneath.
- Does the later one add anything? Partly. Each names a different hidden problem: ownership in B86, values in B279, unprocessed inner material in B352. But the warning that the view won't save you lands again, especially in B352 after the B278 heading and B283's "the view won’t go away".
- Better carrier: B279. The whole subsection is built on it and its heading names it. B352 is a quick joke on the same idea.

### 7. Settle how to respond before it's about anyone, and protect people first
- B181 [The Grown Children Get the Final Review › Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection]: "Decide on the response process ahead of time, before anyone's face is attached to it." / "Keep everyone at risk safe first, right away, then make sure the accusation is investigated competently and fairly."
- B302 [Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go]: "So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular." / "What will constitute cause for immediate action to protect people?"
- Shared point: write down how accusations will be handled before any particular person is involved. Protect people right away, then review fairly.
- Does the later one add anything? B302 adds the steps: temporary restrictions, keeping evidence, recusal, the accused person's reply and appeal, protecting their housing and records, and when to bring in outside help. But its lead instruction is B181's in nearly the same words, so a reader would feel told again.
- Better carrier: B302 sits in the more natural place for a general rule (the section on removal) and spells out the steps. B181's wording is sharper but tied to reports about children.

### 8. The right to leave has to be usable, not only on paper
- B189 [The Grown Children Get the Final Review › The Door Must Open Outward at Adulthood]: "It would be great if the community could also provide resources to smooth the transition, like temporary housing, help with travel, etc."
- B220 [From One Community to a Movement › Federated Communities Make a Movement]: "or the freedom to move is just decorative"
- B248 [Founderism: The Four Questions › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult]: "Each person should have a usable avenue for exit."
- B287 [Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms]: "Otherwise your "right" to leave is only on paper"
- B288 [Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms]: "That's coercive -- they HAVE to stay if they can't afford to go."
- Shared point: freedom to leave means nothing unless the person leaving gets the material means to go (housing, transport, goods).
- Does the later one add anything? B287 and B288 make the same point back to back. B288 adds the good reasons people leave and calls the trap coercion, but "they HAVE to stay if they can't afford to go" is B287's "only on paper" again. B248 had already said it for members and B220 for moving between communities. B189 says it for grown children, a different situation with the same provisions.
- Better carrier: B287. It lists what a community can actually give someone who leaves, with or without money.

### 9. Judge a community by how power actually works, not how it presents itself
- B231 [Founderism: The Four Questions]: "The org chart might look flat and horizontal, while the keys, passwords, titles and even just general reputation all point to one person."
- B233 [Founderism: The Four Questions]: "Sense that former members become morally contaminated the moment they leave."
- B235 [Founderism: The Four Questions]: "Ask these questions about any community, teacher or method."
- B283 [Forming Group › Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View]: "compare this to how the group actually functions (not just how they talk about it in public)" / "How did it go for the last person who left the group?"
- Shared point: when sizing up a community, check what it does against what it says: who really controls things, who owns the land, how people who left are treated.
- Does the later one add anything? Partly. B283 adds how a recent conflict was handled and who can block decisions, and frames the check against the visitor's own values list. But its land and leaver questions are B231 and B233 asked again, and B235 had already told readers to put such questions to any community.
- Better carrier: B283. It sits where the reader is told how to visit and choose. B231 to B235 are aimed at founders.

### 10. Reviews and appeals go to someone with no stake in the decision
- B232 [Founderism: The Four Questions]: "If the founder selects the person to review the founder"
- B248 [Founderism: The Four Questions › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult]: "No single person or permanent group of people should handle grievances and have the power to decide who gets into or stays"
- B267 [The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For]: "review by someone who wasn’t part of the decision"
- B295 [Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly]: "Hint: not the person who recruited them!"
- B297 [Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly]: "ensure there’s always some way to appeal decisions about these roles to a separate authority if needed"
- B303 [Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go]: "What's really needed is that there be a competent layer that isn't reviewing its own conduct." / "Just not whoever's being reviewed!"
- Shared point: whoever has a stake in a decision can't be the one who reviews it.
- Does the later one add anything? This is partly a theme that keeps developing, since each passage applies the rule to a new decision: reviewing the founder, admission, "not now" decisions, full membership, sensitive roles, removal. But B303 states the general rule as if it were new, after the reader has met it five times. B303's real addition is that "outside" doesn't have to mean the state, plus its list of who could review.
- Better carrier: B303. It is the one general statement, with concrete options for who can do the reviewing.

### 11. Plan the parting while everyone is still hopeful (marriage, pre-nup, living will)
- B238 [Founderism: The Four Questions]: "Leaving a commune is the divorce half of it, where the shared resources have to get split up." / "we can hopefully be more conscious about the process and less romantic"
- B288 [Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms]: "Write up the exit terms when it's only theoretical -- like a pre-nup when you're planning the wedding. Not romantic, but protective!"
- B307 [Dissolution and Exit]: "Founders rarely want to discuss dissolution while choosing a name and imagining the first harvest. That is when the discussion needs to happen."
- B309 [Dissolution and Exit]: "Design this like a living will."
- Shared point: agree how shared resources get split while everyone is still hopeful, and be unromantic about it. B238 and B288 both make the comparison to marriage and divorce.
- Does the later one add anything? B288 turns B238's comparison into an instruction (write exit terms now), but the marriage-and-divorce comparison and "less romantic" / "Not romantic" come twice. B307 and B309 carry the same advice to the whole community ending, which is a new case, with a matching comparison (a living will after the pre-nup).
- Better carrier: B288. It sits in the passage about exit terms and says what to do. In B238 the comparison is a side trip in a passage about informal formation.

### 12. Someone impossible to live with gets removed
- B244 [Founderism: The Four Questions › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult]: "If someone starts worshipping Satan, say, they get booted."
- B246 [Founderism: The Four Questions › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult]: "If it gets to the point of being impossible to live with, they will be kicked out."
- B301 [Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go]: "repeatedly violate essential community boundaries, or are just impossible to live with"
- Shared point: a community may expel a member who becomes impossible to live with or crosses its essential lines.
- Does the later one add anything? B301 adds danger to others as grounds, prefers restorative work first, and says the community needn't act as a treatment program. But "impossible to live with" as grounds for removal is B246's claim in the same words, so a reader would feel told again.
- Better carrier: B301. It is the section about removal and gives the fuller grounds. B246 is a passing line in the section on cult risk.

### 13. Some individual rights can't be overridden by the community
- B249 [Founderism: The Four Questions › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult]: "There are some basic human rights that can’t be voted away from you by the majority in a community"
- B303 [Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go]: "autonomy doesn't mean that anyone can erase non-waivable individual rights"
- Shared point: certain individual rights stand above anything the community decides.
- Does the later one add anything? A brief echo. B303 adds that autonomy doesn't remove outside jurisdiction either. A reader would recognize it, but it is one clause.
- Better carrier: B249. It states the rule with its limit (resource availability) and gives examples.

### 14. Don't commit land or property until the group has been through a real conflict
- B274 [Forming Group]: "Don't worry about getting land yet."
- B275 [Forming Group]: "until you've been through at least one real conflict together and REALLY dealt with it"
- B276 [Forming Group]: "I wouldn’t ask that circle to take on a mortgage yet." / "find out how much conflict the group can handle before anyone hires a surveyor"
- B298 [Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly]: "Can you even process a small conflict afterwards? Do a trial run like that before anybody sells a house."
- B346 [Practice Comes Before Property]: "Practice together. Process a real conflict."
- B360 [Practice Comes Before Property › What to Do This Month]: "handle one disagreement, and keep commitments before discussing a shared future"
- B364 [Practice Comes Before Property › What to Do This Month]: "Do those three things before you spend serious time or money looking for land."
- Shared point: test the group through a real conflict, and practice together, before buying land or pooling property.
- Does the later one add anything? B275 is the full version: it says what really dealing with a conflict involves. B274 and B276 bracket it with the same instruction to hold off on land, so the reader gets it three times in three blocks. B276 adds meeting in borrowed space. B298 adds tests of deciding and working together but ends on the same "before anybody sells a house". B346, B360 and B364 tell it again in the closing sections. B360 adds listening and keeping commitments, and B364 ties it to the month's action list.
- Better carrier: B275. Only it says what dealing with a conflict means, and it sits in the section built on this point.

### 15. A small group without land already is a community
- B274 [Forming Group]: "you are already a community, even if it's still an untested one"
- B353 [Practice Comes Before Property › If You Can’t Move]: "Reparenting, somatic practice, jealousy processing, and conflict repair do not wait for a deed."
- B354 [Practice Comes Before Property › If You Can’t Move]: "A weekly group that has practiced together for two years already contains a real piece of community. It may never own land."
- Shared point: a group that practices together is a community before it has land, or without ever having it.
- Does the later one add anything? B354 adds that the group may never own land and can still change how people get through divorce, illness and grief, which speaks to readers who can't move. But its first sentence is B274's claim again. B353 restates the half about not needing land to begin.
- Better carrier: B274. It is the section's thesis and matches its heading. What B354 adds is the case of never owning land.

### 16. Nothing essential should depend on one person
- B277 [Forming Group]: "one exhausted person is the only one who knows where everything is and how it works"
- B287 [Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms]: "It won't do to have the transfer of major resources (land, equipment, stores, outside cash, etc.) dependent on one person's memory or permission."
- Shared point: a group is in danger when its working knowledge or its resources sit in one person's head or hands.
- Does the later one add anything? Partly. B277 is about fragility: one worn-out keeper. B287 adds the power side ("permission"), which echoes B231's keys and passwords all pointing to one person. A reader would still meet the "one person's memory" point twice within ten blocks.
- Better carrier: B287. It covers both memory and permission, and it sits with the other rules about visibility.

### 17. The founding order ends with land
- B289 [Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms]: "vision, then members, then pooling major resources, then land."
- B345 [Practice Comes Before Property]: "Most community projects begin in the wrong order: land first"
- B346 [Practice Comes Before Property]: "Clarify values. Test membership. Decide ownership and exit. Then consider land."
- Shared point: values, members and ownership terms come before land.
- Does the later one add anything? B346 adds practice and a real conflict at the front and puts exit next to ownership. But values, then membership, then ownership, then land is B289's sequence again, and it is also the path the "Forming Group" section had just walked through.
- Better carrier: B346. It is the article's own sequence, in a section named for it. B289 hands the sequence to a book.

### 18. More time gives more evidence, not certainty
- B294 [Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly]: "may be able to pull off an act for months, so time gets you more evidence, not certainty."
- B296 [Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly]: "Even giving it six months will give you more data, but there’s still no “good person” detector."
- B298 [Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly]: "It won't show you anyone's soul, but it can show you whether your way of coordinating works."
- Shared point: watching someone longer helps, but it can't tell you who they really are.
- Does the later one add anything? B296 repeats B294's caveat two blocks later with nothing new on this point. Its new part is a different point: judge conduct, not talk. B298 turns the caveat toward the group and says what a trial run can show (whether your coordination works), which is new.
- Better carrier: B294. It comes with the faker example and the long-visit stage it qualifies.

## 2. Contradictions

### C1. Do visitors get terms?
- B239 [Founderism: The Four Questions]: "Friends who come to visit don't get written terms, though. That's not how anything works"
- B299 [Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly]: "Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters and interns at the commune (yes, even interns!) are entitled to some rights" / "They should have clear terms"
- The clash: B239 says visitors get no written terms. B299 says visitors should have clear terms and a way to get decisions reviewed. Both agree that interns need terms.
- What would reconcile them, though the article doesn't say so: "clear" isn't "written"; B299 applies only "when decisions really affect them"; and B239 describes friends visiting before any formal community exists.
- Which reads as the article's position: B299, because it states a general rule for an existing commune. B239 is about the informal stage around the author's own land.

### C2. Decide in advance, or case by case?
- B247 [Founderism: The Four Questions › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult]: "It depends on so many factors that I can't decide any of this ahead of time. It's up to the people involved."
- B250 [Founderism: The Four Questions › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult]: "It has to be worked out in the moment, context by context."
- B302 [Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go]: "So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular."
- The clash: the section on cult risk says removal decisions (B247) and advice about contact with outsiders (B250) can't be settled in advance. The removal section says settle the process in advance.
- What would reconcile them, though the article doesn't say so: the author can't fix outcomes for every community, but each community should fix its procedure before a case comes up. B250 is about a different topic (contact with outsiders), so it is the weaker half of the clash.
- Which reads as the article's position: B302 for procedure. B247 still stands for the outcome of an individual case, such as an exception for someone who is sick.

### C3. Already a community, or still strangers?
- B274 [Forming Group]: "you are already a community, even if it's still an untested one"
- B276 [Forming Group]: "Until that’s happened, the group is still a circle of promising strangers"
- The clash: two blocks apart, the same untested group is first called a community and then a circle of strangers.
- What would reconcile them, though the article doesn't say so: "untested" in B274 points to the answer (a community that a real conflict hasn't tested yet), but the article never says how both labels hold at once.
- Which reads as the article's position: B274. It matches the section heading, while B276's line is a warning about property.
