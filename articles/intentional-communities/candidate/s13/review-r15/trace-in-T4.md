A safe community cannot promise to keep everyone. If somebody poses a serious or generalized danger, repeatedly violates essential boundaries, or makes ordinary shared life impossible, ejection can be the right answer. Restorative work is useful when it can work on a reasonable timescale; nobody has a duty to turn the commune into a treatment program for a person who is endangering everyone else.

REWRITE:

A community that wants to be safe can't promise to keep everyone. Yeah, that's pretty tough, but sometimes somebody's gotta go. It can be the right thing to eject someone when they're a serious danger to others (or to everyone in general), repeatedly violate essential community boundaries, or are just impossible to live with. If it's possible to do restorative work with the person in question in a reasonable amount of time, all the better. But if the person is dangerous, the community shouldn't have to serve as a treatment program for them.
