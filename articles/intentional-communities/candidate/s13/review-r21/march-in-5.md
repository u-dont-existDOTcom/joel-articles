## Resources, Land, and Exit Terms

Who will have title to the land? Very important! Whoever has it will have enormous power over the rest of the community. If the deed is in the name of the founder, then he or she is really a landlord with a philosophy. If three out of twelve members own everything, then whether or not your literature says everyone is equal, there are two classes of members.

What kind of legal entity (cooperative, trust, association, company, whatever fits your jurisdiction) will hold the land? This needs to be decided before it's bought. Then you'll need to decide who has what voting rights, who has what access and use rights to common resources, how you will account for people's contributions (if at all), how inheritance works, what about outside debt, how improvements get handled, and what happens when someone wants to leave the community. Lots of decisions to make!

And how will you handle the VISIBILITY of what the community has? There will be bottlenecks, sorry! It will be important for people to know what's owned or held in common, what you've set aside, what obligations you have to outsiders, who has authority to hand out the major resources, where conflicts of interest are, etc. It won't do to have the transfer of major resources (land, equipment, stores, outside cash, etc.) dependent on one person's memory or permission.

Good people leave for good reasons -- dying parent, love, work, health, exhaustion, change of mind, etc. You don't want them to lose everything they've put in! That's coercive -- they HAVE to stay if they can't afford to go. Whether or not you use money, you'll need to have a way of providing people with resources when they leave (cash, if you use it, but also tools, transportation, temporary housing, stored up goods, the right to keep using something, or whatever). Otherwise their "right" to leave is only on paper -- it only works when the community happens to have spare money! Write up the exit terms when it's only theoretical -- like a pre-nup when you're planning the wedding. Not romantic, but protective! Much easier than doing it once anybody's furious.

Honestly? For the nuts and bolts of all this (members, resources, land, agreements, etc.), I'd go to Diana Leafe Christian's [*Creating a Life Together*](https://library.uniteddiversity.coop/Ecovillages_and_Low_Impact_Development/Creating_a_Life_Together-Practical_Tools_to_Grow_Ecovillages_and_Intentional_Communities.pdf) (on the reading list at the end).
