# Repeats check: "Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community"

I read the whole article, B0 to B403. I checked every block in "Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community" (B273 to B304) against every other block in that section and against the rest of the article. Only repeats and contradictions that involve at least one block in that section are listed.

"Forming Group" below is short for that section. Its parts are the opening (B273 to B277), "Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View" (B278 to B283), "Resources, Land, and Exit Terms" (B284 to B289), "From Visitor to Member—Slowly" (B290 to B299) and "When Somebody Has to Go" (B300 to B304). Instances outside it are named by their section, and by their subsection where there is one.

Counts: 22 repeats, 2 contradictions, and 2 callbacks that depend on earlier sections.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

### 1. B63, B282: an experiment hardens into doctrine
- B63 (The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together › Founded on Freedom): "When agreements get frozen, religious communities tend to call the frozen version doctrine."
- B282 (Forming Group › Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View): "the experiment ended in 1978, and everything that came out of it has been doctrine ever since."
- Shared point: what started as an experiment gets frozen into doctrine.
- Later instance: makes willingness to experiment a value to declare, and to check against the people you would live with. The doctrine point itself comes a second time, so this is partly a repeat.
- Better placed: B63. That is where the article says agreements have to stay open to lived experience. B282 only needs the instruction.

### 2. B86, B231, B283, B285, B345: whoever holds the land holds the power
- B86 (The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together › 4. The container): "the mess that is created when one person owns the land or has the final say about what will happen on it"
- B231 (Founderism: The Four Questions): "Then follow the practical power. Whose name is on the deed?"
- B283 (Forming Group › Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View): "Who owns the land?"
- B285 (Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): "Whoever has it will have enormous power over the rest of the community."
- B285 (Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): "If the deed is in the name of the founder, then he or she is really a landlord with a philosophy."
- B345 (Practice Comes Before Property): "ownership concentrated because somebody had the money"
- Shared point: holding title to the land gives real power over everyone else, whatever the community says about equality.
- Later instances: B231 makes it one test of a founder's power. B283 makes it a question to ask on a visit. B285 adds that when a few members own everything, there are two classes of members. B345 only names it in its list of the wrong order. By B285 the reader has met the point twice and feels told again, though the "two classes" point is new.
- Better placed: B285. It is the land section, and it gives the fullest version.

### 3. B86, B126, B264, B291: taking in people faster than the group can carry them

- B264 (The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For): "how many people can a group take in without hurting the existing members or the people they are taking in?"

- B291 (Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly): "A membership process is there to protect both the people who are already there and the newcomers."
- Shared point: taking in people, especially people in need, faster than the group can support them hurts the group and the newcomers alike.
- Later instances: B126 points ahead to "The sections on capacity and on membership", so B264 and B291 read as the follow-through. B291 adds The Farm as a real case. Its closing line, though, restates B264's question about protecting existing members and newcomers, and that line reads as told again.
- Better placed: B264. The capacity section is where the limit is worked out. What B291 adds is The Farm example.

### 4. B96, B296: spiritual talk and experiences don't show how someone behaves
- B96 (The Medicine Part - Yes, I'm Naming It): "people who can collect 12 ceremonies and 6 origin stories and still can't say sorry to their housemate."
- B296 (Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly): "No matter how good it sounds when they talk about their spirituality or their therapy, don’t go by that. What do they actually do?"
- Shared point: spiritual or therapeutic experience, and talk about it, is not evidence of how a person treats others.
- Later instance: adds a rule for membership, which is to judge conduct at every stage. The situations differ (who medicine attracts, who to admit), so this reads only faintly as a repeat. B117's "experience collectors" is a third echo in between.
- Better placed: B296. Membership is where the reader has to act on it.

### 5. B181, B302: set the process before it is about a particular person
- B181 (The Grown Children Get the Final Review › Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection): "Decide on the response process ahead of time, before anyone's face is attached to it."
- B181 (The Grown Children Get the Final Review › Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection): "Keep everyone at risk safe first, right away, then make sure the accusation is investigated competently and fairly."
- B302 (Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go): "So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular."
- B302 (Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go): "What counts as cause for immediate protective action? Who decides to put temporary restrictions on someone?"
- Shared point: decide in advance how the community will act against a person (protect people first, then investigate fairly), before any particular person is involved.
- Later instance: widens it from an accusation involving a child to any removal. It lists the procedural questions: evidence, recusal, the person's reply, appeal, and the person's interests during the separation. The "before it's about anyone" rule itself comes a second time.
- Better placed: B302 for the general rule, since it gives the whole procedure. B181 is the natural place only for the child-protection case.

### 6. B183, B232, B267, B297, B303: review has to come from someone other than the decider
- B183 (The Grown Children Get the Final Review › Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection): "need access to competent help and review, hopefully including non-intrusive help from outside the home community."
- B232 (Founderism: The Four Questions): "If the founder selects the person to review the founder, holds the records"
- B267 (The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For): "Any real process would need concrete criteria, review by someone who wasn’t part of the decision"
- B297 (Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly): "ensure there’s always some way to appeal decisions about these roles to a separate authority if needed."
- B303 (Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go): "The basic need here is for a competent level of authority that is not reviewing its own conduct."
- B303 (Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go): "The key is that it's not the same people!"
- Shared point: a decision only gets real review from someone who is neither the decider nor the person being reviewed.
- Later instances: each applies the point to a different decision: a dangerous child, the founder, a "not now" on capacity grounds, access to sensitive roles, and removal. B303 states it as if for the first time, so a reader who has met it three times feels told again. What B303 adds is that "outside" doesn't have to mean the state.
- Better placed: B303. It gives the most general statement and names who can do the reviewing.

### 7. B188 to B189, B220, B248, B288: the right to leave has to be usable, not just on paper
- B188 (The Grown Children Get the Final Review › The Door Must Open Outward at Adulthood): "it doesn't need to be artificially impossible for grown kids to leave."
- B189 (The Grown Children Get the Final Review › The Door Must Open Outward at Adulthood): "It would be great if the community could also provide resources to smooth the transition, like temporary housing, help with travel, etc."
- B220 (From One Community to a Movement › Federated Communities Make a Movement): "all have to be set up, or the freedom to move is just decorative."
- B248 (Founderism: The Four Questions › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult): "Each person should have a usable avenue for exit."
- B288 (Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): "Otherwise their "right" to leave is only on paper -- it can't be exercised unless the community has spare money!"
- Shared point: a right to leave only counts if the person can actually afford to go, so the community has to supply what leaving takes, such as housing and transport.
- Later instances: B220 applies the point to moving between communities. B248 states it as a bare rule. B288 adds that a person who can't afford to leave is being coerced, that exit resources needn't be cash, and that exit terms should be written in advance. After "decorative" and "usable avenue", "only on paper" reads as told again.
- Better placed: B288. The exit-terms section is its natural place, and B288 makes the whole argument.

### 8. B202, B303: Zapatista power crept upward and a long review pushed it back down

## 2. Contradictions

### C1. B239, B299: do visitors get terms?
- B239 (Founderism: The Four Questions): "Friends who come to visit don't get written terms, though. That's not how anything works"
- B299 (Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly): "Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters and interns at the commune (yes, even interns!) are entitled to some rights"
- B299 (Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly): "All of them should have clear terms, and the chance to be heard and to have such decisions reviewed."
- The pull: B239 says visitors get no written terms and implied ones are enough. B299 says visitors, among others, should all have clear terms.
- How they fit (the article doesn't say): B239 is about friends visiting before any formal community exists. B299 is about a commune already up and running, and its terms apply when decisions affect someone's pay, resources, schedule, safety, housing, records or discipline. Also, "clear" need not mean "written". This is mild, but a reader who remembers B239 may stop at "Visitors" in B299.
- Better placed: each fits its own situation. B299 is the one about visitors to a working commune.

### C2. B247, B302: can removal be decided ahead of time?
- B247 (Founderism: The Four Questions › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult): "It depends on so many factors that I can't decide any of this ahead of time. It's up to the people involved."
- B250 (Founderism: The Four Questions › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult): "Nobody can give that advice ahead of time, including me."
- B302 (Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go): "So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular."
- The pull: both B247 and B302 are about expelling a member. B247 says this can't be decided in advance and is up to the people involved. B302 says to work out the process in advance, before it concerns anyone. B250 says something similar about contact with outsiders.
- How they fit (the article doesn't say): B247 is about the outcome in a particular case, such as an exception for someone who is sick, and about the author not deciding for other communities. B302 is about procedure: who decides, how evidence is kept, how to appeal. The article never draws that line. Moderate.
- Better placed: B302 for the procedure, and B247 for judging each case.

## 3. The five repeats a reader would most notice

1. Repeat 19 (a real conflict before pooling property): five instances, two of them in back-to-back paragraphs, and the closing sections say it again.
2. Repeat 22 (no “good person” detector): the same warning three times within five paragraphs.
3. Repeat 2 (whoever holds the land holds the power): five instances across four sections.
4. Repeat 7 (the right to leave must be usable): "decorative", "usable avenue" and "only on paper" make the same point in four sections.
5. Repeat 8 (Zapatista power creep): facts from the Zapatista section are retold about a hundred blocks later.
