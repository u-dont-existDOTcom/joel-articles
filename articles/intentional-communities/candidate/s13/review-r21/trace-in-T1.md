## Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community P6 → P4

CONTEXT BEFORE: Until that’s happened, you’re all still promising strangers, and you don’t want to take out a mortgage together yet. That’s okay! There’s lots you can do! You can all meet in a rented or borrowed space, or at each other’s houses, and find out how much conflict the group can handle before you need a surveyor.

PUBLISHED:

Do the boring capacity budget too: water, housing, food stores, health care, tools, seasonal labor, transport, schooling, market access, outside debts or cash runway if the community uses them, and the few skills whose absence can stop everything. A group can have extraordinary emotional skill and still die because the well fails or one exhausted person is the only one who knows where everything is and how it works.

REWRITE:

You will also want a budget of what you have in the way of resources. This is the boring stuff! But you will need to know what the group can offer in the way of water, housing, food stores, health care, tools, seasonal labor, transportation, access to markets, schooling, etc. Do you have outside debts to pay? How long will your cash reserves last, if the community uses them? What critical skills will you need? Being great at the emotional stuff won’t stop you from being dead in the water if the one person who knows where everything is and how it works gets too exhausted to keep going.

CONTEXT AFTER: Have you noticed people often choose their community in the same way they would choose a vacation rental? “Wow! Look at this beautiful land! And a pond, with the sunset in it!! Who needs a beach?! And the people seem really nice, let’s get in here!” Fast forward two years. “Wait, there’s a vaccination policy?? And you need paperwork for it?!?!”

DEDUP NOTE: The failing well is cut here because section 12 ("The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn't For") says it: "The water well doesn't care how beautiful the new member's heart is." The exhausted person stays here.

## Resources, Land, and Exit Terms P4+5+6 → P3+4

CONTEXT BEFORE: What kind of legal entity (cooperative, trust, association, company, whatever fits your jurisdiction) will hold the land? This needs to be decided before it's bought. Then you'll need to decide who has what voting rights, who has what access and use rights to common resources, how you will account for people's contributions (if at all), how inheritance works, what about outside debt, how improveme

PUBLISHED:

Then make the material system legible. A money-free community still has scarce things and bottlenecks. Members should be able to know what the community owns or holds in common, what is reserved, what obligations exist outside the community, who can allocate major resources, and where conflicts of interest lie. Large transfers of land, equipment, stores, or outside cash should not depend on one person’s memory or permission. Define exit rights in whatever unit makes sense—cash if the community uses it, but also tools, transport, temporary housing, stored goods, or continued use rights—so exit is not a paper right that works only when the community has spare money.

Good people leave for good reasons: a dying parent, love, work, health, exhaustion, or a changed mind. If leaving means forfeiting everything contributed, staying becomes coercive.

Write the exit terms while departure is still hypothetical. This is the community equivalent of discussing a prenup while planning the wedding: unromantic, protective, and much easier before anybody is furious.

REWRITE:

And how will you handle the VISIBILITY of what the community has? It will be important for people to know what's owned or held in common, what you've set aside, what obligations you have to outsiders, who has authority to hand out the major resources, where the bottlenecks and conflicts of interest are, etc. It won't do to have the transfer of major resources (land, equipment, stores, outside cash, etc.) dependent on one person's memory or permission.

Good people leave for good reasons -- dying parent, love, work, health, exhaustion, change of mind, etc. You don't want to make them leave everything they've put in! Whether or not you use money, you'll need to have a way of providing people with resources when they leave (cash, if you use it, but also tools, transportation, temporary housing, stored up goods, the right to keep using something, or whatever). Otherwise their "right" to leave is only on paper -- it can't be exercised unless the community has spare money! That's coercive -- they HAVE to stay if they can't afford to go. Write up the exit terms when it's only theoretical -- like a pre-nup when you're planning the wedding. Not romantic, but protective! Much easier than doing it once everybody's furious.

CONTEXT AFTER: For the nuts and bolts of all this (members, money, land, agreements, etc.), I'd honestly go to Diana Leafe Christian's [*Creating a Life Together*](https://library.uniteddiversity.coop/Ecovillages_and_Low_Impact_Development/Creating_a_Life_Together-Practical_Tools_to_Grow_Ecovillages_and_Intentional_Communities.pdf) (on the reading list at the end).

The exit resources moved from the first rewrite paragraph to the second, next to "That's coercive", so the paper right and the coercion are said once, side by side.
