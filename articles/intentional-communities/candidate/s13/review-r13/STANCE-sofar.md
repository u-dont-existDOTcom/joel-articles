# Stance check: section 13 rewrite ("Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community")

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. Outside review becomes the only kind of independent review**
- Rewrite: "What's really needed is that there be a competent layer that isn't reviewing its own conduct." … "Just not the community itself!"
- Essay, section 12 ("The Math of Absorption"): "Any real process would need concrete criteria, review by someone who wasn't part of the decision". Joel, 2026-10-07: "Why should the home community not control the interaction they have with outside?"
- The published test is about function: the layer must be "not reviewing its own conduct", and members who took no part in the decision can meet it. The rewrite's own "Who will recuse themselves?" assumes such members exist. The exclamation changes the test from what the reviewer did to who the reviewer is.
- A reader would come away believing nobody inside the community may ever review a removal, so any independent review means bringing in outsiders.

**2. The vision becomes the first step**
- Rewrite: "First you get the vision, then you get the members, then you pool the major resources, and lastly you get the land."
- Essay, section 16 ("Practice Comes Before Property"): "Practice together. Process a real conflict. Clarify values. Test membership." And under "What to Do This Month": "Learn to listen, switch roles, handle one disagreement, and keep commitments before discussing a shared future."
- The published line only put pairs in order ("vision before membership"). "First … then you get the members" puts the vision ahead of the people, which also cuts against the rewrite's own opening, where the small group already is the community.
- A reader would come away believing a group fixes its vision first and recruits people to it, instead of practicing together and handling a disagreement before it discusses a shared future.

**3. Provisional-membership terms become questions to think about**
- Rewrite: "Have you thought about these questions: How long will a provisional membership be?"
- Essay, section 11 ("Founderism: The Four Questions"): "If someone is offered something like an internship or a probationary period, that would require written terms."
- The published step is an instruction: "Define the period, rights, responsibilities, review process, and exit." The rewrite only asks whether you've thought about them, then sends only full members' rights to the founding agreements.
- A reader would come away believing provisional terms need thinking through but not setting down.

## Contradictions in the published text

None found. Read within the situation its section describes, each rule in the published section 13 agrees with itself, with the rest of the essay, and with Joel's later positions.

## Questions on the author's own paragraphs

None. "So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular" agrees with section 8: "Decide on the response process ahead of time, before anyone's face is attached to it."

Conflicts in the rewrite: 3. Contradictions in the published text: 0.
