The rewrite keeps the published order and headings; a pair shows all the published paragraphs a rewrite paragraph carries. The rewrite is deliberately loose and spoken (short reactions, run-on lists, "etc."); judge what it says, not its polish. ## Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View P6 → P5

CONTEXT BEFORE: Are you willing to experiment? That’s a real value too! Make sure your list of values declares whether or not you are willing to experiment, because the people next to you may feel that the experiment ended in 1978, and everything that came out of it has been doctrine ever since.

PUBLISHED:

Then compare your values with the group’s actual behavior, not only its public language. Ask how a recent conflict was handled. Ask who can block a decision. Ask what happened to the last person who left. Ask who owns the land. The view will still be there after the uncomfortable conversation.

REWRITE:

Once you know what matters to you, you can see how the group really works, not only what it says about itself. Its last big conflict is a good place to start: how did that get handled? Then there’s who can block a decision, and what happened to the last person who left. (And who owns the land!) After that, you can decide if that’s how you want to live. These are uncomfortable conversations, I know, but the view isn’t going anywhere!

CONTEXT AFTER: Who will have title to the land? Very important! Whoever has it will have enormous power over the rest of the community. If the deed is in the name of the founder, then he or she is really a landlord with a philosophy. If three out of twelve members own everything, then whether or not your literature says everyone is equal, there are two classes of members.
