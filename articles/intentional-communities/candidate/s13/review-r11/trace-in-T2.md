## Resources, Land, and Exit Terms P4 → P3

CONTEXT BEFORE: What legal form will hold the land: a cooperative, trust, association, company, or whatever fits your jurisdiction? Decide that before the land is bought. Then you need to decide voting rights and access/use rights for shared resources, and contribution accounting if you use it. Work out inheritance and outside debt, how improvements get handled, and what happens when somebody leaves. That’s a lot

PUBLISHED:

Then make the material system legible. A money-free community still has scarce things and bottlenecks. Members should be able to know what the community owns or holds in common, what is reserved, what obligations exist outside the community, who can allocate major resources, and where conflicts of interest lie. Large transfers of land, equipment, stores, or outside cash should not depend on one person’s memory or permission. Define exit rights in whatever unit makes sense—cash if the community uses it, but also tools, transport, temporary housing, stored goods, or continued use rights—so exit is not a paper right that works only when the community has spare money.

REWRITE:

Visibility is important even in a money-free community. There are always scarce things and bottlenecks. It is important that people be able to know what the community owns or holds in common, what is set aside, what it owes to outsiders. Who has authority over distribution of major resources? Where are the conflicts of interest? Large transfers (land, equipment, stores or outside cash) need to be recorded somewhere, and can't depend on one person's memory or permission. It is also important to specify what rights people have to resources when they leave the community. Whether or not the community uses money, it still needs to be able to provide people with resources when they leave, in whatever form makes sense (cash if it uses money, but also tools, transportation, temporary housing, stored goods or the right to keep using something). Otherwise the "right" to leave is just something on paper that can only be exercised if the community has spare money. That's not much of a right.

CONTEXT AFTER: Good people leave for good reasons, like a dying parent, love, work, health, exhaustion, or just a simple change of mind. Leaving should not require someone to leave everything they’ve put in, as that’s effectively coercing someone to stay.
