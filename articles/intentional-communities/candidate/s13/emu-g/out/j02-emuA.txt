Don't even worry about getting land yet. If you have a small handful of people who want to make a community, but aren't sure yet, then you are already a community, even if it hasn't been tested yet. Sounds cheesy, but it's true.

Also, don't even think about combining ownership (or any other big resource, for that matter) until you've been through at least one conflict together and REALLY dealt with it. Not dealt with it by writing out a vision statement and shaking hands or agreeing to leave each other alone or any other BS way of "resolving" things. But really, truly dealt with it. This includes the relational pl/ork of each of you naming what really went on, giving each of you the time you need, figuring out what came up for each of you (jealousy, humiliation, fear, whatever), and dealing with it until the emotional pressure changes enough that what ever agreement you come up with means anything. Until then, what's it worth?
