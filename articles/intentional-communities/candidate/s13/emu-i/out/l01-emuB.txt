Resources, Land, and Exit Terms

Intentional Community Formation -- Who Owns the Land? Who will have the title? This is VERY important! Whoever has the title will have a lot of power over the community. If the deed is in the name of the community's founder, they are effectively a landlord who happens to have a certain philosophy. If 3 of the 12 members own everything, you have two classes of members, regardless of what your literature says about equality.

Will it be in the name of a cooperative, a trust, an association, a company, whatever? Decide BEFORE you buy the land! Then you have to decide who has voting rights, who has access to (and use of) all of the common resources, how you'll keep track of people's contributions (if at all), what happens when a title owner dies, can a title owner borrow money outside the community, what happens to improvements when someone leaves, etc., etc.

Even if you don't plan to use money, you need visibility. There will always be things that are in short supply and bottlenecks. It is important to know what the community has, what is set aside, what it owes to people outside the community, who is in charge of distributing larger resources, where conflicts of interest are, etc. You can't have all transfer of land, large equipment, larger stores of goods or outside money, etc., dependent on one person's memory or permission. Also, you need to specify what people's rights to those resources are when they leave. Whether or not you have money, you have to have a way of getting people the resources they need when they leave. If you do have money, that may be easiest. If not, you might need tools, transportation, temporary housing, stores, etc. Otherwise your "right" to leave will only be good on paper, or if the community happens to have enough extra money to help you when you go.

Good people leave for good reasons: a dying parent, love, a need to work somewhere else, health reasons, exhaustion, simple change of mind, etc. It is not good to require them to leave everything they've put in when they go: that is coercion to stay. Write it down when it is all theoretical! It's like drawing up a pre-nup when you're planning your wedding. Not very romantic, but very good for protecting everyone. Much easier than doing it when everybody's mad!
