Resources, Land, and Exit Terms

INTERESTED Parties -- Who will have title to the land? Very important! Whoever has it will have enormous power over the rest of the community. If it's in the name of the "founder", then he or she is really your landlord, with a philosophy. If it's in the names of 3 out of 12 members, then despite what your literature says, there are two classes of members.

2. WHAT kind of legal entity (co-op, trust, association, company, etc.) will hold the land? This needs to be decided before it's bought, so that you can decide who has what voting rights, who has what access to and use of common resources, how you will account for people's contributions (if at all), what happens when an owner dies, can an owner borrow money outside the community and what happens if they do, what happens when someone wants to leave the community, etc., etc. Lots of decisions to make!

3. How will you handle VISIBILITY of this issue? Even if you don't use money, you'll still have scarce resources and bottlenecks. It will be important for people to know what you have, what you've set aside, what you owe, who has authority to distribute the major resources, where conflicts of interest are, etc. It won't do to have the transfer of major resources (land, equipment, stores, outside cash, etc.) dependent on one person's memory or permission. It will be important to have it specified ahead of time what people's rights are to the common resources when they leave the community. Whether or not you use money, you'll need to have a way of providing people with resources when they leave (whether it's cash or tools or transportation or temporary housing or stored up goods or whatever). Otherwise your "right" to leave is only on paper -- it can't be exercised without money!

4. Good people leave for good reasons -- dying parent, love, work, health, exhaustion, change of mind, etc. You don't want to make them leave everything they've put in when they leave! That's a subtle way of saying they HAVE to stay if they can't afford to. Write it up when it's only theoretical -- like a pre-nup when you're planning the wedding. Not romantic, but protective! Much easier that way.

For a fuller sequence, see Diana Leafe Christian's Creating a Life Together (on the reading list at the end) -- vision, then people, then pooling major resources, then land.
