What about people who aren't "in" the commune politically? People who visit, work, rent and intern at the commune (yes, even interns!) are entitled to some rights when decisions really affect them, like pay and access to common resources. They should have the chance to know the terms of their association, to be heard, and to have such decisions reviewed. The same is true for decisions really affecting their schedule, safety, housing, records or discipline. All people are created equal, even the servants. (Wait, when did there become a class of people moving from commune to commune to be servants?)

When Somebody Has to Go

It may not be possible to make the community safe for everyone. Yeah, that's pretty tough, but sometimes somebody's gotta go. It can be the right thing to do when someone is generally or in some way a serious danger to others, repeatedly violates essential community boundaries, or is just generally intolerable to live with. If it's possible to do the necessary work with the person in question in a reasonable amount of time, all the better. But if the person is dangerous, the community shouldn't have to serve as a treatment program for them.

The power to remove people is also susceptible to abuse. It's important to know in advance how it will be used. What will constitute cause for immediate action to protect the community? Who will decide whether to put temporary restrictions on someone? Who will ensure that evidence is preserved? Who will recuse themselves? How will the person be able to reply to the concerns and have the decision reviewed or appealed, without putting anyone in danger? How will their interests in housing, records, family and access to common resources and outside funds (if any) be ensured during the separation? When will help be sought from outside?
