When Somebody Has to Go

There's going to be times when someone has to go. Yeah, it's tough. It's also true. If someone is dangerous (seriously or in a generalized sense), or repeatedly breaks essential community rules, or for whatever reason just can't make it work with the rest of y'all, it's appropriate to eject them from the community, assuming restorative approaches either can't be used or can't work in a reasonable time. It's not necessary or desirable for the entire community to act as that one person's treatment program, especially if they're endangering everyone.

Because this power is so easily abused, though, it's critical to discuss and decide in advance of any particular issue: what the process will be. What constitutes cause for immediate protective action? Who decides to put temporary restrictions on someone's behavior? Who will make sure all the evidence is preserved? Who will recuse themselves from a decision? How will the person be able to reply to concerns and appeal a decision, without creating dangerous situations? What protections will there be for the separated person's interests (housing, records, family contact, common resources, outside funds, etc.) while the separation is being worked out? When is it appropriate to bring in outside help? (More on that next.)

Note that "outside" doesn't have to mean the nation state, though at first, when doing this research, that's where it seemed to have to go. The basic need here is for a competent level of authority that is not reviewing its own actions. That could mean another community, a federation of communities, professionals, autonomous regional institutions, courts, regulatory agencies, emergency services, etc. The key is that it's not the same people! An important correction that came from the Zapatistas: it's ok for that authority to be in a regional institution as long as it's autonomous, and doesn't combine its powers with the other institutions. But when it tried to push power upwards, they pushed it back down again. And no, it still can't decide that individual people don't have certain non-waivable rights.

Also decide on a mediator/ombud person in advance. People won't trust someone they haven't already met, who comes in as a "neutral stranger." Note that this does not obviate the need for evidence, safeguarding, rulings, etc. Mediation is an attempt to restore good human relations. When people can't or won't consent to being in community any more, they need to be separated.
