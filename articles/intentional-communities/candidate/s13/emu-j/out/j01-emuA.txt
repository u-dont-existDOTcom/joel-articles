Until then, it’s still just a group, and maybe you don’t want to take out a mortgage yet. That’s okay! There’s lots you can do! You can all meet in a rented or borrowed space, or at each others houses, and get to the point of dealing with conflict before you need a surveyor.

You will also want a good idea of what you have in the way of resources. This is the boring stuff, but you will need to know what the group can offer in the way of water, housing, food stores, health care, tools, seasonal labor, transportation, access to markets, schooling, etc. Do you have outside debts to pay? How long will your cash reserves last? What critical skills will you need? It’s boring, but great feelings for each other will not stop you from being dead in the water if your well fails, or the one person who knows where everything is and how it works collapses from exhaustion.

Please Choose Your Values Before Falling in Love With the View

It’s too often that people choose communities as if they were choosing a vacation rental. “Wow! Look at this great piece of land! It even has a pond! Look at the sunset reflecting on it!! Who needs the beach! And the people seem really nice! Let’s get in here!” Cut to two years later… “Wait a minute, you have a vaccination policy?? And I need to fill out some papers for it?!?!”

Whenever you go to visit a community, have a list ready of what you believe should be the community’s stance on children, money, property ownership, romance, medicine, food, spiritual practice, technology, privacy, how to deal with conflict, leaving the community, etc. It’s a long list, I know. Put some question marks next to the things you aren’t sure about yet.

You better also include sexuality and gender on that list. Can LGBTQ people live openly in the community? Can they date or be in relationships? Will you protect them? Is it okay to question a child’s sexuality at an early age? What will you do if one of the community’s children turns out to be gay or transgender? Until you’ve answered those questions, your stance on freedom and children is incomplete.

Are you willing to experiment? Make sure your list of values includes whether or not you are willing to experiment. The people next to you may feel that all experimenting ended in 1978, and everything since has been doctrine.

When you have a good idea of what’s important to you, you can look at how a group functions, and make a choice whether or not it functions in a way that is important to you. It may be an uncomfortable conversation, but the view will still be there after you’ve had it.
