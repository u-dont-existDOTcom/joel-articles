Until that point, you’re still just a group of individuals with similar interests. Don’t bother to get a mortgage yet, but get to work! You can meet at a rental, at someone’s house, or at a borrowed space to discuss how much conflict you can handle before someone buys a surveyor’s toolset.

You’re also going to want to make a budget, to find out what you can offer on a practical level. What do you have to provide for water, housing, food, health care, tools, seasonal employment, transportation, schooling, access to markets, etc? Will you have to pay outside debts? If you have outside money, how long can you expect it to last? What are the critical skills that, without them, the group can’t function? This is all very boring, but you can have a great group of people who are emotionally in sync but are dead in the water because they can’t get a well drilled, or because the one person who knows where everything is is too exhausted to keep trying.

Please Choose Your Values Before You Fall in Love With the View

People often choose communities as though they were choosing a vacation: “Oh, wow, this is a great big piece of land! And there’s a pond! Look at the sun going down! It’s beautiful! And the people seem so nice! I want to be part of this!” Then, two years later: “You mean, there’s a vaccination policy? And I have to fill out paperwork to…what?”

When you go to visit any community, take with you a list of your core values regarding children, money, property, romantic relationships, medical care, food, spirituality, technology, privacy, conflict resolution, leaving the community, etc. It’s a long list, and it will probably include things that you’re not yet clear on, and that’s okay.

This list should also include your thoughts on sexuality and gender, and whether or not LGBTQ people should be able to live openly in your community, to date or be in relationships, and be fully protected by the community. You should also include whether or not it’s important that children not be forced to question their sexual identities from a young age, and what should happen if one of the community’s children comes out as gay or transgender. You don’t have your community’s values on freedom and children complete until you answer these questions.

You should also include in this list whether or not you want to experiment with new ideas. Some people may have stopped experimenting back in ’78 and consider the results of those experiments doctrine.

When you have this list well in hand, compare it to the actual functioning of the group you’re joining, not the group’s public face. How was recent conflict handled? Who can block group decisions? How did leaving go for the last person who left? Who owns the land? It will be uncomfortable to talk about these things, but the view isn’t going anywhere.
