When Somebody Has to Go

One of the aims of a safe community is that no-one will ever have to leave. Yeah, it's tough, but it sometimes happens. It can be a good thing, even, if the person is a serious or generalized danger to others, or repeatedly breaks essential boundaries, or is just difficult to live with, and if it appears that there is no reasonable way of doing restorative work with them in a reasonable time. It's not the community's job to be a treatment program for someone who is endangering everyone else.

The power to remove people is easy to abuse, so it's important to have a process for doing it, agreed on not in reference to any particular person. What will constitute cause for immediate action to protect people? Who will decide on temporary restrictions, ensure that evidence is preserved, and recuse themselves? How will the person being separated be able to reply to the concerns against them, and appeal the decision, without endangering people? How will their interests in things like housing, records, family, common resources, outside funds, etc., be ensured while separated? When will outside help be sought to deal with the situation? (More on that next.)

In doing research for this, it occurred to be that it focused too much on the nation-state as the "outside" in this kind of situation. It's not always going to be the nation-state. What's needed is a competent layer up that isn't reviewing itself. This can be another community, a federation, a professional, an autonomous regional institution, a court, a regulatory agency, an emergency service, etc. It just can't be the same people that are being reviewed. The example of the Zapatistas was a good correction for this. They recognized that power was being pushed too far up, and pushed it back down again. But even so, they don't get to eliminate non-waivable individual rights, and aren't subject themselves to outside jurisdiction.

Agree on a mediator or ombud in advance. People often don't trust a "neutral stranger" that they haven't previously met. Note that mediation doesn't remove the need for evidence, safeguarding, ruling, or separation if consent isn't present. It's about helping people to come to a good separation.
