# Stance check: section 13 rewrite ("Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community")

I checked every rewrite sentence that states a position, rule or assessment against the whole published essay and against Joel's later stated positions. Most sentences keep the published meaning. Two don't.

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. The cash budget now depends on whether the community "uses money"**
- Rewrite (capacity-budget paragraph): "Outside debts, or how long the cash will last, if the community uses money."
- Essay, "What This Looks Like From Sénégal" > "Lessons from the Black Tax": "Internal life can be cashless while taxes, hospitals, visas, school fees ... still arrive denominated in money." Also "The Four Parts..." > "4. The container": "a nearly moneyless community with one small collective cash boundary."
- In the published line, the condition was the debts or the runway itself ("if the community uses them"). The rewrite makes the condition money as such. Later on, the rewrite calls these groups "a money-free community".
- What a reader would wrongly believe: a community without internal money has no cash runway to budget. The essay says it still needs collective cash for taxes and other outside payments.

**2. Every visitor is owed explicit terms and a review**
- Rewrite ("From Visitor to Member—Slowly"): "Yes, even the summer volunteer." / "they need clear terms and a way to be heard, and the decision should be open to review." / "The same goes for decisions that really affect their schedule, safety or housing"
- Joel, 2026-10-11 23:12: "nobody gives clear written terms to visiting friends ... there are implied terms, like be nice, be respectful, be honest". He wants written terms only for "something like an internship or a probationary period". Joel, 2026-10-11 18:35: "before critical mass, my rules apply ... i decide who stays, i decide who leaves".
- What a reader would wrongly believe: a friend visiting the founder's land must get explicit terms, a hearing and a reviewable decision before being asked to leave. "Even the summer volunteer" extends that to every short stay.

## Contradictions in the published text

**1. Visitors' rights vs Joel's later positions on terms for visitors**
- Published s13, "From Visitor to Member—Slowly": "Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters, and interns ... need clear terms, a way to be heard, and review." This applies whenever a decision materially affects their housing, discipline and so on.
- It conflicts with Joel's 2026-10-11 positions quoted above. Visiting friends get implied, unwritten terms. Internships and probation get written terms. Before critical mass, he decides who stays. The published passage doesn't separate these cases.
- Still in the rewrite: yes. "Yes, even the summer volunteer." makes it stronger.

Apart from this, I found no place where section 13 takes back its own claims or contradicts the rest of the essay.

## Questions on the author's own paragraphs

None. "So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular" agrees with the children section's "Decide the response process before anyone's face is attached to it."

Conflicts in the rewrite: 2. Contradictions in the published text: 1.
