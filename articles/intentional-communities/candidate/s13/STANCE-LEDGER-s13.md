## Preservation units (one per inseparable meaning)

Under "Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community":
- P1: U1 land can wait; U2 the first community is the small group considering whether to build one.
- P2: U3 before ownership and major resources mix, process one real conflict all the way through.
- P3: U4 don't smooth it over with a vision statement or a truce nobody believes; U5 name what happened, give each person time, find the jealousy, humiliation, fear or old material involved; U6 agree only after the emotional pressure has changed enough that the agreement means something.
- P4: U7 a group that hasn't done this is still a circle of promising strangers; U8 he wouldn't ask that circle to take on a mortgage yet.
- P5: U9 meet in a rented space, a borrowed room or somebody's home; U10 find out the group's conflict capacity before hiring a surveyor (land comes after).
- P6: U11 budget the practical capacity too, all eleven items (water, housing, food stores, health care, tools, seasonal labor, transport, schooling, market access, outside debts or cash runway if the community uses them, the few skills whose absence can stop everything); U12 a group can have great emotional skill and still die because the well fails or one exhausted person is the only one who knows where everything is and how it works.

Under "Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View":
- P1: U13 people choose communities the way they choose vacation rentals (gorgeous land, the pond and the sunset, everybody warm during the visit).
- P2: U14 the pond wins you first; the hard questions (the vaccination policy) come two years later.
- P3: U15 before visiting, write down what you believe about all eleven areas (children, money, ownership, romance, medicine, food, spiritual practice, technology, privacy, conflict, exit); U16 mark where you're unsure.
- P4: U17 add sexuality and gender; U18 the three questions, as questions: can an LGBTQ+ member live openly, form relationships and stay fully protected; should children be taught to question their sexual identities early; what happens if a child grows up gay or trans; U19 a group that hasn't answered them hasn't finished discussing freedom or child-rearing values.
- P5: U20 "willing to experiment" is a real value and has to be declared, because the person beside you may think the experiment ended (in 1978) and its results are doctrine now.
- P6: U21 compare your values with the group's actual behavior, not only its public language; U22 the four questions: how a recent conflict was handled, who can block a decision, what happened to the last person who left, who owns the land; U23 the view will still be there after the uncomfortable conversation.

Under "Resources, Land, and Exit Terms":
- P1: U24 whoever owns the land holds enormous power over the community.
- P2: U25 the founder's name on the deed makes a landlord with a philosophy; U26 three of twelve owning everything makes two membership classes, whatever the welcome packet says about "equal".
- P3: U27 choose the legal form (cooperative, trust, association, company, whatever fits the jurisdiction) before the purchase; U28 decide voting rights, access and use rights for shared resources, contribution accounting if you use it, inheritance, outside debt, improvements, and what happens when somebody leaves.
- P4: U29 make the material system legible: a money-free community still has scarce things and bottlenecks; U30 members can know what's owned or held in common, what's reserved, what obligations exist outside, who can allocate major resources, where conflicts of interest lie; U31 large transfers (land, equipment, stores, outside cash) don't depend on one person's memory or permission; U32 exit rights in whatever unit makes sense (cash if used, tools, transport, temporary housing, stored goods, continued use rights), so exit isn't a paper right that works only when there's spare money.
- P5: U33 good people leave for good reasons (a dying parent, love, work, health, exhaustion, a changed mind); U34 if leaving means forfeiting everything contributed, staying becomes coercive.
- P6: U35 write the exit terms while departure is still hypothetical; U36 like discussing a prenup while planning the wedding: unromantic, protective, and much easier before anybody is furious.
- P7: U37 the full sequence (vision before membership, membership before pooling major resources, major resources before land) is in Diana Leafe Christian's Creating a Life Together (link kept).

Under "From Visitor to Member—Slowly":
- P1: U38 The Farm (link kept) grew fast, many arriving in need, few effective filters; openness felt spiritual; the practical burden piled up anyway.
- P2: U39 a membership process protects existing members and newcomers; nobody benefits from joining a group that can't support what it promised.
- P3: U40 correspond first: have they read the materials, do they understand why the community exists, have they begun the practices; a thoughtful exchange costs far less than a six-month misunderstanding.
- P4: U41 short visit: a week or two in ordinary life; most people can be charming for two weeks, and that's all it establishes (the 😅 joke).
- P5: U42 long visit: long enough for inconvenience and conflict to appear; watch how the person responds when tired, corrected, attracted, disappointed or bored; U43 even a professional faker (link kept) can sometimes perform for months: time gives more evidence, not certainty.
- P6: U44 provisional membership: define the period, rights, responsibilities, review process and exit; the recruiter doesn't control the decision.
- P7: U45 full membership: land and shared-resource rights, voice and long-term obligations, per the founding agreements.
- P8: U46 at each stage, assess concrete conduct, not spiritual vocabulary or therapeutic fluency; U47 vibes are easy, and so is mistaking conformity for safety; six months is a larger sample, not a "good person" detector; U48 for high-consequence roles (children, shared resources, medicine, vehicles, private records): conduct-specific evidence, references where appropriate, staged access, and an independent way to challenge the decision.
- P9: U49 before provisional membership do more than hang out: one real group decision, one ordinary work or shared-resource task, one small conflict processed afterward; U50 a simulation can't reveal a soul but can show whether the coordination system works before anybody sells a house.
- P10: U51 visitors, volunteers, workers, renters and interns have rights without full political membership; U52 when a decision materially affects their compensation or access to shared resources, schedule, safety, housing, records or discipline: clear terms, a way to be heard, and review; U53 egalitarianism that stops at the membership boundary can recreate a servant class between communes.

Under "When Somebody Has to Go":
- P1: U54 a safe community can't promise to keep everyone; U55 the three grounds: serious or generalized danger, repeated violation of essential boundaries, making ordinary shared life impossible; ejection can be right; U56 restorative work is useful on a reasonable timescale; nobody has to turn the commune into a treatment program for someone endangering everyone else.
- P2: U57 the power to remove is one of the easiest to abuse; U58 work out the process before it's about anyone in particular; U59 its parts: what triggers immediate protective action, who preserves evidence, who's recused, who decides temporary restrictions, how the person replies, what review or appeal is possible without endangering others, how housing, access to common resources or outside funds if any, records and family contact are protected during separation, what calls for outside help.
- P3: U60 "outside" doesn't always mean the nation-state; his research (link kept) first leaned too far that way; U61 what matters is a competent layer that isn't reviewing its own conduct: another community, a federation, a professional, an autonomous regional institution, a court, regulator or emergency service; U62 the Zapatistas show autonomous institutions can review local power without becoming the state; U63 autonomy doesn't permit erasing non-waivable individual rights, and doesn't make outside jurisdiction disappear.
- P4: U64 choose a mediator or ombud before the crisis; people who first meet the "neutral stranger" on the day of the explosion often don't trust them; U65 mediation is for repair, not a substitute for evidence, safeguarding, adjudication, or a final separation when consent is gone.
