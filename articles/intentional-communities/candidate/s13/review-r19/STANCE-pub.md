# Stance check: section 13 rewrite (r19) against the whole published essay

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. LGBTQ+ belonging becomes an open question of opinion**

- Rewrite (Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View): "whether or not LGBTQ+ people should be able to live openly in your community ... and be fully protected by the community"
- Essay, section 15 "What This Looks Like From Sénégal" (All is not well in paradise): "Community itself isn’t automatically liberating. Everything depends on who is permitted to belong without pretending."
- The published section 13 asked something different: "Could an LGBTQ+ member live openly, form relationships, and remain fully protected by the community?" That question tests the community. The rewrite asks whether such members *should* be allowed to live openly and be protected, as if either answer were fine.
- A reader would wrongly believe that Joel sees LGBTQ+ members living openly and being protected as a matter of opinion. The essay treats it as the test of whether a community's belonging is real.

**2. The rewrite attributes a "first book" to Joel that the essay doesn't support**

- Rewrite (Resources, Land, and Exit Terms), about *Creating a Life Together*: "It's the first book I'd hand to any group starting out."
- Essay, section 16 "Practice Comes Before Property" (What to Do This Month): "Start the inner pl/ork. Read the free reparenting guide and Hearthwork manual, or begin with another serious practice you already trust."
- The appendix also limits the book's role. It says "start here for sequencing, membership, money, land, and agreements", and its "in Reading Order" list opens with *The Continuum Concept*.
- A reader would wrongly believe that Joel starts a new group with a planning book on land and agreements. The essay starts a group with inner and relational practice, before property.

## Contradictions in the published text

**1. The Zapatistas as the example of review by a regional body (section 13 against section 9)**

- Section 13 (When Somebody Has to Go): "The Zapatistas are an important correction here: autonomous institutions can review local power without becoming the state."
- Section 9 "The Best Model I’ve Seen: Zapatistas": "the coordinating levels are explicitly described as dependent on the communities rather than superior to them". Also: "The Zapatistas reviewed theirs and pushed authority downward."
- Section 13 offers the Zapatistas as proof that a body above a community can review that community's own power. Section 9 praises them for dissolving their Good Government Juntas and making the regional level depend on the communities. A reader who remembers section 9 can fairly ask which Zapatista body reviews local power now. The essay never says the dissolved Juntas did that kind of reviewing. Section 9's version is the one that fits Joel's 2026-10-07 question, "Why should the home community not control the interaction they have with outside?"
- Rewrite: it still has this tension and makes it easier to see. Its added sentence "But when power started creeping upward in their own system, they pushed it back down again" now comes straight after the claim about review.
