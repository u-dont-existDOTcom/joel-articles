# Repeats check: Intentional Communities article (review r19)

I read every block, B0 to B403. Below I report only repeats and contradictions where at least one instance falls in "Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community" (B273–B304). From here on that section is called **Forming Group**. Sections are named by their headings, and " › " marks a subsection. Every quote is in « » and is copied exactly from the article.

## 1. Repeats (in article order)

### 1. Scarcity survives without money (B84, B287)
- B84 (The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together › 4. The container): «Even in a moneyless community, someone can control what's scarce.»
- B287 (Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): «Even if you're a money-free community, you'll still have scarce resources and bottlenecks.»
- Shared point: giving up money doesn't get rid of scarcity, or of the power over scarce things.
- Later instance: a reader would feel told the same thing again. B287's new material only starts after this sentence, with members being able to see what is held in common.
- Better carrier: B84, because it makes the claim where the moneyless model is set out, and it lists what can be controlled.

### 2. Whoever holds the land title holds the power (B85, B86, B231, B232, B283, B285)
- B85 (4. The container): «how to prevent one founder from having control over the land»
- B86 (4. The container): «the mess that is created when one person owns the land or has the final say about what will happen on it»
- B231 (Founderism: The Four Questions): «Then follow the practical power. Whose name is on the deed?»
- B232 (Founderism: The Four Questions): «holds the records, has title to the land, and can kick out complainers»
- B283 (Forming Group › Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View): «you can look at how a group actually functions, not just its public face.» and «Who owns the land?»
- B285 (Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): «If the deed’s in the name of the “founder,” then he or she is really your landlord, with a philosophy.»
- Shared point: the person whose name is on the deed has the real power, whatever the group says about equality.
- Later instances: B283 repeats B231's advice to look past the official picture to who actually holds power, this time as a visitor's checklist. What it adds is asking how the last conflict and the last departure went. B285 makes the founder-on-the-deed point for the third or fourth time. What it adds is the case where three of twelve members own everything, which makes two classes of members. A reader would feel told the deed point again.
- Better carrier: B285, because it sits in the section about land title and gives the point its fullest form.

### 3. Inner work and good hearts can't replace the practical side (B86, B96, B265, B277)
- B86 (4. The container): «any more than medicine makes up for the lack of a written exit agreement.»
- B96 (The Medicine Part - Yes, I'm Naming It): «the insight doesn't do the practical work for it.»
- B265 (The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For): «The water well doesn’t care how beautiful the new member’s heart is.»
- B277 (Forming Group, opening): «Being great at the emotional stuff won’t stop you from being dead in the water if your well fails»
- Shared point: insight, emotional skill or a good heart doesn't keep the practical side running. The failing well appears twice.
- Later instances: B265 applies the point to taking in new members in a small group. B277 adds a resource budget: outside debts, cash reserves and critical skills. But B277's well line comes 12 blocks after B265's and would read as told again.
- Better carrier: B265, because the well image first appears there, tied to the capacity math. What is new in B277 is the budget, not the well.

### 4. Growing faster than the group can carry hurts everyone (B86, B126, B264, B291)
- B86 (4. The container): «when members come on board faster than the group can support them»
- B126 (The Medicine Part › After the Psychedelic Calling): «A group that begins in collective spiritual euphoria can recruit too quickly and promise too much»
- B264 (The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For): «how many people can a group take in without hurting the existing members or the people they are taking in?»
- B291 (Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly): «A membership process is there to protect both the people who are already there and the newcomers.» and «It’s no good for anyone to join a group that can’t deliver what it promised.»
- Shared point: taking people in faster than the group can support them harms both the newcomers and the members already there, and so does promising more than the group can deliver.
- Later instances: B126 points ahead to the capacity and membership sections, so a reader expects B264 and B291 to return to this. What B291 adds is The Farm as an example. But its sentence about protecting both groups restates B264, and its sentence about not delivering what was promised restates B126. A reader would feel those two sentences as told again.
- Better carrier: B264, because the capacity section is where the reasoning about both sides is worked out. B291's own contribution is The Farm.

### 5. Settle the process before it is about a particular person (B181, B302)
- B181 (The Grown Children Get the Final Review › Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection): «Decide on the response process ahead of time, before anyone's face is attached to it.»
- B302 (Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go): «So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular.»
- Shared point: design the response before any specific person is involved.
- Later instance: B302 applies the advice to a different process, removing someone, and adds its own list of questions. But the advice comes back in nearly the same words, so a reader would likely feel told it again. Other passages about agreeing in advance (exit terms in B288, the mediator in B304, dissolution in B309) are worded differently and read as the theme moving forward.
- Better carrier: B181, because its image (no face attached yet) carries the reason for the advice, and it comes first.

### 6. A right to leave is only real if people can afford to leave (B188, B189, B220, B248, B287, B288)
- B188 (The Grown Children Get the Final Review › The Door Must Open Outward at Adulthood): «it doesn't need to be artificially impossible for grown kids to leave.»
- B189 (same subsection): «It would be great if the community could also provide resources to smooth the transition, like temporary housing, help with travel, etc.»
- B220 (From One Community to a Movement › Federated Communities Make a Movement): «or the freedom to move is just decorative.»
- B248 (Founderism › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult): «Each person should have a usable avenue for exit.»
- B287 (Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): «Otherwise your "right" to leave is only on paper -- it can't be exercised unless the community has spare money!» and «specified ahead of time what people's rights are to the common resources when they leave»
- B288 (same subsection, the next block): «That's coercive -- they HAVE to stay if they can't afford to go.»
- Shared point: if leaving isn't backed by resources, people are in effect made to stay.
- Later instances: B188 and B189 are about grown children, and B220 is about people moving between communities, so each brings in a new group. B287 and B288 sit side by side and make the same point twice. Both say that without exit resources the right to leave isn't real, and both say to set exit rights before anyone leaves. What B288 adds is the reasons good people leave, and not making them give up what they put in. A reader would feel told the same thing again from B287 to B288.
- Better carrier: B288, because it gives the coercion point its reasons and the timing. B287's main job is making resources visible.

### 7. The stores, tools and cash shouldn't hang on one person (B231, B277, B287)
- B231 (Founderism: The Four Questions): «the keys, passwords, titles and even just general reputation all point to one person.»
- B277 (Forming Group, opening): «if the one person who knows where everything is and how it works gets too exhausted to keep going.»
- B287 (Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): «It won't do to have the transfer of major resources (land, equipment, stores, outside cash, etc.) dependent on one person's memory or permission.»
- Shared point: the group's key resources shouldn't depend on one person's knowledge or permission.
- Later instances: B277 is about fragility (that one person burning out) rather than power, so it only partly overlaps. B287 restates B231's warning as general practice. What it adds is that members should be able to see what is held in common, what is owed to outsiders, and who has authority over it. A reader would feel partly told again.
- Better carrier: B287, because it sits in the resources section and turns the warning into things members should be able to see.

### 8. Leaving is like divorce, so settle the split while things are happy (B238, B288, B307)
- B238 (Founderism: The Four Questions): «Leaving a commune is the divorce half of it, where the shared resources have to get split up.» and «be more conscious about the process and less romantic.»
- B288 (Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): «Write up the exit terms when it's only theoretical -- like a pre-nup when you're planning the wedding.» and «Not romantic, but protective!»
- B307 (Dissolution and Exit): «rarely want to talk about while they’re picking a name for their community» and «But that’s exactly when it needs to be talked about.»
- Shared point: leaving a commune is like a divorce, so work out how shared things get split at the start, without romance. (B86 already mentions a «written exit agreement» in passing.)
- Later instances: B288 reuses B238's comparison and its turn away from romance, and adds the practical step of writing exit terms early, like a pre-nup. A reader would recognize the same comparison. B307 applies the same timing to the whole community dissolving, which is new ground, though the idea of planning the ending at the joyful start will feel familiar.
- Better carrier: B288, because there the comparison does practical work (when to write exit terms). In B238 it is one step in a story about a community gradually becoming formal.

### 9. Probation and internships need written terms (B239, B295, B299)
- B239 (Founderism: The Four Questions): «If someone is offered something like an internship or a probationary period, that would require written terms.»
- B295 (Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly): «Write down your answers to these questions: How long will a provisional membership be?»
- B299 (same subsection): «Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters and interns at the commune (yes, even interns!) are entitled to some rights» and «All of them should have clear terms»
- Shared point: anyone on probation or an internship needs terms set out in writing.
- Later instances: B295 adds the full list of questions the terms should answer. B299 adds the right to be heard and to have decisions reviewed, and extends clear terms to visitors, volunteers, workers and renters. The written-terms-for-probation part would feel told again. (See contradiction 2 about visitors.)
- Better carrier: B295, because terms for provisional members belong in the membership section, and B295 gives them content.

### 10. Someone who makes living together impossible gets removed (B246, B301)
- B246 (Founderism › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult): «If it gets to the point of being impossible to live with, they will be kicked out.»
- B301 (Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go): «for whatever reason just makes living together impossible for everyone else, it can be the right call to eject them.»
- Shared point: when someone becomes impossible to live with, removing them is justified.
- Later instance: B301 adds danger to others and repeated boundary violations as grounds, and room for restorative work. But the core (impossible to live with, so they go) is told again.
- Better carrier: B301, because it is the section about removing someone.

### 11. Some rights can't be taken away by the community (B249, B303)
- B249 (Founderism › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult): «There are some basic human rights that can’t be voted away from you by the majority in a community»
- B303 (Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go): «autonomy still doesn't mean anyone gets to decide that individual people don't have certain non-waivable rights»
- Shared point: certain individual rights hold whatever the community decides.
- Later instance: B303 adds that autonomy doesn't make outside jurisdiction go away. The point about rights itself is told again.
- Better carrier: B249, because it is the passage about rights. It gives an example (relationships outside the community) and is followed by B251.

### 12. A small group without land is already a community (B274, B349, B353, B354)
- B274 (Forming Group, opening): «you are already a community, even if it's still an untested one.»
- B349 (Practice Comes Before Property › If You Can’t Move): «The most important pl/ork does not require land.»
- B353 (same subsection): «do not wait for a deed.»
- B354 (same subsection): «A weekly group that has practiced together for two years already contains a real piece of community. It may never own land.»
- Shared point: a group practicing together is already a real community, with or without land.
- Later instance: B354 adds that the group may never own land and can still change how people get through divorce, illness and grief. But B274's «already a community», which also echoes the Forming Group heading at B273, is told again.
- Better carrier: B274, because the section is named for that claim.

### 13. Get through a real conflict before you pool property (B275, B276, B298, B360)
- B275 (Forming Group, opening): «until you've been through at least one real conflict together and REALLY dealt with it.»
- B276 (Forming Group, opening): «find out how much conflict the group can handle before you need a surveyor.»
- B298 (Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly): «Can you even process a small conflict afterwards? Do a trial run like that before anybody sells a house.»
- B360 (Practice Comes Before Property › What to Do This Month): «handle one disagreement, and keep commitments before discussing a shared future.»
- Shared point: test the group on a real conflict before committing property or a shared future. (B346 lists it again; see item 14.)
- Later instances: B298 adds making a decision as a group and getting a task done together, and it comes before making someone a provisional member. But «before anybody sells a house» echoes B276's «before you need a surveyor». B360 adds listening, switching roles and keeping commitments. A reader would feel told again both times.
- Better carrier: B275, because it spells out what really dealing with a conflict involves, in the section that opens on this point.

### 14. The right order: practice, values and members first, land last (B289, B345, B346, B364)
- B289 (Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): «For the full sequence -- vision, then members, then pooling major resources, then land»
- B345 (Practice Comes Before Property): «Most community projects begin in the wrong order: land first, vision in a document, ownership concentrated because somebody had the money»
- B346 (Practice Comes Before Property): «Practice together. Process a real conflict. Clarify values. Test membership. Decide ownership and exit. Then consider land.»
- B364 (Practice Comes Before Property › What to Do This Month): «Do those three things before you spend serious time or money looking for land.»
- Shared point: the steps go in a set order, and land comes last. (Forming Group opens the same way at B274: «Don't worry about getting land yet.»)
- Later instances: B345 and B346 sum up the Forming Group order and add practicing together as the first step. A reader would recognize the same sequence told again. The two lists also differ (see contradiction 4). B364 puts land last again, after that month's three steps.
- Better carrier: B346, because it gives the whole order in one place, in a section named for it. B289's main job is pointing to the book.

### 15. More time gives more evidence, not certainty (B294, B296, B298)
- B294 (Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly): «may be able to pull off an act for months, so time gets you more evidence, not certainty.»
- B296 (same subsection): «Six months will give you more data, but there’s still no “good person” detector.»
- B298 (same subsection): «It won't show you anyone's soul, but it can show you whether your way of coordinating works.»
- Shared point: longer visits and trial runs tell you more about a person, but they never settle who that person is.
- Later instances: B296 comes two blocks after B294 and says the same thing. Its new part is judging people by what they do rather than what they say. B298 adds what a trial run does show (whether the group can coordinate), so it only partly repeats.
- Better carrier: B294, because the point grows out of the long-visit test and the example of the professional faker.

## 2. Contradictions

### 1. Should a community decide who may live and love openly? (B149, B281, B327)
- B149 (Jealousy Can Capture Monogamy or Free Love): «I don't think it should be up to the community to decide for everyone what form relationships will take.»
- B281 (Forming Group › Please Choose the Values Before Falling in Love With the View): «whether or not LGBTQ+ people should be able to live openly in your community, to date or be in relationships»
- B327 (What This Looks Like From Sénégal › All is not well in paradise): «Everything depends on who is permitted to belong without pretending.»
- What pulls apart: B281 presents whether LGBTQ+ people can live openly and date as a question each community may answer either way, and says nothing about the answer. B149 says the community shouldn't decide what form relationships take, and B325–B327 treat having to hide as the cruelty underneath communal warmth.
- How a reader might square it: B281 sits in a list of questions readers should answer for themselves, not in a ruling. Some readers will take it that way. Others will feel that the neutral «whether or not» pulls against the stance taken elsewhere. The article doesn't say why the question is left open there.
- Clearer statement of the author's own view: B327, because the author speaks for himself there, while B281 is a checklist for the reader.

### 2. Do visitors get terms? (B239, B299)
- B239 (Founderism: The Four Questions): «Friends who come to visit don't get written terms, though.» and «There are implied terms, like be respectful and be honest, and those shouldn't need to be in writing.»
- B299 (Forming Group › From Visitor to Member—Slowly): «Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters and interns at the commune (yes, even interns!) are entitled to some rights» and «All of them should have clear terms»
- What pulls apart: B239 says visiting friends don't get written terms. B299 counts visitors among the people who should all have clear terms.
- How a reader might square it: B239 is about friends dropping in while a group is still forming informally. B299 is about an established commune, and only «when decisions really affect» their pay, resources, schedule, safety and so on. Also, «clear» need not mean «written». The article doesn't draw that line, so a reader comparing the two may see a conflict.
- More natural place: B299, because it is in the membership section, where the standing of non-members is the topic.

### 3. Decide removal case by case, or set the process in advance? (B247, B302)
- B247 (Founderism › How My Own Model Could Become a Cult): «It depends on so many factors that I can't decide any of this ahead of time. It's up to the people involved.»
- B302 (Forming Group › When Somebody Has to Go): «So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular.»
- What pulls apart: both are about pushing someone out of the community. One says it can't be decided ahead of time; the other says to settle the process before there is a case.
- How a reader might square it: B247 is about whether to make exceptions, which is the substance, and the author is speaking about other people's future communities. B302 is about procedure: who decides, how appeals work, what protections apply. The article doesn't spell out that difference.
- More natural place: B302, because that is the removal section, where the procedure is laid out.

### 4. What comes first, the vision or practice and conflict? (B289, B345, B346)
- B289 (Forming Group › Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): «For the full sequence -- vision, then members, then pooling major resources, then land»
- B345 (Practice Comes Before Property): «land first, vision in a document, ownership concentrated because somebody had the money»
- B346 (Practice Comes Before Property): «Practice together. Process a real conflict. Clarify values. Test membership. Decide ownership and exit. Then consider land.»
- What pulls apart: B289 gives vision first as «the full sequence». B346 puts practice and a real conflict before values, and B345 counts «vision in a document» among the signs of the wrong order.
- How a reader might square it: B345 seems to mean a vision that exists only on paper, as B275 does with «writing out a vision statement and shaking hands». Both lists agree that members come before ownership and that land comes last. The article doesn't say how the two orders fit together.
- More natural place: B346, because its section is about the order of steps.

## 3. The five repeats a reader would most notice
1. Repeat 15: more time gives evidence, not certainty. It comes three times in one subsection (B294, B296, B298).
2. Repeat 6: a right to leave needs resources. B287 and B288 say it back to back.
3. Repeat 3: the failing well, in B265 and then 12 blocks later in B277.
4. Repeat 2: the founder's name on the deed (B85–B86, B231–B232, B285).
5. Repeat 13: a real conflict before property (B275–B276, B298, B360).
