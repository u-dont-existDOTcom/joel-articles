REWRITE:

Provisional membership: decide how long it lasts and what rights and responsibilities come with it, plus how the review works and how they’d leave. Who decides whether they move on to full membership? Not the person who recruited them. Then full membership: rights to the land and shared resources, a voice, and long-term obligations, as your founding agreements set them out.

CONTEXT AFTER: At each stage, judge people by their conduct, however spiritual or therapeutic they sound. Going by vibes is so easy! So is mistaking conformity for safety. Six months gives you a bigger sample, but it won’t tell you who’s a good person.

PUBLISHED:

The power to remove somebody is also one of the easiest powers to abuse. So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular: what conduct triggers immediate protective action, who preserves evidence, who is recused, who decides temporary restrictions, how the person replies, what review or appeal is possible without endangering others, how housing, access to common resources or outside funds if any, records, and family contact are protected during separation, and what calls for outside help.

REWRITE:

But the power to remove somebody is also one of the easiest powers to abuse! ⟦author⟧So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular.⟦/author⟧ The process should say what kind of conduct calls for protective action right away, and who preserves the evidence. Anyone who’s too close to the case steps aside, and it should be clear who decides on temporary restrictions. While they’re separated, their housing, records and contact with family need protecting, and so does their access to common resources, or to outside funds if there are any. The process should also say when to call for outside help.
