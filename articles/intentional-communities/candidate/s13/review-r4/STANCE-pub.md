# Stance check: section 13 rewrite ("Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community")

I checked every sentence in the rewrite that states a position against the whole published essay and against Joel's stated positions. Section numbers follow the essay's top-level headings (s13 is this section).

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. Clear terms for visitors**
- Rewrite: "Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters and interns don’t need full political membership to have rights." ... "they need clear terms and a way to be heard, and the decision should be open to review"
- Conflicts with Joel, 2026-10-11 23:12: "nobody gives clear written terms to visiting friends" and "If they are offered something like an internship or a probationary period then that would require written terms". Also 2026-10-11 18:35: "i decide who stays, i decide who leaves".
- A reader would believe a visiting friend is owed explicit terms, a hearing and a review. Joel says implied terms cover visitors, and only internships or probation need written terms.

**2. Founder on the deed**
- Rewrite: "If the deed is in the founder’s name, the founder is basically a landlord with a philosophy."
- Conflicts with Joel, 2026-10-11 18:35: "before critical mass, my rules apply, because it's my land" and "i divest power to others when i see they are serious about being partners with me". Also 2026-10-14 21:19, where he defines founderism as "the problem of power staying in the hands of the founders longer than it should".
- A reader would believe that a founder holding the deed is a warning sign in itself, with no early stage where the owner's rules legitimately apply.

**3. What has to be settled before buying land**
- Rewrite: "Decide that before the land is bought. Then you need to decide voting rights and access/use rights for shared resources"
- Conflicts with s16, "Practice Comes Before Property": "Decide ownership and exit. Then consider land."
- "Then" can read as "after the purchase". A reader may believe only the legal form has to come before buying, and that voting, inheritance and exit terms can be settled afterward.

**4. Exit terms in advance**
- Rewrite: "Exit terms are best written while nobody is leaving and it’s all still hypothetical."
- Conflicts with s16: "Decide ownership and exit. Then consider land." The published s13 sentence is an instruction: "Write the exit terms while departure is still hypothetical."
- A reader would take writing exit terms early as the better option. The essay makes it a step that has to come before land.

**5. The recruiter's part in the decision**
- Rewrite: "Who decides whether they move on to full membership? Not the person who recruited them."
- Conflicts with s11, "How My Own Model Could Become a Cult": "No individual or permanent clique controls admission." The published s13 has "The recruiter should not control the decision."
- A reader would believe the recruiter must stay out of the decision entirely. The essay only bars one person from controlling it. The rewrite also narrows "the decision" to the step into full membership.

## Contradictions in the published text

**A. Is the forming group a community yet?**
- s13: "The first community is the small group considering whether to build one." Two paragraphs later: "A group that has never done this is still a circle of promising strangers."
- The second sentence takes back the heading's claim. A reader can't tell whether a group that hasn't processed a real conflict counts as a community. The "If You Can't Move" part of s16 is more careful: "A weekly group that has practiced together for two years already contains a real piece of community."
- The rewrite still has this contradiction. Its added line, "Even if it’s just three people around somebody’s kitchen table, that counts", makes the clash sharper.

**B. Founder ownership** (the same conflict as rewrite item 2)
- s13: "If the founder’s name is on the deed, you have a landlord with a philosophy." It also says "Choose the legal form ... before the purchase."
- This conflicts with Joel's positions of 2026-10-11 18:35 and 2026-10-14 21:19, quoted above. Under the published wording, Joel's own plan in s2 ("I do plan to buy land, and people may naturally end up living around me") is the landlord case, and nothing allows for a stage before critical mass.
- The rewrite still has it.

**C. Terms for visitors** (the same conflict as rewrite item 1)
- s13: "Visitors, volunteers, workers, renters, and interns do not need full political membership to have rights." ... "they need clear terms, a way to be heard, and review."
- This conflicts with Joel's 2026-10-11 23:12 position on visiting friends and implied terms. It also conflicts with his 18:35 "i decide who stays, i decide who leaves", which applies before critical mass.
- The rewrite still has it.
