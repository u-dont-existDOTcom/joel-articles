Good people leave for good reasons, often due to a dying parent, love, work, health, exhaustion, or just a simple change of mind. Leaving should not require someone to leave everything they’ve put in, as that’s effectively coercing someone to stay. It’s best to set the terms of letting someone leave when no one is leaving, and it’s just a theoretical discussion. Think of it as a pre-nup while you’re planning the wedding. It’s not that romantic, but it’s a lot easier than doing it when people are angry. For the whole sequence, see Diana Leafe Christian’s Creating a Life Together: First you get the vision, then you get the people, then you pool the major resources, and lastly you get the land.
