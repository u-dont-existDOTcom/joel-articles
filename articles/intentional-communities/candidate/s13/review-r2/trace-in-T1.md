## Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community P2+3 → P2

CONTEXT BEFORE: You don’t need land yet. The small group that’s still deciding whether to build a community already is the first community. Even if it’s just three people around somebody’s kitchen table, that counts.

PUBLISHED:

Before ownership and major resources mix, process one real conflict all the way through.

Don’t smooth it over with a vision statement or settle for a truce nobody believes. Name what happened. Give each person time. Find the jealousy, humiliation, fear, or old material involved. Reach an agreement only after the emotional pressure has changed enough that the agreement means something.

REWRITE:

Don’t combine ownership and major resources without going through at least one real conflict together, start to finish. Not writing a vision statement and shaking hands on it, or settling for a truce nobody believes. I mean actually going through the conflict together, emotionally. I won’t pretend that’s fun. Somebody names what happened, and each person gets time. You find out which jealousy, humiliation, fear or old material is involved, and you stay with it until the emotional pressure has changed enough for an agreement to mean something.

CONTEXT AFTER: Until that’s happened, the group is still a circle of promising strangers, and I wouldn’t ask that circle to take on a mortgage yet. In the meantime there’s plenty to do! Meet in rented or borrowed space, or at someone’s house, and find out how much conflict the group can handle before anyone hires a surveyor.

## Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community P6 → P4

CONTEXT BEFORE: Until that’s happened, the group is still a circle of promising strangers, and I wouldn’t ask that circle to take on a mortgage yet. In the meantime there’s plenty to do! Meet in rented or borrowed space, or at someone’s house, and find out how much conflict the group can handle before anyone hires a surveyor.

PUBLISHED:

Do the boring capacity budget too: water, housing, food stores, health care, tools, seasonal labor, transport, schooling, market access, outside debts or cash runway if the community uses them, and the few skills whose absence can stop everything. A group can have extraordinary emotional skill and still die because the well fails or one exhausted person is the only one who knows where everything is and how it works.

REWRITE:

Also in the meantime, budget what the group can provide on the practical side. Water, housing, food stores, health care. Tools, seasonal labor, transport, schooling, getting to markets. Outside debts and how long the cash will last, if the community uses money. And the few skills that could stop everything if nobody had them. It’s the boring part, I know. But however good everyone is at dealing with each other emotionally, a group can still die because the well fails, or because one exhausted person is the only one who knows where everything is and how it works.

CONTEXT AFTER: People often pick a community the way they’d pick a vacation rental: the land is gorgeous, the sunset shows up in the pond, and everybody seemed really nice when you visited. The pond wins you over on the first date, and then two years later the vaccination policy comes up.
