## From Visitor to Member—Slowly P8 → P6+7

CONTEXT BEFORE: Provisional membership: decide how long it lasts and what rights and responsibilities come with it, plus how the review works and how they’d leave. It’s a bit like being engaged. Whether they move on to full membership shouldn’t be up to the person who recruited them. PUBLISHED:

At each stage, assess concrete conduct rather than spiritual vocabulary or therapeutic fluency. Vibes are easy, but so is mistaking conformity for safety. Six months gives you a larger sample; it does not validate a “good person” detector. For high-consequence roles involving children, shared resources, medicine, vehicles, or private records, use conduct-specific evidence, references where appropriate, staged access, and an independent way to challenge the decision.

REWRITE:

At each stage, judge people by their conduct, however spiritual or therapeutic they sound. It’s easy to go by vibes, and just as easy to mistake conformity for safety. Six months gives you a bigger sample, but it won’t tell you who’s a good person. I’m not sure anything can.

If someone’s going to have a role with real consequences, around children, shared resources, medicine, vehicles or private records, you want evidence of how they handle that specific thing. It might sound bureaucratic for a commune. Use references where they make sense and give access in stages. There should also be an independent way to challenge the decision.

CONTEXT AFTER: Before anyone becomes a provisional member, do more than hang out together. It’ll feel a little artificial, and that’s okay. Make one real group decision, do one ordinary work or shared-resource task, and process one small conflict afterward. A trial run like that won’t show you anyone’s soul, but it can show you whether your way of coordinating works before anybody sells a house.
