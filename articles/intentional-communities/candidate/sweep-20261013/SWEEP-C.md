# SWEEP-C: lost functions

No losses found in sections 1, 7 or 10.

## Section 2 (Communes Are Hot Again)

1. Published: “…enough relational capacity to confront the problem before resentment becomes the real government.” Rewrite: “It requires developing healthier forms of relationships.” Lost: what relational capacity is for: confronting freeloading before resentment takes over governing.

## Section 3 (Why Communities Keep Dying)

2. Published: “That requires practice between peers…” Rewrite: “…and that also has peer practice and enough play…” Lost: that peer practice is what makes guru-free depth possible for unfinished people; it is now an add-on, and prior healing an entry condition.

## Section 6 (Technosphere)

3. Published: “A catastrophic-illness agreement should protect choice, access to common resources or outside funds when needed, transportation, privacy, and the possibility that the community’s preferred approach won’t be enough.” Rewrite: “…their choices will be protected… Medical privacy should be discussed, but may not be guaranteed…” Lost: that the agreement secures funds, transport and the chance the community’s approach falls short; privacy goes from protected to unguaranteed.

## Section 8 (Grown Children)

4. Published: “The village can carry meals, supervision, teaching, protection, play, and much of fathering.” Rewrite: “If the father is unknown, the whole community can do the fathering…” Lost: what the village does for children; only fathering (for an unknown father) remains.

5. Published: “…a reason to dismiss a report. A frightened or confused child may tell a story imperfectly and still need protection.” Rewrite: “taken seriously if they are known to be honest”; “may get mixed up telling what happened, and that's to be expected.” Lost: that a muddled account still needs protection and must not be dismissed; being heard now depends on a record of honesty.

6. Published: “…competent help and review that the home community does not control.” Rewrite: “…competent help and review, hopefully including non-intrusive help from outside the home community.” Lost: that the review must be independent of the home community.

7. Published: “If it cannot, it can still avoid punishing departure or withholding the knowledge and records a young adult needs to function elsewhere.” Rewrite: “It would be great if the community could also provide resources to smooth the transition…” Lost: the minimum a resource-poor community still owes a leaver.

8. Published: “Goodwill doesn’t answer those questions after a child is already living inside the disagreement.” Rewrite: gone, with its heading “Discuss Child-Rearing Values Before Children Are Caught Between Them.” Lost: why child-rearing principles must be settled beforehand.

## Section 9 (Zapatistas)

9. Published: “…the coordinating levels are explicitly described as dependent on the communities rather than superior to them.” Rewrite: gone (nearest: “moved authority downward toward a Local Autonomous Government in each community”). Lost: that coordination above the village persists but answers to the communities.

10. Published: “What they demonstrate is that major necessities and public functions can be taken out of ordinary market purchase and governed collectively.” Rewrite: “…don't prove my whole money-free economy goal. They still use money internally in some cases, not others…” Lost: what the Zapatista example does prove, and the collective work behind schools and clinics; only the negative remains.

11. Published: “Outside help can build capability, but it can also create gatekeepers and uneven dependence when the specialist, donor, or organization remains indispensable.” Rewrite: “…making efforts to remain politically independent from donors and more self-sustaining.” Lost: the general warning that indispensable helpers become gatekeepers.

## Section 11 (Founderism)

12. Published: “Medicine prerequisites can create gatekeepers with power over people at their most open.”; “Abstaining cannot reduce membership rights.” Rewrite: “Well, it is gatekeeping. Some people simply should not be let in the gate”; “nobody has to take medicine to have equal respect in the community.” Lost: the danger of gatekeepers’ power over people at their most open (now defended), and abstainers’ guaranteed membership rights (now respect).

13. Published: “…the principles are commitments I choose for my own practice, never powers the community gains over me.” Rewrite: “…a dynamic, living, always re-assessed balance between what you and the community owe each other, and what remains your own domain.” Lost: the line that the principles bind only whoever chose them and give the community no authority over members.

14. Published: “It does not authorize anyone to extract that material. Members can decline to share without producing a therapeutic explanation for the refusal.” Rewrite: gone (nearest: “privacy also can be resistance to the communal spirit”). Lost: that “nothing suppressed” gives no one a right to demand disclosure, and members may refuse without explaining.

15. Published: “‘Your resistance is your wound talking’ is one of the most efficient sentences ever invented for avoiding an argument… leave…” Rewrite: “Sometimes it really is their wounded ego projection, and that should be acknowledged.” Lost: that the “wound” label is an evasion and a sign to leave; the reader now learns that calling dissent projection can be fair.

16. Published: “…a floor the majority cannot vote away: … independent medical care, private thought, … a way to report harm without asking the accused person’s friends for permission…” Rewrite: “…basic human rights that can’t be voted away… unless it's a question of resource availability as a whole.” Lost: those three guarantees; what remains depends on resources and community interest.
