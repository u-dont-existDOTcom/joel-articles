# Sweep D: stance conflicts after Joel's changes of 2026-10-13

Checked HUMANIZED-SO-FAR.md, every section except 4 and 12 (HTML notes ignored), against his three positions:

- **A.** Practice isn't purely individual: "Each of us guides our own practice, and our practices weave the community into the quilt that keeps us all warm and safe."
- **B.** The community screens who joins, and applicants screen themselves first: "before you submit your application to the community, submit it to yourself."
- **C.** That screening power "remains dangerously tilted towards elitism."

**Result: 2 findings, both against C.** No sentence outside sections 4 and 12 makes practice or authority purely individual, and none says a community can't or shouldn't screen or decline members.

## Findings

### 1. Section 5, "The Medicine Part - Yes, I'm Naming It", subsection "After the Psychedelic Calling", last paragraph (line 263)

**Possibly his:** the note on section 5 says the whole section is his own text of 2026-10-06.

> That answers the reasonable objection that communes are just privileged escape pods, which gets double dipped with the objection that psychedelics are also privileged escape pods (or, alternatively, as some say, "a vacation for the poor").

- **Pulls against:** C.
- **Why a reader would notice:** section 5 declares the privilege objection answered by training people to start more communities, and section 12 later concedes that the power to turn applicants away stays "dangerously tilted towards elitism," which leaves that objection open at the door.

### 2. Section 13, "Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community", subsection "From Visitor to Member—Slowly", second paragraph (line 611)

**Not marked as his:** this section has no lane note.

> A membership process protects existing members and newcomers.

- **Pulls against:** C.
- **Why a reader would notice:** section 12 has said that even with concrete criteria, outside review and a way to revisit "not now," the veto stays dangerously tilted towards elitism, and this sentence presents the same process purely as protection, newcomers included.
- **Why I don't count it as resolved nearby:** the subsection later says "assess concrete conduct rather than spiritual vocabulary or therapeutic fluency" and "Vibes are easy, but so is mistaking conformity for safety," which meets his worry about less articulate applicants, but nothing there says danger remains once the safeguards are in place, as his "Even then" does.
