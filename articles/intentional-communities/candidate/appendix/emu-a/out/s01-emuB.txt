Appendix: Watch These, Then Read These

Films

Where’s Utopia? (1988) is a documentary by my dad, with Jerry Mintz.

A Place Called Chiapas (1998) is about the early days of the Zapatistas.

American Commune (2013) is about The Farm, narrated by two sisters who grew up there. It’s important for this article to see things through the eyes of now-grown children.

Commune (2005) is about Black Bear Ranch, and the relationship implications of radical freedom.

A New We takes us on a tour of a bunch of different ecovillages in Europe.

Within Reach follows a couple as they bike 6500 miles, visiting a hundred or so communities.

Seeking the Good Life in America compares Twin Oaks, Acorn and Light Morning.

Voices of Cohousing is a good introduction to the partial-community option, with high neighborliness and more private autonomy.

Inside the Bruderhof shows a high-commitment option with a religious focus and a good deal of surrendered autonomy. They also have a hefty Youtube channel.

Living in the Future: Lammas gives a good picture of the interaction between communitybuilding and land-use/planning law.

Books & Papers, in Reading Order

For Child-Rearing, the foundational natural child-rearing book is Continuum Concept (1975) by Jean Liedloff. She wrote it after spending time with the Ye’kuana people, but be cautious about reading it as anthropologically accurate. A more practical and accessible book is Hunt, Gather, Parent (2021) by Michaeleen Doucleff, about how kids can meaningfully participate in family life, be autonomous, and be cared for by the whole community.

For practical guides to building a community, I recommend starting with Creating a Life Together by Diana Leafe Christian for nuts and bolts stuff about membership, money, land, agreements, etc. For a guide to doing it all in a whole-systems way, check out Building Belonging by Yana Ludwig.

There are a variety of governance manuals you might want to check out. Many Voices One Song by Rau & Koch-Gonzalez is a practical sociocracy manual. Building United Judgment by Schaub et al is about consensus. The Cooperative Culture Handbook by Ludwig & Gimnig has a bunch of useful exercises on how to be more cooperative, deal with conflict, build group culture, etc.

Communities That Abide by Dmitry Orlov makes a fairly uncomfortable argument that communities survive longer if they have communal property, separate themselves, create boundaries, and commit to an ideology. It’s a good reminder that community cohesion and community capture have a lot in common. Measuring Success in Intentional Communities by Rubin, Willis & Ludwig is a good reminder that community longevity is not necessarily a good indicator of whether people are thriving in them. Commitment and Community by Rosabeth Moss Kanter is a classic that compared a bunch of 19th century communes and identified some key ingredients for building commitment. Alienation and Charisma by Benjamin Zablocki reports on some good field research and underscores the importance of charisma. The Tyranny of Structurelessness by Jo Freeman (1970) is a classic essay about how an informal hierarchy is even worse than an explicit one.

The Fire and the Word by Gloria Munoz Ramirez and Subcomandante Marcos is a really helpful primer on how the Zapatista movement came about, their practice-before-theory approach, how they built autonomous institutions, and the original Good Government Boards. The Zapatistas have an excellent archive, Enlace Zapatista, which includes their 2023 explanation of the new autonomy.

If you’re curious about how I came to these conclusions, I did a lot more research than made it into this article. You can see the results of that research in this report.
