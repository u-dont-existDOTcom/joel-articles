Appendix: Watch These, Then Read These

Films

Where’s Utopia? (1988), a film by my dad (with Jerry Mintz)

A Place Called Chiapas (1998) about the early years of the Zapatista movement

American Commune (2013) is a film about The Farm told through the eyes of two grown sisters who were raised there; their vantage point is why I highlight this film in this article

Commune (2005) documents life at Black Bear Ranch and explores the way their ideas about radical freedom shaped the way they related to each other

A New We tours a variety of European ecovillages

Within Reach follows a couple as they bike 6500 miles and visit ~100 communities

Seeking the Good Life in America compares three nearby communities: Twin Oaks, Acorn, and Light Morning

Voices of Cohousing includes interviews about the appeal of partial participation communities which allow more autonomy but create neighborliness

Inside the Bruderhof is a film about a high-commitment religious community and the tradeoffs they make in terms of autonomy; they also have a pretty extensive YouTube channel

Living in the Future: Lammas shows a community building process and some of the messiness around land-use and planning law

Books and Papers (in order of suggested reading)

For Child-Rearing (in order of suggested reading)

The Continuum Concept by Jean Liedloff (1975) is the classic book on natural childrearing; she draws on her time with the Ye’kuana. Take it with a grain of salt from an anthropological perspective but it is an important book to read

Hunt, Gather, Parent by Michaeleen Doucleff (2021) is a really good book to read for practical ideas on how to include kids in the fabric of family life in a way that allows them autonomy and that distributes the work of caring for children

Community-Building Guides (in order of suggested reading)

Creating a Life Together by Diana Leafe Christian is a good place to start for practical advice on how to think about membership, money, land, agreements, etc.

Building Belonging by Yana Ludwig is a whole systems guide to intentional community

Many Voices One Song by Rau & Koch-Gonzalez is a practical manual for how to use sociocracy

Building United Judgment by Schaub et al. is a manual for how to use consensus decision-making

The Cooperative Culture Handbook by Ludwig & Gimnig is a set of exercises for how to build a culture of cooperation, conflict, etc.

On Communities That Last (and whether that’s actually what we want)

Communities That Abide by Dmitry Orlov makes the uncomfortable case that most communities that have lasted a long time had (1) communal property, (2) some degree of separation, (3) strong boundaries, and (4) a compelling and all-binding ideology. It makes the case that community cohesion and community capture are similar.

Measuring Success in Intentional Communities by Rubin, Willis, and Ludwig is a paper that asks whether longevity is an appropriate measure of community success when communities may last without their members flourishing

Commitment and Community by Rosabeth Moss Kanter compares a variety of 19th century communes and describes what they did to build commitment

Alienation and Charisma by Benjamin Zablocki is a book based on his field research of communes and their use of charisma

The Tyranny of Structurelessness by Jo Freeman (1970) is a classic essay on why an unacknowledged hierarchy is worse than an explicit one

The Fire and the Word by Gloria Muñoz Ramírez and Subcomandante Marcos is a book about how the Zapatista movement came to be, how they tend to do practice before theory, how they’ve built autonomous institutions, and how they did Good Government Boards originally

Enlace Zapatista has an archive of information from the Zapatistas, including their 2023 explanations of their new autonomy
