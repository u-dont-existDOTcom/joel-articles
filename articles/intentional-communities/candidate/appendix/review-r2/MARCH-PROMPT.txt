Below are 12 paragraphs from the reading list at the end of a blog article about building intentional communities, each one on its own, with its sentences numbered.

Label every numbered sentence of every paragraph S (STEP) or B (BREAK):
- S, a step: the sentence moves the paragraph one step further along a sequence, the way a manual or a program does: the next instruction, the next fact or case, a rule, a reason, or a condition or caution attached to a step.
- B, a break: the sentence stops that forward march, even for a moment. For example: the writer reacts or exclaims; an aside that loosens what was just said ("Or put it in your own words."); a pause, waiting, or a permission ("If it doesn't come, that's okay for today."); stopping to consider what something might feel like or be like ("It might be more of a mood. Sadness, maybe."); just staying with something, like listening or being present, instead of doing the next thing; a story or memory; a joke; a question; the writer's own doubt or opinion stepping back from the sequence.
Judge what the sentence does in its paragraph, not its grammar: an instruction to wait or just listen can be a break, and a plain statement can be a step.
