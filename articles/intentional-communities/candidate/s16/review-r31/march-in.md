Get two or three people together to form a practice group. Call it that, not a commune! Meet regularly, and practice listening and switching roles, and get through at least one disagreement together, and keep your commitments to each other. One disagreement is enough, I promise! Hold off on talking about a shared future until you've done all of that.

As for where to find these people, I'd try an unprogrammed Quaker meeting, or a restorative-practice group. An open-hearted congregation is another option, or a mature recovery group, or a meditation group. Or someone you know who came back from a long retreat kinder, rather than more impressed with themselves. You never know!

Go visit some actual communities too. Really, go! Twin Oaks, East Wind and Acorn all take visitors. So do other intentional communities, and a lot of ecovillages. Check their current visitor requirements first, though.

You'll learn things on a visit that no manifesto can teach you, like how much togetherness you actually enjoy, what kind of privacy you need, and which labor system drives you bananas. And then there are the communal showers. Will they feel liberating, or send you off pricing studio apartments?

Do those three things before you spend serious time or money looking for land. The land can wait!
