Work on the inside stuff. Start the pl/ork. Read the guide to reparenting or the Hearthwork manual, or something else equally serious, and start practicing.

Get a few people together – two or three – and start practicing together. Don’t call it a commune, and you don’t have to actually be a commune. Just get together regularly.

You may be able to find some people to do this with at a Quaker meeting, a restorative practice group, an open-hearted religious congregation, a mature recovery group, a meditation group, or by finding people who have recently returned from a long retreat without an ego-inflation.

Check out some existing communities. Twin Oaks will welcome visitors, as will East Wind, Acorn, and many other intentional communities and ecovillages. (Check to see what is required of visitors at this time.)

This will help you to figure out how much togetherness you need and want, how to handle privacy, what kind of labor system you might like, whether or not you want communal showers, etc.

Do these three things before you spend serious time and money looking for a piece of land.

There is no application form, and I’m not selling a retreat. The manuals are free. Where there is real community, there will be community-gathering.

My dad was formed by the Bruderhof community, and now, many decades later, I am helping to publish his film and writing these words.

Maybe some of you will find something here useful enough to build on, and maybe you’ll teach some others what you’ve learned, and they’ll build on it, too.

If you build something real, then maybe we’ll hear about it. If you teach others to build real things, it hardly matters.
