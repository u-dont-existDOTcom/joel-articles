Start working on it inside yourself: Read the Reparenting guide and/or the Hearthwork manual (both free; you’ll probably want to do some other serious practice in parallel).

Gather a few people (2-3) and start a practice group. Don’t call it a commune, yet. Get together regularly.

You might find them at Quaker meetings (unprogrammed; not all are, unfortunately); restorative practice groups; open-hearted religious congregations; mature recovery groups; meditation groups; people who’ve gone away on long retreats and come back not ego-inflated.

Visit some real communities. Twin Oaks is one. East Wind is another. Acorn is another. Many of the ecovillages are open to visitors, too. (But read up on what they require of visitors nowadays.)

These visits will help you find out what you like and don’t like about togetherness, privacy, labor systems, communal showers, etc.

Don’t invest serious time or money in looking for land until you’ve done these three things.

There is no application form you need to fill out. There is no retreat you need to buy. The manuals are free. Just start making community around you, wherever you are.

My father was shaped by the Bruderhof community. I’m doing this publication / film to try to carry it forward.

If you do it, maybe you’ll carry it forward, too. If you teach other people how to do it, too, even better.

If you do either, maybe we’ll get to meet. If you teach others, we may not need to meet.
