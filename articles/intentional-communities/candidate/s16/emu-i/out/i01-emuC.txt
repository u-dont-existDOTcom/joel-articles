Work on the inside stuff yourself. Start the pl/ork. Read the reparenting guide and hearthwork manual, or pick some other serious practice and practice it.

Find some other people to practice with. Not to form a commune, just a practice group. Two is enough; three is better.

Where will you find such people? In Quaker meetings, in restorative practice groups, in open-hearted religious congregations, in mature recovery groups, in meditation practice groups, among people who have just come off long retreats and discovered they’re not better than everybody else.

Visit actual communities. Twin Oaks will welcome you, as will East Wind and Acorn and many others, including a lot of the ecovillages. Read up on their requirements for visitors.

When you do that you will get a much better idea of whether you want all that togetherness or not, how much privacy you really need, what kind of labor system you want to work with, and whether communal showers are a good idea or you’d rather spend all your money on a studio apartment.

Do these things before you start spending serious time or money looking for land.

You’ll notice there’s no place on this page for you to apply, or to sign up for a retreat. The manuals are all free. I’m hoping that enough people will get enough of the idea of community to do something about it.

My father was formed by the Bruderhof community; publishing his film and writing this page is my way of being formed by it too.

Maybe someone will do something with it, and maybe that something will teach someone else to do something, and so on.

If you do something real with it, maybe we’ll meet. If you teach other people to do something real with it, it doesn’t matter.
