Find two or three other people and meet regularly. Call it a practice group, not a commune. Practice listening and switching roles. Get through at least one disagreement too, and keep your commitments. One disagreement is enough! You can talk about a shared future later.

Go visit some real communities. Really, go! Twin Oaks is one. East Wind is another, and so is Acorn, and there are plenty of other intentional communities. Lots of ecovillages are open to visitors too. Check their current visitor requirements first.

By visiting some communities, you'll get a better idea of how much togetherness you enjoy and what kind of privacy you need, and what kind of labor system irritates you. And whether communal showers feel liberating, or make you start looking at studio apartments! And no manifesto is going to tell you any of that!

Do those three things before you spend serious time or money looking for land. I know!

I'm not trying to sell you a retreat or get you to fill out an application or anything. The manuals I mentioned are free. I've just noticed that wherever people are taking real care of each other and playing, feeling useful and having room to become more honest, community tends to gather.
