Start doing some inner pl/ork. Get a copy of the free reparenting guide, and/or the Hearthwork manual, or something else you consider serious and start practicing before you bring an audience into it!

Get a couple of people together to form a practice group. I don't mean commune. I mean a couple of people you can meet with regularly and practice listening, role switching, etc. See if you can't get through at least one disagreement (one is enough!), and keep your commitments to each other for a while.

Where might you find some people to do this with? Are there any unprogrammed Quaker meetings around? Any groups working on restorative practices? Open-hearted religious congregations? Mature recovery or meditation groups? People you know who have returned from long retreats seeming kinder rather than more self-important?

Have you actually visited any communities? There are many intentional communities (Twin Oaks, East Wind, Acorn, etc.) that are happy to have visitors, and many ecovillages that may also be able to accommodate you.

You'll learn a lot that isn't written in any manifesto - how much togetherness you want/like, how much privacy you need, what kind of labor doing system doesn't drive you bananas, whether throwing in with folks for shower time is great, or whether you should start figuring out what folks might pay for a studio apartment.

Do these three things, at least, before you invest a lot of time and money in trying to find a place for your future community. I know, I know.

Now, I'm not selling anything here. I'm not even asking you to come to a retreat or apply to come do life with some of US! The manuals are free. It just seems that where ever people gather to do life in community, they end up doing something like the above in order to find the care, play, usefulness, and honesty they're looking for.
