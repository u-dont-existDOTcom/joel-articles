It would be good to start working on your inner pl/ork. Pick up a copy of the Reparenting Guide and the Hearthwork Manual (both free) or whatever you consider serious work, and practice some!

It would be good to get together with a couple of other people for some practice at forming a group. I know you're anxious to form a commune, but call it a practice group for now, and meet for a while. Practice listening, switching off, being upset with each other at least once, and sticking with it despite that. Just once is enough!

Where would you find such people, you ask? Well, are there any unprogrammed Quaker meetings nearby? How about groups practicing restorative practices? Do you know of any open-hearted religious congregations? Maybe a mature recovery or meditation group? Do you know anyone who's recently returned from a long retreat and seems kind?

Have you ever been to a community? If not, you ought to check some out. Twin Oaks, East Wind, and Acorn communities, among many others, are used to having visitors come by. Many of the ecovillages are as well.

You'll learn a lot that you won't find in manifestos: how much time you want to spend together, what kinds of privacy you need, what kinds of systems for doing labor drive you bananas, and whether hanging out with folks in the shower will eventually lead to more togetherness or a need to start pricing studio apartments.

Do these three things, dear seeker, before you spend all that time and money looking for a place to move to! I know, I know!

P.S. This is not a sales pitch. I'm not asking you to come to a retreat or fill out an application. The manuals are free. I'm just saying that if you want community, you'll find it where people are working to be more caring, playful, useful, and honest.
