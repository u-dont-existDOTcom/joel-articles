# Practice Comes Before Property

Most people building community go about it in the wrong order. They get the land first, then write the vision down in a document, then let whoever had the money end up owning it. And the emotional preparation? That gets put off until later, when everyone has more time.

The better order is less cinematic: 1) practice together, 2) process a real conflict, 3) clarify your values, 4) test membership, 5) decide ownership and exit, and 6) only then think about land. I know, it wouldn't make much of a movie!

Likewise, the economic side should go in stages. You shouldn't try to fly straight to the economic destination! Only after the group has proven it can reliably provide for everybody, and fairly decide who gets what from the common, should it move to a common purse. And cutting back on outside money, or getting rid of it, should come from what the group has shown it can do. ("Money is impure!" is a weird thing to say while somebody else is quietly paying the tax bill.)

## If You Can’t Move

I realize that for most people reading this there are a lot of reasons why they can't just head off and go build community on some land. Jobs, kids, pets, debt, a mortgage, a sick parent, or several of these arranged in a circle... That's fine! The most important pl/ork doesn't need land anyway.

People sometimes buy forty acres and then bring all their unprocessed baggage with them to a more scenic location. The view's better, I'll give them that! But now the conflict has goats.

The inner pl/ork can start right where you live, though. [Peer counseling](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling) only needs two people and enough privacy to talk. And reparenting, somatic work, jealousy, conflict repair, etc etc? None of that has to wait for a deed either!

A group that practices together weekly for two years may never own land. So what? It can still make a difference in how people get through divorce, illness, grief, parenting and plain old loneliness.

Here's an idea if you're remote, or stuck where you are: build the part of it you actually can. Have a weekly meal with two households. Set up a counseling exchange with one friend, in person or by video. Share tools and childcare, or transport and food prep. Or a sauna! And if you're hippie enough, add acro-yoga, dance improv, a mothers' circle or a [cuddle puddle](http://cuddleparty.com/).

Trust me on this one. If three households keep showing up for each other like this for six months, you'll learn more than you would from another year of daydreaming about the ideal village.

## What to Do This Month

Start working on the inner pl/ork. With what? The free [reparenting guide](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/inner-child-self-love-tips-and-guided) and [Hearthwork manual](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling), or another serious practice you already trust. And practice before you have an audience!

Find two or three people to form a practice group with (call it that, not a commune). Meet regularly, and learn to listen, switch roles, handle one disagreement and keep your commitments before you start talking about a shared future. One disagreement is enough!

Where might you find them? Maybe among unprogrammed Quakers, restorative-practice groups, open-hearted congregations, mature recovery groups or meditation groups. Or people who came back from a long retreat kinder, rather than more impressed with themselves.

Have you ever visited an actual community? Go! [Twin Oaks](https://twinoaks.org/), [East Wind](http://eastwind.org/), [Acorn](https://www.ic.org/directory/acorn-community-farm/) and other [intentional communities](https://www.ic.org/) take visitors, and so do a lot of [ecovillages](https://ecovillage.org/). Just check their current visitor requirements first.

What do you get out of a visit? Things no manifesto can tell you. How much togetherness you actually enjoy, for one, and what kind of privacy you need. Or which labor system drives you nuts! And whether communal showers feel liberating or send you off pricing studio apartments.

What about the land? Do those three things first, before you spend serious time or money looking for it.

I'm not trying to sell you a retreat or get you to fill out an application or anything, the manuals I mentioned are free. I've just noticed that community tends to gather wherever people find real care, play, usefulness and room to become more honest.
