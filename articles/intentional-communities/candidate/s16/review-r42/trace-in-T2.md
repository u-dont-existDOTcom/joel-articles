## What to Do This Month H3-P4

CONTEXT BEFORE: Find two or three other people and meet regularly. This is not quite the same thing as forming a commune. Call it a practice group. Practice the skills you need to practice, like listening and switching roles. Get through at least one disagreement too, and keep your commitments. One disagreement is enough! You can talk about a shared future later.

PUBLISHED:

Possible places to meet people include unprogrammed Quaker meetings, restorative-practice groups, open-hearted congregations, mature recovery communities, meditation groups, and people who came back from a long retreat kinder rather than more impressed with themselves.

REWRITE:

Where do you find these people? Try an unprogrammed Quaker meeting, a restorative-practice group, an open-hearted congregation, a mature recovery group, a meditation group, or find some people who have gone on long retreats and come back kinder, rather than more impressed with themselves.

CONTEXT AFTER: Go visit some real communities. Really, go! [Twin Oaks](https://twinoaks.org/) is one. [East Wind](http://eastwind.org/) is another, and so is [Acorn](https://www.ic.org/directory/acorn-community-farm/), and there are plenty of other [intentional communities](https://www.ic.org/). Lots of [ecovillages](https://ecovillage.org/) are open to visitors too. Check their requirements first.

And whether communal showers feel liberating, or you'll start looking at studio apartments for rent right after the first communal shower you take! And no manifesto is going to tell you any of that!
