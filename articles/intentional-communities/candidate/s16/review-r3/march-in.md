One of the main problems I see with how people go about building community is that they do the steps in the wrong order. They get the land first, put the vision in a document, let ownership pile up with whoever had the money, and put off the emotional preparation until everyone has more time. When's that going to be?

The better order is less cinematic, more like this: practice together, process a real conflict, clarify your values, test membership, decide ownership and exit, and only then think about land. I know, it wouldn't make much of a movie!

But there's no reason you can't start the inner pl/ork where you live. And [peer counseling](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling)? It only needs two people and enough privacy to talk. Reparenting, somatic work, jealousy, conflict repair, etc. don't have to wait for a deed either!

A group that practices together weekly for two years may never own land, and it can still make a difference in how people get through divorce, illness, grief, parenting and plain old loneliness. That counts for a lot.

If three households can commit to showing up for each other like this for six months, you'll learn more than you would from another year of daydreaming about the ideal village. Trust me on this one.

Start working on the inner pl/ork, whether it's following the free [reparenting guide](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/inner-child-self-love-tips-and-guided) and [Hearthwork manual](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling) or another serious practice you already trust. And practice before you have an audience, okay?

Find two or three people to form a practice group with (call it that, not a commune, at least for now). Meet regularly, and learn to listen, switch roles, handle one disagreement and keep your commitments before you start talking about a shared future. One disagreement is plenty to start with!

Then go visit actual communities, like [Twin Oaks](https://twinoaks.org/), [East Wind](http://eastwind.org/), [Acorn](https://www.ic.org/directory/acorn-community-farm/) or other [intentional communities](https://www.ic.org/) (a lot of [ecovillages](https://ecovillage.org/) take visitors too). Just check their current visitor requirements first. Nobody likes surprise guests, right?

What do you get out of a visit? Stuff no manifesto can tell you: how much togetherness you actually enjoy, what kind of privacy you need, which labor system drives you nuts, and whether communal showers feel liberating or send you off pricing studio apartments.

So do those three things first, before you spend serious time or money looking for land. The land will still be there!
