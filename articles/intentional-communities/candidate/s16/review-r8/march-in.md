Land, childcare, housing, food, obligations, etc, etc. A lot of the time, all that goes through the extended family and the local memory, alongside the formal systems. Who needs a workshop called "Rebuilding Intergenerational Belonging" when some aunt has already given three people a task?

But these days it can be soul destroying. People have a lot less of the communal life that held the obligation up (the shared land, work, childcare, food and daily belonging), but they still carry it. So if one of them makes it into the cash economy, it's really hard to build any independence or long-term stability, because they may be expected to carry everybody. How can they get ahead, when all these people are dependent on them? And the relatives stay dependent too, since the payments don't give them a real alternative. It's the ancestral ethic playing out inside an individualistic cash economy. It can keep people alive, but it eats away at both sides too.

Most people building community go about it in the wrong order. They get the land first, then write the vision down in a document, then let whoever had the money end up owning it. And the emotional preparation? They'll do that later, when everyone has more time.

The inner pl/ork can start right where you live. [Peer counseling](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling) only needs two people and enough privacy to talk. And reparenting, somatic work, jealousy, conflict repair, etc etc can happen pretty much anywhere. No deed required!

Trust me on this one. If three households keep showing up for each other like this for six months, you'll learn more than you would from another year of daydreaming about the ideal village.

Where can you find them? Maybe at an unprogrammed Quaker meeting, or a restorative-practice group. Open-hearted congregations, mature recovery groups and meditation groups are worth a try too. And look for the people who came back from a long retreat kinder, rather than more impressed with themselves!

Then go visit actual communities! [Twin Oaks](https://twinoaks.org/), [East Wind](http://eastwind.org/) and [Acorn](https://www.ic.org/directory/acorn-community-farm/) take visitors, and so do other [intentional communities](https://www.ic.org/) and a lot of [ecovillages](https://ecovillage.org/). Ever been to one? Just check their current visitor requirements first.

You'll find out things on a visit that no manifesto can tell you. How much togetherness you actually enjoy, for one, and what kind of privacy you need. Or which labor system drives you nuts! And whether communal showers feel liberating or send you off pricing studio apartments.
