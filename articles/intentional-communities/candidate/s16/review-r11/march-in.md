The better order is less cinematic: 1) practice together, 2) process a real conflict, 3) clarify your values, 4) test membership, 5) decide ownership and exit, and 6) only then think about land. I know, it wouldn't make much of a movie!

Here's an idea if you're remote, or stuck where you are: build the part of it you actually can. Have a weekly meal with two households. Set up a counseling exchange with one friend, in person or by video. Share tools and childcare, or transport and food prep. Or a sauna! And if you're hippie enough, add acro-yoga, dance improv, a mothers' circle or a [cuddle puddle](http://cuddleparty.com/).

Where might you find them? Maybe among unprogrammed Quakers, restorative-practice groups, open-hearted congregations, mature recovery groups or meditation groups. Or people who came back from a long retreat kinder, rather than more impressed with themselves.

Have you ever visited an actual community? Go! [Twin Oaks](https://twinoaks.org/), [East Wind](http://eastwind.org/), [Acorn](https://www.ic.org/directory/acorn-community-farm/) and other [intentional communities](https://www.ic.org/) take visitors, and so do a lot of [ecovillages](https://ecovillage.org/). Just check their current visitor requirements first.
