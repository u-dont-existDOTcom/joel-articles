You might be able to find some people along these lines in unprogrammed Quaker meeting places, restorative practice groups, open-hearted congregations, mature recovery groups, meditation groups, or other places where people return from long retreats with kindness rather than an inflated sense of self-importance.

Many communities like Twin Oaks , East Wind , Acorn , and other intentional communities and ecovillages are open to visitors. Check out their requirements .

You'll learn a lot about what you like and don't like about togetherness, privacy, labor systems, communal showers, and whatever else you can think of.

Do this before looking at land or otherwise getting serious about forming a community.

I don't have an application for you to fill out, or a retreat for you to attend. The manuals are free. Wherever there is care, play, usefulness, and the potential for honesty, community will arise.
