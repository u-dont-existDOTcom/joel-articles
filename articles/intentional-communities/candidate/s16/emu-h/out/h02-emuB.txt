Find two or three other people who are interested in the same things you are and meet regularly. This is not quite the same thing as forming a commune. Call it a practice group. Practice the skills you need to practice: listening, switching, disagreeing, committing, etc. You can talk about forming a community later.

Where do you find these people? Try an unprogrammed Quaker meeting, a restorative practice group, an open-hearted congregation, a mature recovery group, a meditation group, or find some people who have gone on long retreats and come back nice.

Go visit some real communities. Twin Oaks, East Wind, Acorn, and a variety of other communities are used to having people visit them. Many ecovillages are too. Check their requirements.

By visiting some communities, you'll get a better idea if you want a lot of togetherness or some privacy, what kind of labor system you want to be part of, whether or not you want to take communal showers, or if you'll immediately begin looking at studio apartments for rent after the first communal shower you take.

Do these things before you start looking for land, if that's what you're thinking of doing.

There is no application to fill out, there is no retreat you need to go on, there are just some free manuals to read. Community is just where you're at when you're caring for each other, playing together, realizing how useful you all are, and becoming more honest with each other.
