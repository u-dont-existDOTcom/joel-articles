Form a practice group. It’s not quite a commune yet, but find two (or better, three) other people and meet regularly to practice listening, role switching, conflict management, and commitment.

Where do you find people? A non-programmed Quaker group, a restorative practice group, a fairly open-hearted religious congregation, a mature recovery group, some people returning from a long retreat who are not full of self-importance.

Visit some communities. Twin Oaks, East Wind, Acorn, and other communities are used to visitors, and many ecovillages as well. Check their requirements.

It will be helpful to see whether you like being together with people all the time, whether you can work with a labor system, whether communal showers will work for you, or whether you will constantly be calculating how much a studio apartment would rent for in the area.

Do these things before you spend time (and maybe money) looking for land.

There’s no application, and no retreat for you to attend. The manuals are free. Community is something that happens when people treat each other with care, and learn to be playful, useful, and honest.
