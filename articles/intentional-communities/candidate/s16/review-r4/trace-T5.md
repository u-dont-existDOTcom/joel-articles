## H2-P6 → H2-P6x

1. "Three households reliably showing up for one another for six months" → "If three households can commit to showing up for each other like this for six months": EQUIVALENT. "Commit ... for six months" carries the dependable follow-through of "reliably"; "can" is the idiom for "manage to", so a reader still takes the benefit to come from the households actually keeping it up. The "If" only spells out the scenario the published phrase already sets up. "like this" points to the practices listed just before (weekly meal, counseling exchange, shared tools and childcare): clear, same claim.
2. "will teach you more than another year of imagining the ideal village" → "you'll learn more than you would from another year of daydreaming about the ideal village": EQUIVALENT. Same comparison; "another year" and "the ideal village" kept; "daydreaming" is the same activity in plainer words.
3. ADDED: nothing that states something new. "Trust me on this one." is the pause: it vouches for the claim and adds no fact, cause or stronger claim.
