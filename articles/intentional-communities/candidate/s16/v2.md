# Practice Comes Before Property

One of the main problems I see with how people go about building community is that they do the steps in the wrong order. Sound familiar? They get the land first, put the vision in a document, let ownership pile up with whoever had the money, and put off the emotional preparation until everyone has more time.

The better order is less cinematic, more like this: practice together, process a real conflict, clarify your values, test membership, decide ownership and exit, and only then think about land. I know, it wouldn't make much of a movie!

Likewise, the economic side has to go in stages. You're probably not going to be able to fly straight to the economic destination! Only after the group has proven it can reliably provide for everybody, and fairly decide who gets what from the common, should it move to a common purse. And cutting back on outside money, or getting rid of it, comes from what the group has shown it can do. ("Money is impure!" is a weird thing to say while somebody else is quietly paying the tax bill.)

## If You Can’t Move

I realize that for most people reading this there are a lot of reasons why they can't just head off and go build community on some land. Jobs, kids, pets, debt, a mortgage, a sick parent, or several of these arranged in a circle... That's fine! The most important pl/ork doesn't need land anyway.

People sometimes buy forty acres and then bring all their unprocessed baggage with them to a more scenic location. The view's better, I'll give them that! But now the conflict has goats.

But there's no reason you can't start the inner pl/ork where you live. And [peer counseling](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling)? It only needs two people and enough privacy to talk. Reparenting, somatic work, jealousy, conflict repair and the rest don't have to wait for a deed either!

A group that practices together weekly for two years may never own land. So what? It can still make a difference in how people get through divorce, illness, grief, parenting and plain old loneliness.

Remote or constrained readers can build the portion available. Hold a weekly meal with two households. Arrange a counseling exchange with one friend in person or by video. Share tools and childcare. Or a sauna! Add acro-yoga, dance improvisation, a mothers' circle, or a [cuddle puddle](http://cuddleparty.com/) if you're hippie enough.

Trust me on this one. If three households can commit to showing up for each other like this for six months, you'll learn more than you would from another year of daydreaming about the ideal village.

## What to Do This Month

Start working on the inner pl/ork. With what? Maybe the free [reparenting guide](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/inner-child-self-love-tips-and-guided) and [Hearthwork manual](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling), or another serious practice you already trust. And practice before you have an audience!

Find two or three people to form a practice group with (call it that, not a commune, at least for now). Meet regularly, and learn to listen, switch roles, handle one disagreement and keep your commitments before you start talking about a shared future. One disagreement is plenty to start with!

Where do you find them? Maybe try unprogrammed Quaker meetings, restorative practice groups, open-hearted congregations, mature recovery groups, meditation groups, or people who came back from a long retreat kinder rather than more impressed with themselves.

Then go visit actual communities, like [Twin Oaks](https://twinoaks.org/), [East Wind](http://eastwind.org/), [Acorn](https://www.ic.org/directory/acorn-community-farm/) or other [intentional communities](https://www.ic.org/) (a lot of [ecovillages](https://ecovillage.org/) take visitors too). Just check their current visitor requirements first. Nobody likes surprise guests, right?

What do you get out of a visit? Stuff no manifesto can tell you: how much togetherness you actually enjoy, what kind of privacy you need, which labor system drives you nuts, and whether communal showers feel liberating or send you off pricing studio apartments.

So do those three things first, before you spend serious time or money looking for land. The land will still be there!

I'm not trying to sell you a retreat or get you to fill out an application or anything, the manuals are free. I've just noticed that community tends to gather wherever people find real care, play, usefulness and room to become more honest.
