# Practice Comes Before Property

Most people try and do community in the wrong order. "First let's get land!" "Then let's write down a vision document!" "And whoever's putting in the money can own the place!" And the emotional side of things? That doesn't get done until "later on, when everyone has more time".

It would be better to get together to practice first, and process a real conflict, and get clear on your values, and test membership, and decide ownership and exit, and then, after all that, think about land. Less cinematic, I know! (It would make for a pretty bad movie.)

Same with money: do it in stages. Don't try and fly right to the economic destination in one go! Wait until the group can reliably provide for everyone, and fairly decide who gets what from the common, before it goes to a common purse. And work on reducing (or eliminating) outside money based on what the group has shown it CAN do, rather than because "money is impure!" (That sounds kind of weird when somebody else is quietly paying the tax bill.)

## If You Can’t Move

I realize that for most people reading this there are a lot of reasons why they can't just head off and go build community on some land. Jobs, kids, pets, debt, a mortgage, a sick parent, or several of these arranged in a circle... That's fine! The most important pl/ork doesn't need land anyway.

People sometimes buy forty acres and then bring all their unprocessed stuff with them to a more scenic location. It's a more beautiful view for sure, but now you have the conflict PLUS goats.

Your inner pl/ork can actually start right now, right where you live. [Peer counseling](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling) just requires 2 people and a private place to sit down and talk. Reparenting work, somatic work, working on jealousy, working on conflict repair, etc etc, can all start before you own a single square inch of dirt. No deed required!

What if, after two years of practicing together every week, the group still doesn't own land? Would that be a waste of time? Nope. It can still change how people get through divorce, illness, grief, parenting and plain old loneliness.

Here's an idea if you're remote, or stuck where you are: build the part of it you actually can. Have a weekly meal with two households. Set up a counseling exchange with one friend, in person or by video. Share tools or childcare. Transport or food prep work too. Or a sauna! And if you're hippie enough, add acro-yoga, dance improv, a mothers' circle or a [cuddle puddle](http://cuddleparty.com/).

Trust me on this one. If three households keep showing up for each other like this for six months, you'll learn more than you would from another year of daydreaming about the ideal village.

## What to Do This Month

Start working on the inner pl/ork. With what? The free [reparenting guide](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/inner-child-self-love-tips-and-guided) and [Hearthwork manual](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling), or another serious practice you already trust. And practice before you have an audience!

Find two or three people to form a practice group with (call it that, not a commune). Meet regularly, and learn to listen, switch roles, handle one disagreement and keep your commitments before you start talking about a shared future. One disagreement is enough!

Where can you find some of these people? Are there any unprogrammed Quaker meetings near you? How about restorative-practice groups? Maybe there's an open-hearted congregation, or a mature recovery group, or a meditation group you can check out. Or maybe you know someone who came back from a long retreat kinder, rather than more impressed with themselves.

Have you ever been to an actual community? If you haven't, you ought to! [Twin Oaks](https://twinoaks.org/), [East Wind](http://eastwind.org/), [Acorn](https://www.ic.org/directory/acorn-community-farm/) and other [intentional communities](https://www.ic.org/) take visitors, and so do a lot of [ecovillages](https://ecovillage.org/) (just check their current visitor requirements before you go).

What will you learn? So much, and none of it is in any manifesto! How much togetherness you actually like, for one, and what kind of privacy you need. And which labor system drives you bananas! And whether showering with folks feels liberating, or makes you want to start pricing studio apartments.

Do those three things before you spend a lot of time or money looking for land. I know, I know!

I'm not selling anything here, by the way. I'm not even asking you to come to a retreat or fill out an application, and the manuals I mentioned are free. I've just noticed that community tends to gather wherever people find real care, play, usefulness and room to become more honest.
