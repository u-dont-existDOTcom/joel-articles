Start with a cluster of households where all basic expenses are taken care of in the cluster, and anyone making money on the outside contributes a set amount. Only after you've proven that you can take care of people's provisions, and can fairly determine who gets what from the common purse, should you start lessening or removing the need to make money "on the outside." If You Can't Get to This Right Away

I realize that for most people reading this there are a lot of reasons why they can't head off to buy land on a forested mountain. Jobs, kids, pets, debt, a mortgage, a sick parent…

But it's really, really tempting for people to start buying forty acres of land, and then bring all their baggage (literal, or material baggage) with them to the new location.

Do the inner pl/ork where you are. Start peer counseling. Learn the basics of reparenting. Take up somatic work. Start dealing with jealousy, and learn how to process conflict.

Even if you never buy land, you can have a group of people practicing this stuff together for two years, and you will be ahead of the curve, and be able to make a difference in how people deal with divorce, illness, grief, being a parent, and feeling lonely.

If you're reading this far away, or are trapped in some way, do what you can. Have a couple of other households over for dinner once a week. Commit to counseling a friend (maybe over the inter-tubes). Share the use of tools, or child care, or transportation, or food, or sauna time. If you're hippie enough, practice acro-yoga, or dance improvisation, or a mothers' circle, or a cuddle puddle.

If three households can commit to showing up for each other like this for half a year, you'll learn more than you would from continuing to daydream about what your community might be like.

What you can do in your first month

Do the inner pl/ork. Read the free Reparenting Guide , or the Hearthwork Manual , or whatever you feel called to.

Find a couple of people to do this with. Call it a practice group, or a commune, whatever.

Go to a Quaker meeting, or a restorative practice group, or an open-hearted church, or a mature recovery group, or a meditation group, or find a friend who just came back from a long retreat and has been reborn.

Go visit a community or two, or three. Twin Oaks , East Wind , the Acorn , tons of other intentional communities, and many ecovillages allow you to visit (though you should check with them for up-to-date requirements).

Then you'll know how much you like being together, having privacy, different kinds of labor systems, and whether or not you can adjust to communal showers, or will always feel like you should own your own two-bedroom studio apartment.

Do these things before you spend too much time looking for land.

(I'm not selling you a retreat, or asking you to fill out an application to join our community. I'm giving you the manuals for free, and am currently in Sénégal, about to buy some land, but not forming a commune. I've seen that community forms wherever people take it seriously.)

My dad was transformed by his time with the Bruderhof commune, and went on to make a movie , start a research organization , and bring me into a series of communal experiments. Decades later, he's helping to publish the movie, and I'm writing this.

Maybe you'll help to build something good from this. Maybe you'll teach someone else, who will build something good.

If you build something real, maybe we'll hear about it. If you teach someone else how to build something real, it won't matter.
