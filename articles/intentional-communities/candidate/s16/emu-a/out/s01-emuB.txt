Practice Comes Before Property

One of the main problems I see with how people go about building community is that they’re doing the steps in the wrong order.

It’s more like this: practice, process, figure out what you want, figure out who you want in (and how to deal with who you don’t), figure out who’s going to own what, figure out exit policies, and only then figure out land.

Similarly, for the economic side, start with a cluster of people taking as much as they can of the basic stuff out of the purchase process. Then people who are able to make money outside bring in a certain amount. Once the group is able to consistently provide everything members need without people having to bring in money from outside, it can be a common purse. At that point, people can stop bringing in money from outside, or reduce it as much as possible. Don’t start from declaring money outside impure.

If You Can’t Move

Lots of people who are reading this can’t immediately drop everything and go build community on some land. They have jobs, kids, pets, debts, mortgages, sick parents, etc.

They sometimes end up committing to a bunch of land they can’t handle, and then importing all their material problems to a nice beautiful place with goats.

But there’s no reason you can’t at least start doing some of the inner pl/ork where you are. Work on peer counseling, reparenting, somatic practices, jealousy, conflict, etc.

Even if you never get to building a community on land, if you get a group of people practicing together weekly for two years, you’ll be changing the world.

If you can’t move to where like-minded people are, or if you can’t drop your life and do it fulltime, do as much of this as you can. Get a couple of households to do weekly meals, do counseling with someone, in person or over video chat. Share resources, do chores for each other’s kids, carpool, bulk cook, build a sauna and open it to your neighborhood, practice acro-yoga, have dance improv parties, have a mothers circle, make a cuddle puddle, whatever you’re comfortable with and hippie enough to do.

If you can get 3 households to do it regularly for half a year, it will be more than enough.

What To Do For The First Month

Start working on the inner pl/ork, whether it’s following the free reparenting guide and hearthwork manual or something else.

Find 2-3 people to form a practice group with.

You can try going to Quaker meetings (non-programmed ones), groups practicing restorative practices, open-hearted religious groups, mature recovery groups, meditation groups, or find people who have done long retreats and come back humble, etc.

If you can travel, visit some communities like Twin Oaks, East Wind, Acorn or others ( many are listed here , and a lot of ecovillages are open to visitors, check their requirements ). It’s very useful to see what you want and don’t want in terms of togetherness, privacy, labor system, communal showers, etc.

Don’t go looking for land until you’ve done the above.

I’m not trying to sell you a membership application or a retreat or anything, the manuals are free. I’m working on buying land in Sénégal, but it’s not like I’m starting a commune or anything. Just know that wherever people are doing this, they will end up building a community of some sort or another.

My dad was heavily influenced by the Bruderhof commune. In his turn, he made a movie, started a research organization, and carried me through various communal experiments. Which in turn is making me publish the movie and write these posts decades later.

Maybe some of you will end up building something. Maybe you’ll teach other people to build something.

If you do build something, maybe we’ll hear about it. But if you teach other people to build something, it won’t matter.
