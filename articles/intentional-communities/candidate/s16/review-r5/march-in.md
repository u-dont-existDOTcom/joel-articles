Most people building community do the steps in the wrong order. Sound familiar? They get the land first, put the vision in a document, let ownership pile up with whoever had the money, and put off the emotional preparation until everyone has more time.

Likewise, the economic side should go in stages. You shouldn't try to fly straight to the economic destination! Only after the group has proven it can reliably provide for everybody, and fairly decide who gets what from the common, should it move to a common purse. And cutting back on outside money, or getting rid of it, should come from what the group has shown it can do. ("Money is impure!" is a weird thing to say while somebody else is quietly paying the tax bill.)

Remote or constrained readers can build the portion available. Hold a weekly meal with two households. Arrange a counseling exchange with one friend in person or by video. Share tools and childcare, or transport and food preparation. Or a sauna! Add acro-yoga, dance improvisation, a mothers' circle, or a [cuddle puddle](http://cuddleparty.com/) if you're hippie enough.

Start working on the inner pl/ork. With what? The free [reparenting guide](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/inner-child-self-love-tips-and-guided) and [Hearthwork manual](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling), or another serious practice you already trust. And practice before you have an audience!

Find two or three people to form a practice group with (call it that, not a commune). Meet regularly, and learn to listen, switch roles, handle one disagreement and keep your commitments before you start talking about a shared future. One disagreement is enough!

Then go visit actual communities, like [Twin Oaks](https://twinoaks.org/), [East Wind](http://eastwind.org/), [Acorn](https://www.ic.org/directory/acorn-community-farm/) or other [intentional communities](https://www.ic.org/) (a lot of [ecovillages](https://ecovillage.org/) take visitors too). Ever been to one? Just check their current visitor requirements first.

What about the land? Do those three things first, before you spend serious time or money looking for it.

I'm not trying to sell you a retreat or get you to fill out an application or anything, the manuals I mentioned are free. I've just noticed that community tends to gather wherever people find real care, play, usefulness and room to become more honest.

In the villages and families I've spent time around, things often go through the extended family and the local memory, alongside the formal systems. Whether it's land, childcare, housing, food, obligations, etc, etc. Who needs a workshop called "Rebuilding Intergenerational Belonging" when some aunt has already given three people a task?
