# Repeats check s2b: "Communes Are Hot Again 🔥"

Scope: repeats and contradictions that involve at least one block of "Communes Are Hot Again 🔥" (B25 to B43, including its subsection "The Freeloader Problem", B35 to B43). Every quote is copied exactly from the article, and every instance names its section.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

**1. Humans need small tribal groups (B6, B28)**
- B6, "The New Age May Dawn Suddenly": "human beings need small tribal communities again"
- B28, "Communes Are Hot Again 🔥": "We evolved to live in small groups where everybody knows everybody."
- Later adds the anomie explanation (shared norms and close ties fall apart in large societies). The base claim is the one the reader already had as the father's conclusion, so it is partly told again.
- Better: B28, because it gives the reason. B6 is the natural place for the father's version.

**2. People sense something is wrong and want change (B27, B41)**
- B27, "Communes Are Hot Again 🔥": "they agree that something about the current arrangement is starving them"
- B41, "Communes Are Hot Again 🔥" (The Freeloader Problem): "They just know they are tired and alone and want to change it."
- Only B41's last sentence overlaps. The rest of B41 (they don't know why earlier communes failed) is new, but that sentence reads as told again, mildly.
- Better: B27, where the felt need is first set up.

**3. AI upheaval makes community a backup plan (B32, B137, B138)**
- B32, "Communes Are Hot Again 🔥": "forming a village sounded like a good backup plan"
- B137, "Which Connections to the Technosphere Do You Keep?": "Maybe millions of people will lose their jobs to AI."
- B138, same section: "it makes more sense to me to focus on cultivating more human independence from the technosphere"
- Later adds the author's own choice (build independence rather than wait for certainty) and the technosphere framing. The premise that AI is changing jobs and the future is told again.
- Better: B138, which carries the argument. B32 is the natural place for the guess about why interest rose.

**4. People avoid responsibility, and removing a boss does not fix it (B36, B39, B40)**
- B36, "Communes Are Hot Again 🔥" (The Freeloader Problem): "take on the responsibility that anarchism requires"
- B39, same subsection: "some people really would rather let somebody else take responsibility"
- B40, same subsection: "taking away the idea of a boss does not make everyone less passive"
- B39 adds a picture (the same few people do the maintenance, unofficial leaders gain power) and B40 adds remedies (named stewardship, a plan for leeching). But B39's "because" clause and B40's last sentence restate B36's objection twice more within five blocks. A reader would feel told the same thing again.
- Better: B39, because it explains how the problem plays out.

**5. People who want the village feeling without the work (B39, B272)**
- B39, "Communes Are Hot Again 🔥" (The Freeloader Problem): "the same three people end up doing all the maintenance while everybody else talks about freedom"
- B272, "The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn't For": "You want the feeling of village life"
- B272 adds a different step, a self-screening test before applying. The underlying point (some want the benefits without governance and maintenance) comes back as a mild echo.
- Better: both sit where they belong. B39 carries the diagnosis; B272 carries the self-check.

**6. The warnings apply to the author too (B42, B228, B237)**
- B42, "Communes Are Hot Again 🔥" (The Freeloader Problem): "All of the warnings in this article apply to me, too."
- B228, "Founderism: The Four Questions": "I can see it playing out if I were to be a founder as well"
- B237, same section: "And ask them about me, if people end up living on land I buy."
- B228 adds the personal reason (his guides would make him the judge of whether others "understood" them). B237 adds the instruction to put the four questions to him. The core of B42 is told again twice.
- Better: B237, where the instruction sits beside the questions. B42 comes before any warnings have been given.

**7. He plans to buy land and a community may form around him (B42, B238)**
- B42, "Communes Are Hot Again 🔥" (The Freeloader Problem): "people may naturally end up living around me"
- B238, "Founderism: The Four Questions": "communities which form naturally over time"
- B238 adds why founderism is worse in communities that form this way (no formal rules until a critical mass decides to formalize). The plan itself is told again.
- Better: B238, which does the analysis. B42 only states the plan.

**8. He is not recruiting or selling anything (B42, B365)**
- B42, "Communes Are Hot Again 🔥" (The Freeloader Problem): "I am not currently recruiting anyone or founding a community"
- B365, "Practice Comes Before Property" (What to Do This Month): "not trying to sell you a retreat or get you to fill out an application or anything"
- B365 adds that the manuals are free and a closing observation about where community gathers. The disclaimer itself reads as told again.
- Better: B42, where the disclosure explains why he can write freely. B365 repeats it at the close.

### Callbacks (not repeats, listed separately)

- B131 to B26 and B27: "Loneliness is only one reason building community feels urgent." (Which Connections to the Technosphere Do You Keep?) relies on the loneliness material in Communes Are Hot Again 🔥.
- B137 to B33: the joke "before Chantress Seba finishes going through all the applications to her commune" (Which Connections to the Technosphere Do You Keep?) relies on B33, "many thousands of people appeared to be interested" (Communes Are Hot Again 🔥), a section back.

It adds the health-outcome finding, so it is a callback, not a repeat.

Checked and not counted: B39 "unofficial leaders start gaining power" (Communes Are Hot Again 🔥) and B114 "someone everyone trusts and gradually stops questioning" (The Medicine Part, My Ceremony Principles). Same mechanism, different situation (communal property versus medicine ceremonies).
