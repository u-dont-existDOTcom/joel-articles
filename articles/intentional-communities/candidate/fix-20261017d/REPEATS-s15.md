# Repeats check, s15: "What This Looks Like From Sénégal" (B315 to B344)

Scope: only repeats and contradictions with at least one block in that section. Its three parts: Sénégal (opening) = B315-B324; Sénégal (All is not well in paradise) = B325-B334; Sénégal (Lessons from the Black Tax) = B335-B344. Every quote is copied exactly.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

1. B85, B221, B339. A nearly moneyless community still needs a small shared cash pot for outside bills.
- B85 (The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together, 4. The container): "nearly entirely moneyless, but has a small collective cash boundary on the outside"
- B221 (From One Community to a Movement, Federated Communities Make a Movement): "If the legal or economic environment requires money, the federation also needs to have a cash budget for dealing with it."
- B339 (Sénégal, Lessons from the Black Tax): "the commune has to work out how much shared cash it keeps for these things"
Later instance: B339 mostly tells the idea again, but adds a longer list of outside costs (hospital bills, visas, school fees, family obligations) and the question of who handles the money. B221 adds the federation level. B85 sits in the natural place (the money stages); B339 has the fuller list.

2. B86, B339, B340. Control of outside money turns into status or power.
- B86 (Four Parts, 4. The container): "and the outside world’s currency, to name a few"
- B339 (Sénégal, Black Tax): "how to stop access to outside money from creating a new status hierarchy"
- B340 (Sénégal, Black Tax): "buy their way out of everyday communal work, or to buy a greater say in the political system"
Later instance: B339 and B340 are neighbors and a reader feels told twice. B340 adds the concrete forms and the point about outside jobs draining the group's people and skills. B340 carries it better because it names how the power shows up; B86 is the general principle.

3. B319, B320, B331. Daily life in Sénégal is full of prayer, music, joking and people.
- B319 (Sénégal, opening): "Five times a day the prayers pour out into the streets."
- B320 (Sénégal, opening): "People are outside laughing for hours at a time."
- B331 (Sénégal, All is not well in paradise): "with the music, the animals, the joking, the praying, and just being around people all the time"
Later instance: B331's list is told again; what it adds is the sheep and the "another world" feeling. B319 and B320 carry the point better because they are specific; B331 is a recap.

4. B319, B322. Spiritual imagery on public walls.
- B319 (Sénégal, opening): "in the pictures everywhere, in the murals, in the names, in the conversations"
- B322 (Sénégal, opening): "And elsewhere there are murals of Sufi leaders."
Later instance: adds the concrete example (the slogan, the Sufi leaders) and the contrast with the author's hometown walls. Only a light echo. B322 carries it better because it is specific.

5. B322, B324. The same graffiti slogan, two blocks apart.
- B322 (Sénégal, opening): "Tolerance! Openness! Union! Happiness! Love!"
- B324 (Sénégal, opening): "Graffiti in Dakar, Sénégal: Tolerance! Openness! Union! Happiness! Love!"
Later instance: adds nothing but the caption label for the photo, so a reader sees the same five words twice. B322 carries the point, since it gives the reaction to the slogan.

6. B326, B327, B328. Sénégal's communal warmth has hard limits for people who do not fit.
- B326 (Sénégal, All is not well in paradise): "The warmth has hard limits here."
- B327 (same): "I'm not going to praise communal belonging and ignore the people who have to hide who they are"
- B328 (same): "spiritually alive, generous, funny and super communal, while still becoming cruel to those who break its social categories"
Later instance: B327 adds the law and the facts. B328 adds the general lesson ("Being in community isn't automatically liberating"), but its "it's both" half tells B326 and B327 again, so a reader feels told twice. B328 is the more natural place, since it follows the evidence; B326 is the setup.

7. B326, B328, B344. The same "generosity alongside limits" point, restated at the end of the section.
- B328 (Sénégal, All is not well in paradise): "Being in community isn't automatically liberating."
- B344 (Sénégal, Lessons from the Black Tax): "Of course that generosity lives right alongside watchfulness, social conservatism, bureaucracy and all the usual complications of a real country."
Later instance: mostly told again; adds only "watchfulness" and "bureaucracy". B328 carries it better (it has the evidence and the lesson); B344 is a closing caveat to a practical note.

8. B334, B342. Inherited communal life in Africa is falling apart.
- B334 (Sénégal, All is not well in paradise): "Of course, the old communal ways here are changing."
- B342 (Sénégal, Lessons from the Black Tax): "A lot of Africans start out with a communal life they inherited, and watch parts of it fall apart."
Later instance: its first clause is told again. It adds the contrast with Westerners and the point about urban Africans alienated from both city and tradition. B334 carries the erosion (it has the details); B342 is the natural place for the contrast.

9. B343, B344. Learn the local language.
- B343 (Sénégal, Black Tax): "be respectful, contribute something, learn the language"
- B344 (Sénégal, Black Tax): "French and Wolof matter a lot more than English."
Later instance: adds which languages. A light echo only. B343 is the natural place (advice to visitors); B344 has the specifics.

## Deliberate callbacks that rely on something far back (not repeats)
- B338 (Sénégal, Black Tax): "the shared land, work, childcare, food and daily belonging" recalls B321 (Sénégal, opening): "land, childcare, housing, food, obligations". Reads as a callback; adds work and belonging.
- B340 (Sénégal, Black Tax): "In the first stage, where people working outside should contribute an agreed share" needs B83 (Four Parts, 4. The container): "they bring an agreed share of that income in". It also needs the common-purse stage in B84.
- B342 (Sénégal, Black Tax): "Westerners often start out isolated and try to rebuild a tribe." recalls B6 (The New Age May Dawn Suddenly): "human beings need small tribal communities again" and B28 (Communes Are Hot Again): "We evolved to live in small groups where everybody knows everybody."
- B348 (Practice Comes Before Property): "That sounds kind of weird when somebody else is paying the tax bill." leans on the taxes in B85 and B339.

## 2. Contradictions
None a reasonable reader would see. Checked and set aside:
- B321 (communal life is handled by extended family) against B334 and B338 (it is eroding): B321 is limited to "the villages and families I've spent time around", and B334 opens "Of course", so the change is stated.
- B329 (people avoid confrontation over theft, "shadow side") against B330 (the moringa thefts are shrugged off as relationship): B330 reads as an instance, not an opposite.
- B344 (residency "pretty cheap and straightforward") against "bureaucracy" in the same block: personal experience against a general remark.
- B318 ("a strange Western phrase") against B343 ("far from some Western lifestyle niche"): the phrase is Western, the issue is not; B342 bridges them.
- B322 (graffiti "Tolerance! Openness!") against B327 (the law): deliberate irony, resolved by B328.
