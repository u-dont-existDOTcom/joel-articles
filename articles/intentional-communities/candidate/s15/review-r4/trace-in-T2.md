## What This Looks Like From Sénégal H1-P4

CONTEXT BEFORE: And the humor! People are outside laughing for hours at a time. It's the most joking culture I've ever been in, and I keep wondering if I'll ever understand the really complicated jokes in Wolof. Basic conversation is one thing. But knowing why twelve people are suddenly crying with laughter? That's graduate school.

PUBLISHED:

In the villages and families I’ve spent time around, land, childcare, housing, food, and obligation often pass through extended family and local memory alongside formal systems. Nobody needs to announce a workshop called “Rebuilding Intergenerational Belonging.” An aunt has already assigned three people a task.

REWRITE:

In the villages and families I've spent time around, a lot of the things we think we have to "re-invent" to form intentional communities are simply the way things are done. Land, childcare, housing, food, obligations, etc. etc. all get taken care of by the extended family and local memory, in addition to whatever formal systems there might be. Who needs a workshop called "Rebuilding Intergenerational Belonging" when some aunt has already given three people a task?

CONTEXT AFTER: Even in Dakar, I see things like this spray painted on walls: "Tolerance! Openness! Union! Happiness! Love!" And elsewhere there are murals of Sufi leaders. Spiritual graffiti still surprises me! Where I grew up, a city wall was either selling you a product or showing off somebody's inability to draw a penis.
