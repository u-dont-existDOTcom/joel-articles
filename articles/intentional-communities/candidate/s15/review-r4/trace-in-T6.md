## Lessons from the Black Tax H3-P2

CONTEXT BEFORE: Then there's the so-called "Black Tax," a legacy of the old communal system: if you've got any money at all, you're expected to share it with your parents, your siblings, to pay school fees, medical bills, funerals, etc. I understand the instinct. In the ancestral way of doing things nobody made it alone, and the people who fed and raised you had a real claim on whatever you made of yourself later

PUBLISHED:

The modern version can become soul-destroying because the obligation survived while much of the communal life supporting it disappeared. The land, labor, childcare, food, and daily belonging are no longer fully shared, but the person earning cash may still be expected to carry everybody. They struggle to build any independence or long-term stability, while the relatives receiving the money remain dependent because the payments don’t create a viable alternative. The ancestral ethic is still alive inside an individualistic cash economy, where it can keep people alive while quietly consuming both sides.

REWRITE:

The problem is that it can be very soul destroying in today's world. The obligation is still there, but most people no longer live close enough to share land, work, childcare, food etc. and feel part of a larger whole, while whoever earns the cash can still be expected to carry everybody. So it's hard for them to become independent or build any long-term stability. How are they supposed to get ahead? And yet the relatives stay dependent, because the payments don't give them a real alternative. So the ancestral ethic carries on inside the individualistic cash economy, keeping people alive but gnawing at both sides.

CONTEXT AFTER: It's also one of the thorny boundary issues of living without money inside a money-based society. Life inside the commune can go without cash, but you'll still need money for taxes, hospital bills, visas, school fees, family obligations, buying in tools etc. That doesn't mean going back to individuals working for wages and bringing them into the commune, but it does mean someone has to handle it!
