## All is not well in paradise H2-P2

CONTEXT BEFORE: But does this mean I think Sénégal is some magical place that's kept its communal paradise? No! The warmth has hard limits, and for LGBTQ+ people who cross them, it can be brutal.

PUBLISHED:

In March 2026, Sénégal enacted [Law No. 2026-08](https://www.humandignitytrust.org/resources/law-no-2026-08-of-march-27-2026-amending-article-319-of-law-no-65-60-of-july-21-1965/), raising the penalty for consensual same-sex intimacy to five to ten years in prison. The amended article also criminalizes public “apology” for homosexuality and certain financing or support intended to promote it. The law comes within the context of an existing reality of arrests, family rejection, harassment, assault, and mob violence—some say prison actually protects homosexuals from the mob. I don’t want to praise communal belonging while ignoring people who survive that belonging by hiding who they are—or by leaving.

REWRITE:

Sénégal passed a law in March 2026 ([Law No. 2026-08](https://www.humandignitytrust.org/resources/law-no-2026-08-of-march-27-2026-amending-article-319-of-law-no-65-60-of-july-21-1965/)) that increased the penalty for consensual same-sex intimacy to 5 to 10 years in prison, and it also made public "apology" for homosexuality a crime, along with certain financing or support meant to promote it. Homosexuals were already facing arrests, family rejection, harassment, assault and mob violence, but some say they're actually protected once they're arrested, because then at least the mob can't get at them. I can't just praise the virtues of community and ignore the people who have to hide who they are to survive in it, or leave.

CONTEXT AFTER: So which is it? Well, in a way, it's both. It's possible for a society to be incredibly spiritually alive, generous, funny and super communal, while at the same time being extremely cruel to those who break its social categories. It's interesting to see that being in community isn't necessarily liberating for everyone, it depends on whether you're allowed to belong without pretending.
