# Stance check: section 15 rewrite ("What This Looks Like From Sénégal")

Section numbers follow the essay's top-level order (1 intro, 4 Four Parts, 8 Grown Children, 13 Forming Group, 16 Practice Comes Before Property). "Pub." means the published section 15. Confidence is in brackets.

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. Outside wages reversed [high]**
- Rewrite: "That doesn't mean going back to individuals working for wages and bringing them into the commune"
- Essay, sec. 4 (The container): "anyone earning outside contributes an agreed share to the common"; "Later, outside wages and income from community businesses go into a common purse." Sec. 16 repeats this path. Pub. said only "not whether to recreate individual wages inside the commune."
- Reader wrongly believes: the commune should not take in members' outside wages. That is the opposite of the essay's staged path, and of the rewrite's own next paragraph ("some people work outside and contribute an agreed share").

**2. Sénégal's villages already do it all [high]**
- Rewrite: "Land, childcare, housing, food, obligations, etc. etc. all get taken care of by the extended family and local memory"; also "a lot of the things we think we have to 're-invent' ... are simply the way things are done"
- Essay, pub. sec. 15: "often pass through extended family and local memory alongside formal systems"; "no longer fully shared" (Black Tax); "I don't want to turn this into Sénégal as a surviving communal paradise either."
- Reader wrongly believes: the villages fully provide all of this and there is little left to build. "Often" and "alongside formal systems" are gone.

**3. Cause of the Black Tax problem changed [medium]**
- Rewrite: "most people no longer live close enough to share land, work, childcare, food etc."
- Essay, pub. sec. 15 (Black Tax): "much of the communal life supporting it disappeared"; "The land, labor, childcare, food, and daily belonging are no longer fully shared."
- Reader wrongly believes: the problem is physical distance and almost everyone has lost sharing, not that communal life has partly eroded.

**4. Locals' assumption becomes Joel's own claim [medium-low]**
- Rewrite: "often sounds like a strange Western phrase. Don't human beings already do that?"
- Essay: pub. sec. 15: "a strange Western phrase for something people assume human beings already do"; sec. 1: "human beings need small tribal communities again."
- Reader wrongly believes: Joel himself holds that people already live in intentional community, rather than reporting what people there assume.

**5. Added verdict on young people leaving [medium]**
- Rewrite: "(some young men even make the dangerous journey by sea to Europe). And can you blame them?"
- Essay, pub. sec. 15: "Men take dangerous sea routes toward Europe." The essay only describes this. It gives no verdict on migration anywhere.
- Reader wrongly believes: Joel excuses or endorses the departures, the sea route included.

**6. "Never" for a direction of travel [medium-low]**
- Rewrite: "Otherwise the commune will never develop a common purse"
- Essay, pub. sec. 15: "remains a dormitory for the cash economy instead of moving toward a common purse." Sec. 16: "move to a common purse only after it can provision members reliably."
- Reader wrongly believes: draining outside jobs permanently rule out a common purse, rather than stalling the move toward one.

**7. "May" becomes "will" [low]**
- Rewrite: "people will go to pretty funny lengths to avoid confrontation"
- Essay, pub. sec. 15: "People may avoid confrontation so thoroughly that everybody knows who stole the sheep"
- Reader wrongly believes: avoidance is the culture's rule, not something that can happen.

**8. Advice softened [low]**
- Rewrite: "go wanting to contribute, learn some of the language"
- Essay, pub. sec. 15: "Go respectfully, contribute something, learn the language"
- Reader wrongly believes: intending to contribute and learning a few words is enough. The rewrite itself says French and Wolof "matter a lot more than English."

**9. Hedge dropped [low]**
- Rewrite: "different loyalties, different consumption habits etc. etc."
- Essay, pub. sec. 15: "set of loyalties, and often new consumption expectations"
- Reader wrongly believes: outside work always changes how members consume.

## Against the author's stated positions

No rewrite sentence conflicts with any of them. None of the stated positions addresses Sénégal, the Black Tax, outside work, or the money path in this section. Item 1 conflicts with the essay's own text, not with a newer statement.

## Contradictions in the published text

None that a reasonable reader would see in pub. section 15, and none that the rewrite has fixed. Checked and not counted:

- Pub. "The question is not whether to recreate individual wages inside the commune" against sec. 4 "Members may still keep income from outside work." The previous pub. sentence limits the rule to "a money-free commune," so the scope is clear. The rewrite dropped that scope, which is how item 1 arose.
- The "Tolerance! Openness!" graffiti against the anti-LGBTQ+ law. The section names the tension itself: "Community itself isn't automatically liberating."
- "inexpensive and straightforward" residency against "bureaucracy." These are different claims.
- The Black Tax "real claim" on later earnings against sec. 13 "If leaving means forfeiting everything contributed, staying becomes coercive." The section describes an ancestral family system and says "I understand the instinct," not a rule for a new community.
- Zapatista migrants "retaining defined communal obligations" against sec. 8 "leaving should not be made artificially impossible." Negotiated obligations do not bar leaving.

Conflicts in the rewrite: 9. Contradictions in the published text: 0.
