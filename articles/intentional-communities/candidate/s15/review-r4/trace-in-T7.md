## Lessons from the Black Tax H3-P4

CONTEXT BEFORE: It's also one of the thorny boundary issues of living without money inside a money-based society. Life inside the commune can go without cash, but you'll still need money for taxes, hospital bills, visas, school fees, family obligations, buying in tools etc. That doesn't mean going back to individuals working for wages and bringing them into the commune, but it does mean someone has to handle it! 

PUBLISHED:

Outside work complicates that boundary even before a common purse. It does not only bring cash. It brings another schedule, status system, social world, set of loyalties, and often new consumption expectations. During the first stage, an outside earner should contribute an agreed share, but money cannot buy exemption from ordinary communal work or extra political authority. The group also has to notice when outside jobs are draining the people and skills needed to build shared livelihoods. Otherwise the commune remains a dormitory for the cash economy instead of moving toward a common purse.

REWRITE:

Another aspect of outside work is that it's not just money you're bringing into the commune. You're also bringing in a different schedule, a different status system, a different social world, different loyalties, different consumption habits etc. etc. And you can't exactly leave all that at the door, can you? In the first stage, where some people work outside and contribute an agreed share, it's really important that they can't use the money to buy their way out of communal work, or to buy a greater say in the political system. It's also important that outside jobs don't drain the energy and skills out of the people who could be building the communal livelihoods. Otherwise the commune will never develop a common purse, and it will stay a dormitory for the money economy.
