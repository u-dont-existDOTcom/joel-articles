All is not well in paradise

But is this a paradise that still exists? Well no.

In March 2026, Senegal passed a law (Law #2026-08) stiffening the punishment for homosexuality to between 5 and 10 years in jail for “consensual” same-sex intimacy, and also criminalizing the “apology of homosexuality,” which includes certain kinds of financing and support. In addition to that, there’s been a lot of arrests, family rejection, harassment, assault, and occasional mob actions towards homosexuals here. Some people say that, when they’re arrested, they’re actually safe in jail from the mob. I don’t want to praise the communal aspect of life here without acknowledging how difficult it is for those who are homosexual (and who must either hide or leave).

So, in a way, it’s both. It is a very spiritually alive, super-communal, and funny place to be, while also being very cruel towards those who break categories. It’s interesting to see that being in community is not necessarily a way of liberation for everyone.

One other shadow side of our relatively forgiving social culture here is that, to avoid confrontation, people will do some pretty hilarious things. For instance, who is stealing our sheep? We all know, but nobody wants to cause a stink by bringing it up.

On a related note, someone is stealing the pods off of our moringa tree every morning. Anybody could just plant a moringa tree in their own yard if they wanted the highly nutritious pods… but apparently the relationship they have with our tree is more entertaining. 😂

Between the music, the animals, the joking, the praying, and the community, it’s really like living in a different world.

Of course, the old ways here are changing. Young people want to move to the city, get a job, get a phone, eat imported food, be part of the wider world, etc… (some young men even risk their lives by trying to sail to Europe). And can you blame them? As families become less reliant on each other, they become more reliant on cash and individual opportunities.
