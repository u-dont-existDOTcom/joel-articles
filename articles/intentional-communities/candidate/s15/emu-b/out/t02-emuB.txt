All is not well in paradise

But, does this mean that I think Sénégal is this magical place that has preserved it’s communal paradise? No!

Sénégal passed a law in March of 2026, (Law No. 2026-08) increasing the penalty for consensual same-sex intimacy to 5-10 years in prison, and also criminalized “apology” for homosexuality, as well as some financing and support for promoting homosexuality. Homosexuals were already being arrested, rejected by their families, harassed, assaulted, and attacked by mobs, but some say they are actually safer once arrested because at least then they are safe from the mobs. I don’t want to overly extol the virtues of being part of a community and ignore those who have to hide to survive in them, or flee from them.

It is possible for a society to be incredibly spiritually alive, generous, funny, and super communal, while at the same time being extremely cruel to those who break their social categories. Depending on who you are, being part of a community may or may not be liberating.

Even the very forgiving social culture that tries to avoid confrontation has it’s downsides, but it’s often funny. You know who stole your sheep, but nobody wants to make a stink about it.

Also, people steal my Moringa pods every morning. I have no idea who, but they could easily just plant a Moringa tree if they want the pods so badly. Maybe they enjoy the relationship with me too much to give me the satisfaction of knowing who is stealing them. 😂

Almost everyone has sheep (even me!), and they are super cute! It can feel like you’re living in a different world, with the music, the animals, the joking, the praying, and just being around people all the time.

The “old way” is changing, with young people wanting to move to the cities, have jobs, phones, eat “bought” food, and participate in the broader world (some of the guys are even attempting to make the dangerous journey by sea to Europe). Can you blame them? With families that used to share almost everything, now needing money and the possibility of individual opportunity, I don’t think so.
