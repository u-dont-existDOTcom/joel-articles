# Section 14 pairs (T2): published paragraph and rewrite

Section 14 is "Dissolution and Exit". The rewrite keeps the published order; a pair shows all the published paragraphs a rewrite paragraph carries. The rewrite is deliberately loose and spoken; judge what it says, not its polish. Each pair has a DEDUP NOTE: the author asked for repeats to be cut, so check that the cut point is said where the note says instead of reporting it lost.

## Dissolution and Exit P2 → P2

CONTEXT BEFORE: Dissolution is something founders rarely want to talk about while they’re picking a name for their community and looking forward to its first harvest. Who would? But that’s exactly when it needs to be talked about.

PUBLISHED:

If the community ends, what happens to the land, outside debts, buildings, equipment, common stores or funds, and each member’s agreed rights? More immediately: who keeps people housed and fed, gets medications and care to whoever needs them, keeps childcare and schooling going, bridges food, housing, care, outside cash or benefits if relevant, provides transport, maintains records and family contact, takes responsibility for animals and land obligations, and handles unresolved claims?

REWRITE:

If the community dissolves, what happens to the land and any outside debts? What about its buildings, equipment, common stores or funds, and each member’s agreed rights? More immediately, who makes sure everyone still has a place to live and food to eat, and gets their medications and the other care they need? Who keeps childcare going, and the kids’ schooling? And somebody has to take responsibility for bridging any outside cash or benefits people depend on, if relevant, and for transportation, the records, family contact, the animals, land obligations, any unresolved claims, etc. That’s a lot of loose ends!

CONTEXT AFTER: Think of it like a living will, and assign a bridge or successor for each critical function in advance, so basic life doesn’t depend on reviving the commune. The institution may not be salvageable, but who’s making breakfast tomorrow?

DEDUP NOTE: (This rewrite also turns four of the eight questions into one sentence with a list; every item should still be there.) The second question about food, housing and care is cut (the first two questions ask who keeps people housed, fed and cared for); the question keeps its own part, the outside cash or benefits.
