# Repeats check: Dissolution and Exit

The "Dissolution and Exit" section runs from B305 to B312, and its text is in B307 to B312. I checked every block in it against the others in the section and against every other block in the article. This report only covers findings that involve at least one of those blocks. Each block's section is named by the nearest heading above it.

## 1. Repeats

**1. B238, B288, B307: plan the ending at the start**

- B238 (Founderism: The Four Questions): "When joining or forming a commune, we can hopefully be more conscious about the process and less romantic."
- B288 (Resources, Land, and Exit Terms): "Write up the exit terms when it's only theoretical -- like a pre-nup when you're planning the wedding. Not romantic, but protective!"
- B307 (Dissolution and Exit): "looking forward to its first harvest. Who would? But that’s exactly when it needs to be talked about."
- Shared point: settle how things will end while everyone is still hopeful and the ending is only theoretical, even though nobody wants to.
- What the later ones add: B288 adds what B238 only points toward. It gives a concrete instruction (write the exit terms), the pre-nup comparison, and a reason ("Much easier than doing it once everybody's furious."). B307 adds only a change of subject, from one member leaving to the whole community ending. On the timing itself, a reader would feel told the same thing again.
- Better: B288. It gives the instruction, the comparison and the reason together, in the section whose job is exit terms.

**2. B312, B366: the Bruderhof shaped my father's work and my path**

- B312 (Dissolution and Exit): "My father carried the Bruderhof with him long after he left it. It shaped the foundation he started and the film he made."
- B366 (What to Do This Month, the closing lines under Practice Comes Before Property): "The Bruderhof shaped my father. He made a film, founded a research organization, and carried me through communal experiments."
- Shared point: the Bruderhof shaped my father's foundation, his film, and the communities he took me through.
- What the later one adds: only "Decades later I’m publishing the film and writing this", which leads into the point about transmission in B367. Apart from that, near the end of the article a reader would feel told the same thing again, in nearly the same words. B312's own extra is "long after he left it", the part that ties it to leaving.
- More natural place: B366. It closes the frame the article opens with (B2 to B11) and leads straight into B367.

## Callbacks that rely on something sections back

These are listed separately, as asked, and are not counted as repeats.

- B312 (Dissolution and Exit) calls back to the opening, under the title "The New Age May Dawn Suddenly". The opening introduces the film (B2), the Bruderhof (B3), the foundation (B6: "He eventually founded the Aquarian Research Foundation") and the communities (B11: "I spent much of my childhood being carried through"). ## 2. Contradictions

I found no contradictions that involve a block in Dissolution and Exit. These were the closest pairs, and none of them reads as a contradiction:

- B307 against B236 (Founderism: The Four Questions). B236 says a community that forms among friends has "no formal rules until people decide to come together to make them". B307 says dissolution has to be talked about at the founding. But B307's moment is the formal founding, when the community gets a name. In B236's case, that is the point where people decide to formalize.
- B310 against B302 (When Somebody Has to Go). B302 asks "Who will make sure all the evidence is preserved?" when someone is being removed. B310 wants a group to end "without turning every friendship into evidence". One is about removing someone for cause and the other is about a whole group ending, so a reader wouldn't see a clash.
- B312 against B379 (Films, in the appendix). B312 treats the Bruderhof as formative, while B379 notes "the autonomy it requires members to surrender". Both can be true, and the article doesn't set them against each other.

## 3. The repeats a reader would most notice

2. Repeat 1 (B288 and B307): the same advice, given for two kinds of ending.

## Close calls, not counted

Each pair below shares some ground, but in every case the later passage either adds its own step or is a different kind of passage.

- B288 (Resources, Land, and Exit Terms) and B310 (Dissolution and Exit). B310's "transition support they agreed on" recalls B288's "a way of providing people with resources when they leave". Its ending without a fight recalls B288's "once everybody's furious" and B238's divorce that "often ends up in court". But B310's point is new: when the whole group ends, people keep their dignity and can tell a coherent story. B189 (The Door Must Open Outward at Adulthood) makes the support point for grown children, which is a different situation.
- B302 (When Somebody Has to Go) and B308 (Dissolution and Exit). Both list practical needs during a separation, and several items overlap: "(housing, records, family contact, access to common resources, outside funds if any)" and "for the records, for family contact". But B302 is about one person being removed. B308 is about the whole community ending, and it adds food, medication, childcare, schooling, animals, land obligations and unresolved claims.
- B308 and B309 (both Dissolution and Exit). "who’s making breakfast tomorrow?" asks B308's "a place to live and food to eat" question again. But it closes B309's instruction, and it adds that the institution itself may be beyond saving.
- B307 and B309 (both Dissolution and Exit). B309's "Think of it like a living will" and "in advance" repeat B307's timing. B309's new step is naming a stand-in for each critical function.
- B311 and B312 (both Dissolution and Exit). Both show something lasting beyond membership, but different things: former members helping each other, and a community shaping one man's life's work.
- B311 (Dissolution and Exit) and B373 (Films, in the appendix). Both describe American Commune, as "two sisters who grew up on The Farm" and as "The Farm through the eyes of two sisters raised there." B373 is an appendix entry, and it makes a different point: the grown children's view.
- B307 (Dissolution and Exit) and B345 (Practice Comes Before Property). "Decide ownership and exit. Then consider land." also puts exit decisions early, but it is a stated summary of the whole forming sequence.
