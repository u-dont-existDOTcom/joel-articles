# Section 14 pairs (T3): published paragraph and rewrite

Section 14 is "Dissolution and Exit". The rewrite keeps the published order; a pair shows all the published paragraphs a rewrite paragraph carries. The rewrite is deliberately loose and spoken; judge what it says, not its polish. Each pair has a DEDUP NOTE: the author asked for repeats to be cut, so check that the cut point is said where the note says instead of reporting it lost.

## Dissolution and Exit P2 → P2

CONTEXT BEFORE: Dissolution is something founders rarely want to talk about while they’re picking a name for their community and looking forward to its first harvest. Who would? But that’s exactly when it needs to be talked about.

PUBLISHED:

If the community ends, what happens to the land, outside debts, buildings, equipment, common stores or funds, and each member’s agreed rights? More immediately: who keeps people housed and fed, gets medications and care to whoever needs them, keeps childcare and schooling going, bridges food, housing, care, outside cash or benefits if relevant, provides transport, maintains records and family contact, takes responsibility for animals and land obligations, and handles unresolved claims?

REWRITE:

If the community dissolves, what happens to the land and any outside debts? What about its buildings, equipment, common stores or funds, and each member’s agreed rights? More immediately, who makes sure everyone still has a place to live and food to eat, and gets their medications and the other care they need? Who keeps childcare going, and the kids’ schooling? And somebody has to take responsibility for bridging any outside cash or benefits people depend on, if relevant. Also for transportation, and for the records and family contact. Then there’s the animals and land obligations, and any unresolved claims. That’s a lot of loose ends!

CONTEXT AFTER: Think of it like a living will, and assign a bridge or successor for each critical function in advance, so basic life doesn’t depend on reviving the commune. The institution may not be salvageable, but who’s making breakfast tomorrow?

DEDUP NOTE: (The rewrite also says the last items as short statements instead of questions; every item should still be there.) The second question about food, housing and care is cut (the first two questions ask who keeps people housed, fed and cared for); the question keeps its own part, the outside cash or benefits.

## Dissolution and Exit P5+P7 → P5

CONTEXT BEFORE: Dissolution terms are also a way for a group to end without turning every friendship into evidence. Who wants that? People may leave hurt, but they keep the transition support they agreed on, and their dignity. And they can tell a coherent story of what happened.

PUBLISHED:

There is consolation in the fact that institutions and relationships do not die at the same time. *American Commune* follows two sisters raised at The Farm. When one later became ill, former members she had not seen in years reached out with support. The communal economy had changed dramatically; the human bonds had not vanished.

Even after the legal entity ends, some of what people learned to give one another may continue.

REWRITE:

Institutions and relationships don’t die at the same time, and some of what people learned to give one another may go on too. What a relief! The film *American Commune* follows two sisters who grew up on The Farm. When one of them later became ill, former members who hadn’t seen her in years reached out with support. The Farm’s communal economy had changed dramatically, but the bonds hadn’t vanished.

CONTEXT AFTER: My father carried the Bruderhof with him long after he left it. It shaped the foundation he started and the film he made. It shaped the places he took me, too, and the questions I’m still asking (as you may have noticed!).

DEDUP NOTE: The published last paragraph ("Even after the legal entity ends, some of what people learned to give one another may continue.") told P5's opening point again; its own part, what people learned to give one another, is now in P5's first sentence, and the section ends on the father paragraph (CONTEXT AFTER).
