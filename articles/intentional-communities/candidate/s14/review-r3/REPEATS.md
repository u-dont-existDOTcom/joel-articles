# Repeats check, round 3: "Dissolution and Exit"

**Scope.** I read all 410 blocks, B0 to B409. "Dissolution and Exit" is B311 to B319. B311 is the heading and B312 is an image, so its text is B313 to B319. I compared each of those blocks with every other block there and with the rest of the article. Only repeats and contradictions that involve at least one of those blocks are listed. Every quote was copied from the article and checked against its block. Sections are named by their headings.

**Counts.** Repeats: 6 (3 where the later passage tells the reader the same thing again, 3 where it adds something). Contradictions: 0. Callbacks that rely on earlier sections, listed separately: 3. Close calls looked at and not counted: 6 on repeats, 3 on contradictions.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

**1. Unwelcome ending questions should be settled early** (B85, B293, B313)
- B85, section "The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together", subsection "4. The container": "These are less exciting topics to discuss spiritually."
- B293, section "Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community", subsection "Resources, Land, and Exit Terms": "Write the exit terms while departure is still hypothetical."
- B313, section "Dissolution and Exit": "Dissolution is something founders probably don’t want to talk about" and, in the same paragraph, "But that’s exactly when it needs to be talked about."
- Shared point: how things end is an unwelcome topic at the start, so it has to be settled early, before anyone is upset.
- Does the later one add something? Partly. B313 changes the subject from one member leaving (B293) to the whole community ending, and it opens the question list in B314. Its reasoning is the reasoning B85 and B293 already gave. B293 says the terms are "unromantic, protective, and much easier before anybody is furious", and it sets them against a happy beginning (a wedding there, a name and a first harvest in B313). A reader is told that part again.
- Which carries it better? B293, because it gives the reason and the prenup comparison in two sentences. B313 is the natural place for the dissolution version, since its question list follows.

**2. Someone must keep people housed, fed and cared for** (B314, twice inside one block)
- B314, section "Dissolution and Exit": "who is responsible for ensuring that everyone has a place to live and food to eat"
- B314, section "Dissolution and Exit": "Who takes responsibility for bridging gaps in food, housing, care, external income/benefits, etc., if applicable?"
- Shared point: who keeps members housed, fed and cared for once the commune stops providing it.
- Does the later one add something? Almost nothing. It adds "external income/benefits". Two other questions sit between the two (medications and other care; childcare and school), and "care" is named again in the second. The only reading under which the second is new is that it covers gaps in outside support, but the food, housing and care part is the same ask. A reader would feel told the same thing again.
- Which carries it better? Neither clearly. The first is the plainer statement, and B315's "bridge/successor" echoes the second one's "bridging gaps".

**3. When the institution dies, the bonds and the learning go on** (B317, B319)
- B317, section "Dissolution and Exit": "Institutions and the relationships that make them up don’t have to die at the same time."
- B319, section "Dissolution and Exit": "Even if the legal entity dies, some of what people learned to give one another can go on."
- Shared point: the organization can end while what people built with each other continues.
- Does the later one add something? Told again. B319 restates B317's opening sentence as a closing, and each puts a feeling beat right on the point ("What a relief!" in B317, "That gives me hope." in B319). The only addition is "what people learned to give one another" (practice, not only ties). The strongest reading against is that B319 is the lesson drawn from the two examples in B317 and B318, but it is the lesson B317 opened with.
- Which carries it better? B317, because it has the concrete case (the Farm sisters). B319 adds no new case.

**4. American Commune described twice** (B317, B380)
- B317, section "Dissolution and Exit": "American Commune follows two sisters who grew up on The Farm."
- B380, section "Appendix: Watch These, Then Read These", subsection "Films": "The Farm through the eyes of two sisters raised there."
- Shared point: what the film is, two sisters raised on The Farm.
- Does the later one add something? Mostly yes, for an appendix entry: the year, the link, and why the film matters here ("For this article, the grown children’s perspective is essential."). The premise itself is told again. An appendix entry is also where a reader expects a short description, so this is the weakest of the six.
- Which carries it better? They do different jobs. B317 carries the story (the illness and the support), and B380 is the natural place for the citation.

**5. The Bruderhof shaped the father's foundation, film and trips through communes** (B318, B373)
- B318, section "Dissolution and Exit": "It shaped the foundation he started, the film he made, the places he took me" (just before it: "My father carried the Bruderhof with him long after he left it.")
- B373, section "Practice Comes Before Property", subsection "What to Do This Month": "He made a film, founded a research organization, and carried me through communal experiments." (just before it: "The Bruderhof shaped my father.")
- Shared point: the Bruderhof shaped the three things the father later did.
- Does the later one add something? Told again. B373 adds only that the author is now publishing the film ("Decades later I’m publishing the film and writing this."). B318 adds only "and the questions I’m still asking". Both lists restate what B2, B6 and B11 already told (see section 4). The strongest reading against is that B373 is a deliberate return to the opening, but B318 has already given the same recap in the same terms.
- Which carries it better? B373 sits in the more natural place: it is the article's last return to the father, whom the opening sets up, and it leads straight into B374.

**6. What people pass on does not depend on the institution surviving** (B319, B374)
- B319, section "Dissolution and Exit": "Even if the legal entity dies, some of what people learned to give one another can go on."
- B374, section "Practice Comes Before Property", subsection "What to Do This Month": "The transmission does not require one institution, one founder, or one official version."
- Shared point: what is passed on outlives any one institution.
- Does the later one add something? Partly. B374 is about this article's ideas reaching people who build and teach others ("Maybe that community will teach somebody who builds another."), and it adds "one founder, or one official version". The comfort that something outlives the institution is the one B319 already gave. This is the closest of the six to a theme that is being developed rather than a repeat. It counts only if a reader hears B374 as the same comfort again.
- Which carries it better? Each is at home where it is: B319 for a community that ends, B374 for the article's close.

## 2. Contradictions

None that a reasonable reader would see. Three pairs were looked at and not counted:
- Settling things ahead of time against working them out in the moment. B313 ("exactly when it needs to be talked about") and B315 ("assign a bridge/successor for each critical function in advance") are in "Dissolution and Exit". B247 ("It depends on so many factors that I can't decide any of this ahead of time.") and B250 ("Nobody can give that advice ahead of time, including me.") are in "Founderism: The Four Questions", subsection "How My Own Model Could Become a Cult". B247 and B250 are about judgment calls in a particular case (keeping a sick member who doesn't fit, a hostile grandmother). B313 and B315 are about having the process and the responsible people in place. Both can stand. They come closest in B314's "family contact", which asks who takes responsibility, not what the answer is.
- Housing and food for whom. B314 ("who is responsible for ensuring that everyone has a place to live and food to eat") is about existing members when the commune ends. B269 ("A founding group shouldn’t pretend it can give you those.") is in "The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For" and is about a newcomer who arrives in crisis. Different people, different moment.
- Timing. B313 speaks to founders who are already "picking a name" and expecting a harvest. B236, in "Founderism: The Four Questions", says "no formal community and no formal rules until people decide to come together to make them". That is an earlier stage, before there is anything to write rules for.

## 5. Close calls on repeats, looked at and not counted

- Advance planning is asked for in several places. B313 and B315 (Dissolution and Exit), B308 and B310 (section "Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community", subsection "When Somebody Has to Go"), B88 (section "The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together", subsection "4. The container"). B308: "So work out the process before it's about anyone in particular". B310: "Also choose a mediator or ombud before the crisis." B315: "assign a bridge/successor for each critical function in advance". B88: "If one of the four gets skipped, it will need to be dealt with eventually, at high stress". Each asks for advance planning of a different thing (a removal process, a mediator, successors for critical functions, the whole container), so I read it as one theme with a new step each time. Only B293 and B313 are counted (item 1), because they also share the same argument.
- Same checklist items for different events. B308 (subsection "When Somebody Has to Go"): "how housing, access to common resources or outside funds if any, records, and family contact are protected during separation". B314: "Who takes responsibility for transportation, for the records, for family contact, for the animals". Housing, records and family contact come up in both, but for removing one member in one and for the whole commune ending in the other.
- Basic functions in a capacity budget and in the dissolution questions. B279, section "Your Little Forming Group Already Is the Community": "water, housing, food stores, health care, tools, seasonal labor, transport, schooling, market access, outside debts". B314: "Who keeps childcare going and makes sure the kids continue to go to school?" B279 asks whether the group can sustain itself now. B314 asks who covers these if it stops.
- What agreed terms spare people. B316: "People may leave hurt, but they keep the transition support they agreed on, and their dignity." B293 (subsection "Resources, Land, and Exit Terms"): "unromantic, protective, and much easier before anybody is furious". B238, section "Founderism: The Four Questions": "unfortunately often ends up in court". All three say terms agreed in advance spare people the worst version of a split, but B316 names benefits of its own (friendships not turned into evidence, agreed transition support, dignity, a coherent story).
- Three lines that say the institution may die while something continues. B315: "The institution may not be salvageable, but who’s making breakfast tomorrow?" is about daily provisioning. B317 is about relationships. B319 is about what people learned to give. Only B317 and B319 are counted (item 3), because B315 is about something different.
- B316 next to B317. B316: "a way for a group to end without turning every friendship into evidence". B317: "Institutions and the relationships that make them up don’t have to die at the same time." Both are about relationships surviving an ending, but B316 is about how to end (with agreed terms) and B317 is an example of bonds outlasting the institution. I read it as a step forward, not a repeat.
