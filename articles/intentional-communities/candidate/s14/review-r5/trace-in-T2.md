# Section 14 pairs (T2): published paragraph and rewrite

Section 14 is "Dissolution and Exit", seven paragraphs. The rewrite keeps the published order, one rewrite paragraph for each published one. P6 is about the author's own father.

## P5 → P5

CONTEXT BEFORE: Dissolution terms are also a way for a group to end without turning every friendship into evidence. Who wants that? People may leave hurt, but they keep the transition support they agreed on, and their dignity. And they can tell a coherent story of what happened.

PUBLISHED:

There is consolation in the fact that institutions and relationships do not die at the same time. *American Commune* follows two sisters raised at The Farm. When one later became ill, former members she had not seen in years reached out with support. The communal economy had changed dramatically; the human bonds had not vanished.

REWRITE:

Institutions and relationships don’t die at the same time. What a relief! The film *American Commune* follows two sisters who grew up on The Farm. When one of them later became ill, former members who hadn’t seen her in years reached out with support. The communal economy had changed dramatically, but the bonds hadn’t vanished.

CONTEXT AFTER: My father carried the Bruderhof with him long after he left it. It shaped the foundation he started and the film he made. It shaped the places he took me, too, and the questions I’m still asking (as you may have noticed!).
