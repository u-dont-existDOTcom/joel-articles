## P2 → P2

CONTEXT BEFORE: Dissolution is something founders rarely want to talk about while they’re picking a name for their community and looking forward to its first harvest. Who would? But that’s exactly when it needs to be talked about.

PUBLISHED:

If the community ends, what happens to the land, outside debts, buildings, equipment, common stores or funds, and each member’s agreed rights? More immediately: who keeps people housed and fed, gets medications and care to whoever needs them, keeps childcare and schooling going, bridges food, housing, care, outside cash or benefits if relevant, provides transport, maintains records and family contact, takes responsibility for animals and land obligations, and handles unresolved claims?

REWRITE:

If the community dissolves, what happens to the land and any outside debts? What about its buildings, equipment, common stores or funds? What happens to each member’s agreed rights? More immediately, who is responsible for ensuring that everyone has a place to live and food to eat? Who takes responsibility for making sure people get their medications and the other care they need? Who keeps childcare going and makes sure the kids continue to go to school? Who takes responsibility for tiding people over until they’re housed and settled again, including with outside cash or benefits, if relevant? Who takes responsibility for transportation, for the records, for family contact, for the animals, for making sure land obligations are met, and for any unresolved claims? That’s a lot of loose ends!

CONTEXT AFTER: Think of it like a living will, and assign a bridge/successor for each critical function in advance, so basic life doesn’t depend on reviving the commune. The institution may not be salvageable, but who’s making breakfast tomorrow?
