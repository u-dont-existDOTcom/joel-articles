Dissolution and Exit

This is the last thing anyone wants to talk about when they are picking out a name for their new commune, celebrating the imaginings of what will be when the project comes full bloom. But it is crucial to talk about from the start.

What happens when the commune is no more? Who is responsible for the land, debts, buildings, equipment, common stores, common funds, member rights, etc. in a dissolution scenario? More importantly, who will see to it that everyone is housed, fed, gets their medication, care, child care, schooling, etc.? Who will see to it that everyone has bridge support for food, housing, care, outside resources, benefits, etc., if relevant? Transportation, records, family contact, animal responsibility, land obligation, other potential claims, etc.?

As with a living will, it’s best to have someone already chosen to take over key functions of the “body” even if the “body” (the institution) cannot be salvaged.

Having these things in place allows a group to dissolve with the utmost consideration for the people involved, allowing them to leave with the utmost dignity possible, even if they are wounded.

Finally, be consoled by the fact that while institutions and relationships are mortal, they don’t always die at the same time. I have followed the news of two sisters from the Farm over the years. When one recently became ill, even people from the Farm who she hadn’t seen in years reached out to her. Although the economic aspects of the community have changed drastically over the years, the bonds remain.

The Bruderhof has had a similar affect on my life. Though I am no longer a member, it still influences the foundation I started, the film I made, the places I have traveled to, and the questions I still ask.

Some things, even after the legal entity is gone, live on.
