How accurate are the fears about the future? I don't know exactly. Maybe AI will improve the lot of humanity enormously. Maybe millions of people will lose their jobs to AI. Maybe AI will become the administrative layer between humanity and whatever it is we're trying to do. Maybe all of these things will happen before Chantress Seba finishes going through all the applications to her commune. 😂

On a personal level, it makes more sense to me to focus on cultivating more human independence than to wait for certainty. It's better to have community and find out I didn't need an exit than to need an exit after spending ten years optimizing my feed, right?

What connections to the technosphere would you keep, and which would you limit, if you were to design a community?

Medical care, banking, phones, vehicles, schools, supply chains, government ID, internet and energy are each a separate decision. If you opt out of them completely, it can cause needless suffering. If you depend on them completely, the outside system can reach directly into every essential function.

The Amish, over generations, have made community decisions about where and if they want to use a piece of technology based on how it affects their community. Different Amish communities make different decisions. Sometimes they use it at work but restrict it at home. Sometimes they share it instead of everyone owning one. Sometimes they hold off on using it until they know more about its social effects. Whatever else you think of the Amish, they at least understand that adopting a technology is a decision for the group to make. It doesn't just get downloaded onto your device while you sleep.

Medical care is one of the toughest connections to work out. A community may prefer natural care, but what happens if someone in the community needs trauma surgery, antibiotics, dentistry, emergency obstetric care, or any number of specialist services? The community should hash out how people in it are going to have access to care. What kind of care is going to be guaranteed to community members by the community using common resources? What things might require outside dollars or some form of insurance? And how will the community deal with people who choose to accept (or not accept) different levels of medical care? These are all questions that a community should consider and decide on in advance.

No member should have to jump through ideological hoops when they're scared or sick. The community should have pre-existing agreements in place so that if a member gets catastrophically ill, their choices will be protected, they will have access to common resources or external funds if needed, they will be transported if needed, their privacy will be maintained, and there's room for the possibility that the community's preferred methods are not sufficient.

At the same time, having social connections is measurably good for a person's health. The Surgeon General's advisory notes an increased risk for a range of health conditions among people who are socially isolated. Community's not a panacea, but it can make a difference, day to day, in the conditions people get sick, heal, age, and die in.
