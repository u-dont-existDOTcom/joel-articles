Which Connections to the Technosphere Do You Keep?

Loneliness is only one reason this feels urgent. I wrote separately about the wider polycrisis:

I don’t know exactly how much of the future fear is accurate. AI may improve life enormously, destroy millions of jobs, become the administrative layer between us and nearly everything, or do all three before Chantress Seba gets through her commune applications.

I’d rather build more human independence than wait for certainty. I’d rather have community and discover I didn’t need an exit than need an exit after spending ten years optimizing my feed.

The useful design question is: which connections to the technosphere do you keep, and which do you limit?

Medical care, banking, phones, vehicles, schools, supply chains, government identification, internet access, and energy are separate decisions. Full rejection can create needless suffering. Full dependence means the outside system can reach directly into every essential function.

The Amish have spent generations evaluating technologies according to their effects on community life. Different groups reach different answers. A tool may be accepted at work and restricted at home, shared rather than individually owned, or delayed until its social effect becomes clearer. Whatever else you think of the Amish, they at least understand that adoption is a communal decision rather than an automatic software update.
