# Romance Advice I Wish My Parents Taught Me: two sections

### Spirituality to bypass intimacy

I’ve used spirituality as a replacement for intimacy a lot because intimacy was simply unavailable. As a boy and young man, I was so deprived of female touch that it was driving me insane. Meditation sometimes helped me cope better with the deprivation, but it wasn’t a cure. I remember once as I was entering into an ayahuasca ego death, I said, “Actually, I really just want a girlfriend.” Right then, the crazy trip abruptly ended, and a voice said, “Ok then go find one.” Nobody believes this story when I tell it.

I don’t believe most people can be truly happy celibate. People generally aren’t so serious about spiritual liberation that giving up sex and partnership for life makes sense. The Buddha didn’t advise everyone to become monastics.

If becoming a monastic genuinely makes you happier and more peaceful, stay. It might work for some very advanced people, or if your sexual desire is already low, or you’re old, etc.

But how do you know what you’re not aware of? Inner-child reparenting and self-hypnosis can help a lot with that, because often the inner child is being ignored while the adult is feeling some kind of liberation.

And if you’re so happy and free, what happens when you’re alone in a room with a super beautiful girl and she wants you? Or vice versa for women. Do you feel like you’re losing some control? Are you afraid?

Bhante Gunaratana, a famous Buddhist teacher, tells a story like this in his autobiography. A super beautiful girl at a retreat he was teaching snuck into his room and waited for him in bed, naked. He went nuts and started screaming at her “Get out!” at the top of his lungs. Obviously he was still repressing something, and she was adept at unintentionally uncovering it.

Robes do nothing. I was mildly molested in public by the abbot of one of Thailand’s largest Buddhist retreat centers. In Thailand, parents have very good reasons not to leave children alone with monks. Tibet is worse.

Forced celibacy doesn’t abolish anyone’s sexuality. For most people it eventually sends it somewhere weird. Why wouldn’t it?

There are practical reasons to become a monastic too. In a poor country it can mean free food, a place to live, status, and escape from ordinary responsibilities. In the West, the free food may matter less, but a person who received no particular respect as a layman can become Venerable Somebody and suddenly people bow to him.

Of course, he may sincerely want liberation, too. People can want several things at once.

## Can making love be a spiritual practice?

I’m Buddhist, and the early Buddhist texts don’t teach tantric sex as a path to awakening. But they also don’t show serious awakening as something reserved for monastics.

In DN 16, the Buddha talks about people who had died in the town of Nādikā and mentions 510 lay stream-enterers, 96 once-returners, and more than fifty lay non-returners. Those are the first three of the four stages of awakening.

SN 55.24 has an almost comic complaint. When the Buddha declared Sarakāni a stream-enterer, other Sakyans grumbled, in effect, “Who isn’t a stream-enterer these days?”

And when the married couple Nakulapitā and Nakulamātā asked the Buddha how they could remain together in future lives, AN 4.55 gave this advice:

“When both husband and wife are faithful and generous, restraintful, living righteously, speaking pleasant words to each other—they come together in the future, sharing the same desires and living in bliss.”

Marriage, sex, and household life are not automatically on the opposite side from serious spiritual development.

Kim Anami goes further and treats sexual energy itself as a path into healing, creativity, worldly action, and spiritual awakening.

She has also collected a ton of stories from students who say the effects spill out of sex into health, work, money, creativity, the rest of life. Her jade-egg practice is a preliminary training for the cervical O: pelvic-floor strength, attention, and arousal without needing a partner.

Brad and Pam Keene are the example I really like. Pam says her Kundalini process completed while they were making love and opened into a lasting universal-consciousness awakening. She later helped Brad awaken too, so the marriage itself became part of the path.

In the previous section, I mentioned the divine feminine and divine masculine energies that you can channel to keep eroticism alive. But there’s really something more special about the in-love paradise that Twin Flames (TFs) experience than a simple Divine Masculine + Divine Feminine formula. Maybe a better way to put it, as my friend Cody says, is Yin/Yang, which still has the masculine/feminine in it, but allows for a more broad type of complementary symbiosis since, each contains the seed of the other.
