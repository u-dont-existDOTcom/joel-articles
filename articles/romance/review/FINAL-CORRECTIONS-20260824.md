## C. Factual corrections required before publication

### C1 — AN 4.55 is supported, but the displayed blockquote is not an exact Bodhi quotation

Current blockquote:

> `"When both husband and wife are faithful and generous, restraintful, living righteously, speaking pleasant words to each other—they come together in the future, sharing the same desires and living in bliss."`

Evidence: AN 4.55 does have Nakulapitā and Nakulamātā ask how to remain together in future lives. The Buddha tells them that husband and wife matched in faith, ethical conduct, generosity, and wisdom can be reunited after death. The present block is a synthesis, not an exact Bhikkhu Bodhi quotation.

Recommended publication repair: remove quotation marks/block formatting and paraphrase directly, e.g.:

`AN 4.55 tells them that if husband and wife are matched in faith, ethical conduct, generosity, and wisdom, they can meet again in a future life.`

Source: https://suttacentral.net/an4.55/en/bodhi

### C2 — broad negative about the Buddha recommending monasticism is not established

Current:

`The Buddha didn’t generally advise people to become monastics.`

The sources used in this article establish something narrower: early Buddhist texts give substantial teachings to householders and describe lay followers reaching high stages of awakening. They do not establish the broad negative `didn't generally advise` claim.

Recommended realization:

`The early Buddhist texts don't treat household life as spiritually meaningless either. They give substantial teachings to lay people and describe lay followers reaching high stages of awakening.`

This fits the DN 16 / SN 55.24 / AN 4.55 evidence already used later.

### C3 — Victorian-origin claim is historically contradicted

Current:

`Where did the idea of promising to have romantic interest in your partner come from, anyway? It turns out it never existed in any culture I'm aware of until the Victorian age, when romantic feelings were elevated in England to the status of immutable truth.`

Problem: companionate marriage and the ideal of marrying for love were already developing in England by the eighteenth century, before the Victorian period. The stronger `never existed in any culture` claim is not supportable.

Recommended factual skeleton for Joel to re-realize:

`The modern ideal of marrying for romantic love was already growing in England by the eighteenth century, before the Victorians. What still seems strange to me is turning a feeling into something a vow is supposed to guarantee forever.`

Key research: companionate marriage predates the Victorian era; DOI `10.1075/cilt.358.12gar` and related Oxford histories.

Owner decision needed on exact voice because this corrects a historical premise while preserving the vows argument.

### C4 — agriculture/property/Industrial-Revolution monogamy story needs rebuilding

Current core:

`Sexclusivity started gaining sway around the time we started planting carrots and peas, and owning land... By the Industrial Revolution, this ironically bureaucratic basis of romance became the only definition of marriage, by law.`

What survives the research:

- humans form strong pair bonds but do not map neatly onto perfect sexual monogamy;
- marriage systems are extremely variable;
- property, inheritance, subsistence, and ecology matter;
- some intensive agricultural/property systems favored socially imposed monogamy through inheritance incentives;
- other agricultural systems supported or intensified polygyny;
- England criminalized bigamy in **1604**, long before the Industrial Revolution.

So neither `agriculture → sexclusivity` as one direction nor the Industrial-Revolution legal date is supportable.

Suggested factually safe skeleton, not proposed as final Joel voice:

`It's hard to find sexually monogamous animals, have you ever looked? Humans don't fit neatly into one mating system either. We form strong pair bonds, but societies have varied enormously in what they allow sexually and matrimonially. Property and inheritance helped shape marriage rules, but not in one direction: some intensive agricultural systems favored socially imposed monogamy, while others supported polygyny. And England was criminalizing bigamy in 1604, long before the Industrial Revolution.`

Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3013474/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3260845/ ; PMID 20069721; legal history of the Bigamy Act 1604.

Owner rewrite strongly preferred because the current paragraph is rhetorically important and the factual correction is substantial.

### C6 — stepchild/family-instability sentence is directionally plausible but too causal and general

Current:

`When children have parental figures come and go, it can damage their ability to have good relationships as adults.`

Research does associate family instability and repeated transitions with later outcomes, including relationship/union patterns, but effects vary by transition, family, selection, and outcome. The evidence does not justify the current broad causal wording as a general rule.

Two clean options:

1. Narrow it: `Repeated changes in parental figures can affect children later too, including how they form relationships as adults.`
2. Delete the empirical generalization and let the moral point stand on its own: if you've acted as a parent for years, the child's bond is not disposable when the romance ends.

Owner choice required because the second option is a substantive deletion even though the protected child-continuity function survives.

## D. Precision/source improvements that do not currently block publication by themselves

### D1 — cervical/vagus evidence

The Komisaruk/Whipple core is supported: women with complete spinal-cord injury could still experience orgasm during vaginocervical stimulation, and brain imaging supported a vagal route bypassing the spinal cord. `That laboratory evidence establishes the uniqueness of the phenomenon` overstates what the small studies establish.

Safer realization:

`Komisaruk and Whipple found that women with complete spinal-cord injuries could still experience orgasm from vaginocervical stimulation, with brain imaging consistent with a vagal pathway that bypasses the spinal cord.`

Keep Anami/Richardson's broader afterglow/healing claims explicitly attributed; the lab studies do not establish those claims.

### D2 — Somé/Dagara provenance

The source supports **Somé's account** of her Dagara community/tradition. It is safer to write `Somé describes marriage in her Dagara community...` rather than present her account as independent ethnographic consensus for all Dagara people.

### D3 — state-dependent learning

State-dependent memory is real. Applying it to psychedelic relationship insight is a reasonable inference, not direct proof that altered-state insights fail to transfer without integration. Keep the mechanism/application distinction local if this paragraph is edited.

### D4 — loneliness

Population evidence strongly associates loneliness/social isolation with higher mortality. `Loneliness kills` is defensible as rhetorical compression, but it should not be treated as individual causal proof.

### D5 — pair-bond neurochemistry

Oxytocin, dopamine, and vasopressin are involved in bonding, but human evidence is less deterministic than the classic animal pair-bond literature. The current sentence should not be expanded into a universal `sex causes attachment` rule.
