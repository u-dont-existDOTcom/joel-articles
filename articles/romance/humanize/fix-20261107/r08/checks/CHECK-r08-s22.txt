Often times the breakup is hard, but the aftermath is worse. Public demonization should be avoided, but I don’t think that means hiding the truth about the relationship to save face. Sometimes the truth is that you were abused, coerced, or seriously deceived. How much you say depends on what happened and why you’re telling people, and so does who you tell and how publicly.

Once you’re out, you can see things you genuinely couldn’t see while you were bonded. Look at what you contributed, but also try to understand whatever was true in your ex’s perspective, including the conflicts they were carrying inside themselves, so you don’t flatten them into a villain.

It might feel like they were fake all along, since that would explain everything. But check whether that’s really the simplest explanation before you settle on it. It helps to ask people who aren’t invested in seeing you as innocent, because a therapist, a pastor, or even a stranger may see the evidence more clearly than a lifelong friend. Even the curiosity itself can be therapeutic for you.

Spiritual practice during an ending is critical, but don’t use it to get around the feelings. Pray for your ex’s wellbeing. Remember what was real and good, and don’t use your pain as permission to hate them. Avoid the New Age belief that everyone is simply a mirror of you. No, that isn’t true. We partly mirror one another, but each person was formed by plenty that had nothing to do with you. No matter how enlightened the Buddha was, his cousin Devadatta still kept trying to kill him. Looking honestly at your part doesn’t erase theirs. Sometimes it helps to ask, “What would Mr. Rogers do?” (if you’re old enough to remember that show).

I also learned that public demonization doesn’t always have to be the last chapter. If you avoid contact forever, they might never apologize and learn honesty. But it can help to keep distance for a while until cooler heads prevail. It can be tempting to let them back into your life after a private apology, and especially after a public one, but keep in mind you deserve a stable love. You’re not a yo-yo.
