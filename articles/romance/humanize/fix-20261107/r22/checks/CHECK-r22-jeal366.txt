I do believe two people can choose sexual exclusivity honestly. They can admit that attraction to other people exists and decide not to act on it each day.

I’d rather be able to say “she’s cute” about a woman at the next table, and have my girlfriend say the same about some guy, without it turning into a fight. If only the serious crushes get mentioned, every mention turns into a confession, so people end up hiding all of them.

Plenty of couples vow to be together forever with nobody else, and far fewer actually live that way. In a polyamorous relationship the boundaries might be more negotiable, but then somebody has to sit down and negotiate them, down to who gets to stay the night.

Polyamory can be dishonest too: a lot of the time, I think people use it to cope because they can’t connect deeply with one person, or to say “I can’t help myself” out loud without necessarily doing the inner work that would make the arrangement ethical. If you can’t do monogamy, it’s more honest to say so, since pretending sometimes does more damage than an affair would have.

I learned most of this by screwing it up. Some of it I just thought through, and I’m still not sure about all of it.
