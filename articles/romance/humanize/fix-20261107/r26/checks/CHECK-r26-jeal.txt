These days, even the brief inkling of attraction to another person becomes almost a sure sign that sexual infidelity is next. That’s why I felt I had no choice but to address the issue head on when B. wanted to marry me. I told her, “I can’t fully commit to you if I’m still attracted to other women, so before I do, let’s see if I can fix that.”

I meditated for a while and surprised my own self to find something like a switch in my mind. It felt like evolutionary programming around whether I had found my life mate and true love. I asked myself, “Is she the one?”

The inner Magic 8 Ball came back YES, and I flipped off the mate-finding switch.

It worked about 99% of the time. I was no longer interested in other women, aside from one extremely beautiful girl who grabbed my attention once.

That amazed me, although B. never quite believed the switch was real. She even started hallucinating that I was looking at or talking to other women when I wasn’t. So her jealousy was not simply tracking what I was doing in the present. More likely it was insecure attachment from childhood trying to justify its continued existence.

Jealousy is always insecurity, but sometimes that insecurity can be an important indicator that the relationship actually is not secure, rather than just a remembered script from childhood. Although, either way, it can become a self-fulfilling prophecy.

Instead of stewing in jealousy and throwing out microaggressions now and then, speak your heart without hiding the insecurity behind anger, as in the above video I found on IG. In a monogamous relationship, you might say:

“I notice you pay attention to this other girl more than me, and it makes me feel unloved and insecure. Can we talk about it? Do you really want to explore things with her? If so that’s fine, but don’t make the decision lightly, because it’s irreversible.”

Maybe he really is neglecting you or wanting to be with someone else. Maybe the “attention” was one glance and your baseline insecurity inflated it into a threat that it wasn’t. That part should not be projected onto your partner. Every couple has to work out for themselves what actually feels healthy.

I do believe two people can choose sexual exclusivity honestly. They can admit that attraction to other people exists and decide not to act on it each day.

For that to stay honest, I’d rather we could both mention attractions to other people casually, like saying “she’s cute” about a woman at the next table. If only the serious ones ever get mentioned, then any mention sounds serious, and people stop mentioning them at all.

Lots of couples promise forever and nobody else, and far fewer actually live that way, so a lot of them end up pretending. Polyamory leaves those rules open, so the boundaries have to be negotiated out loud, down to who gets to stay the night.

Polyamory can be dishonest too, though, like when someone who can’t get close to any one person dates four at once and calls it polyamory. Or when someone who can’t stop cheating just renames it polyamory, without necessarily doing the work on themselves that would make it ethical. Still, if you can’t do monogamy, it’s more honest to say so up front than to promise it and pretend, since pretending can do more damage than an affair would have.

I learned most of this by screwing it up. Some of it I just thought through, and I’m still not sure about all of it.
