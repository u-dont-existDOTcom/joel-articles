# Two Pillars Don’t Hold The Roof Up

Even if I found my twin flame, she’d still be one person. The polarity could be perfect and we’d still need other people around us.

Here’s how this looks for me: whenever something serious comes up in my relationships, we each run to our own friends for support. Then I resent the one-sided story she’s telling, she resents the one-sided story I’m telling. Some of the friends may even be exes who want us to break up, and eventually neither of us wants to talk to the other’s friends anymore. That’s a pretty terrible time to be trying to run the rest of life with two people. [[MEDIUM: If one person goes down, the other becomes the whole backup system for housing, money, children, health, and emotional crises.]] [[MEDIUM: Who even has time for the relationship itself at that point?]]

[[MEDIUM: Maybe an unusually strong couple can get away without much outside support, but I think that’s rare.]] [[MEDIUM: Often, a friend who actually knows us both sees the dysfunctional pattern before either of us does.]] On the other hand, If both people are falling apart, there is only so much anyone else can do.

Separate confidants can be helpful and harmful. [[MEDIUM: Sometimes you need privacy from your partner and somewhere else to say what you’re feeling.]] But if your confidant doesn’t have a good connection with your partner, the conversation can turn into an echo chamber very easily.

Having the same confidant creates the opposite problem: now you may not have a release valve when you want privacy from your partner, so neither way is perfect.

If you do have separate confidants, you need enough emotional maturity to remember they will automatically be biased toward you. [[MEDIUM: They’re only hearing your side, usually while you’re upset, and they may just be trying to protect you, so take what they say with a grain of salt.]]
