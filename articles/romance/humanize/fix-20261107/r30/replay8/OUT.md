# Two Pillars Don’t Hold The Roof Up

Even if I found my twin flame, she’d still be one person. The polarity could be perfect and we’d still need other people around us. Maybe an unusually strong couple can get away without much outside support, but I think that’s rare. If one person goes down, the other becomes the whole backup system for housing, money, children, health, and emotional crises.

Separate confidants can be helpful and harmful. Sometimes you need privacy from your partner and somewhere else to say what you’re feeling. But if your confidant doesn’t have a good connection with your partner, the conversation can turn into an echo chamber very easily. Whenever something serious comes up in my relationships, we each run to our own friends for support. Then I resent the one-sided story she’s telling, she resents the one-sided story I’m telling. Some of the friends may even be exes who want us to break up, and eventually neither of us wants to talk to the other’s friends anymore.

So if you do have separate confidants, you need enough emotional maturity to remember they’re only hearing your side, usually while you’re upset, and they may just be trying to protect you, so take what they say with a grain of salt.

Often, a friend who actually knows us both sees the dysfunctional pattern before either of us does. But with the same confidant you may not have a release valve when you want privacy from your partner, so neither way is perfect. And if both people are falling apart, there is only so much anyone else can do.
