# Doing it consciously 🧭

B397: I learned most of this by screwing it up. Some of it I just thought through, and I'm still not sure about all of it.

## Imagination as a discernment tool

B399: We all fantasize about a new exciting person we just met, right? The useful thing about the fantasy is that it shows me what I want. But I usually make the mistake of playing out just the first part of the fantasy. So I might start with the regular lovey sexy stuff, and that’s fine because that’s what’s drawing me in. Then after that, when I have a bit of wisdom available, I continue onto the mundane and difficult scenes. I’m not trying to force anything or oppose anything. I just try to open my eyes when they want to close, ignore, look away. Here’s my checklist:

B400: - What’s it feel like after we have sex? Do we feel comfortable and happy about that, or do we feel guilty that we gave in to temptation too quickly and now there’s some weird feelings? Name the feelings and explore.

B401: - How about a week into the relationship? What’s starting to get annoying perhaps? What are we trying not to say and not to notice?

B402: - Get in the ring before the ring gets on you. Let’s imagine we have a real fight about money, jealousy, or something else important. What do we do to handle the conflict?

B403: - Fast forward. Now we’ve got kids. What might be the values conflicts in how we raise them? Schools, screen time, vaxxes, religion, helicoptering vs freedom, food, gender & sexual orientation issues.

B404: - Rewind. What if we disagree on having kids?

B405: - Fast forward five years. What has each of us gained or given up, and do we have any resentment about it?

B407: This isn’t to make predictions and we’re not consulting the magic 8 ball nor doing Istikhara rituals here. I mean you can do that too if you want. Distinguish between fears and what really seems likely just as you do between wishes and reality.

B408: If every scene is beautiful, consider that you may be, and almost certainly are, still rehearsing the relationship you want. There’s nothing wrong with expecting the best, but it takes work to get there in a wise way.

B409: Psychedelics can make this same problem of idealization much stronger.

## Psychedelic Timing

B411: This section will be relevant to some readers and easy for others to skip.

B412: Before I say anything good about psychedelics for relationships, the warning has to come first. I don’t think sexual consent should be negotiated for the first time while high. The drug is changing how safe, loving, open, and connected everything feels. Advance planning doesn’t turn getting high and starting a relationship into a safe procedure. People need that warning before they take it, because once you’re inside that feeling, sober caveats may sound ridiculous.

B413: Starting a relationship through psychedelics has another danger. Key and I met and then spent a few days taking iboga with friends. We got to know each other very deeply that way, and that was great. But it was especially her higher self I was getting to know. I tend to idealize people even sober, and that gets worse when I’m high. It was still beautiful to see the real higher self potential in her, I’m not denying that. But that information didn’t help me understand how we would get along in normal life.

B414: I’ve also had friends get together at MDMA parties and stuff. It’s rarely a good idea. The intimacy can be completely real without telling you whether the two of you actually work together sober. Or you might even sometimes have the opposite problem, like I had with H.D., where the connection continues to deepen while sober, and it becomes something that one person might cling to desperately, never imagining they could find that again.

B415: The obvious advice is to wait before making large promises and spend time together sober, around other people, and in ordinary situations. Write down what each of you learned, do some peer counseling, check with friends to see if you’re too pie-in-the-sky. But the problem is, you won’t necessarily be interested in sober advice when you’re high. You’re “beyond all that.” And the high can continue after sobriety because intimacy itself is like rose-colored glasses.

### Returning to the heart to get unstuck

B417: Psychedelics, especially certain types of shrooms, very pure LSD, very pure therapeutic-grade MDMA, ayahuasca, loveyhuasca haoma, and iboga, can be extremely useful when a couple is trapped in defensive stories.

B418: With Bee and Key, there were many times when we could argue forever sober, then take something and finally understand each other. It could feel like we were telepathically synced. Instead of each person defending their version, we directly understood what the other was perceiving, feeling, misunderstanding.

B419: But the problem is most people are lazy about integrating what they learn during this window of clarity. We enjoyed the understanding while it lasted, and then the same defensive story would resurface later. In neuroscience, this is called state-dependent learning. What you learn in altered states doesn’t automatically translate into your normal sober mind state without the work to do that.

### Measuring the benefits

B421: Probably one of the most important benefits we can hope to get long term from any therapy is the ability to express what we feel in a non-combative way. Otherwise, people tend to ping pong between not expressing or downplaying their feelings and expressing them combatively. MDMA can make this temporarily much easier because all the problems can look so small and silly. The question is what happens when you’re sober again.

B422: So I would say the therapy worked if a week or two later, you and your partner can comfortably say, “Honey, I’m feeling unappreciated right now. I know you’re not trying to make me feel that way, and maybe you feel like I’m making you feel that way, but I’d like us to take some time soon to discuss the issue beneath this in a kinder way. What do you say?”

B423: If you don’t have a partner, you can do the same thing with whoever you tend to have trouble relating with, in a less lovey way but still with compassion.

B424: It’s not always possible in that moment to change a defensive posture into non-defensive loving communication. That’s why I suggest setting a time soon with a nice intention, like a ritual. Sober is fine. You can put some rose petals around you or something to build the feeling, or play some song you both like.

## Why all of this sounds artificial

B426: By now this probably sounds terribly artificial. Is anyone actually going to do scheduled check-ins, delayed cohabitation, conversations about hypothetical disasters, imagination exercises, not to mention resisting the heat-of-the-moment stuff and continuing your own reparenting practices when a warm body is available to do some of the soothing?

B427: Well, yeah. It is painfully artificial. In a healthy communal society, the couple would have access to witnesses, elders, friends, and other emotional outlets and resources. Most of us these days don’t have that, so we’re trying to recreate consciously and clumsily some of what the wider field would have done without a checklist.

# If you’re already in it

B429: Most of this article is about what to do before jumping head first into head over shoulders. But if you’re already married with children, advice about slowing down arrives a little late.

B430: I wish I had a special method for fixing that. Mostly I learned the obvious thing the hard way: one person can’t fix the relationship (unless they’re the main problem in it).

B431: With Bee and Key, the biggest thing sitting underneath everything was trust. They wanted to be with me and would say they trusted me, but really they didn’t trust me, themself, or anyone else. It was all or nothing: blind trust, or none. I kept saying, “It’s not possible to have a relationship without trust.” I think that rigidity came from trauma and never really growing up.

B432 [HIS NEW VERSION, 22 Nov: read it as context, don't report it]: Even dishonesty was sometimes hard for me to define in these relationships. Bee once opened a door and, a second later, said she hadn’t opened it. That drove me crazy! I mean, why would she lie like that? On the other hand, although she was technically lying, there was no strategy behind it, and she was usually quite honest. Non-strategic lying is literally diagnosed as "pathological lying," so I should have seen it more like an illness than a failure of virtue.

B433: Some things were less mysterious: they were acting as if the trust problem wasn’t there because they still wanted the relationship. Attractions to other people sometimes went unsaid too, and sometimes got imagined from nowhere. Some people keep a relationship like this going for a long time, just sweeping everything under the rug.

B434: On the other hand, sometimes I was more honest than my partner could handle, which was its own kind of damage. Ask whether both of you actually want to prioritize radical honesty, first. But the other person also can’t keep saying “not now” forever and think that’s going to save the relationship.

B435: This is the time for agape to shine, because the best thing for both of you may not be holding onto the relationship pattern you originally committed to or dreamt of.

B436: When did you two last dance? And not the “we dance around our problems” joke (LOL).. If that’s where things are at, the trust is out the window. Ouch. I know that one from experience with my first 2 wives and K, too. That generally marks the point of no return, so try not to wait for that. Because once you’re there, as my dad explained a thousand times whenever we had a visitor, unconscious resentment begins to snowball, and we feel colder together than alone. Pretty soon, old Romeo & Juliette might get wandering eye syndrome.

## Outside help can sometimes break the loop fast

B438: Bee and I were once desperately misunderstanding each other, so we called my friend Eshwar, the only friend of mine whom she respected. He told a related story about something that had happened with his sister. Somehow, Bee immediately understood what I had been trying to say. It was like magic.

B439: So, don’t think of outside help as a formal last resort. I think of it as a part of building community when we learn to rely on each other for this kind of intimate, real-life support. It could be calling up a mutual friend, peer counselor, couples therapist, doing Imago dialogue, Restorative Circle, and/or joining a men’s or women’s group. The key is that both people need to respect the person or process enough to hear something new.

B440: What they say is also partly true: “The only one you can change is yourself.” Not fully true, because we do have influence, and changing ourself does ripple out. Restart whatever you stopped doing because the relationship was soothing you: inner-child reparenting, meditation, body practices, peer counseling.

## What will honesty produce?

B442: Many people ask this question of honesty, but it’s unfair. Honesty doesn’t produce a certain outcome. It is its own reward. It might repair the relationship, open it, turn the romance into friendship, preserve the family through separation, or show you that ending it is better. Temporary separation helped Bee and I. It also became more permanent, although I’ve always thought maybe we would get back together in a few decades. If the other person won’t look, you can’t make it mutual. Then you have to decide what you can live with.

# Children

B444: I think everyone generally agrees that parents have more responsibility to children than they do to each other, since children can’t take care of themselves.

B445: Before having or adopting children, sit down for an imagination session to figure out whether you two lovebirds can preserve some civil co-parenting capacity after a terrible breakup. That’s the actual long-term commitment.

B446: When it comes to stepchildren, even if they aren’t technically your children, they still become attached. If you’ve been acting as their parent for years, it’s not right to vanish from their life just because the marriage ended. You don’t have legal parental rights, but you can stay a mentor or close friend, and hopefully your partner would agree with this. When children have parental figures come and go, it can damage their ability to have securely attached relationships as adults.

B447: It’s standard advice to never recruit children into the adult war. They get their own relationship with the other parent. However, this was particularly difficult during my first marriage, because Ann was very much unfit at the time to care for our son, Bear. I did fight for custody, but, as my lawyer told me, there was no point, because men generally lose. Eventually I exhausted my options for fighting, and I was left with weekly supervised visits.

B448: Ann was brainwashing Bear into believing that I was “breaking his bones” at these visits even though he didn’t know what bones were. That’s when I told him I had to stop the visits before I end up in jail. So it was for my protection, but his as well, because if he realizes one day his dad is in jail because his mom manipulated him into telling a dangerous story, that could really wreck him.

B449: As it is now, he may miss me, but he is well cared for (I think) by his grandmother, and probably soon he’ll seek me out when he gets his own phone.

B450: I know I sound like a broken record, like my dad, but still: It takes a village. The nuclear family, built on parents’ romance, can often be the worst arrangement for children. If there’s trouble in paradise, parents will try to protect the kids by hiding their tension. But the truth is, children absorb that unresolved tension and integrate it into their very being. In a community, romance is one bond among several, and adult stability for children doesn’t rise and fall with it.

B453: Whether to leave and how to leave are separate questions.

B454: You don’t owe it to them to try counseling unless you also owe it to yourself. If you actually don’t want a good relationship with them anymore, go. No amount of counseling will fix something that has nothing at the base of it.

B455: If you love them and wish it could work, ask if they feel the same, and then go to counseling. On the other hand, even when you love each other, sometimes you already know the issue is beyond what counseling could fix.

### Gaslighting in the dark

B457: Do you have irreconcilable memories of key events? I did with both my first and second wives. I took my first wife, Ann, to two counselors. The male counselor eventually told us, “I’ve never told anyone this, but you guys need to split up for at least six months.”

B458: There’s no way to resolve a situation where one person remembers that you ruined her life on purpose, while you remember that she was having a manic episode and had to be committed for her own safety. We also didn’t have mutual friends who could corroborate what happened—only friends on each side who were partial.

B459: Many say it’s best to save face and work things out privately, but I’d disagree. If you don’t have friends who can help with the conflict, then you’re really stuck (and you might wanna find some new friends). But do it honestly, with the goal of helping the conflict, not just getting approval for your own interpretation. Both people should get a chance to speak, and good friends should hear both sides before validating either.

B460: I can believe a friend’s experience and still tell them that my ability to know what is really going on is extremely limited. If someone says, “I don’t have all the evidence,” I trust their thinking more. Living in community would help because your friends would not only hear two stories afterward. Sometimes they would actually see how the two of you treat each other when you argue.

B461: If you’ve been there for someone, don’t abandon them in a crisis unless it’s unsafe for you to stay or they’re creating the crisis to keep you, or they aren’t willing to help themself. You may need to arrange other care, but that’s no reason to pretend the romance still has a future.

## After leaving

B463 [HIS NEW VERSION, 22 Nov: read it as context, don't report it]: Often times the breakup is hard, but the aftermath is worse. Public demonization should be avoided, but I don’t think that means hiding the truth about the relationship to save face. Sometimes, the truth is that you were abused, coerced, or seriously deceived. So how much you say, and to whom, really depends on what happened and why you’re telling people.

B464 [HIS NEW VERSION, 22 Nov: read it as context, don't report it]: Once you’re out, you can see things you genuinely couldn’t see while you were bonded. Look at what you contributed to how things turned out. And don’t paint your ex as a cartoon villain: try to understand whatever was true in their perspective too, including the conflicts they were carrying inside themselves.

B465: Sometimes the easiest story is that they were fake all along, because it explains everything. Grab it only if it really is the simplest explanation of what happened. People who aren’t heavily invested in seeing your innocence can help you tell, and a therapist, pastor, or even a stranger may see the evidence more clearly than a lifelong friend. Even the curiosity itself can be therapeutic for you.

B466 [HIS NEW VERSION, 22 Nov: read it as context, don't report it]: Spiritual practice during an ending is critical, but don’t use it to get around the feelings. Pray for your ex’s wellbeing. Remember what was real and good, and don’t use your pain as permission to hate them. Avoid the New Age belief that everyone is simply a mirror of you. Of course, we do partly mirror one another, but each person was formed by plenty that had nothing to do with you. No matter how enlightened the Buddha was, his cousin Devadatta still kept trying to kill him: looking honestly at your part doesn’t erase theirs. Sometimes it helps to ask, “What would Mr. Rogers do?” (if you’re old enough to remember that show).

B467 [HIS NEW VERSION, 22 Nov: read it as context, don't report it]: I also learned that public demonization doesn’t always have to be the last chapter, even if things ended on that bitter note. If you avoid contact forever, they might never apologize and learn honesty. But it can help to keep distance for a while until cooler heads prevail. On the other hand, it can be tempting to let them back into your life after a private apology, and especially after a public one, but keep in mind you deserve a stable love. You’re not a yo-yo.

## What I gained from loss

B469: At this point, I can say I’m better off because of what happened with Bee, Key, and H., without pretending every part of those relationships was beneficial or destined. Loss taught me things love alone never would, or to paraphrase Rumi, “You must keep breaking your heart until it stays open.” (The real quote: “The reed must be torn from its bed, cut, hollowed out, and pierced with holes (wounded) before it can channel the breath of the musician into music.”)

B470: From Bee, I learned what it means for me to be really in love. I learned the kind of woman I actually want: deeply spiritual, sensitive, creative, unique, somebody who inspires me and makes me question everything.

B471: Key repeated a lot of that, which is why I fell in love with her. I learned some of the same harsh lessons from both of them: don’t take openness and curiosity to be fully good. They can also indicate that someone hasn’t developed their own sense of self or opinions, or enough inner adult.

B472: From H., I learned that I can be extremely close to someone and feel completely understood and supported without actually falling in love. I still treasure the support we gave/give each other.

B473: Too many people judge a relationship only by the result, so start measuring what you actually learned from it instead. Relationships are a process of growth in many dimensions. The result is not only whether you stayed together.

# Tough Love

B475: Romantic love is not easy, and the things that actually make it work are not very marketable. I can’t sell you the fix with one click or one monthly subscription to my love class. (Just kidding, click here!)

B476: A relationship like this is rare, and that’s probably why romantic love has been seen with suspicion as a destabilizing force throughout history. One of the best books that makes that point today might be the one I was reading with Key: The Spirit of Intimacy, by Sobonfu Somé. Somé came from the Dagara tribe in Burkina Faso. To her people, marriage is an arranged, ritualized spiritual and communal responsibility. They’re suspicious of romance. But one of her ideas fits what I’m trying to say: the relationship is bigger and more important than the egos in it. She calls it the spirit of the relationship.

B477: That spirit is like the universe living its desire to know itself through us, which might be why we see ourselves in ways we can’t imagine prior. Nobody can command the spirit to remain. At our best, we serve it and listen for what Spirit wants to change. When we start thinking we own it or deserve it, that presumptuousness tends to push it away.

B478: My parents agreed with Somé that community matters and that we serve Spirit. They were also not romantic, though they were loving and poly. I actually wished they were in love with each other, I guess, although I couldn’t have articulated that since I didn’t even have that concept at the time. Also reminiscent of Somé, they also wore rose-colored glasses and taught me that everybody was basically good. So I learned something about love and community, but not how hard romance is without a community around it, or how much damage basically good people can do when their fears, trauma, and undeveloped parts take over.

B479: It took me many decades to figure out what probably everyone else already knows: real private pathology exists. But pathology festers when the environment is perfect for it. The dominant culture hermetically seals two people together and calls that a luxurious privacy. It tells them that sealed room should provide all the fresh air you’d get from a lover, family, community, therapist, and so on. Then it treats the predictable failure as privately caused as well, like mold that grows out of nowhere.

B480: The myths around romantic love become real because we co-create them. We act as though jealousy proves love, permanent feelings can be promised, conflict should stay hidden, and the relationship failed if it ended. Then we suffer inside ideas that were never natural laws, and we walk blindly into our first, second, third, fourth, and fifth marriages!

B481: It might be that I wrote this whole article for my son, Bear, who sadly, I last saw when he was about five, right before he was supposed to get the Rosenblum sex talk.

B482: Bear, sex can be what you do when you’re older and find a friend you love in a super special way that makes you want to raise children together. Don’t imagine you love each other just because you want each other. Don’t feel pressure to promise what your heart will feel. Let the love and bond it produces blossom honestly as your hearts sync. Learn about each other before getting too close, and make sure to keep friends and community around who know both of you.

B483: I believe Rumi was right: A sacred relationship will open and purify your hearts regardless of whether it ends.
