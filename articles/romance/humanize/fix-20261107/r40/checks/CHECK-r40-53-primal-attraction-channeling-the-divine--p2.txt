What I would say to that depressing take is, Cooper is sort of right, but he doesn't go far enough. Anyone can watch his video and then do the things he says to do and thus stand out from the crowd like he does (until everyone does). But that's superficial posturing, and he even proudly shows how he postures with carefully planned photo shoots for his social media, avoiding the "soy boy smile" which is actually his natural one.

I believe the real way for someone to stand out, is to be their unique, authentic, beautiful (no-)self, because there's no competition there. Nobody can ever beat me at being me (especially since I don't exist!). Cooper might retort that's simply not what attracts women on average, and again, I'd concede that point, because I'm not interested in average women and the superficial connections I'd have with them. On the other hand, one thing that I've noticed is that many women are very attracted not precisely to confidence in general, but rather to a specific type of confidence which is produced by deep relaxation/flow state. When I've been at my most relaxed, I've literally had beautiful women stop their cars in the middle of the road to ask me to get in (thankfully, I didn't, but it was tempting).

I think the key in life in general is to understand that human psychology, in general, is quite nasty, but the ugliness is only dust covering the gem inside us which every spiritual tradition points to. It is by touching the divine ocean of the heart that we can relate to each other from above the animalistic mind and the ancestral trauma. It doesn't mean we suddenly find unattractive people to be attractive, but I do find that as I have matured spiritually and emotionally, I've lost interest in 99% of the women I previously saw as attractive, because now I'm seeing their ego before I get in too deep. And while it's hard to sell this idea of greater selectivity to someone who already can't find a mate, I personally prefer to be alone than to settle for just another pretty war bride.

So while Cooper thinks it's a constant struggle to stay on top, so your mate will continue desiring you more than anyone else, I'm saying that the best mate you could ever hope for is the one who will know after connecting with you over time, that they will never be able to replace you and the love connection they share with you.

Having explained my objections to lowering romantic expectations to the common denominator, I do agree with Cooper that men and women should have contrasting roles to the extent they find that natural.
