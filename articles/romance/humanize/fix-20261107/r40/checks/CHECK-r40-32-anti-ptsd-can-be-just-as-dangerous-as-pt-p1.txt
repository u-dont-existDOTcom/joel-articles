When I’m in love, I see everything just from the love angle. I should probably admit that before I give anybody more advice.

A friend in my TherapyCircle chat was talking about PTSD recently, and I said, “I have anti-PTSD.” I coined it as a joke, but even though it feels nice to have Anti-PTSD, it can leave me at more risk than if I had PTSD. Instead of expecting danger, I keep expecting each new woman and each new situation to finally be the beautiful exception. I see the good and I give the benefit of the doubt long after I should have doubted the benefit. Then the false positive resolves into the same old pattern and gives me actual PTSD, like Ping Pong.

I grew up assuming the best of everybody because that’s what my parents taught me. Now I’m trying to be more suspicious and check for red flags before I’m alone with somebody I badly want to please. Why does she contact me only sometimes? Is that a pattern, or just life getting in the way normally? Is she communicating enough for me to really know her interest level and what’s underneath it? Would my twin flame behave like this? That last one's my peculiar question. Not everybody is searching for somebody who will naturally channel the divine in their presence.

My second marriage to Bee is the clearest example. I knew within a week of her visiting that she saw me as her father. I didn’t know where that might lead, and it seemed like the only avenue to her heart, so I went with it.

A lot of women have seen me that way, and I don't necessarily see it as pathological. The question is whether they can hold it lightly, playfully, or whether it turns into something all-enveloping, and they start taking everything they feel about their dad out on me as if I’m actually him. With Bee, the main issues were the BPD and that her aunts had badly witchcrafted her as a child (which she saw as underlying the BPD).
