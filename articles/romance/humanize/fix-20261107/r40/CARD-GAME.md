## First batch: approved (his message of 2026-11-24 19:58 UTC: "cards look good, you could keep the money card")

The parts he just added or rewrote: his new opening of "Primal attraction" and his version of "Muses & Directors". Each

### New cards

1. **Testing each other** (Attraction & Polarity). *Prompt:* Do either of you ever test the other (pick a small fight,
   push a little disrespect, pull away) to see what they'll do? *Follow-up:* What were you really trying to find out, and
   how could you ask for that directly instead? *Why:* The guide describes "shit-testing" as a way to find out whether a
   partner is strong, and the old wish for someone who is dangerous to anyone who might harm you, never to you.
2. **Posturing or you?** (Attraction & Polarity). *Prompt:* What do you do to stay attractive to your partner that is
   really posturing, and what is just you? *Follow-up:* Which of your partner's unperformed, natural ways do you find
   most attractive, the kind of thing they might hide? *Why:* Advice to keep standing out from the crowd can be copied by
   anyone; nobody can beat you at being you.
3. **The relaxed kind of confidence** (Attraction & Polarity). *Prompt:* When are you most relaxed with each other, and
   what does that do to how attracted you feel? *Follow-up:* What pulls you out of that state, and what brings you back?
   *Why:* The guide says the confidence that draws people most is the kind that comes from deep relaxation, a flow state,
   not confidence in general.
4. **Irreplaceable, not competing** (Love & Wanting). *Prompt:* Do you feel you have to keep competing to stay the one
   your partner wants most, or that you simply can't be replaced? *Follow-up:* What has your partner done over time that
   made you feel irreplaceable, or what would? *Why:* Against the idea of a constant struggle to stay on top, the guide
   says the best partner is the one who comes to know, after time together, that they could never replace you or the love
   you share.
5. **Engine and steering wheel** (Attraction & Polarity). *Prompt:* Who usually brings the spark, the motivation and the
   feeling that life is worth doing, and who gives it a direction and makes it happen? *Follow-up:* Does either of you
   micromanage the other's part, or wait for the other to take an initiative you'd quietly like them to take? *Why:* The
   guide's picture is the woman as the engine and the man as the steering wheel: he gets motivation from her, gives it
   direction, and she still wants him to take initiative.

### Cards to correct

- **love-03**: its "why" quotes a line he cut ("What makes someone desirable is not the same question as what makes them
  loved"). New "why": In "It always goes deeper", the question is how much she loves him and how much she loves what he
  provides; he doesn't need the answer to be that what he provides plays no part, only that what they have grows bigger
  than it.
- **polarity-05**: its "why" asks "whether polarity can stay alive inside safety", the question his new opening dropped.
  New "why": The guide says feeling safe makes room for desire but doesn't spark it, and the more safety and closeness
  there are, the more polarity a couple needs.
- **polarity-01** (money or competence as rank): kept, as he said. Its source paragraph went with his version of "Muses &
  Directors", so its "why" no longer says the guide says it. New "why": Money, education, status or competence can
  quietly turn into rank: "my house, my rules", one person feeling useless, or the other pressured to shrink. The guide's
  "Romance Shouldn't Be Adjudicated" warns that once a role has to be proven, something has already become fake. Its
  source: "Romance Shouldn't Be Adjudicated".

## Second batch, for his approval (2026-11-24)

The deck already covers many of the sections it doesn't name (jealousy at a gathering, fawning, irreconcilable memories,
the tiny household habit, alone time, integration after altered states, the sober ritual, the twin flame idea). These
twelve cover what it doesn't, his clarifications of this week included.

1. **The undefined thing** (Clarity; situationships). *Prompt:* Is any part of what's between you being kept
   undefined? Who gets something out of it staying vague, and what does each of you quietly hope it is? *Follow-up:* If one
   of you asked "Why are you making this heavy?", what would the other really be trying to find out? *Why:* The guide calls
   the situationship a relationship that fills a void only accidentally, maybe just enough to keep you from looking for
   anything deeper, and suggests hearing "Why are you making this heavy?" as "Are you about to give up what we've got?"
2. **Before "partner"** (Clarity; How to find a partner). *Prompt:* What did you see of each other before either of you
   started thinking of the other as a potential partner? *Follow-up:* Once you did, what started being performed, by
   either of you? *Why:* Once you see every new person as a potential partner, you perform for them and they perform for
   you; the guide suggests looking for people who match you without directly looking for a romantic match.
3. **What you already knew** (Clarity; Dating App Horror Show). *Prompt:* What did the first profile, chats or dates
   already tell you about each other that you set aside because the other person was attractive and interested?
   *Follow-up:* Which of those early signs turned out true, and which turned out wrong? *Why:* His first wife's dating
   profile told him things he ignored because she was cute and interested, and both turned out true; an app can be a fine
   introduction if you use it as a proper gate.
4. **When the window closes** (Clarity; The window of clarity). *Prompt:* At what point does your clear view of a new
   person start closing: the first kiss, when sex becomes likely, after sex, moving in? *Follow-up:* What would you want to
   ask or decide before that point, while you can still see? *Why:* For him, the window of objectivity closes once sex
   seems sure to happen soon: he idealizes her more, and afterward may feel unable to leave.
5. **Why me?** (Love & Wanting; Holding up a leaky dam). *Prompt:* Why are you with each other and not somebody else?
   *Follow-up:* Which part of "this is the right person" did you actually learn about each other, and which part is that
   you're attached now? *Why:* Attachment can turn "I'm taking a chance on her" into "she's the right person" without
   anything new learned; if the answer to "Why am I special to you?" is basically "We had sex and got hitched", that's a
   damn shame.
6. **Swallow it or throw it** (Conflict & Repair; Measuring the benefits). *Prompt:* When something hurts, do you tend to
   downplay it and then let it out combatively later? *Follow-up:* What would it sound like to say, calmly and soon, "I'm
   feeling unappreciated right now, and I'd like us to talk about what's beneath it in a kinder way"? *Why:* The guide
   counts the ability to say what you feel in a non-combative way as one of the most important things any therapy can
   give, instead of ping-ponging between not saying it and saying it as an attack.
7. **What honesty might open** (Endings; What will honesty produce?). *Prompt:* Is there a truth you're holding back
   because you can't predict what it will do to the relationship? *Follow-up:* What might it open, even if it changes the
   romance: repair, friendship, a family kept together through a separation? *Why:* The guide says honesty doesn't produce
   a certain outcome; it is its own reward, and it might repair the relationship, open it, turn it into friendship, or show
   that ending it is better.
8. **The switch** (Trust & Jealousy; Jealousy on trial). *Prompt:* Since choosing each other, has something like a
   "looking for someone better" switch turned off in you? If part of it is still on, how would you want that handled
   honestly? *Follow-up:* What would help the more jealous one believe it, when the jealousy may be older than this
   relationship? *Why:* He describes finding something like a mate-finding switch in his mind and turning it off; it worked
   about 99% of the time, and still his partner never quite believed it, which points to insecure attachment from
   childhood more than to anything he was doing.
9. **Toward love, or around it?** (Trust & Jealousy; Jealousy on trial). *Prompt:* If either of you is drawn to an open
   or poly arrangement, is it a way toward more love, a way around deeper intimacy, or a way out of guilt? *Follow-up:*
   What would make the choice honest either way? *Why:* The guide says polyamory can be a way of escaping deeper
   intimacy, like his college friend with four girlfriends, or of escaping guilt, as when someone who kept cheating finally
   "comes out" as poly; still, coming clean is right.
10. **A promise doing closeness's job** (Promises; Labels Are Descriptive, Not Prescriptive). *Prompt:* Is anything you've
    promised each other, out loud or on paper, standing in for closeness you don't have yet? *Follow-up:* What would you
    need to talk about so the promise isn't carrying that weight? *Why:* Commitment grows out of relational depth, not a
    label; a promise that has to stand in for that closeness is more like a red flag, while an honest vow promises actions
    on top of it.
11. **Basically good people** (Inner Child; Tough Love). *Prompt:* Did you grow up believing people are basically good?
    How has that belief served you in love, and when has it blinded you? *Follow-up:* What did your parents' way of loving
    teach you, and what did it leave out? *Why:* His parents, like Somé, trusted the universe and taught him everybody was
    basically good; he learned something about love and community, but not how hard romance is without a community around
    it, or how much damage basically good people can do when their fears, trauma and undeveloped parts take over.
12. **Passionate and stable** (Love & Wanting; Tough Love). *Prompt:* What makes your relationship passionate, and what
    makes it stable? Which one do you tend to give up for the other? *Follow-up:* What would keep both alive for the next
    year? *Why:* The guide says a passionate romantic relationship that's also stable is rare, which is probably why
    romantic love has been distrusted as a destabilizing force throughout history.
