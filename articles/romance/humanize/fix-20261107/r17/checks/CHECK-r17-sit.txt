And then there’s the situationship, which is hard to define because that’s basically the point. Usually it’s a happenstance relationship based on sex. There’s less communication, and what communication exists is more superficial. Yet it is obviously some kind of relationship even while one or both people refuse to name it. You’re regularly having sex, going out together, spending time alone together, and doing things you wouldn’t do with a regular friend, so it feels like it’s filling a relationship void, but only accidentally and occasionally—maybe just enough to keep you from looking for anything deeper.

It can go either way, but often the man wants to keep it undefined while the woman wants commitment, partly because she’s the one risking pregnancy. Whoever keeps it undefined gets the fun without (most of) the responsibility. But people still get pregnant and attached, whatever they call it. The one keeping it that way still ends up with the existential void another superficial fix creates. Doomscrolling entertains you too.

And “Why are you making this heavy?” can be a real question. They may actually mean: don’t you want the fun, and are you about to give it up? My answer would be: I’m trying to understand whether this is really fulfilling me or if it’s drainingly superficial.

I had something sort of like that with H., although ours was much deeper than what people normally mean by situationship, and painfully uneven. We were (and still are) BFFs, and spent nearly all our time together. She was falling in love with me, and I wasn’t falling in love with her. She felt more like a sister to me, partly because I’m not attracted to white women.
