According to myself, I was ahead of Bee, Key, and H. in maturity, emotional stability, self-love, and self-awareness. I also had advantages they didn’t, including a happier childhood and more moolah. As you might imagine, all of that affected the balance between us.

They were all ahead of me in childlike wonder and fascinating creative intuition, which is what attracted me. Sometimes their intuition was wrong and they trusted it too much, but I still loved that quality in them, since I tend to be more analytical.

They were also all more social than me. They could organize people for a ceremony or group gathering very easily. I felt like I needed that if I ever wanted to start a commune, because I’m more introverted.

I’m good at talking to people when there’s a purpose. I’m not as good at rousing everybody up and encouraging them to do things or go places with me, because I tend to feel that everyone should simply do as they please.

So I do believe in mutual coaching and complementarity.

I could teach these women about loving and reparenting their inner child, Buddhism, meditation, and other things I understood better. They could pull me into life more, show me where I was missing people, and sometimes be the skeptic when I was seeing the best in someone and missing their hidden motives.

When it works, we take turns, me teaching one day and learning the next, and that’s healthy.

It’s time to bring in other support once a partner has become the default caregiver and that’s taken for granted, or the romance could collapse under the burden. Maybe the romance actually does need less intensity, maybe it becomes friendship, or maybe it ends. Asking the question out loud is usually where it starts, though I can’t answer it for others (and couldn’t always answer it for myself).
