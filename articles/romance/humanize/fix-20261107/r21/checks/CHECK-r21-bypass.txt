I’ve used spirituality as a replacement for intimacy a lot because intimacy was simply unavailable. As a boy and young man, I was so deprived of female touch that it was driving me insane. Meditation sometimes helped me cope better with the deprivation, but it wasn’t a cure. I remember once as I was entering into an ayahuasca ego death, I said, “Actually, I really just want a girlfriend.” Right then, the crazy trip abruptly ended, and a voice said, “Ok then go find one.” Nobody believes this story when I tell it.

I don’t believe most people can be truly happy celibate. People generally aren’t so serious about spiritual liberation that giving up sex and partnership for life makes sense. The Buddha didn’t advise everyone to become monastics.

If becoming a monastic genuinely makes you happier and more peaceful, stay. It might work for some very advanced people, or if your sexual desire is already low, or you’re old, etc.

But how do you know what you’re not aware of? Inner-child reparenting and self-hypnosis can help a lot with that, because often the inner child is being ignored while the adult is feeling some kind of liberation.

And if you’re so happy and free, what happens when you’re alone in a room with a super beautiful girl and she wants you? Or vice versa for women. Do you feel like you’re losing some control? Are you afraid?

Bhante Gunaratana, a famous Buddhist teacher, tells a story like this in his autobiography. A super beautiful girl at a retreat he was teaching snuck into his room and waited for him in bed, naked. He went nuts and started screaming at her “Get out!” at the top of his lungs. Obviously he was still repressing something, and she was adept at unintentionally uncovering it.

Robes do nothing. I was mildly molested in public by the abbot of one of Thailand’s largest Buddhist retreat centers. In Thailand, parents have very good reasons not to leave children alone with monks. Tibet is worse.

Forced celibacy doesn’t magically abolish anyone’s sexuality. For most people, it eventually just makes them more perverted, since they have no legitimate way to express it.

Some people become monks for practical reasons too. In a poor country, they're given free food, free shelter and massive unearned respect, without any expectation that they have to hold down a job or raise a family. And even where the free food doesn’t matter as much, like in some wealthy area of the West, a guy who was a nobody as a layman gets to be Venerable Nobody, and people bow to him as he preaches how they can also become Nobody some day.

Of course, Mr. Nobody may sincerely want liberation, too. People can want several things at once.
