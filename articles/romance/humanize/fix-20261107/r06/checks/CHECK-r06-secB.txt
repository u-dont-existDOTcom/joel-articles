When I’m in love, my partner is my everything. Everything else drops away. I still try to make some time for friends, but really all I want to do is be with her.

That little paradise is wonderful, but it contains the seed of its own destruction. Part of why it feels like a magical wonderland is that you can suddenly stop parenting yourself now that you have someone who’s fully got your back. This makes you soft and weak, kinda like a sweet treat or heroin. It is wonderful to have their love and support, but don’t let that bring you away from your own self-love, reparenting, meditation, yoga, and other healing practices that keep you independently healthy. Otherwise, you’ll end up blaming them when you feel bad.

So don’t let your world get so small. Don’t abandon your friends unless they really aren’t good for the relationship. And if they aren’t, ask why. Are they bad friends? Are they slyly interested in one of you romantically? Or is the relationship so weak that it can’t handle anybody with an outside view?

You don’t need to spend as much time with friends as you did before the romance. But keep some room to explore life on your own.

For me, that also means keeping close friends, outside therapy when I need it, a mission of my own, separate interests, some connection to community, and the ability to be alone.

Toft also points out that a man can get feminine energy from nature, music, poetry, art, flowers, food, and other parts of life. I like that idea. A girlfriend shouldn’t have to be my only source of beauty, softness, sensuality, and inspiration.

When I’m already overwhelmed and suddenly announce, “I need time for myself!” it can easily sound like I need time away from her. So I’d rather make alone time normal from early on.

And bring each other out into the world. See people together. Go places. Don’t just disappear into each other.
