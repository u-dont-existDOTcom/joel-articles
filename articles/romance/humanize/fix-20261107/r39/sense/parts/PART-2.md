## The part to read

[66] ## Beware idealization in either direction

[67] You can legitimately see someone as amazing when you don’t need anything back from seeing them that way. Once you start identifying with the way you see them, like she’s perfect and I’m the one destiny finally sent to find her, you’re already lost in the sauce.

[68] I’ve learned to get nervous when somebody "knows" too much about me/us too soon. You know how it goes: “We’re twin flames!” or “I’m Lord Krishna and she’s Radha!” Okay, maybe I really am way better than the other guys she’s found. But if she hasn’t found anything about me that bothers her yet, she hasn’t found all of me.

[69] For example, I still love women I’ve loved before, and sometimes I talk about them, with no intention of making my current partner jealous, just because I’m thinking about them and I don’t want to hide it. If she can’t handle the truth that I still love them, she isn’t ready for me.

[70] If she says, “Can you talk less about your exes?” Well, yes, I'll happily oblige, since maybe I do talk about them too much sometimes. But I'm hoping that doesn't mean she also wants to pretend I'm not thinking of them just because I don't blast it out loud. And I also can't stand the double standard if she's talking about her exes while I can't talk about mine.

[71] ## Anti-PTSD Can Be Just As Dangerous As PTSD

[72] A friend in my TherapyCircle chat was talking about PTSD recently, and I said, “I have anti-PTSD.” I coined it as a joke, but even though it feels nice to have Anti-PTSD, it can leave me at more risk than if I had PTSD. Instead of expecting danger, I keep expecting each new woman and each new situation to finally be the beautiful exception. I see the good and I give the benefit of the doubt long after I should have doubted the benefit. Then the false positive resolves into the same old pattern and gives me actual PTSD, like Ping Pong.

[73] I grew up assuming the best of everybody because that’s what my parents taught me. Now I’m trying to be more suspicious and check for red flags before I’m alone with somebody I badly want to please. Why does she contact me only sometimes? Is that a pattern, or just life getting in the way normally? Is she communicating enough for me to really know her interest level and what’s underneath it? Would my twin flame behave like this? That last one's my peculiar question. Not everybody is searching for somebody who will naturally channel the divine in their presence.

[74] A lot of women have seen me that way, and I don't necessarily see it as pathological. The question is whether they can hold it lightly, playfully, or whether it turns into something all-enveloping, and they start taking everything they feel about their dad out on me as if I’m actually him. With Bee, the main issues were the BPD and that her aunts had badly witchcrafted her as a child (which she saw as underlying the BPD).

[75] I don’t know whether that unconditional love ended up being true. I think it was true in a part of her, and it may also have been the BPD idealization. You have to look at everything from multiple angles. But when you’re in love, you see everything from the love angle. That’s the reality shift I’m talking about. The love angle is great if the ground underneath the relationship is prepared for it, but if the rose-colored glasses go on when you’re with the wrong person, they make it even harder to understand what’s real.

[76] So I became the father in a relationship where I wanted an equally responsible playmate. Part of what happened may have been that she eventually grew up, and a daughter who grows up leaves home. But that wasn't even really 1% of the whole story. The two years in-between contained enough insanity for several lifetimes.

[77] That doesn’t mean I’d advise somebody to knowingly enter a dangerous relationship because it may be worth it. Going in, it helps to recognize your pattern and stop idealizing, without becoming suspicious and closed, so you have some idea what you’re getting into.

[78] ## Seeing idealization right

[79] Partners should help each other grow. Who else is going to do it, your mom and dad? You’re already grown to them. Everybody else will usually be a smaller influence, so you’d better choose someone who helps you grow well. Like the FM Laeti song down in the Twin Flames section says, “Inspire her to see the best in herself, actualize that,” though if something severe is happening, sometimes a demand or boundary is necessary too.

[80] ## If slow isn’t realistic for you

[81] So my main strategy has been to avoid heat-of-the-moment situations until I’m ready. Avoiding those situations has definitely saved me from having ten times the body count, but it doesn’t work reliably, because the invitation that leads there often starts out small, like “Hey, wanna go do something together?” Maybe she wants me to come to an event, a meditation teaching, or a walk in nature.

[82] Phone or video first can help, as can meeting up around other people and deciding beforehand that we aren’t going home together. Islamic and conservative Christian cultures put much stronger barriers than these around a man and woman being alone before marriage. I don’t except their helicoptering solution, but they noticed something real. We don’t live in tribes anymore, where people around us might know both of us well enough to slow this down.

[83] ## Just in case

[84] We’re human though, so expecting perfect behavior is a recipe for disaster. That’s where Gandarusa comes in for men. It’s a traditional Indonesian contraceptive herb that looks about 99% effective, with health benefits (including libido and for HIV), and rarely any side effects. You have to take it daily and it takes a month to become effective. But attachment starts before pregnancy anyway, so even with contraception, I can still get emotionally attached to somebody I barely know.

[85] ## Holding up a leaky dam

[86] Once I’m attached, “I’m taking a chance on her” starts turning into “she’s the right person” after we’ve been together for a while. But could I explain that to an objective friend? Sometimes I actually learned something about her, but other times all that changed is I’m attached now, and that just makes me less likely to question it.

[87] ## Slow & steady may win the race, but turtles have problems too!

[88] Going slowly can show you how somebody’s moods wibble and wobble over time, how they treat you and other people, but it can’t tell you how good the sex will be or how either of you will act afterward. You can know somebody for 20 years and then discover on the first night that the polarity isn’t there. Touch or smell can feel wrong, or your desire levels or kinks don’t match up.

[89] Some of those things might get better if both partners are committed to helping each other through it, but sometimes bad sexual fit still feels like, “Let’s just be friends!” Saying, “The sex isn’t working for me,” might sound like, “I only love you if the sex is good.” But that vague love word again needs clarifying. Universal love is the basis of any good relationship of any kind. It doesn’t make someone special the way romance does. Romantic love generally needs good sex too, or else we’re basically just friends who care deeply about each other.

[90] ## Slow & steady isn’t KO’d yet

[91] Go slowly if you can, then, and learn what you can about each other before sex, but there are things you’ll only find out once you’re having it, like how your bodies respond to each other.

[92] ## The Relationship As Crucible

[93] It was during my first marriage that I read about Dr. David Schnarch’s Crucible Approach in Passionate Marriage. Schnarch saw marriage as a natural “people-growing process.” His idea of differentiation was learning to hold onto yourself while staying close, especially when intimacy brings anxiety and unresolved problems to the surface, which is definitely what relationship has done to me.

[94] One warning before I romanticize the crucible too much: sometimes (as in my first marriage) this isn’t two wounded people triggering each other but one person terrorizing or controlling the other. If you’re scared to say no or tell the truth, or of what happens if you leave, that’s way past a mutual communication exercise, so get other people involved and think about safety first.

[95] Toft sees the crucible of his marriage as his main spiritual practice. He also continues his meditation, yoga, prayer, and church activities, but sees them as secondary. I understand what he means, because it’s much easier for me to feel spiritually advanced when nobody is close enough to expose the parts of me that aren’t.

[96] ## From flow to nitty gritty

[97] Living alone is easy in so many ways, like I can make my bed whenever I want. When I have a girlfriend, making the bed in the morning can become one of the ways I show her I love her. If I don’t do it well, somehow it may become a bad omen that I don’t care about her, which is a lot of emotional weight for a bed.

[98] Dealing with somebody else’s emotional insecurities can also be exhausting. It’s easy to feel free when I don’t have to do that, especially since I’m a bit of an introvert and 10% autistic. All that works wonderfully until I’m in a romantic relationship and suddenly I’m expected to mind-read, silences included, and check in constantly, without ever manipulating her. Bee would accuse me of manipulating her even when I was making an effort not to, and ironically, she actually wanted me to manipulate her more.

[99] ## Reverting to babyhood

[100] Relationship may activate wounds that were already there. Sometimes it may open a level of the heart that is simply very young and vulnerable, whether you knew there was a wound there or not. You become like a baby again. There’s a reason lovers call each other “baby.” And after all, Christ said "unless you change and become like little children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 18:3). Well, the Kingdom of Heaven may require extensive therapy to arrive at if being a baby was difficult the first time for either of us.

[101] As mentioned with Bee, but also with Key, and H., there was always some sense that they were remembering how their dad treated them and judging me according to how close to him—or different from him—I was. That put me in a tough position. I want to play the role they need in some way, but I want to hold onto the actual reality of me being me, with no need for putting on a show.

[102] ## Don’t make your partner your whole world

[103] Part of why that little paradise feels like a magical wonderland is that you can suddenly stop parenting yourself now that you have someone who’s fully got your back. This makes you soft and weak, kinda like a sweet treat or heroin. It is wonderful to have their love and support, but don’t let that bring you away from your own self-love, reparenting, meditation, yoga, and other healing practices that keep you independently healthy. Otherwise, you’ll end up blaming them when you feel bad.

[104] For me, that room is where I keep a mission of my own, separate interests and some connection to community. I also get outside therapy when I need it, and I make sure I can still be alone.

[105] Toft also points out that a man can get feminine energy from nature, music, poetry, art, flowers, food, and other parts of life. I agree, it's not fair to myself or to my girlfriend/wife if she has to be my only source of beauty, softness, sensuality, and inspiration.

[106] When I’m already overwhelmed and suddenly announce, “I need time for myself!” it can easily sound like I need time away from her. It's always better to have some balance from the get-go rather than as a reaction after you see something is unsustainable, so make alone time normal from early on.

[107] And bring each other out into the world. Meet other folks together and explore new places, don’t just disappear into each other.

[108] ## Disconnect to connect

[109] Self-reflection is a good place to start, but it can’t see what it can’t see. Your partner is also usually trying to keep you, so they have an ulterior motive, as sweet and expected as it may be.

[110] That’s why I recommend peer counseling outside the couple. If my partner is the only person I bring all my pain to, she also ends up carrying whatever I do when I still don’t understand it.

[111] Crying also contains a type of automatic erratic breathwork. And breathwork is one of the easiest ways I know to clam down. Reduced breathing or just continuous/circular Systema breathing is especially useful to me when my partner is getting me upset and flustered with drama. Sometimes she’s deliberately trying to do that, and other times it’s hard to tell, although if I'm stuck thinking about what's on purpose I'm already lost. I explain more of the body-based therapy options in my Somatic Modalities guide.

[112] Meditation is also helpful, but for most people, I would still put inner-child reparenting, peer counseling, and body-based work first, or make those the main things they meditate on. Many people say they cannot meditate or have not gotten to that stage yet, and meditation without grounding in those approaches can easily be a road into spiritual bypassing.

[113] ## Babies growing up again

[114] Nobody has to be completely healed before entering a relationship, but I think that as we learn more about ourselves through the crucible, we should naturally raise our standards. Maybe at first we need the drama to access our primal emotions, but after years of that, we’re exhausted by what we previously hoped to find.

[115] I already said that before dating, the present-day adult has to be able to parent whatever intimacy wakes up inside you. Once you’re together, more of that stuff comes out. Inner-child reparenting means parenting the child part instead of surreptitiously hiring the partner for the job.

[129] ## Romance Shouldn’t Be Adjudicated

[130] The moment I have to prove that I’m the man, something has already become fake, which I hate. Than it's like I have to defend the identity every time I'm not leading the way confidently & correctly, or god forbid if I need help or I cry!

[131] I'm not really like that, and I do like to decide things together. Bee once called me her “wife” because of that. I don’t recommend that as a polarity exercise, by the way.  🫣

[132] But I do have a masculine side, and when a woman appreciates that masculine side of me, it tends to come out by itself even more.

[133] And women also shouldn't have to perform softness, or cute helplessness every minute to prove their femininity. Surrender means so much more when she could take control, but she doesn't, like an offering in trust.

[134] But in some sense, being the one who's supposed to be more childish can be even harder than having the more responsible gender role. She may worry that I don’t find her young and beautiful enough, or that she’s too emotional, difficult, and needy. [136] Anami talks a lot about women needing to learn to receive a man's help, leadership, or attempts at giving her pleasure, without feeling like it costs them their agency. Sometimes that’s as ordinary as a woman who knows perfectly well how to drive asking me to drive, because she likes how it feels not to.

[137] As her man, I can help her come into her femininity by, for example, asking, “Honey, how do you see this intuitively?” And that's actually one of the powerful sides of femininity, so it can be a good bridge also if she's a bit stuck in masculine mode. And then she might ask me to help think through the practical side to lift me up and help me shine.
