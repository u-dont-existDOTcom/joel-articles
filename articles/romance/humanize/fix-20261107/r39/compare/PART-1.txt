# The same paragraphs as the live post has them

## Talk about making love before you do it

You may not know all the answers yet. “I don’t know, but I’m willing to find out honestly with you” is a perfectly good answer.

## Labels Are Descriptive, Not Prescriptive

Here’s the funny thing many people don’t understand: labels don’t create commitment. They can still expose whether two people think they’re in the same relationship, which matters a lot.

The full conversation is so big that almost nobody would have casual sex if they took it seriously. Which is why almost nobody has the full conversation. I can’t decide what is okay for anybody else. I can say that most people who defend casual sex are not being very honest with themselves about pregnancy, attachment, or what they might owe the person afterward.

## Spirituality to bypass intimacy

And if you’re so happy and free, what happens when you’re alone in a room with a super beautiful girl and she wants you? Or vice versa for women. Do you feel like you’re losing some control? Are you afraid?

## It always goes deeper

I got a smaller and much less romantic test later. Bee gave me some shoes to try that didn’t fit. I wore them for a day, and somehow my knee ended up messed up for about a month. She did nurse me, but a bit reluctantly. She also kept pushing me toward thinking maybe it was partly in my head or wasn’t really that bad. I understood why—neither of us could believe one day in the wrong shoes could do that—but it still felt dismissive, and maybe partly reflected what she wanted to be true.

## And deeper is the point

Sometimes they may even want you to find sexual satisfaction elsewhere if they can’t give it, because they love you and want you to be happy. Maybe they don’t. Nobody is owed that as proof of love.

## Dating App Horror Show

I always tell young guys especially: don’t worry about not finding a girl. Worry about finding the wrong girl. Girls are usually more selective, but they still need to think twice, because they often go for guys who aren’t respectful or mature.

I’m almost embarrassed to admit, but I recently tried Hinge again for a few days while I was in Kenya after my last breakup. I was getting something like a hundred girls interested in my profile every day, so I looked through a lot of profiles. Some were cute but none seemed deep. So the app did tell me something useful.

## The Missing Middle

It also didn’t exactly solve the problem. In late-eighteenth-century New England, bundling coexisted with a lot of premarital pregnancy: 30–40 percent of brides were already pregnant. So it wasn’t exactly a reliable way to keep courtship nonsexual. I don’t think we need to bring bundling back, but we do need some socially recognized way to explore tenderness and attraction without immediately sliding toward sex, cohabitation, and a life bond.

## Peeking thru the window

Before the wisdom window closes, the main thing is to find out whether you are actually compatible. Do you have the same goals and values, especially around children, religion, politics, health, love, and romance? Where do you want to live? Do you both want to find a community? What kind of relationship do you want with technology and money?

See their negative side first too. If there is no negative side, that’s a red flag for fakery. Ask whether the spiritual story they tell about themselves survives an inconvenient PMS afternoon. How recent is the healing story—two months or two years? Jealousy, abandonment, possessiveness, and the “I’m so much better than I used to be” story may not be understood until there is stress and entanglement. By then, of course, you’re stressed and entangled. 😆

## The conversation about flaws

Card games and social gatherings don’t tell you everything, but I wouldn’t ask someone out of the blue, “What did you do wrong in your past relationships?” That would be a pretty weird date.

## My flaws

My own blind spot is that I have trouble understanding where people are at mentally and emotionally. A partner may agree with everything I say and then tell me later, “Didn’t you understand I was FAWNING?”

No, I really didn’t. If you’re fawning, tell me when you can, because I can’t know what’s happening in your head just because it feels obvious to you.

## Things get tricky fast

Couples withdraw from friends who aren’t supportive of the romantic dyad, and they also stop telling friends the whole truth. Sharp questions start to feel rude. An obvious problem dissolves into, “but I love her,” and the small world of the relationship starts feeling more real than the rest of life. The relationship can close off at exactly the point when both people most need a view from outside it.

## Beware idealization in either direction

Legitimately seeing someone as amazing doesn’t depend on what the seeing gives you. Once you start identifying with the way you see them—she’s perfect, this is destiny, I’m the one who finally found her—you’re already lost in the sauce.

## Anti-PTSD Can Be Just As Dangerous As PTSD

I don’t know whether that unconditional love ended up being true. I think it was true in a part of her, and it may also have been the BPD idealization. You have to look at everything from multiple angles. But when you’re in love, you see everything from the love angle. That’s the reality shift I’m talking about. It’s great if the ground underneath is prepared for it. If the rose-colored glasses go on when you’re with the wrong person, they make it even harder to understand what’s real.

That doesn’t mean I’d advise somebody to knowingly enter a dangerous relationship because it may be worth it. Going in, it helps to recognize your pattern, stop idealizing without becoming suspicious and closed, and have some idea what you’re getting into.

## Seeing idealization right

Partners should help each other grow. Who else is going to do it? Your mom and dad? You’re already grown to them. Everybody else will usually be a smaller influence, so you’d better choose someone who helps you grow well. Like the FM Laeti song down in the Twin Flames section says, “Inspire her to see the best in herself, actualize that.” If something severe is happening, sometimes a demand or boundary is necessary too.

## If slow isn’t realistic for you

So my main strategy has been to avoid heat-of-the-moment situations until I’m ready. That has definitely saved me from having ten times the body count, but it doesn’t work reliably because the request often starts out small. “Hey, wanna go do something together?” Maybe she wants me to come to an event, a meditation teaching, or a walk in nature.

## Slow & steady may win the race, but turtles have problems too!

Going slowly can show you how somebody’s moods move over time, how they treat you, and how they treat other people. It still can’t tell you how good the sex will be or how either of you will act afterward.

You can know somebody for twenty years and then discover on the first night that the polarity isn’t there, touch or smell feels wrong, your desire levels or kinks don’t fit, one person is sexually shut down, or the bodies just don’t connect.

Some of those things might get better if both partners are committed to helping each other through it. Sometimes bad sexual fit still feels like, “Let’s just be friends!”

Saying, “The sex isn’t working for me,” may sound like, “I only love you if the sex is good.” But universal love doesn’t make someone special. It isn’t romance. It’s the basis of any good relationship of any kind.

I can still love her and not want to be in a romantic relationship with her. Romantic love generally needs good sex too, or we’re friends who care deeply about each other.
