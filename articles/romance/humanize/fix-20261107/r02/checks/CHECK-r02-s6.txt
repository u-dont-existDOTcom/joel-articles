Modern life barely has an intermediate stage between being interested in somebody and getting sexually entangled. One old example is bundling, a mostly extinct practice now especially associated with the Amish, even though it existed much more widely. Courting couples could spend the night together in bed, usually clothed, with the idea that they could talk and be close without having sex. That’s pretty much the missing middle I mean.

It didn’t exactly solve the problem, though. In late-eighteenth-century New England, 30–40 percent of brides were already pregnant on their wedding day, and bundling was common then. So much for keeping courtship nonsexual. I’m not saying we should bring bundling back. But we need some socially recognized way to explore tenderness and attraction without sliding straight into sex, moving in together, and a life bond.
