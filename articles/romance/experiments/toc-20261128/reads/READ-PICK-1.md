## Part 2. What gets in the way

**Headings that don't say what's under them or read two ways**
- "Starting on the Right Foot" (line 21) has 0 words and its first child, "The Window of Clarity", doesn't say what it clears. The whole start-of-relationship block (flaws, idealization, going slow) hides behind these two vague lines.
- "Conscious Tools" (line 58) has 25 words and is a grab bag: imagining hard parts, psychedelic timing, "why this sounds artificial". "Tools" doesn't tell a reader that psychedelics are in there.
- "Tough Love" (line 72) is last, has 620 words, and says nothing. It could be a closing note, advice on being firm, or a section on love that is hard.
- "Who Holds a Couple Up?" (line 53) is 666 words and could mean community, friends, family or finances. It's a top-level heading with no children to hint at its content.
- "Anti-PTSD Is Risky Too" (line 28) is jargon. A reader can't tell it is about assuming the best of people, and it is the longest subsection.
- "The Missing Middle" (line 20) sits under finding a partner but sounds like a stage between interest and sex. "Affection and the Simmer" (line 6) is the other candidate for that, so the two compete.
- "Labels Only Describe" (line 9) sits under casual sex, but it reads as being about commitment labels (boyfriend/girlfriend).
- "Fantasy" (line 47) is one word. It could be sexual fantasy or idealizing.
- "Should You Leave?" (line 68) has only 103 words and one child. "Children" just above makes it unclear whether leaving is about kids.
- "De-ontologizing FTW" (line 52) is jargon, though playful. "Twin Flames?" (line 50) is clear enough on its own.
- "Not Ready, but Lucky" (line 11) doesn't say what it's about.
- "Reverting to Babyhood" and "Growing Up Again" (lines 37, 40) are near-opposites that are hard to tell apart from the titles.
- "Ahead in Different Ways" (line 42) could be about pace, careers or maturity.
