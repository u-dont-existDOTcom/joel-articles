# Situations

1. You love your girlfriend and want her badly, but when you imagine her being happier with someone else you feel sick. You want to know whether wanting her and wanting her to be happy can ever fit together.
2. You've been flirting with someone for weeks and sex is clearly coming. You want to know what the two of you should actually talk about before you get undressed, even if it kills the mood.
3. You've been married eight years and the flirting stopped long ago. Sex only happens at bedtime when you're both tired, and you both feel taken for granted, so you want practical ways to bring the spark back.
4. A coworker suggests a no-strings night, saying it's just for fun. You're tempted, but you wonder what could go wrong the next morning and what you'd owe each other.
5. For months you've been sleeping with someone, going out with them and spending nights at their place, but neither of you will say what this is. Lately you feel emptier after each visit, and when you try to talk about it they say, "Why are you making this heavy?"
6. You and your partner have called each other boyfriend and girlfriend for three years, but he still keeps one foot out the door. You wonder whether a label or a promise is what makes someone committed.
7. You're lonely and badly want a partner, but every relationship you've had ends in the same disaster. A friend says you shouldn't date right now, and you want to know if they're right.
8. You've meditated for years and half believe the real path is giving up romance and sex altogether, yet part of you aches for a girlfriend. You wonder whether that ache means you're not as free as you think.
9. Your partner became seriously ill and sex is no longer possible between you. You worry that without it you're just roommates, or that you should look for it somewhere else.
10. A friend wants you to join a dating app and you're tempted by the attention, but you've had bad experiences swiping. You want to know how to use one without getting excited by every cute profile.
11. You hate apps and would rather meet someone who already shares your world. You wonder whether joining a community, or visiting communal living places together with someone, is a good way to find a partner and get to know them.
12. You wish there were something between being interested in someone and ending up in bed or moving in together. You'd like a stage where the two of you can explore tenderness slowly without it turning into sex.
13. After sleeping with someone you barely knew, you suddenly feel stuck and can't see any of their flaws, though a week ago you were unsure. You want to know why closeness takes away your judgment so fast.
14. You've just started seeing someone great and want a practical list of ways to find out whether you're really compatible, including how they act when stressed, before you get too attached.
15. The person you're dating says all of their exes were awful. You want to know when and how to ask about their past relationships, and which answers should worry you.
16. Your new partner says you're perfect, hasn't found a single flaw in you, and keeps saying you were destined to meet. It feels amazing and a little suspicious.
17. You always assume the best about new partners and keep giving them chances long after you should have noticed warning signs. You want to learn to spot red flags without turning cynical.
18. You know yourself: when you're alone with someone you want, you'll give in even if you planned to talk about important things first. You want ways to arrange things so you slow down.
19. You've been sleeping with someone for a few weeks and they suggest moving in together. It feels right, but you couldn't explain to a friend why.
20. Since you started dating, you've dropped your friends, your hobbies and your daily practices, and your girlfriend is your whole world. It's blissful, but you wonder if it's healthy and how to get your own space back without hurting her.
21. When your wife is in emotional pain, you feel dragged down in your own body, and she's the only person you tell anything to. You want somewhere else to put your own pain.
22. Your girlfriend just told you she feels like your patient because she asks you about every health and emotional problem. You did help her, but she's right that it has become a pattern.
23. You and your partner are happy and feel safe together, yet the passion is gone. Everything you read says desire needs distance and mystery, and you don't want to live apart.
24. Your girlfriend pulled back after you kept needing her reassurance. You're a man and you don't understand why your needing her and pursuing her put her off.
25. You were sure your ex was the only person you could ever love this deeply, and now you've fallen just as hard for someone else. You wonder what that says about the idea of one and only soulmate.
26. You see your partner's best, most beautiful self as the real her and her annoying habits as not really her. You wonder whether to accept all of her exactly as she is or to nudge her toward that best self.
27. You and your partner had a huge fight and each of you ran to your own friends. Now both groups of friends are on opposite sides, and neither of you trusts the other's friends.
28. You and someone you're dating agreed to "be together", but you suspect you each picture a different future. You want to know how to find out what you each mean, including what happens if one of you falls for someone else.
29. You're planning a wedding but feel uneasy promising to love your fiancé forever, since you can't know how you'll feel in twenty years. You wonder what you can honestly promise instead.
30. Your boyfriend glances at other women and it makes you feel unloved, so you stew and make snide comments. You want to know how to speak up honestly about your jealousy and how to handle attraction to other people inside a faithful relationship.
31. You've just met someone exciting and can only picture the good parts. You'd like a way to imagine the harder parts of life with them, like fights, money and kids, before you get in deeper.
32. You and your date want to take mushrooms or MDMA the first night you sleep together, or your new relationship began at a festival where everyone was high. You wonder whether that's wise.
33. You and your partner keep misunderstanding each other no matter how you explain, and every conversation ends in the same loop. You want to know who to bring in to help, and when.
34. You're afraid that telling your partner the whole truth about how unhappy you are will wreck the marriage. You want to know what honesty can actually do for a relationship in trouble.
35. Your relationship has been bad for a long time, and your partner begs you to try couples counseling. You're not sure you want to stay at all, and wonder whether you owe them counseling first.
36. You and your wife remember the worst events of your marriage in completely opposite ways, and each of you has friends who only hear one side. Counseling went nowhere, and you don't know how to get unstuck.
37. Your relationship just ended badly. Your ex is now apologizing and wants you back, and you're tempted to tell everyone how awful they were. You want to know how to handle the aftermath.
38. A breakup left you feeling like you wasted years. You want a way to see what you actually gained from relationships that didn't last.
39. You've helped raise your girlfriend's kids for years and she just ended things. You wonder whether you should stay in their lives, and how to keep the kids out of the fight.
40. You feel like a failure because your marriage ended, and everyone acts as if it's a private flaw in the two of you. You want an explanation of why modern couples seem set up to struggle.
41. You're a woman, or gay, and you wonder whether advice about relationships written by a straight man will apply to you at all.
42. Before you trust relationship advice from a stranger, you want to know how tested it is and how sure the author is of it.
43. You read about scheduled check-ins, waiting to move in together and talking through imaginary disasters, and think it sounds far too clinical for real love. You wonder whether anyone actually lives like that.
44. You're already married with kids and the problems started years ago. Most advice you find is about how to begin a relationship, and you wonder whether there's anything here for you.
