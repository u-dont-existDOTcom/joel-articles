## 1
QUOTE: In my experience there can also be physical loosening of unification. My percentages are just a playful way of saying how much binding has dissolved.
HARD: “My percentages” appears before any percentage is introduced, “physical loosening of unification” is undefined jargon, and the AN 11.12 sentence just before it (recollecting the Buddha etc.) is never tied to the loosening claim.
SCORE: 3
PLAIN: On top of the recollection route to ease and joy in AN 11.12, I find there can also be a physical loosening of unification, meaning the binding that normally holds all my senses together as one solid experience. I’ll use percentages as a playful way of saying how much of that binding has dissolved.

## 2
QUOTE: It also shows the limit: a living arahant still has preferences and experiences pleasure and pain, but won’t be reborn again. Thus, in this article, saṃsāric means this constructed, dukkha-(suffering/unsatisfactoriness)-capable functioning itself, not only the craving that produces another birth.
HARD: “It” and “the limit” point back to Iti 44 without saying the limit of what, and “Thus” jumps to a new definition of saṃsāric with the reasoning (an arahant is done with rebirth but still has the functioning) left out.
SCORE: 3
PLAIN: Iti 44 also shows the limit of nibbāna while you’re alive: a living arahant still has preferences and still feels pleasure and pain, but won’t be reborn again. So there’s no more rebirth ahead, yet the constructed functioning that can feel pleasure and pain is still running. That’s why, in this article, “saṃsāric” means that constructed functioning itself, the part that’s capable of dukkha (suffering or unsatisfactoriness), and not only the craving that produces another birth.

## 3
QUOTE: Every time someone claims to experience pure awareness (sat chit ananda – being, consciousness, bliss), they can still describe how it feels. So that non-duality is false.
HARD: The jump from “they can still describe how it feels” to “so that non-duality is false” is missing its middle step (if it can be described as felt, there are still two things, not one), and the sentence before it (“awares apart from what it awares”) is hard to parse.
SCORE: 3
PLAIN: Every time someone claims to experience pure awareness (sat chit ananda: being, consciousness, bliss), they can still tell you what it feels like. But if it can be described as something felt, then there’s still a feeler and a felt, so it isn’t really one undivided thing. That’s why I say that kind of non-duality is false.

## 4

## 5
QUOTE: Because we don’t now “exist” as the natural laws themselves, but rather as a fabricated abstraction from them, pain, unsatisfactoriness, and injustice still hurt and still call for a response. But once we can experientially touch ultimate reality and realize the path out of the illusory cycle, those problems don’t hold the same ultimate psychological power over us.
HARD: “Exist as the natural laws” versus “a fabricated abstraction from them” is abstract with no example, the “because” link to pain still hurting isn’t spelled out, “ultimate reality” and “illusory cycle” are undefined, and the next line (“Plato’s cave, but more imminent”) adds another unexplained comparison.
SCORE: 3
PLAIN: Everything is happening according to natural laws, but we don’t live as those laws themselves. We live as a made-up abstraction built out of them, the unified sense of self. Because that’s what we are right now, pain, unsatisfactoriness, and injustice still hurt and still call for a response. But once you can touch ultimate reality (that already-perfect nature) for yourself, and see the path out of the illusory cycle of birth, death, joy, and suffering called saṃsāra, those problems stop having the same ultimate psychological power over you.

## 8
QUOTE: There’s also a strange middle ground where I can have several parallel experiences, each internally bound and therefore not nibbāna, but hinting at it – for example, becoming two different solid birds flying. Ud 5.10 similarly has monastics split into a thousand bodies to clean rooms faster until someone touches one and they merge back into one.
HARD: “Each internally bound and therefore not nibbāna” gives no reason, “two different solid birds flying” isn’t unpacked, and “similarly” doesn’t say what the Ud 5.10 story shares with it.
SCORE: 2
PLAIN: There’s also a strange middle ground where I can have several experiences going on in parallel, like being two different solid birds flying at once. Each of those is still bound up inside itself, so it isn’t nibbāna (nibbāna means unbinding), but it hints at it. Ud 5.10 has something similar: monastics split into a thousand bodies to clean rooms faster, until someone touches one of them and they merge back into one.

## 9
QUOTE: What came apart in my room experience was the fastening of sensory flows into one established consciousness, self, world, and renewed existence. The room looked like it was unweaving, and the suttas’ tangles, knots, and nets make that metaphor feel less accidental.
HARD: “The fastening of sensory flows into one established consciousness, self, world, and renewed existence” is a chain of abstract nouns, with “established consciousness” and “renewed existence” unexplained.
SCORE: 2
PLAIN: What came apart in my room experience was the fastening together of my sensory flows. Normally that fastening makes them into one settled consciousness, one self, one world, and the kind of existence that gets renewed in another birth. In the room it looked like everything was being unwoven. And since the suttas keep talking in tangles, knots, and nets, I don’t think that weaving picture is an accident.

## 10
QUOTE: And it doesn’t take multiple sense streams together to suffer: one stream is enough. Even if all that remained were a single channel of pleasure/pain, that still leaves pain in there. The pain, like a plane, is able to land because there’s somewhere solid to land.
HARD: “One stream is enough” sits oddly with the earlier point that unification gives suffering somewhere to land, so the reader can’t tell how a single channel counts as “somewhere solid,” and the plane image only arrives after the claim.
SCORE: 2
PLAIN: You don’t need several sense streams working together to suffer; one stream is enough. Say all that was left was a single channel of pleasure and pain. Pain would still be in there, because pain is like a plane: it can only land if there’s somewhere solid to land, and even one channel gives it that.

## 12

SCORE: 2

The trouble is only when they keep tangling. “Wise” (or lucky) flows tangle and release freely, a bit like how fields, or the energy strings in String Theory, bind into quantum particles, which then annihilate each other back into stringy energy flows.
