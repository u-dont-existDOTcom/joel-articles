## 1
QUOTE: MN 137 tells practitioners to use unification to relinquish diversity, then non-identification to relinquish unity. In MN 121, at every depth of unification the meditator audits the remaining disturbance and finds “just this much – that which depends on oneness.” Unity itself is the last stress.
HARD: "relinquish diversity", "non-identification" and "audits the remaining disturbance" are abstract and unexplained, "unification", "unity" and "oneness" look like three different things, and two sutta claims plus his conclusion are crammed together with no step showing why unity is "the last stress".
SCORE: 3
PLAIN: MN 137 gives a two-step recipe. First you use unification (gathering the mind into one) to let go of diversity (the mind spread across lots of different things). Then you use non-identification (not taking anything to be “me”) to let go of unity too. In MN 121, at every depth of unification, the meditator checks what disturbance is still left, and what they find is “just this much – that which depends on oneness.” Put together, that says unity itself is the last stress.
