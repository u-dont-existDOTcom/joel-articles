Well, there is the question whether the words have been preserved well, but as they were transmitted communally for quite a while, and we have a number of early collections which have been transmitted separately and which agree to a large extend (that doesn’t mean there aren’t variants, sometimes extensive rearrangements and later additions), the words have come down to us relatively well. Which still leaves the practical problem.

How is a person supposed to benefit from listening to the Buddha's discourse in DN3 and MN56 when the Buddha did not teach until the student was "ready, pliable, rid of hindrances, elated, and confident"? Yet the Buddha's teaching which led the student to gain the Dhamma-eye is presented to any person in sight by a person who is holding up a map of the stages.
