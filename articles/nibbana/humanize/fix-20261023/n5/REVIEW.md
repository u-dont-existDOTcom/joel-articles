# Review of 4 rewritten passages (n5)

## Item 1

1. MEANING: KEPT. "creates" becomes "leads to", but it is the same causal link from "the picture on the box hasn't come down" to the practical problem. Not a coincidence, not weaker or stronger. Nothing lost or added.
2. SENSE: OK. Reads naturally after the "puzzle pieces / picture on the box" line and sets up the new section about maps.

VERDICT: OK

## Item 2

1. MEANING: CHANGED (minor).
   - ADDED claim: "In several suttas, most notably DN 3 and MN 56" widens the scope from the two suttas Joel names to an unnamed larger set, with an implied ranking. PUBLISHED names only DN 3 and MN 56. A reader will take "several suttas" as a new claim.
   - Slightly softened and broadened: "the teaching that opens the Dhamma-eye" becomes "the Dhamma that leads to opening the Dhamma-eye". That is one step more indirect, and "the Dhamma" can read as the whole body of teaching rather than the specific teaching that opens the Dhamma-eye.
   - KEPT: the five-quality readiness quote, the student's mind, and the withholding logic ("waits until ... before giving" and "does not teach ... until" say the same thing).
2. SENSE: OK. The sentence is clear and leads into the question that follows.

VERDICT: CHANGED

Smallest fix: "In both DN 3 and MN 56, the Buddha does not teach the Dhamma that opens the Dhamma-eye until a student's mind is “ready, pliable, rid of hindrances, elated, and confident.”"

## Item 3

1. MEANING: CHANGED (minor).
   - LOST: the "map" image. PUBLISHED says a stage map is what lets people skip, which ties to the section title "A Map Can Save Years". NOW has only "lay out the stages".
   - LOST: "the answer", meaning the withheld teaching from the previous sentence. "skip to the answer" becomes "skip ahead", which can read as jumping forward through stages rather than grabbing the thing the Buddha holds back until the student is ready.
   - ADDED implication: "we lay out ... in a way that allows" makes it the authors' choice of layout and suggests some layouts would not allow skipping. PUBLISHED says a stage map itself lets anyone skip.
2. SENSE: OK. "the stages" has no antecedent inside the paragraph, so a reader might briefly take it as the readiness list (ready, pliable, ...). The section heading ("A Map ...") and the next paragraph (Dharma Overground, "pull the trigger") settle it.

VERDICT: CHANGED

Smallest fix: "What happens when we lay out the stages as a map that lets anyone skip to the answer?"

## Item 4

1. MEANING: CHANGED (minor).
   - Narrowed: "connected everywhere" (connection throughout) becomes "everywhere they touched, they were connected" (connection only at points of contact, close to a tautology). Next to "Though disjoint", it suggests the flows are connected only where they meet.
   - Relation dropped: "connected ... without becoming one experience or one witnessed scene" (connected yet did not merge) becomes a flat, separate "This was not one experience, nor was it one scene being seen by a witness". "This" can be read as the whole event rather than the flows' connection.
   - KEPT: disjoint but not isolated, into and through each other, no one experience, no witnessed scene. Nothing added as a claim ("a witness" just unpacks "witnessed").
2. SENSE: OK. The paragraph reads clearly and leads into "Nothing observed them from behind." It is consistent with AFTER ("no single place from which it was seen").

VERDICT: CHANGED

Smallest fix: "Though disjoint, they were not isolated; the flows ran into and through each other, and they were connected everywhere. That connection did not make them one experience, nor one scene being seen by a witness."
