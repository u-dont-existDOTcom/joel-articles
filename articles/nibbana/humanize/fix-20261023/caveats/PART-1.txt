## Why Did I Write This?
I’m so glad you asked! That shows you’re curious and a deep thinker. 😜
Well, it’s not because I was bored or I like to write! I’ve been infinitely blessed in this lifetime to find the liberation that the Buddha pointed to (Nirvāṇa in Sanskrit, or Nibbāna in Pāli) in a very roundabout and crazy way. I want to share my experience and understandings because if it weren’t for others who shared theirs with me, I would never have gotten to this point. There’s so much confusion about the Buddha’s teaching, but many people have part of the answer. As the saying goes, we stand on the shoulders of giants.
## My Crazy Wonderful Life
Speaking of shoulders, I was lucky to grow up in a very special family. My parents ran a nonprofit called The Aquarian Research Foundation, whose mission was to find out how the planet could have a positive future. You can see my dad’s film Where’s Utopia. You can understand my early life from this pic:
[an image]
Our house was situated right in the middle of a ghetto area in Philadelphia, PA, USA. Our street was between the ultra ghetto behind us and a literal mansion with its own forest on the other side. Every night, there were police helicopters, gunshots, screaming, sirens, murder & mayhem. We had kids throw cherry bombs into our living room every night for like a year until we caught them on camera and showed their parents. Some neighbor kids threw a rock at my head when I was two years old. My mom said she saw the rock swerve away from me at the last second, miraculously. I definitely feel I’ve been protected by angels.
On the other hand, we also had a lot of ghosts in that house and they kept daring me to jump out the windows. My parents had to put bars on the windows to block me from accepting the dare!
[an image]
Around that same age, I told my mom (as she later told me), “I think this is one of my last lifetimes.” She didn’t understand. I also had a recurring dream as a child, in which there was only “vastness” with nothing else in it. It now reminds me of the immaterial meditative absorption the Buddha taught as the Infinity of Space. But my dad, though he was a meditator, never taught me to meditate. So I became more of a materialist as I got older, not interested in spirituality or religion.
## Realizing the world we live in
Like my family, I felt the weight of the world on my shoulders, beginning at the age of 12 when I really started to understand that life is brutal for most people (especially in our ghetto). There was nothing to complain about in my life, but I became convinced that being happy myself, while others weren’t, was selfish. I had this poster in my room that really resonated with me, by the famous socialist, Eugene V. Debs. I cried myself to sleep every night. I told this to a therapist in college and she actually agreed it was a good reason to be depressed!
[an image]
I got into political activism in my later teenage years, and volunteered full-time for the African People’s Socialist Party. That did give me some sense of purpose, but eventually I dropped out of that after visiting Cuba and seeing that socialism, while it did have benefits, was not the full solution to the problems of humanity. Later, I visited the Zapatistas in Chiapas, Mexico, which did actually give me more hope about how humans could live happily (explained in my Community article). But how could we copy their spiritual anarcho-communism without having their culture to start with? It seemed like the first step was to change the education system if I wanted to create a new culture of love.
[a video]
So I then studied Education & History at Temple University, with the goal of helping children in public schools to feel more empowered as agents of change in the world, and teaching them about other possible ways of life for humanity. But when I finally finished my M.Ed. program and started student-teaching at Olney High School, my students threw me a curveball. They said, “Why are you trying to make us think? We don’t want to learn.” Then they slashed my bike tire. Boy was I dumb to waste all those years preparing for an imaginary career!
## Time for A Radical Change of Mind
That’s also when I first tried a drug, at age 26. My friend Emile gave me a puff of weed. It was normal for him, but as it was my first, it gave a mind-blowing synesthesia experience. Colors had texture, numbers had smell, and my life story was being enacted by a cartoon mouse in the center of a stage!
[an image]
What I learned from that crazy trip was that the limits of reality I had thought were fundamental weren’t. He suggested I try magic mushrooms next. So I got some Tampanensis truffles (“philosopher’s stones”) from the Netherlands by mail. That trip started off with uncontrollable laughter, and then, as I sat outside in a hammock in nature, suddenly “everything made sense.” I understood that everyone’s mind is operating just due to conditions. I was able to go into the mind of anyone in history and understand how it worked. Everyone is always making the best decision they know how to make in that moment (which is also something I learned in Re-evaluation Counseling prior, but didn’t grasp at a deep level).
That doesn’t mean you should stop trying to do your best and holding yourself accountable, just because you are doing your best. 😜 Karma (or kamma in Pali) is real, and it’s based on the conditioned intentional state. Homer explains it well:

Oh well, everything may not be perfect for Michael, I guess. Anyway, that taught me once again that the limits of reality were completely unknown to me. I wanted to go deeper, so I googled “most powerful psychedelic.” It came up with “ayahuasca.” I read a trip report on Erowid where a guy drank a huge amount and became the entire universe, with infinite love & vision. I thought, “no.. you can’t be the whole universe. I gotta see that.” So I followed his ridiculously overdosed recipe (my Haoma protocol gives a much nicer trip) and within about 15 minutes of drinking a pot of this tea, I was lying on my bed, realizing some rando on the interwebz tricked me into poisoning myself and I was dying.
I saw infinitely multicolored rainbow mummy aliens dancing hypnotically in front of me and I heard this bird song on loop:
[an image]
I saw my life flash before my eyes like a story flipbook and my head felt like it was about to explode. I was OK with dying at this point, just didn’t want everyone to think I wasted my life’s potential due to stupidity. But I eventually just gave up on life, and that’s when my mind exploded. 🤯🤯🤯
There was no more little me, and no more body. I became the entire universe, seeing everything, being everything, knowing and loving everything! Each universe would expand for billions of years, contract to a point, and then explode. Those explosions were insane!
MN 36 says that on his awakening night the Buddha remembered lives across “many eons of cosmic contraction” and expansion. I hadn’t read it yet, so it didn’t prime me, but the coinkydink was cool.
[an image]
These unity experiences could be insanely euphoric. Heck, unification is arguably the main motivating factor for humanity, if ya know what I mean. Expand that urge across the universe and it can resemble making sweet beautiful love multiplied by infinity. I have spent hours yelling “OH MY GOD” after experiences like that. And it became impossible for me to even kill bugs to this day.
## When the Afterglow Wore Off
But the afterglow only lasted 1-2 weeks, and then it almost felt like a reverse realization would set in: “Oh, all that sense of perfection I had was just because I took a drug. I’m not one with the universe. How dumb.” And then ironically, that lack of belief in the experience would cause me to want to experience it again. So I got stuck on the aya treadmill for some months. It was only after I finally decided “OK, this experience must point to something real regardless of what initiated it,” that I was freed from that loop.
But taking the experience seriously also had its price. If you seriously believe you’ve been the All & Everything, then you can’t be satisfied anymore with a normal life. I ended up breaking up with my otherwise wonderful girlfriend, as I didn’t feel we had anything in common anymore.
[an image]
I had to struggle with a bit of a Messiah complex. I felt it was my mission to spread this experience to everyone on Earth. The ayahuasca told me to go down to Cuba, do aya with Fidel Castro, and then he could get everyone on the island doing it, and then it would spread from there because people would visit Cuba and do aya. I was really gonna execute that plan, but I’m glad I stalled long enough to give up, even though I felt like a failure. Now looking back on it, it seems so absurd. As if everyone would have my experience, and as if there was not already a huge ayahuasca religious tradition in the Amazon jungle area. But that’s what happens when you experience apotheosis I guess.
[an image]
At some point I decided to do a further test: if this experience was really real, I shouldn’t need to take a drug to have it. So I meditated very determinedly while sober, and indeed, I was able to have the same experience that way. So it was decided then…
And yet, despite having access to incredible peak states “at will” (with enough effort & time), I still felt a basic void in my heart. I remember one time, while an ayahuasca trip was coming on strongly, I thought, “I really just want a girlfriend.” Surprisingly, the aya said, “OK, fine, then go find one.” And with that, the trip completely aborted, instantly. It seems like my crown chakra was open, but my heart chakra was only open after an ego death. Could a better girlfriend be the answer for this? Or was I missing something fundamental in my “enlightenment”?
## Was I enlightened or deluded?
Almost everything I found in the modern seeker marketplace confirmed my experience was basically the ultimate one. Browse through the teachers on Buddha at the Gas Pump, and universal consciousness is like the default spiritual answer. Advaita, the Hindu nondual tradition, and its modern neo-Advaita offshoot supplied a complete philosophy for what I’d already experienced. It was beautiful, intoxicating, and comparatively easy: we are all one universal, happy awareness.
[a video]
Many people get stuck at that stage, because it’s a nice one. But I’m a curious cat with less than 9 lives left, and I wanted to know about this guy called Buddha who was strangely absent from Buddha at the Gas Pump interviews!
[an image]
So, I started branching off into Buddhist books to see if I could challenge myself & go further. The Buddha’s habit of breaking experience into aggregates – the five changing components of a person – form, sensation, perception, mind, and consciousness, seemed almost perversely backward. I had found the One. Why was this man taking everything apart?
## 🫶🏿 May All Beings Be Loving, Peaceful & Free 🫶🏿
But one actually useful thing I got from reading all those Buddhist books was the idea that I could actually be of benefit to all beings by radiating love in all directions. So that’s when I started practicing loving-kindness (mettā) meditation. I was astounded at how it cured my depression instantly, and it worked every time. I started connecting it to mantra practice and it was incredible how I could walk down the ghetto streets and even violent crazy people would realize there was no point in fighting me after they felt my vibes! (note: didn’t work for the thugs in Kenya)
[a video]
So then I started preaching that loving kindness meditation + mantra, but a lot of people couldn’t get it to work, because the Buddha’s advice was too old-fashioned.
In Ud 5.1, the Buddha exclaims:
Having gone around in all directions with the mind,
There is surely no one found who is loved more than oneself.
In the same way others each love themselves,
Therefore one who cares for himself should not harm another.
In the modern world, tho, I meet many people who seem to hate themselves! 🥲
It took me many years to finally learn that these folks need to build a loving inner adult before dissolving the cosmic witness (that one universal awareness behind everything). And the Buddha actually hints at this in a new translation of the Mettā Sutta. Interestingly, my first wife was the one who told me she couldn’t let go of ego until she built it up first, although neither of us really understood what that entailed.
