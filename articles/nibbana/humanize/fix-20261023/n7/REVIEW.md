## Item 1

1. MEANING: CHANGED.
- LOST: "pull either away and the other falls" (the collapse consequence of the mutual dependence). "like Jenga" shrinks to a bare "(Jenga!)" hung on "lean against each other", with no stated link (Jenga = pull a piece and it falls; two leaning reed bundles are not a tower). Nothing in NOW now says that removing either bundle topples the other, which is the sutta point WAS carried.
- WEAKENED: WAS says flatly "Part of the problem is that..."; NOW adds "I think", so the claim is less certain.
- KEPT: the Buddha-or-recorders caveat; the drawers/pieces image; Ud 1.10 direct sensations as the gateway; SN 12.44 contact chain building "the world"; SN 12.67 consciousness and mind-and-body as two reed bundles leaning together.
- Minor shifts, not new claims: "left" becomes "stored" (slightly more deliberate); "he talks about / explains" credits the Buddha himself where WAS had the suttas "point to / give / have"; "For instance" and "but" added ("but" frames Ud 1.10 and SN 12.44 as contrasting, which fits "different drawers"); "of the puzzle" added (matches the later "puzzle pieces").

2. SENSE: OK, with one soft spot. It flows from BEFORE ("part of the problem" = no clear account of nibbāna) into DN 15, MN 140 and "the two keep each other standing" ("lean against each other" still sets that up). "(Jenga!)" on its own is cryptic: a reader sees a joke but cannot tell what it maps to, because the falling-when-pulled part is gone. "in a chain in SN 12.44" has two "in"s but is readable.

Smallest fix: drop "I think" (restore "Part of the problem is that..."), and make the last sentence: "He talks about how consciousness and mind-and-body are like two bundles of reeds that lean against each other in SN 12.67 – pull either away and the other falls, like Jenga."

VERDICT: CHANGED
