The problem is, though the Buddha (or whoever wrote down what he said) knew where all these pieces fit together, it looks like he put them in different drawers. So one place, he describes it as it coming from direct sensations (Ud 1.10). Another place (SN 12.44) he says it is the whole chain starting from contact that creates ‘the world’. Another place, he talks about consciousness and mind/body being like two bundles of reeds, leaning on each other, and if one falls, the other falls (SN 12.67; like two jenga towers, leaning on each other). Then there’s the whole DO chain (DN 15). And then there’s going through the different unity states of no-form, and then beyond no-form (MN 140). The truth is, it’s not like Advaita where it comes from consciousness first, and it’s not like materialism where it starts from matter, because as the reeds sutta says, they hold each other up.
