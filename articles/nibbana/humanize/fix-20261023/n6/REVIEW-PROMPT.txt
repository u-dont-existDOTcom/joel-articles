## Item 4 (the first paragraph of the section "Why nibbāna is “awakening”"; the section before it ends on stream entry's "release date" (at most seven more lives))

BEFORE:
Stream entry’s greatest prize is a “release date”: AN 3.88 allows at most seven more lives before final awakening. There is actually quite a lot of modern academic evidence for rebirth. But who’s going around getting reborn if no permanent self exists? Buddhists don’t imagine any permanent self-essence/soul moving between bodies. Rather, rebirth is seen as a causal process: there is a continuity (often called a “mind-stream”), but there is not a “thing” that’s moving from one life to the next. Craving and clinging lead to becoming, and becoming leads to birth. More like a driverless car. The later Buddhist philosopher Milindapañha compared rebirth to one lamp being used to lighting another.

PARAGRAPH WITH WAS:
The lucid-dream comparison from earlier also shapes how I understand the seven-life limit said to be had by the stream-enterer. Once the dream is known as a dream, it becomes naturally difficult to remain stuck in it, similar to how hard it is to be absorbed in a movie when the lights go on and you remember you’re in a theater. If you’ve ever had a lucid dream, you know what I’m talking about.
