# Review of 4 rewritten passages (n6)

## Item 1

MEANING: CHANGED / LOST.
- LOST: "when one thing perceives another" (a perceiver and a perceived, i.e. duality) became "perception of something" (one object, no two-sided relation). That duality is the contrast with the no-footing, undifferentiated awareness just described, and it leads into the later "differentiation" point.
- CHANGED: "established when..." (the occasion) became "established based on..." (consciousness grounded in perception). That is a different dependency claim. "Perception" also reads as a technical term, and it is not a link in the BEFORE chain (eye + forms -> eye-consciousness).
- LOST: "The texts distinguish" (the suttas draw the distinction) became passive "This is distinguished", with no agent. It can now read as Joel's own or an editor's distinction rather than the texts'.
- ADDED: "also viññāṇaṃ". Harmless; it is implied by the paragraph ("Pāli doesn't give me separate nouns") and is not a new claim.

SENSE: OK. "This" could point at the whole previous sentence rather than the non-manifesting awareness, but context settles it. No word stops the reader.

Smallest fix: "The texts distinguish this from ordinary sense-consciousness (also viññāṇaṃ), which is established when one thing perceives another."

VERDICT: CHANGED

## Item 2

MEANING: KEPT. Same speaker (the Buddha), same source (MN 75), same contrast (what others perceive as sensual pleasure, he feels as pain), same use ("So enlightenment isn't a simple bliss switch").
- Trivial shifts: "felt" -> "feels" (past to present; same claim in the usual present tense for what a text says), "ordinary" -> "normal", added "Also,".
- ADDED: "this quote" calls it a quote while the clause is still a paraphrase ("says that..."). Casual wording, not a doctrinal claim.

SENSE: OK. It fits between the AN 11.12 sentence and "So enlightenment isn't a simple bliss switch."

VERDICT: OK

## Item 3

MEANING: KEPT. Dhamma, "now", intuitive versus merely conceptual, and coherence are all there.
- Shifts: one criterion ("feel intuitively coherent") is now two linked sub-questions ("make intuitive sense" + "come across as a coherent picture"). "Feel" became "make sense / come across", which is slightly less experiential. "To you" and "picture" were added.
- "Coherent picture" alone could be satisfied conceptually, but "intuitive" in the first clause and the closing "as opposed to merely conceptual" keep the contrast. No new claim is added.

SENSE: OK. It reads as one self-check question in the list. The dashed aside is clear, and the elliptical "as opposed to merely conceptual" is understandable.

VERDICT: OK

## Item 4

MEANING: CHANGED (mild to moderate).
- LOST: "also shapes how I understand the seven-life limit" (Joel's own interpretive lens) became "there's the idea... and it relates in that...". The personal-reading framing is gone, so the link now reads as a plain statement of what the doctrine "relates" to. That is firmer and less clearly his interpretation. "It" is also ambiguous (the idea or the analogy).
- LOST: "becomes naturally difficult" -> "it's hard". "Naturally" and "becomes" carried the idea that the difficulty is the automatic consequence of knowing it is a dream. Only "once" is left to carry it.
- LOST: "absorbed in a movie" -> "stay in a movie". "Absorbed" (immersion) is gone, and "stay in a movie" could be read as staying in the seat or the theater. The next sentence's "you remember you're in a theater" mostly rescues it.
- KEPT: the hedge ("said to be" -> "there's the idea that"), the stream-enterer, the "seven more lives at most" limit, the lights coming on, and the theater. "Only" is a small added emphasis consistent with BEFORE ("at most seven more lives").

SENSE: OK, with one stumble. "It relates in that..." is vague and makes the reader work out why the seven-life limit follows from the dream point. The WAS wording ("shapes how I understand") signalled that more clearly. The paragraph is still understandable.

Smallest fix: "To follow up on the lucid dreaming analogy I made earlier, there's the idea that the stream-enterer has only seven more lives at most, and that analogy shapes how I understand it: once you know you're in a dream, it naturally gets hard to stay stuck in the dream. It's like how hard it is to stay absorbed in a movie once the lights come on and you remember you're in a theater."

VERDICT: CHANGED
