## Nibbāna is describable but unimaginable
A fish could hear a precise description of air – how it moves and what breathing there is like – and still have no way to imagine being outside water. If nibbāna were imaginable, many people would have already imagined and written this account, and the Buddha would be one more person who imagined something and talked about it. Many psychedelic trips would be nibbāna instead of just unity.
It felt entirely different from the ayahuasca ego deaths: no cosmic orgasms and no screaming. The bliss was deeper, subtler, and vastly more peaceful. I believe the Dhammapada is right to call unbinding the foremost ease – the traditional line often rendered “nibbāna is the highest happiness.”
I have no idea how long this lasted. Time belonged to the unified world that was absent. Eventually, the ordinary body, room, witness, and biography formed again.
## Your fingers are already enlightened, but your hand is deluded.
Some months after that first full nibbāna room experience, I fell asleep in my car while meditating. It was parked, don’t worry! Although I later found out it wasn’t my car.
Before “I” woke up to notice them, my fingers came online all by themselves. Each finger was its own complete experiential flow. I mean it, there were literally 10 distinct, self-presenting, indescribably blissful, and absolutely perfect, finger flows happening at the same time. (These were light flows in finger form—not my anatomical fingers each running a little mind of their own.)
Then “my” mind came back, the perfection vanished, and “I” realized how silly those perfections were. I literally said: “Oh, those are just my fingers!” 😂
That was it, the magic was over.
The finger episode was a small flashback of my full first nibbāna experience of the room itself unweaving, which I analyze further down. Nibbāna is the word the Buddha used to refer to the deathless, perfect reality that’s misapprehended as saṃsāra, the beginningless cycle of birth, death, joy & suffering.
I call the fingers event body-limited nibbāna and the room event body-uncontained nibbāna. Both were full unweavings of unified consciousness; the difference was scope. Nibbāna matters so much to me because the room event became the fruition that I believe launched me into stream entry—the first irreversible stage of Buddhist awakening, where rebirth is now on its way out. I’ll define those terms more carefully below.
## Stream entered
Since that first nibbāna room experience, life has never felt like a Chinese finger trap the way it did before. The self and “my story” lost most of their authority; the Dhamma stopped being a mystery, and I began looking for a book that explained it as well as I could so I wouldn’t have to write this article. Selfishness and suffering dropped dramatically. I never wanted to kill myself again, felt angry and sad less often – even after my first wife tried to kill my family and stole my son – felt less trapped, behaved somewhat less foolishly, and became less inclined to hide things or leave them unsaid.
Paradoxically, seeing that the self I had taken as real did not exist gave me confidence, like realizing inside a dream that it is a dream. It didn’t free me immediately from the marriage or prevent later bad relationship decisions, but I never again felt the infinite desperation of owning an irreparable life.
To me, this was doubtlessly the first stage of enlightenment that the suttas called “Stream Entry.” And I was also convinced that anyone who experienced this must be a stream-enterer. However, I now suspect a full unweaving nibbāna can be had without causing stream entry.

## Spiritual Bypass Warning
## Spiritual realization isn’t enough these days
Because personal suffering had lost so much credibility for me, I preached nibbāna to people whom I later understood often needed inner-child reparenting. Partly because I knew nothing about reparenting nor spiritual bypassing, I treated trauma as an imaginary dream problem. Interestingly, around the same time, I also had a relevant dream, in which I became lucid and realized there was no point even talking to the dream character I was with. She retorted, “I may be just a dream character, but you’re still mean!”🥲 So, I apologize to the folks that I wasn’t such a good spiritual friend to, although I did my best, as we all do.
If you don’t bring the light of awareness and love to your darkness (“shadow work”) and heal your inner child traumas, any other spiritual transcendence you do will become a form of spiritual bypassing, which will turn you into an egotistical asshole like Sadhguru, Teja Anand, or Artem Boytsov.
You can tell when you are listening to fake gurus because their answers to questions are flippantly self-righteous, due to their own avoidance of their feelings. They continually talk from the perspective of some ultimate reality and then denigrate anyone who “doesn’t get it.” Sadhguru even mentioned in one video that anyone who follows him is an idiot, and his followers lapped that up!
We humans love to deceive ourselves, and we almost beg others to deceive us with praise and flattery, while at the same time we decry the fakeness of society.
On the other hand, more genuine “non-gurus” like my friend Eshwar, will explain their own tortuous personal path to awakening, in which shadow work was 99% of the journey.

## What “Already Perfect” Means
Life has its ups and downs. The ups feel great, but the downs are the diamonds in the rough! I’ve always had my deepest realizations and releases when I was fed up with life. What about you? I started thinking about how the average life goes: You have a few birthdays, try to satisfy desires as they arise, watch your health decline, and then die.
Maybe, if you had a good life, you leave the world a better place as well, and those who survive you continue through the same cycle. Then the question becomes: what’s next? A lot of people are afraid that nothing is next – that there is no next. But what if they’re afraid of the wrong thing?
I didn’t believe in rebirth in my last lifetime, and I still don’t. 😛 But if Buddhism is right about rebirth – I return to that evidence later on – life is that beginningless cycle of saṃsāra. A lot of people might be delighted to learn they don’t need to spend $2m per year to live forever like Bryan Johnson, who btw has questioned whether he might be wasting his time with the “Live Forever” campaign.
This question of rebirth became personal after my father died. In the video below, I tell the story of an experience in which I met him in his mid-life transition, and helped him understand why he was headed for rebirth on Earth. He definitely thought everything was already perfect in his heavenly realm, and really didn’t want to come back here!
By “already perfect,” I mean that everything – including our efforts to improve it – is happening according to natural laws. You’ve already tasted a bit of the already-perfect nature if you’ve ever been in a flow state, where you’re no longer consciously directing your efforts but are having more fun & performing better than you otherwise would.
Everything is happening according to natural laws, but we don’t live as those laws themselves. We live as a made-up abstraction built out of them, the unified sense of self. Because that’s what we are right now, pain, unsatisfactoriness, and injustice still hurt and still call for a response. But once you can touch ultimate reality (that already-perfect nature) for yourself, and see the path out of the illusory cycle of saṃsāra, those problems stop having the same ultimate psychological power over you. Similar to Plato’s cave, but more imminent.
I originally stole the title of this essay about 15 years ago from a question asked by a man dying of cancer, on the now defunct FB group of AwakeningToReality.com. As he was in the long process of dying, he thought to himself, “What if everything is already perfect (without me)?” For him, it was a final liberating insight, while for someone at a different stage of life, I’d suggest trying to see how, as the Buddha taught in MN 129, this lifetime is the “perfect human rebirth” because it gives you an opportunity to finally end rebirth!
## Free Will Vs Conditions
Because everything is just conditions, things work and kinda make sense; otherwise, it would be a bunch of random chaos. Even if we don’t fully understand anything, electrons keep orbiting according to quantum rules which eventually collapse unknowable superpositions into predictable paths around atomic nuclei, the toaster predictably toasts, children hopefully grow up (maybe a bad example), and the sun keeps rising and setting. There was never an electron that felt like she was in the wrong orbital and wished she had ended up around a more handsome nucleus (I assume)! So, if everything is so perfect, why do we want things to be different? Maybe it’s just a hilarious misunderstanding!
How would your life be different if, instead of having to drink some whack Amazon jungle plants to get interested in meditation & Buddhism, then marry a psycho and go on a Hero’s Quest to figure this out, you learned in kindergarten that nothing happens apart from conditions?
Actually, if you ask a kindergartner, you might realize they already basically understand conditions before it gets stamped out of them. I’ve seen a number of kids, when parents tell them that “God created the universe,” respond: “Then who created God?”
Libertarian free will is defined as a chooser standing outside all conditions and causing a choice from nowhere. Well, that’s logically impossible. Of course, choices are choicing. And those choices do matter, because they become conditions for what happens next. But there’s ultimately no puppeteer self outside the web of conditions (Indra’s Net) pulling its strings. Meditative training is the web of the universe itself learning to pull more wisely.
Even after the little king or queen fantasy disappears, the mind scurries into a subtler place: "Fine, then I must be the awareness that makes everything real." But that's just pushing the problem of what is real further down the lane. Now there's something being labeled as awareness, which for some reason is more real than something else, even though the awareness needs the thing it's aware of in order to call itself awareness. And those folks call themselves "non-dualists" in the sense of monism, everything is One (Advaita Vedanta). Clearly, that kind of non-duality is an oxymoron, false on the face of it (like my dad's favorite oxymoron: "military intelligence"). What I find instead, and what I believe the Buddha was pointing to, are aware energy flows and the processes that gather them into a self & world.

Not because of “free will,” but because my boss, Oliver, told me to! 🤪 Put another way, “already perfect without you” means experience is complete before anyone owns it.
