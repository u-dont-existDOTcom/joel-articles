# S015 reads

Sentences as numbered:
- S1: Interestingly, both Bāhiya and I were really desperate for an end to suffering right now .
- S2: That seems to have been key for us, although we did have a bunch of preparation as well.
- S3: Some people go to retreats hoping to experience what happened to me.
- S4: I couldn't afford that, but luckily being broke helped more (as it did for the Buddha).
- S5: So did a severely unstable marriage that made me feel I had completely failed at life!

## 1. Needed

UNNEEDED: none.

Notes on what each is for:
- S1 names the starting point (desperation), the section's job.
- S2 says desperation was the key. "a bunch of preparation" implies the reader should know preparation also counted; it names none, so the writer could say what the preparation was or point to where it is covered.
- S3 sets up the contrast that makes S4 make sense (retreats, then "couldn't afford that").
- S4 gives the first concrete cause (being broke).
- S5 gives the second concrete cause (the marriage).

## 2. His fixes

- S1: 21, 10 — "Interestingly" announces (listed as an ai tell); "right now" could mean the moment of the story or the moment of wanting; the stray space before the period is a typo. His way would just state the desperation.
- S2: 4, 10 — "a bunch of preparation" is vague about what counts; "That" could mean the desperation or the wanting an end to suffering.
- S3: 4, 10 — "what happened to me" is a label for the awakening, and the section before told Bāhiya's story, not his, so a quick reader may not know what happened.
- S4: 4 — "helped more" does not say more than what, or how being broke helped.
- S5: 10 — "So did" leaves out the verb, so it could mean "helped more" or "helped" in general; ending on the exclamation is a summing-up tone.

## 3. Fit

- S2: CAN'T TELL — "a bunch of preparation" gives the reader nothing to compare their own situation with.
- S4: FROM yes — "being broke ... as it did for the Buddha": the Buddha is known as a prince who gave up wealth, so "broke" reads as wrong or confusing; MISREAD too, since "luckily" about poverty may sound flippant.
- S5: MISREAD yes — after "luckily", "So did" a failed marriage reads as if a failed marriage is a lucky help, which could sound like advice.
