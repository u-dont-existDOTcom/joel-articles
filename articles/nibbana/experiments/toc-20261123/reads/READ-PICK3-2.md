## Part 2. What gets in the way

- Line 22 "Spiritual Bypass Warning" (0 words) is a bare parent with two children. Its name could mean a warning about teachers who dismiss problems as illusion, or a warning to the reader about their own bypassing. Its first child, "Realization Isn't Enough", doesn't say which.
- Line 21 "The Enlightenment Manual" has only 22 words, and its first child is a 0-word heading. The manual begins with a warning, not a manual. It is also a catch-all: everything from "What's Next After Death?" to "Come and See for Yourself!" sits under it, including theory, history and culture sections that are not manual steps.
- Lines 25 to 44 (death, "Already Perfect", free will, meaning of Nibbāna, volume knobs, experience, contact, light) look like explanation, not manual. They sit between the warning and the practice ("Try a Tiny Bit of Nibbāna", line 54), so someone who wants the practice has to scroll far.
- Line 54 "Try a Tiny Bit of Nibbāna" is a child of "Science Can't Settle This" (line 53, 92 words). The practice is under a science heading, which seems the wrong parent. Lines 55 and 56 follow it there too.
- Line 53 "Science Can't Settle This" can be read two ways: as the science section, or as a caveat that leads into the practice. The practice children make it look like the second.
- Lines 55 "Did It Work or Go Wrong?" and 60 "Vanishing "I": Dangerous?" overlap. A reader in a frightening meditation could go to either, and the second is in the Appendix, far from the first. The Appendix (line 59, 0 words) also doesn't say that it holds safety material.
- Line 47 "AtR’s Authentication Culture": "AtR" is not explained, so a reader can't tell it is an online community, and the heading doesn't say it covers whether to trust a verdict. The apostrophe also differs from the straight ones elsewhere.
- Line 48 "What Is Stream Entry?" and line 24 in the sidebar of "Stream Entered" (line 20): a reader who sees "Stream Entered" and "What Is Stream Entry?" can't tell which has the definition and which the personal story. Line 50 "Am I a Stream-Enterer?" sits under the definition, which is fine, but the author's own claim (line 20) and the checklist (line 50) are far apart.
- Line 16 "The Room Event" says nothing about what happened. It is the author's deepest experience, with three children (17 to 19) of mixed kinds: "Nibbāna Is Unimaginable" (a claim), "Enlightened Fingers" (an episode), "Two Scopes of Nibbāna" (a concept). "Two Scopes" may be a duplicate of "Nibbāna's Volume Knobs" (line 29) and "My Nibbāna Taxonomy" (line 30).
- Lines 28 "What "Nibbāna" Means" and 29 "Nibbāna's Volume Knobs" and 31 "Is Nibbāna an Experience?" and 17 "Nibbāna Is Unimaginable": four headings with Nibbāna in them and no way to tell which is the definition, the grading, the argument and the personal claim.
- Line 8 "Loving-Kindness (Mettā)" is a child of "Enlightened or Deluded?" (171 words), and its topic is not what the parent suggests. A teacher of loving-kindness would not look under this parent.
- Line 14 "Dōgen's Myriad Things" is under "The Bāhiya Sutta" and the name doesn't say it is a Zen teacher's take on the same event. It is only 64 words and easy to miss.
- Line 11 "The Map Past Oneness" and line 12 "The Suttas on Unification" are close: both could hold the jhāna-before-insight question, and "Map" doesn't say whose maps.
- Line 7 "Enlightened or Deluded?" and line 9 "Useless Peak States?" ask similar questions about the same experiences. Line 6 "The Afterglow Wore Off" is a third.
- Line 33 "Awareness Is Plural" is the first child of "Contact Builds a World" (line 32, 181 words). The parent text doesn't show, so a reader sees two lines that sound alike, and "Plural" doesn't say it is a reply to "everything is one awareness".
- Line 34 "Ignorance Is the Root" and 35 "Why the Tangle Is Heavy" and 36 "Binding Makes the Mind" and 37 "Capacity for Suffering": "Tangle" and "Binding" are not defined, and "Capacity for Suffering" may sound like a section on whether feeling no self ends suffering.
- Line 39 to 43 (five light headings) under "Nibbāna as Light Flows": "Sutta Light Language", "Go Into the Light", "Isn't It "Unconditioned"?", "Why Light Isn't Fabricated", "Light Has No Time". The last four are close to each other; physics, photons and relativity don't show. "Go Into the Light" could be the near-death light, and so competes with "What's Next After Death?".
- Line 25 "What's Next After Death?" and line 49 "Practice Outlasts Death" and line 51 "Who Gets Reborn?": three death headings, not obviously different.
- Line 26 "What Is "Already Perfect"?" is the article's title idea but sits mid-manual, and "Free Will vs Conditions" (27) follows it without a visible link.
- Line 44 "What the Books Never Say" and line 10 "Why Monks Stay Vague" say nearly the same thing in two places, one in the story and one in the manual. Line 45 "How the Teaching Faded" is close again.
- Line 46 "The Risk of Skipping Ahead" could mean the risk to a reader who jumps ahead in this article, not the worry about publishing the technique.
- Line 57 "A Cow Killed Bāhiya" repeats line 13 and line 15's Bāhiya theme, and "Bāhiya Sutta" is far from it. "Come and See for Yourself!" (58) is the ending, but the sidebar doesn't say it.
- Line 1 "Introduction & Navigation" and line 2 "Why Did I Write This?" are fine. Line 3 "My Crazy Wonderful Life" (315 words) has no hint that it is the author's life story with ten children.
