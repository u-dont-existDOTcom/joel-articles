## 1. Experiential emotional psychotherapy

### Why this earns space

The article already describes noticing sensations, releasing activation, feeling accompanied, and opening emotional material. Aquatic bodywork supplies loving holding; Brainspotting supplies exploratory attention. Another description of warmth or bodily awareness would add little.

The missing explanation is what happens when a person repeatedly **moves away from emotional engagement itself**: explaining everything, attacking themselves, appeasing the therapist, or becoming ashamed of having a feeling. This gives readers a reason to seek an actively facilitating psychotherapist even when they already know how to notice their body.

The shared function deserves one subsection. The individual brand names can be brief search terms within it.

### Who might investigate which approach?

The following are **provisional questions about fit, inferred from treatment procedures**. They are not a validated assignment test or comparative efficacy ranking.

| Obstacle a reader recognizes | Approach to investigate | What to ask the therapist to explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I understand the story, but repeatedly analyze, withdraw, comply or attack myself when a feeling appears. | Intensive Short-Term Dynamic Psychotherapy (ISTDP) | How would you notice that shift with me, help me approach the feeling, and adjust the work if I become overwhelmed? |
| Feeling or expressing grief, affection, pride or assertiveness seems dangerous, shameful or selfish. | Affect Phobia Therapy (APT) | How would we approach the feared feeling gradually while making its anxiety manageable? |
| I struggle to stay with emotion in a relationship or to take in care when someone offers it. | Accelerated Experiential Dynamic Psychotherapy (AEDP) | How would you help me feel accompanied, notice what happens in my body, and explore a helpful change as it occurs? |

ISTDP's developer describes both direct work on avoidance and a graded, supportive format with little or no challenge initially. APT explicitly applies gradual emotional exposure. AEDP's framework includes bodily tracking, relational support and active defense work. These are overlapping emphases, not exclusive capacities. [ISTDP methods](https://www.psykologtidsskriftet.no/artikkel/2021as09ae-Intensive-Short-term-Dynamic-Psychotherapy-Methods-Evidence-Indications-and-Limitations-) · [APT chapter](https://www.affectphobiatherapy.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Affect-Focused-Short-Term-Dynamic-Therapy.pdf) · [AEDP framework](https://aedpinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/91-Aff-Ch-Ps-9.19.25-with-DESCRIPTIONS.pdf)

### Content brief and word budget

**190–230 words.** Begin with the recognizable obstacle, explain active therapist involvement, then briefly distinguish the three emphases. Include one sentence that these therapies can move between current relationships, bodily feeling and memories, so their position in Layer 3 does not make them a compulsory preliminary stage.

The article should explain challenge and facilitation separately: noticing an avoidance pattern is one action; helping the person experience something differently is another. Warmth can accompany directness. A fixed ranking from gentle to forceful would mislead.

Retain the reader's freedom to slow down, disagree with an interpretation, or refuse an exercise. In particular, do not import a theory that everyone must discover anger or that anger is universally the deepest feeling. That is an editorial fit criterion; treatment-outcome studies do not settle such an emotion theory.

## 2. Imagery rescripting

### Why this earns space

Reparenting, protective/nurturing functions and imagined inner-child contact are already part of the article's purpose. Their absence must not be claimed. The addition gives readers a **specific clinical term and a research-linked route** for obtaining structured help with that work.

### Reader fit and evidential limit

The practical fit is a painful remembered scene in which helplessness or unmet needs remain emotionally active, and a willingness to work imaginatively with protection, care or agency. In the relevant protocol, the therapist initially intervenes within the imagined scene; later the patient's adult self does so.

A randomized trial in 155 adults with childhood-trauma-related PTSD found improvement with both imagery rescripting and EMDR, without significant between-treatment differences. That supports offering a meaningful option; it does not prove exact equivalence, superiority, or the effectiveness of every practice called reparenting. Nor does it justify assigning all shame to rescripting and all fear to EMDR. [Primary trial](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/imagery-rescripting-and-eye-movement-desensitisation-and-reprocessing-as-treatment-for-adults-with-posttraumatic-stress-disorder-from-childhood-trauma-randomised-clinical-trial/A97132EE142BA88DA207E8CCB0044475)

### Content brief and exact placement

**75–100 words.** Explain the imagined intervention, who might want that kind of work, and one linked evidence sentence. Explicitly distinguish deliberate therapeutic imagination from establishing what historically happened.

- Target: **Layer 4: Unstick the Memories You Can Actually Point To**.

## 3. EAET and Pain Reprocessing Therapy

### Why this earns space

The live article already connects emotional stress and physical symptoms. The new contribution is a defined **persistent-pain treatment choice**, with evidence beyond the existing anecdote.

Keep the branch focused on persistent pain that has been assessed. The reader's fear or stress alone cannot establish the source of pain, and these trials do not explain the article's example of acute neurological or cardiac-like symptoms.

### Who might investigate which approach?

**Pain Reprocessing Therapy (PRT)** is worth discussing when assessment supports a substantial pain-amplification component and fear of pain or movement remains central. Its trial used an individualized medical assessment followed by guided work on interpreting and approaching sensations and movement. It also addressed difficult emotions; it is not an emotion-free treatment. The pivotal study concerned selected primary chronic back pain. [PRT trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2784694)

**Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy (EAET)** is worth discussing when persistent pain coexists with avoided emotion, difficult relationships or substantial distress that the person wants to explore. Its procedures include emotional experience, writing and communication practice, together with pain education. An older-veteran trial favored EAET over CBT for pain, but findings cannot be generalized to every pain condition. [EAET trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2819961)

These are overlapping possible targets. The research pass did not identify a direct randomized EAET-versus-PRT comparison. Their separate trials cannot tell a reader which will work better for them.

### Content brief and exact placement

**130–170 words.** Include the assessed-pain scope, the two practical emphases, their overlap, and concise linked evidence. Leave numerical trial tables in the research notes.

- Insert a short, unnumbered section **If persistent pain is part of the problem** after Layer 5.
- Exact preceding paragraph: **Inner-child reparenting is another form of integration therapy, although it’s more all-encompassing and less purely cognitive, so it can fit in this layer as well as beyond it.**
- Following heading: **Optional High-Intensity State-Shift and Release Practices**.
- Use a heading at the same level as the other major, unnumbered sections. This is an indication-specific branch, not a sixth layer or something to postpone until all five layers are completed.
- Avoid placing it immediately after the existing Somatic Experiencing physical-symptoms anecdote; that adjacency would imply an evidential connection the pain trials do not establish.

## Matching evidence to keep in research notes

There is some exploratory treatment-matching evidence, but it is insufficient for a reader assignment rule. In the older-veteran EAET study, baseline distress moderated relative benefit over CBT; depression/anxiety moderation was exploratory, and PTSD moderation was explicitly post hoc in a subsample. This does not select EAET over PRT. PRT's analyses of changes in fear-related pain beliefs concern possible mechanisms during treatment; they do not validate high baseline fear as a rule for choosing PRT.

An independent research reviewer also located a secondary imagery-rescripting/EMDR moderator analysis involving comorbid depression. It remains a follow-up research lead in this proposal; it is not needed for the main recommendation and no depression-based selection rule is proposed.
