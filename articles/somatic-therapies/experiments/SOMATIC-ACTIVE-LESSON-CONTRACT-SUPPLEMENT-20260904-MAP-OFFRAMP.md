## A22 — the five stages apply only when therapeutic processing is actually helping

**Trigger:** any prose presenting the five-stage map or describing movement through it.

**Required behavior:** preserve Joel's new cross-stage option to stop processing for now and return attention to ordinary life. The stages describe kinds/timings of therapeutic work when therapeutic work is useful; they are not a mandate to keep doing therapy.

**FAIL if:**
- prose implies everyone should always locate themselves on a therapy stage and continue processing;
- `stop therapy / stop analyzing / do ordinary life` is treated automatically as failure, denial, or avoidance;
- the off-ramp is omitted where its absence would make the map read as compulsory.

**Also FAIL if:** prose turns the off-ramp into `Stage 0`, `Stage 6`, or another therapy assignment/checklist item. Its point is that sometimes less therapy is the useful move.

## A23 — stopping processing and avoiding processing are both real possibilities

**Trigger:** prose interpreting a person's wish to stop analyzing/processing.

**Required behavior:** preserve both live possibilities without resolving them by formula: processing itself can become an unhelpful rumination loop, and refusing to look can also be avoidance when painful material genuinely needs attention. The article's governing practical standard is what actually increases freedom/function/life participation over time.

**FAIL if:** prose makes `stop processing` automatically healthy or automatically avoidant, or creates a tidy diagnostic checklist pretending to settle the difference mechanically.

## A24 — ordinary life is available throughout the map, not saved for Stage 5

**Trigger:** any Stage 5 or overview prose about `Build a Life`, functioning, exercise, relationships, work, play, rest, routine, or social life.

**Required behavior:** Stage 5 names deliberate integration, differentiation, meaning, identity, values, boundaries, agency, and life direction. It does **not** mean the person waits until Stage 5 to exercise, work, socialize, play, rest, cook dinner, enjoy life, or forget about trauma for a while.

**FAIL if:** the map reads as Stages 1–4 = therapy and Stage 5 = finally live.

## A25 — a real numbered map is allowed; do not add checklist aftercare around it

**Trigger:** `The Five Stages at a Glance`.

**Required behavior:** the article genuinely contains five stages, so a numbered five-item overview is not itself a model-shaped taxonomy error. Keep each item only as detailed as needed to orient the reader to the later section.

**FAIL if:** the genuine five-item map is surrounded by a second layer of model packaging: readiness questions, disclaimer paragraph, evidence caveat, `these can overlap` aftercare, repeated `this is not linear` explanation, recap slogan, or a sixth pseudo-item for the Jules/MCT off-ramp when those functions are already established elsewhere.

The Introduction and early Jules/MCT paragraph already carry the flexibility/anti-compulsion context. Trust the reader.

## A26 — Stage 1 `Keep Choice` includes choice about whether to process at all

**Trigger:** overview or Stage 1 prose explaining `Stay Present and Keep Choice`.

**Required behavior:** stopping/orienting/return capacity is not only a prerequisite for deeper work. `Choice` also means retaining enough agency to decide that the useful next move is to continue, change dose, change method, or stop therapeutic processing for now.

**FAIL if:** Stage 1 becomes only `build capacity so you can progress to Stage 2`.

**Required behavior:** correct material errors, but do not expand a stage gloss into specialist protocol taxonomy merely because the named modalities differ in details. Each overview line should communicate the reader-relevant function of that stage.

**FAIL if:** precision obscures the map's practical distinction or re-creates the previously owner-rejected clause-by-clause defensiveness.
