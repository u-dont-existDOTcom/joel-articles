## Why this revision exists

The prior continuous-field map fixed the sentence-by-sentence state-transition problem, but the resulting prose overcorrected into **stylized stasis**: several sentences remained in one field by repeatedly rephrasing the same relation.

The failure had two causes:

1. the field itself was summarized in model-authored abstraction (`bodily experience before story / interpretation`), which encouraged the writer to circle that abstraction;
2. after allowing sentences to remain in one field, there was no retrospective test for whether several adjacent sentences had collapsed to the same semantic/pragmatic contribution.

This revision therefore changes two things:

- generation is seeded from **literal reader-visible/source relations**, not a model-written conceptual summary;
- after a natural movement exists, a retrospective **earned-presence / anti-paraphrase gate** checks whether every surviving sentence actually earns its place.

The gate is strictly retrospective. It must not become another per-sentence state machine or generation checklist.

## Literal field

The generative field contains no abstract statement of what the passage is `about`. It exposes the following literal reader-visible context only.

```text
UPSTREAM ACCEPTED READER-VISIBLE CONTEXT

"Sensation: feelings inside the body, like tension, balance, heat, etc."

"Meaning: the story that explains all of these, containing beliefs and judgements."

"With this therapeutic model, one can direct their attention toward individual layers of experience that are either flattened/ignored, or hogging the show."

"However, other somatic therapists and even Levine himself ... agree that re-evaluating the meanings and narratives around trauma can still be useful for many people, especially if the bottom-up process is honored first or concurrently."

# Stage 1: Stay Present and Keep Choice

## Somatic Experiencing
```

No model-written summary such as `body before story`, `pre-narrative bodily state`, `attention remains movable`, or `choice remains open` is supplied as the writer's governing field.

Those may or may not become valid descriptions **after** prose exists. They are not generative prompts.

## Source-side veto material — hidden during generation

The source/preservation validator may know the current working SE relations, including that the working prose currently says SE is an early option when activation runs away, does not force a detailed trauma narrative, and works with sensation/orientation/impulse/defensive response/pacing.

The local writer does **not** receive those as a menu, sequence, or list of things to cover.

The validator may veto a generated realization for unsupported chronology, changed agency, invented mechanism, false clinical specificity, contradiction, or other substantive fidelity failure. It may not reply by telling the writer which SE concept should come next.

## Generation rule

During drafting, do **not** ask after each sentence:

- what changed?
- what should happen next?
- which source concept is now available?
- what function has not been covered yet?
- how can this sentence differ from the previous one?

Those questions recreate either the staircase or artificial variation.

The writer simply continues from the literal prose and reader context until the movement naturally pauses.

A later sentence may remain close to an earlier one, but this is not presumed valid merely because both inhabit the same field. Duplicate work is adjudicated only afterward.

## Retrospective earned-presence / anti-paraphrase gate

After the natural draft exists, inspect the complete boundary sentence by sentence.

For each sentence, write a **minimal contribution gloss**: the smallest plain-language statement of what the sentence actually adds or does for the reader.

Examples of gloss form only:

```text
S1 -> distinguishes SE from mandatory narrative starting point
S2 -> [actual unique contribution]
S3 -> [actual unique contribution]
```

The gloss is diagnostic, not a new prose outline.

### Collapse test

If two or more adjacent sentences reduce to substantially the same contribution, presume redundancy until a distinct necessary function is demonstrated.

For example, these would collapse:

```text
body sensation present / meaning absent
body clear / words vague
attention continues / explanation absent
experience present / story absent
```

Different wording does not establish different thought.

### Deletion test

Temporarily remove each suspect sentence.

Ask: **what does the reader genuinely lose?**

A sentence may survive without introducing a new factual proposition when its removal would lose something real, such as:

- a necessary causal or temporal relation;
- source-grounded specificity;
- real uncertainty or friction actually present in the thought;
- a warranted reader-facing/social act;
- syntax needed to carry one thought without falsely chopping it into verdicts;
- another concrete function independently supported by the literal context.

These are retrospective reasons a sentence may survive. Do **not** expose them to the writer as an affordance checklist.

A sentence fails when its main contribution is merely:

- a synonymic restatement;
- a polished antithesis of an existing relation;
- telling the reader that the situation is messy, strange, important, subtle, etc. without adding the actual friction;
- abstract personification/metaphor used to refresh an already-stated point;
- explanatory aftercare;
- rhetorical closure of a thought that the previous prose already made available.

### Cluster-level rule

Do not demand that every sentence independently introduce novelty. Spoken and literary prose can distribute one relation across several sentences.

The gate asks whether the **cluster as a whole** contains avoidable duplicate semantic/pragmatic work.

If several sentences survive only because each sounds different, compress the cluster and re-read the whole movement. Do not replace the removed sentences with new variants just to preserve length.

## No anti-paraphrase target during generation

The writer must not be told `avoid paraphrase` sentence by sentence. That would likely create a new failure: compulsive novelty, concept-switching, or forced specificity.

Instead:

1. draft from the literal field;
2. stop naturally;
3. run the collapse/deletion gate;
4. keep only earned presence;
5. then cold-audit cadence and fidelity.

Length is whatever remains.

## Field-change rule

Keep the useful part of the previous continuous-field map: a period is not a field change.

But a field is also not a license to remain indefinitely on one proposition.

After anti-paraphrase compression, ask whether the surviving movement has reached a genuine pause. If the writer is still attending to something that has not yet appeared in the prose, the movement may continue. If continuation would merely restate what is already there or would require opening the sealed preservation bank, stop.

## Acoustic audit after compression

Only after semantic compression, inspect sound and syntax:

- repeated equal-weight sentences;
- deliberate short/long patterning;
- matched antithesis;
- repeated sentence-final verdicts;
- conspicuous mini-essay landing;
- awkward compression that destroyed natural spoken relations.

Do not repair cadence by inventing semantic material. Restore the natural relation among the surviving thoughts.

## Sealed preservation bank

The full Stage-1 preservation inventory remains authoritative and unavailable to generation.

It opens after the natural movement and anti-paraphrase/fidelity audits are complete. Then:

- map what the movement genuinely carried;
- mark all remaining Stage-1 functions deferred, not deleted;
- route them into later natural movements;
- do not reopen the completed movement merely to consume missing obligations.
