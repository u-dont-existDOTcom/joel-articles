## Explicit cadence-score representation

Dimensions:

- duration: short / medium / long / sprawling;
- closure: open / partial / landed;
- relation: continue / deepen / interrupt / detour / return;
- pressure: rise / suspend / release;
- syntax: simple / accumulating / embedded / trailing;
- paragraph role: opening pressure / development / detour / consequence / stop.

The target is a pressure contour, not a sentence-length recipe. A model candidate fails if the nominally varied lengths still perform repeated `state -> explain/judge -> stop` jobs.

## Provisional candidate

### Somatic Experiencing

Somatic Experiencing is probably where I'd start when the body reacts faster than you can choose. You don't have to tell the whole story; you notice what is happening and see whether you can stay close to it without disappearing into it, then look around, come back, maybe go toward it again. Titration and pendulation are names for that back-and-forth, while orientation is basically the part where you remember the room you are in now. The preparation can become a trap, though. If one target is clear and you can stay with it, months of proving that you're ready may just postpone the thing you came to treat. Sometimes even the idea of a target is too far ahead: the child-state has swallowed everything, neither Nurturer nor Protector is available, and I would be trying to get another person involved, borrow some adult perspective—even from a neutral witness—and do one ordinary Protector thing before worrying about the memory. Gentle self-hypnosis can fit here too if it keeps you in the present instead of taking you deeper, and sometimes coming back means deciding you don't want to go in again today.
