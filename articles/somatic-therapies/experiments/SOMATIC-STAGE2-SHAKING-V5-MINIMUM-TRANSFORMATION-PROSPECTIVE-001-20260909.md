## [**Shaking Qigong**](http://shakingclass.innersignalselfhypnosis.com/) / Shaking Medicine

> Shaking Qigong is basically shaking plus qigong. I’m linking to a $10-per-month Skool class my friend Louka took because he had already tried many different healing and somatic modalities, including basic TRE, without getting much help. This was the one that finally helped him.

If you want a more active way of moving what you found, or you aren't feeling much from SE in the first place, you can try TRE or Shaking Qigong. You should still be able to stop, orient, and settle afterward.

A regular practice might run 10–45 minutes, especially for chronic freeze, non-verbal emotional processing, stress that accumulates between therapy sessions, or leftover activation after a deeper session.

Possible non-qigong explanations include rhythmic movement, reduced muscular armoring, greater awareness of bodily sensation, a shift out of fight-or-flight, release of chronic tension, and completion of interrupted defensive impulses. In qigong, *qi* means vital energy, and the same shaking is described as removing energetic stagnation, increasing qi circulation, dissolving stored emotional charge, and reconnecting breath, body, and movement.

What I would not do is turn shaking into a catharsis contest, treat it as the primary trauma-processing modality for everybody, or use it as a substitute for skilled help when somebody is highly dissociative or unstable.

## After Shaking

Afterward, EFT, massage, restorative yoga, gentle yin, etc. could help with grounding or a softer physical exploration of the body.

With yin I keep the same caveat from Stage 1: prop it, keep the holds shorter when necessary, and leave the pose if it starts feeling coercive. A shorter hold your body can actually tolerate may be more useful than an ambitious five-minute shape.
