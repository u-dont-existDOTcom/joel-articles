`Titration, pendulation, and orientation are all ways of asking: can you touch something difficult in a small enough dose, notice what happens, and come back?`

This is the strongest existing source-first opening material.

Why it survives provisionally:

- it is specific to Somatic Experiencing rather than generic mindfulness;
- it develops the Stage-1 heading instead of paraphrasing it;
- it gives the reader an actual practical relation: approach in a bounded dose and return;
- it does not reopen the completed meaning/narrative discussion;

- the technical terms are immediately glossed by the reader-facing question rather than packaged into separate definitions;
- it is already current working prose, so no new lexical/provenance delta is introduced by selecting it.

Residual risk: three named techniques in one opening sentence can still read like terminology packaging. However, the sentence gives them one shared practical meaning rather than three equal mini-definitions, and deleting the names would alter substantive source content rather than merely remove aftercare.

Disposition: **provisional survivor / use as the next source-first opening seed for owner judgment and larger-boundary audit.**
