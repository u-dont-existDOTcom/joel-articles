## 1. EMDR / trauma-focused CBT are not all simply exposure therapies

Owner text says:
`All of these are exposure therapies.`

Finding: **needs correction.**

- VA/DoD and NICE group PE, CPT/trauma-focused cognitive therapies, and EMDR under strongly supported **trauma-focused psychotherapies**, but EMDR is listed separately from trauma-focused CBT and is not normally classified simply as an exposure therapy.
- Prolonged Exposure is explicitly exposure-based.
- Trauma-focused CBT is a family including CPT, cognitive therapy for PTSD, narrative exposure therapy, and PE; these methods differ in the role and form of exposure and cognitive restructuring.
- EMDR targets traumatic memories while using bilateral stimulation and includes negative/positive cognition, affect, physical sensation, body scan, closure, and reevaluation.

Editorial implication: change the umbrella from `exposure therapies` to `trauma-focused therapies`; distinguish PE/exposure from cognitive therapies and treat EMDR as a bridge case rather than forcing it into the top-down/exposure bucket.

## 2. `slowly increase exposure ... then CBT goes further after that` overgeneralizes the protocols

Finding: **needs correction.**

- This is a fair sketch of PE's gradual approach to avoided memories/situations but not an accurate common sequence for all trauma-focused CBT or EMDR.
- NICE describes trauma-focused CBT as involving trauma-memory processing, trauma-related emotions, meaning restructuring, and overcoming avoidance; these are components of treatment rather than necessarily `exposure first, regular CBT after`.
- EMDR has its own eight-phase protocol rather than the proposed exposure-then-CBT sequence.

Editorial implication: preserve the owner's top-down critique by naming concrete examples rather than assigning one mechanism/order to the whole evidence-based set.

## 3. Dropout/re-traumatization critique is directionally usable but current wording is factually wrong

Owner text says:
`exposure therapy can itself be re-traumatizing, which isn't captured in the positive evidence, because drop-outs from the studies aren't studied.`

Finding: **repair, do not discard the critique.**

- Dropout is studied and meta-analysed. A 2020 systematic review/meta-analysis found pooled dropout of 16% across PTSD psychotherapy RCTs and 18% in trauma-focused versus 14% in non-trauma-focused therapies.
- Critically for Joel's point, the same review found that reasons for dropout and adverse events were **not universally or consistently reported**, so it could not meta-analyse why people left; it explicitly says the evidence base often provides few explanations for dropout and limited adverse-event data.
- A 2024 meta-analysis found no evidence of overall PTSD symptom exacerbation at mid-treatment from trauma-focused therapies, while acknowledging that very early exacerbation and people who dropped out before assessment could be missed.
- Payne/Levine/Crane-Godreau's SE theory explicitly describes renewed failed defensive activation as a possible route to `re-traumatization` and contrasts SE's titration/pendulation with more confrontative exposure.

Editorial implication: attribute re-traumatization risk to Levine/SE's model rather than present it as settled general evidence; replace `drop-outs aren't studied` with the stronger accurate point that dropout is measured but **reasons/adverse experiences leading to dropout have often been poorly characterized**.

## 4. SIBAM is sound; `root causes` and exact coupling language need precision

Owner SIBAM expansion—Sensation, Image, Behavior, Affect, Meaning—is correct in substance.

Finding: **minimum precision edits.**

- SIBAM is a model of experiential channels / information processing and dissociation.
- Dissociation can be described as fragmentation/undercoupling among channels.
- Overcoupling can bind channels so tightly that one triggers the others and is associated with hyperarousal/hypervigilance; avoid defining all hyperarousal simply as `the layers are too fused`.
- Dissociation and hyperarousal are better described as manifestations/patterns of dysregulation rather than themselves `root causes of dysfunction`.
- `Behavior` includes voluntary/involuntary behavior, posture, gestures and defensive action tendencies; interrupted/aborted defensive responses are especially important in Levine's trauma model but not the whole definition of the B channel.
- Keep affect and raw somatic sensation more distinct where possible.

Editorial implication: preserve the list and cadence; change only the claims around it.

## 5. `meaning often handles itself` is unusually well supported; EMDR comparison is the weak part

Owner text says that after bodily discharge the meaning layer often handles itself, while later cognitive reevaluation can still help.

Finding: **strongly supported by the cited SE paper.**

Payne/Levine/Crane-Godreau state that cognitive re-evaluations often emerge spontaneously during or shortly after autonomic/kinesthetic discharge and that cognitive restructuring becomes easier as CRN functioning normalizes. They also explicitly say faulty cortical appraisal sometimes should be addressed with methods reminiscent of conventional cognitive restructuring.

Owner text then says EMDR uses the body `more as a barometer of distress rather than as the intelligent instrument of self-healing.`

Finding: **needs conceptual correction.**

- EMDR's Adaptive Information Processing model itself posits an innate information-processing/healing system.
- EMDR tracks images, beliefs, emotions, physical sensations and uses a body scan; it expects new associations and adaptive meanings to emerge during reprocessing.
- Therefore `body as barometer rather than intelligent instrument of self-healing` is too reductive.

Editorial implication: treat EMDR as a **bridge case**: memory-targeted and highly structured, but not cleanly top-down; its AIP theory shares the idea that adaptive processing can unfold rather than all meaning being argued into place.
