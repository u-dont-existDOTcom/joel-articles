**As it would read:**

> ## Can't Stay With a Feeling?
>
> If you can feel your body just fine by now but you still slip away the moment a feeling comes up in a session (you start explaining it, or agree with whatever the therapist says just to get past it), there's [Intensive Short-Term Dynamic Psychotherapy](https://psykologtidsskriftet.no/debatt/2021/09/intensive-short-term-dynamic-psychotherapy-methods-evidence-indications-and). The ISTDP therapist catches you each time they notice you slipping, and asks what you're feeling right then.
>
> It's usually anxiety about the feeling that makes you slip away, so the therapist also watches for its signs, like tense muscles, sighing, or your thinking going foggy, to tell when it's getting to be too much for you. If you can only take a little of that anxiety, like most people who try this therapy, you'd get a slower version of it, where the therapist stops to help you settle whenever the anxiety climbs.
>
> If it's one feeling in particular that scares you, like grief, [Affect Phobia Therapy](https://psykologtidsskriftet.no/node/15971/pdf) treats that fear a lot like a phobia, getting you used to the feeling bit by bit, at a pace your anxiety can handle.
>
> And if what you can't let in is kindness, when someone cares about you and it just bounces off, there's [Accelerated Experiential Dynamic Psychotherapy](https://www.apa.org/pubs/videos/4310759). The AEDP therapist stays with you through whatever comes up. When some of their kindness does get in, they ask what that's like and where you feel it in your body.
>
> All three keep steering you back toward your feelings, which can get to be too intense sometimes. So before booking, I'd ask the kind of thing I'd ask a water therapist: "What happens if I get overwhelmed, or if you say I'm dodging a feeling and I don't think I am?" I'd want to hear that they would change the approach, listen to me and be gentler.
