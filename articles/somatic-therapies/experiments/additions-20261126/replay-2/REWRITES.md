## A1-P1

Maybe you can feel your body just fine by now, but you still slip away the moment a feeling comes up in a session: you start explaining the feeling, or you agree with whatever your therapist says just to get past it. For that there's [Intensive Short-Term Dynamic Psychotherapy](https://psykologtidsskriftet.no/debatt/2021/09/intensive-short-term-dynamic-psychotherapy-methods-evidence-indications-and) (ISTDP). Each time you slip away like that, the ISTDP therapist points it out, then asks what you're feeling right then.

## A1-P5

All three of these therapies keep steering you back toward your feelings, so at times a session can bring up more feeling than you can take. That's why, before booking, I'd ask the kind of thing I'd ask a water therapist: "What happens if I get overwhelmed, or if you say I'm dodging a feeling and I don't think I am?" I'd want to hear that they'd stop and help me settle before going on, and that when I disagree they'd ask me what I think is happening instead of pushing their own read.

## A3-P1

If your pain has lasted months, though a doctor has checked and found no injury or disease to explain it, there are two therapies I'd look into. In the first, [Pain Reprocessing Therapy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8482298) (PRT), you learn to treat the pain as a false alarm. The pain is real, but your brain is making it, signaling danger where the doctors haven't found any injury. You learn it's a false alarm partly by paying close attention to how the pain feels in your body, without being scared of it. Then, as you're able, you slowly go back to things you've been avoiding because of the pain, like going for walks.

## A3-P3

The second therapy, [Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03918642) (EAET), is for pain that seems bound up with stress, or with anger and other feelings you've held back from the people in your life. In EAET you spend much of the time letting those feelings out. Then you rehearse in role-play what you'd say to someone you've never stood up to.

## A3-P4

For older veterans, EAET eased pain better than CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy). In a 2024 trial of veterans aged 60 and up, 63% of those who got EAET had their pain drop by at least 30% afterward, compared to 17% with CBT. A 30% drop is a lower bar than getting your pain down to 0 or 1 out of 10, which is what PRT's trial counted. So you can't compare EAET's 63% with PRT's 66%.

## IC2

If the tests keep coming back normal while that investigation goes on, I'd also try one of the two talk therapies I go into in [my somatic guide](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/201523223/pain-that-wont-quit): Pain Reprocessing Therapy (PRT) or Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy (EAET). Both treat the pain as real. In PRT you learn to read it as a false alarm from your brain, then slowly go back to what you've been avoiding. In EAET you let out anger and other feelings you've held back. Both have helped people with long-lasting back, muscle and joint pain.
