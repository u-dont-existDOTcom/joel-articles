# READ-NEEDED-FIT-3

## 1. Needed read

A1 NEEDS (for: a reader who slides away from feelings deciding whether one of these therapists fits)
- S1: lets the reader recognise their own pattern (explaining, agreeing) and decide "that's me".
- S2: says what to look for: a therapist who catches the slide and keeps you with the feeling.
- S3: shows what ISTDP does, so the reader can judge whether being interrupted at each slide suits them.
- S4: implies an act: if you overwhelm easily, ask for the slower, supportive version.
- S5: for a reader with one feared feeling (grief), shows Affect Phobia Therapy is the gradual-approach one.
- S6: for a reader who deflects care, shows AEDP is the one that starts with not being alone.
- S7: permission to try this now instead of waiting to finish earlier layers; "overlap a lot" gives nothing to do.
- S8: the question to ask a therapist before booking.
- S9: the rule for deciding after the answer: room to slow down and disagree, or keep looking.
UNNEEDED A1: S7 first clause "These overlap a lot," (rest of S7 is needed).

A2 NEEDS (for: a reader with a childhood scene that still leaves them helpless deciding whether rescripting fits)
- S1: says who it is for (one scene, still helpless) and what it is.
- S2: shows what you would do in a session and who protects you, and links to the inner child guide.
- S3: evidence it works about as well as EMDR for childhood-origin PTSD (155 adults, neither ahead).
- S4: implies an act: you can do it without an accurate memory. It does not say that, and it does not say it is the same re-entry into the memory that the EMDR paragraph warned about (act missing: go slowly, ground afterward).
UNNEEDED A2: none.

A3 NEEDS (for: a reader with months of pain, nothing found, deciding whether one of two therapies fits)
- S1: confirms the reader is the target (months, doctor found nothing).
- S2: shows what PRT asks of you (feel pain as a false alarm, resume avoided activities).
- S3: evidence for PRT (151 adults, 66% vs 20% placebo, held at a year).
- S4: shows what EAET asks of you and who it suits (pain tangled with feelings and relationships).
- S5: evidence for EAET (63% vs 17% CBT, older veterans, 30% drop not pain-free).
- S6: how to choose: one that sounds like your pain, then judge it; "nobody has compared" says do not expect a winner. No act for checking the therapist is trained in it.
UNNEEDED A3: none.

Findings in part 1:
- A1-S7: "These overlap a lot," gives the reader nothing to do; cut it ("They also work with memories and relationships, so you don't need to finish this layer first.").
- A2-S1/S2: implies re-entering the memory but not the caution; add a clause tying to the EMDR paragraph above ("like EMDR it goes back into the memory, so go slowly and ground afterward").
- A3-S6: no pre-booking step like A1-S8; add "ask whether they have trained in it."

## 2. Fit read

- A1-S7: "this layer" and "finish ... first" are unclear (which layer, finish before what); say "you don't need to finish the earlier layers before trying one."
- A1-S5: "the way a fear of spiders gets treated" imports a phobia-clinic picture; the guide calls exposure re-traumatizing; add "slowly, and you can stop".
- A2-S4: "it can heal without telling you what really happened" reads as the scene healing and as claiming the story doesn't matter; say "so you don't need a clear or exact memory of what happened."
- A3 (whole): follows from Layer 5 only loosely; the section before is beliefs, stories and forgiveness and pain hasn't been the topic; add one bridging clause to S1 ("The same rethinking and feeling-work is used on pain").
- A3-S5: a reader not an older veteran can't tell if it applies; add "(in older veterans; less is known for others)".

## 3. Contradictions

- A2-S4 "it can heal without telling you what really happened back then" vs Layer 4 intro "put it back together in a healthier way, while still being honest about what happened". Fix: say rescripting changes the felt meaning and the true event stays as it was.
- A1-S5 "brings you closer to it a little at a time, the way a fear of spiders gets treated" vs "exposure desensitization therapy can itself be re-traumatizing". Fix: add the S9-style slow-down option here.
