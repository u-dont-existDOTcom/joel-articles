# READ-HIS-FIXES-11 (draft 11: the new and changed sentences)

MARKED:
- A1-S4: 13 — "the anxiety that's making you slip away" comes in as if already said (S2 and S3 never name anxiety), and the sentence doesn't say what the watching is for until S5. His way: "What makes you slip away is anxiety, so the therapist also watches for its signs, like tense muscles, sighing, or your thinking going foggy, to tell when it's getting to be too much for you."
- A1-S5: 5 — the paragraph turns from "you" to "most people ... they", leaving the reader outside it, and it can read as "most people can't take this therapy". His way: "If you can't take much of that anxiety, and most people who try this therapy can't, you'd get a slower version, where the therapist stops to help you settle whenever it climbs."
- A3-S3: 1, 10 — four things in one sentence (real, false alarm, what a false alarm is, how you learn it); "partly by paying close attention" hangs off the end where it reads as how the brain signals, and "it" could be the pain or the alarm. His way: "Pain Reprocessing Therapy teaches you that although your pain is real, it's a false alarm, meaning your brain is signaling danger where there isn't any. You learn that partly by paying close attention to how the pain feels in your body."
- IC1-S1: 10 — "steps into the memory with you as the adult you needed" can be read as you being the adult; then "do that" has two things to point at. His way: "... called Imagery Rescripting, where a therapist steps into the memory alongside you and acts as the adult you needed, until you learn to be that adult yourself."
- IC2-S2: 7 — the reader gets two long names and trial results but never what kind of thing these are (pills? bodywork?), so can't tell whether to look. His way: say it in the same sentence with words the somatic guide already has: "In both, you work with a therapist on the false alarm your brain keeps sounding. They treat the pain as real, and in trials they've helped people with chronic back pain and other muscle and joint pain." (Check "false alarm" fits the second therapy before using it; otherwise "Both are therapies you do by talking and feeling, with a therapist".)
