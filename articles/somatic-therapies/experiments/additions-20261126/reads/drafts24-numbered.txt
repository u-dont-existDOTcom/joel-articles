## IC1, in "Re-do Trauma Memories (inner child guide)"
Where: a paragraph of its own at the end of the section, after his J. paragraph ("... it might take years to imagine that correction."), before the heading "How should discipline have been done right?"
The sentence before it (his): Whatever the situation was, we will contrast it with the corrected version. For my friend J., whose dad savagely beat him every time he smiled, I'd have the corrected version be, he smiles, and his dad smiles, happy to see him happy. Ok, that one might seem too easy, although for J, it might take years to imagine that correction.
IC1-S1: If a memory feels too big to redo on your own, there's an actual clinical therapy for that, called Imagery Rescripting, which I only found out about after I wrote this section.
IC1-S2: The therapist steps into the memory as the ideal adult, protecting and nurturing your inner child, until you learn to do that yourself.

## IC2, in "Physical Pain? (inner child guide)"
Where: a paragraph of its own at the end of the section, after his "I'd ask for a chronic pain specialist ..." paragraph, before the heading "Listen To Your Body, Or Else!"
The sentence before it (his): I'd ask for a chronic pain specialist and a written plan for the flare-ups you can see coming, including the new or severe signs that mean you go to the ER right away, but don't let amelioration of symptoms be the end of the investigation.
IC2-S1: If the tests keep coming back normal while that investigation goes on, I'd also try one of the two talk therapies I go into in my somatic guide, Pain Reprocessing Therapy or Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy.
IC2-S2: Neither one sees the pain as imaginary, and they've helped people with long-lasting back, muscle and joint pain.
