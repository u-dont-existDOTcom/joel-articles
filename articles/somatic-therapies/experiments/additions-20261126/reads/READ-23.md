## Against his fixes
MARKED:
- IC2-S1: 10 — "which both treat the pain as real and have helped people with long-lasting back, muscle and joint pain". Draft 22 had "between them they've helped"; the join dropped "between them", so "both" now carries over to "have helped" and the sentence says each of the two therapies has helped with all three kinds of pain. That is the quick-read problem READ-21 pointed out and draft 22 had fixed, and it goes against A3-S4 (see Contradictions). Rewrite, with only those two words put back: "If the tests keep coming back normal while that investigation goes on, I'd also try one of the two talk therapies I go into in my somatic guide, Pain Reprocessing Therapy or Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy, which both treat the pain as real and between them have helped people with long-lasting back, muscle and joint pain."

## Needed, fit, contradictions
1. Needed: both are needed.
   - A3-S3 says what you actually do in the first therapy. Without it S2's "you learn to treat the pain as a false alarm" has no how.
   - IC2-S1 is the whole of the addition: its first half tells a reader of the inner child guide when to follow the pointer and where, and its "which" clause gives them a reason to ("as real" answers that guide's worry that normal tests mean the pain is imagined).
2. Fit: none for A3-S3; one MISREAD for IC2-S1, the same point as the mark above.
   - A3-S3, "without being scared of it", was checked for CAN'T TELL (can you stop being scared because a sentence says so?). It describes what you practise with the therapist, under S2's "you learn", so it reads as the thing learned and not as an order.
   - A3-S3, "things you've been avoiding", was checked for CAN'T TELL. A reader with pain knows what they've been avoiding, and "like going for walks" gives the size of the thing meant.
   - IC2-S1: MISREAD yes. A reader with joint pain takes "both ... have helped people with ... joint pain" to mean either therapy has been shown to help their kind of pain, and the draft only supports that for back pain in the first. "between them" removes it.
3. Contradictions: one, in IC2-S1.
   - IC2-S1 ("which both ... have helped people with long-lasting back, muscle and joint pain") against A3-S4 ("Pain Reprocessing Therapy's first big trial was only in mild to moderate chronic back pain"): as joined, IC2-S1 says the first therapy has helped with muscle and joint pain too, and A3-S4 is the writer's own checked statement that its evidence is back pain only. READ-22 passed this pair because of "between them". Put those words back and the two agree again.
   - The draft still doesn't say what kind of pain the veterans in A3-S9 had, so "muscle and joint" rests on a fact the draft doesn't state. As far as I know that trial was in chronic musculoskeletal pain, which would cover it, but I didn't check the source here. Nothing in the draft contradicts it.
   - IC2-S1 ("treat the pain as real") against A3-S2 ("treat the pain as a false alarm"): no conflict, since A3-S2 itself says the brain "is making real pain", and the two sentences go into different articles.
   - A3-S3 ("it's a false alarm", "without being scared of it") agrees with A3-S2 ("signaling danger where the doctors haven't found any injury").
   - A3-S3 ("slowly going back") against A3-S5 ("four weeks of the therapy"): no conflict.
   - A3-S3 ("Part of how you learn") against A3-S7 and S8 (the second therapy's own methods): they describe different therapies, so nothing clashes.
