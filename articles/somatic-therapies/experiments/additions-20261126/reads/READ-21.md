# Reads of draft 21 (the changed sentences): against his fixes; needed, fit, contradictions

Judged: A1-S10, A3-S1, A3-S2, A3-S3, A3-S4, IC2-S2. The rest of the draft was read as context only.

## Against his fixes
MARKED:
- A3-S2: 1 and 8, light — the colon promises to say what a false alarm is, and the first words after it are "real pain", so for a moment the reader has "a false alarm: real pain" side by side and has to read on to see they aren't being equated. What follows the colon is a noun phrase with a clause hung on it ("real pain, made by your brain signaling danger where ..."), carrying three ideas (it's real, the brain makes it, nothing was found) with no verb of its own. His way gives it a clause. Rewrite, kept as one sentence: "In the first, Pain Reprocessing Therapy, you learn to treat the pain as a false alarm, meaning the pain is real but it comes from your brain signaling danger where the doctors haven't found any injury." Or as two: "... as a false alarm. The pain is real, but your brain is making it by signaling danger where the doctors haven't found any injury." (The "real but" contrast answers a worry this reader has, that a false alarm means imagined pain, so item 3 doesn't apply; READ-20b said the same.)

No other changed sentence is marked.
- A1-S10 was checked against 6 and 18 (a ten-word line that ends the section). It isn't a summing-up: it gives the answer to listen for after S9's question, and its two halves match the question's two halves (slow down for overwhelmed, listen for the disagreement).
- A3-S1 was checked against 5 and 13. It opens on the reader's own case (months of pain, nothing found) before it says there are two therapies, so the reader knows at once whether the section is theirs.
- A3-S3 was checked against 11 and 12. It opens on what the sentence is for ("how you learn it's a false alarm"), which ties it to S2, and then gives the means. "Part of how you learn ... is by paying" is loose grammar, but it reads as speech and nobody trips on it.
- A3-S4 was checked against 10 and 12. "it" is the pain, the same thing "it" meant at the end of S3, and "also" answers S3's "Part of".
- IC2-S2 was checked against 19 and 21. "back, muscle and joint pain" is a list of concrete things, so it stays whole. "see the pain as real" is plain enough.

## Needed, fit, contradictions
1. Needed: all six are needed.
   - A1-S10 tells the reader what a good answer to S9's question sounds like. Without it they have a question and no way to judge the reply.
   - A3-S1 tells the reader whether the section is about them, and that two therapies are coming.
   - A3-S2 says what the first therapy teaches, and keeps "false alarm" from reading as "imaginary".
   - A3-S3 and A3-S4 say what you would actually do in it.
   - IC2-S2 gives a reader of the inner child guide a reason to follow the pointer in IC2-S1.
2. Fit: none (no ANYWAY, CAN'T TELL, FROM or MISREAD).
   - A3-S3's "without being scared of it" was checked for CAN'T TELL. S2 has said this is something you learn in the therapy, and S3 opens on "Part of how you learn", so it reads as what you practise there, not as an order to stop being scared.
   - A3-S2's "a false alarm: real pain" was checked for MISREAD. The rest of the sentence clears it up, so it is marked above as a wording matter only.
   - A3-S4's "like going for walks" was checked for FROM. It is an everyday example and fits a reader with pain of any kind.
3. Contradictions: none, with one thing to look at.
   - IC2-S2 against A3-S5: "they've helped people with long-lasting back, muscle and joint pain" is true of the two therapies taken together, but read quickly it says each of them helped with all three, and A3-S5 says the first one's big trial "was only in mild to moderate chronic back pain". The draft never says what kind of pain the veterans in A3-S10 had, so "muscle and joint" rests on a fact the draft doesn't state. If that is wanted exact: "They both see the pain as real, and between them they've helped people with long-lasting back, muscle and joint pain." Not marked, since a reader of the inner child guide doesn't trip on it.
   - A3-S1 ("a doctor has checked and found no injury or disease") against A3-S2 ("where the doctors haven't found any injury"): they agree. "A doctor" becoming "the doctors", and "injury or disease" becoming "injury", is not something a reader trips on.
   - A3-S2's "real pain" agrees with IC2-S2 ("They both see the pain as real").
   - A3-S3 ("it's a false alarm") repeats S2's term on purpose and agrees with it. S3's "Part of" and S4's "also" hold together as the two things you do.
   - A3-S4 ("slowly go back to things you've been avoiding") against A3-S6 ("four weeks of the therapy"): no conflict.
   - A3-S1's "two therapies" agrees with A3-S7 ("The second therapy"), A3-S12 and A3-S13, and with IC2-S1's "the two talk therapies".
   - A1-S10 ("slow down") against A1-S2 ("catches you at it each time") and A1-S8 ("keep steering you back"): S8's "which can get to be too much" stands between them and is why the question gets asked. A1-S4 already says a slower version exists.
