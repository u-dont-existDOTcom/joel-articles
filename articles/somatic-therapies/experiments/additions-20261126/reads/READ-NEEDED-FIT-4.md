# Read of drafts 4 (A1, A2, A3), needed read, fit read, contradictions

Read fresh against LIVE-TEXT-20261126.md. Finding ids: N = needed, F = fit, C = contradiction.

## 1. Needed read

### A1, Can't Stay With a Feeling? (after Brainspotting's "Further reading", before Layer 4)

Reader: someone in Layer 3 who can already sense their body but slides off a feeling when it rises in a session. They are deciding whether to look for a different kind of therapist, and which of three named therapies to look up.

NEEDS:
- S1: lets the reader recognize the problem as theirs.
- S2: the act: look for a therapist who catches the sliding and keeps you with the feeling.
- S3: a name to search and what the therapy does (points out each slide, turns you back, watches for anxiety).
- S4: implies an act it doesn't say: if you overwhelm easily, ask for the step-at-a-time version.
- S5: picks the therapy if one feeling (grief) is the scary one.
- S6: picks the therapy if the trouble is letting kindness in.
- S7: name plus what it does for that trouble.
- S8: tells the reader whether they must switch therapists later.
- S9: the question to ask before booking.
- S10: the test for the answer, and the act (keep looking).

UNNEEDED:
- S4 clause "Most people who come to it get overwhelmed easily": the reader can't use a claim about other patients and it is unsourced. Keep only the act (see N1).

Findings:
- N1. A1-S4 is a claim about "most people" with the act left out. Fix: "If you get overwhelmed easily, ask for the version that goes a step at a time, with the therapist helping you settle when the anxiety climbs."
- N2. Missing act: how to find or check a therapist in these. A1 gives no "ask whether they trained in it" (A3-S10 has that for pain). Fix: add one clause: search the full name and ask where they trained.

### A2, Imagery Rescripting (after the EMDR section, before Hypnosis)

Reader: someone with one childhood scene that still leaves them helpless, deciding whether to try rescripting, probably against EMDR, which the guide has just described.

NEEDS:
- S1: self-recognition and what the therapy is for.
- S2: what actually happens.
- S3: who steps in, and a link to the guide's own method.
- S4: who the evidence covers.
- S5: how it compares with EMDR (the choice the reader faces).
- S6: reassurance that the new scene doesn't rewrite the facts.
- S7: caution that implies two acts (go slowly, ground afterward).

UNNEEDED: none. (The number 155 is kept: the guide gives study sizes elsewhere, "111 veterans".)

Findings:
- N3. Missing: nothing helps the reader choose between rescripting and EMDR. S5 says neither is ahead, so the reader is left with a coin flip. Fix: one line of criteria, for example "try this one if picturing someone stepping in feels right to you, EMDR if you'd rather not make up a scene."
- N4. Missing act: where to find it and what to ask. Fix: "Ask a trauma therapist whether they've trained in imagery rescripting," and say it is often offered inside schema therapy (check before saying).

### A3, Pain That Won't Quit? (h1, after Layer 5's inner-child paragraph, before the Optional section)

Reader: someone whose pain has lasted months, deciding whether to try one of two therapies and which.

NEEDS:
- S1: signpost back to Layer 5 (see F8).
- S2: who this is for (the condition that makes it relevant).
- S3: what Pain Reprocessing Therapy is.
- S4: what you do in it.
- S5: the evidence, with its limit (back pain only) so the reader can weigh it.
- S6: picks the other therapy if feelings and relationships are tangled in the pain.
- S7: what to expect in it.
- S8: its evidence, its group, and the clause "a lower bar than pain-free", which stops the reader comparing 63% to 66%.
- S9: how to choose.
- S10: vetting the therapist and the guide's own check.

UNNEEDED: none.

Findings:
- N5. Missing act: S2 says "a doctor has found no injury or disease", but not what to do if no doctor has looked. Fix: add "if no doctor has checked yet, start there."
- N6. S5 and S8 give narrow evidence (chronic back pain; veterans 60 and up) and imply an act they don't say. Fix: one sentence after S8: "If your pain or age doesn't match, the evidence for you is thinner, so treat it as a reasonable try, not a promise."
- N7. S9 "start with the one that sounds more like your pain" gives no way to tell. Fix: restate the two criteria: body-alarm pain that makes you afraid to move, versus pain tied to held-back anger or stress with other people.

## 2. Fit read

- F1. A1-S1 (CAN'T TELL). People who slide away from feelings mostly don't notice they do it; S2 even says the therapist "catches you". The reader can't tell whether this is them. Fix: add a cue they can check, such as "you notice afterward that you talked about a feeling without having it, or a therapist has pointed it out."
- F2. A1-S6 and S7 (unclear reference). S6 is a different problem (can't take in kindness), then S7 says "following what that feeling does in your body" and no feeling has been named. Fix: "...then following what the feeling of being cared for does in your body," and open S6 with "Or the feeling that's hard to stay with is being cared for."
- F3. A2-S3 (unclear reference, voice). "my inner child guide" isn't how the live guide names it (it says "inner-child therapy" and "inner child reparenting"), and it puts a claim about Joel's own method in his mouth ("the way I re-do trauma memories") that I can't verify from the guide. "At first the therapist, later your adult self" also needs checking: some versions of rescripting begin with the adult self. Fix: use the guide's phrase and link it, and confirm the order and the claim with Joel.
- F4. A2-S6 (MISREAD). "so it changes how the memory feels" doesn't follow from "you make up on purpose"; the reason it leaves the facts alone is that you know it's invented, and "make up a scene" can read as false memory. Fix: "You know the new scene is invented, so it changes how the memory feels, not what you believe happened."
- F5. A2-S4 (FROM). "PTSD" evidence is a clinic frame, and the reader above only said one scene leaves them helpless; they may not have PTSD. Fix: "The best evidence is in adults with PTSD from childhood; for one stuck scene without PTSD it's less tested."
- F6. A3 heading level versus S1 (doesn't follow). A3 is an h1, a peer of the layers, but S1 says "The rethinking in Layer 5" as if inside it, and Layer 5 is about beliefs, boundaries and forgiveness, not pain. Fix: make it an h2 inside Layer 5 and say "The same rethinking works on pain."
- F7. A3-S2 (clash with guide's subject). The guide is about trauma, so "If you came to all of this because of pain" assumes a reader the guide hasn't had. It also misses the link to the "Physical brain health" section, which lists pain. Fix: "If pain is part of what brought you here," and link that section.
- F8. A3-S4 (MISREAD, tone). "a false alarm from your brain" can sound like "it's in your head", and "start doing the things you've been avoiding" sounds like pushing through pain, against the guide's invitational, never "push harder" stance. Fix: "...a false alarm, though real pain," and "gradually, with the therapist, start doing...".
- F9. A3-S10 (unclear reference). "judge it the way I describe at the end": "it" could be the therapy or the therapist, and "the end" has no name. Fix: "judge whether it's helping the way I describe in 'How I Judge Whether Any of This Is Helping'."
- F10. A3-S8 and S9 (verify). "Its main trial so far" is doubtful, since Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy has earlier trials (for example fibromyalgia), and "Nobody has tested the two against each other yet" dates quickly. Fix: check both against sources and say "the best trial so far."

## 3. Contradictions

- C1. Guide: "Every year we get like 50 new kinds of therapies, with 100 new kinds of ex-spurts... Most of them are actually just permutations and combinations of the ones already invented." Additions: A3-S2 "two newer therapies are worth knowing about", plus six named therapies across A1 to A3. Not a flat contradiction, but a reader of the guide expects a warning, and these are newer therapies recommended without saying how they differ from what is already there. Fix: drop "newer" and say, in a clause each, what the therapy adds beyond the layer it sits in.
- C2. A1-S2 "a few short-term talk therapies" versus A1-S8 "you can keep working with the same therapist through the next two layers." Short-term therapy and a stay through two more layers can't both be assumed. The guide also says "lasting complex trauma recovery tends to have some obvious layers". Fix: "if you want to, and if they offer longer work."
- C3. Guide, EMDR section: "going directly into the trauma memory can sometimes be re-traumatizing... which is one reason I have all these other layers here." A1-S8: "All three also go into memories," said to people who have just been told they overwhelm easily. The guide's reason for the layers is to delay memory work until you can stay present. Fix: "...go into memories later, when you're ready, so you can stay with the same therapist."
