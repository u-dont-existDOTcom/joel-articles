# Read of draft 10 (A1, IC1, IC2): needed, fit, contradictions

## 1. Needed read

Every sentence in A1, IC1 and IC2 does something for the reader. UNNEEDED: none. Two sentences don't hold together as written:

1. **A1-S4**: "watches your body for ... a foggy head". A foggy head isn't something seen on a body, so the list breaks on its third item. Fix (also covers finding 3): "The therapist also watches for signs of the anxiety that's making you slip away, like tense muscles, sighing or your thinking going foggy, so they don't push you further than you can handle."

2. **A1-S8**: S7 says this therapy is for kindness bouncing off, but S8 never comes back to kindness. The reader is left asking how "not feeling alone" fixes that, when the therapist's care is the very thing that bounces off. Fix: "The therapist first makes sure you don't feel alone with the feelings that come up, including what it's like to have them care about you, then follows where you feel them in your body." (The added clause is my reading of the therapy, not one of the checked facts; check it before use.)

## 2. Fit read

3. **A1-S4**: MISREAD yes. "to see how much you can take" reads as the therapist testing your limit, which alarms the reader this section is for. S5 only corrects it a sentence later. Fix: the rewrite in finding 1.

4. **A1-S10**: CAN'T TELL yes. The question in S9 asks about slipping away and overwhelm, so the answer can't show whether you could tell them they've got your feeling wrong. Fix S9: "As with water therapy, I'd ask before booking: 'What do you do when I start slipping away from a feeling, what if I get overwhelmed, and what if I think you've read me wrong?'" S10 stays.

5. **IC1-S1**: MISREAD yes. "a therapist steps in as the ideal adult" reads as the therapist becoming your ideal parent in real life, not entering the imagined memory. "The ideal adult" is also not a term in this section, which speaks of "perfected future characters". Fix: "If a memory feels too big to redo on your own, there's even a clinical therapy for this, called Imagery Rescripting. At first the therapist steps into the imagined scene as the adult you needed, and later you step in yourself."

6. **IC2-S1**: MISREAD yes. Two paragraphs after "normal test results don't prove the pain is imaginary", a bare pointer to two psychological therapies reads as "it's in your head after all", from the same writer who just sided with the reader against doctors who shrugged. It also reads as if both therapies were tried on all three kinds of pain. Fix: see finding 8.

## 3. Contradictions

7. **A1-S6 vs the guide's "The Dominant Trauma-Memory Therapies Are Missing Something"**: the guide says slowly increasing exposure "can itself be re-traumatizing". A1-S6 recommends getting used to a feared feeling "bit by bit", the same logic, with no word on why this is different. Fix: "If it's one feeling in particular that scares you, like grief, Affect Phobia Therapy treats that fear a lot like a phobia. It gets you used to the feeling itself, not to reliving a memory, bit by bit and at a pace you can handle." ("Not to reliving a memory" is not one of the checked facts; check it before use. "A pace your anxiety can handle" is also changed, since it's you who handles the pace.)

8. **IC2-S1 vs A3-S2 and A3-S3**: IC2 says to look into these "while the investigation goes on". A3 offers them only once "a doctor has checked and found no injury or disease", and Pain Reprocessing Therapy teaches that the pain is "a false alarm". A reader whose cause is still open (the section's own example is a moldy house) follows the link and finds they don't meet the condition, or starts a therapy that tells them nothing is wrong. Fix: "If the checks keep coming back normal, keep investigating, and in the meantime you might look into two therapies that treat the pain as real: Pain Reprocessing Therapy, which helped in a trial with chronic back pain, and Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy, which helped in one with muscle and joint pain." ("Treat the pain as real" is not one of the checked facts; check it before use.)

Checked and not marked: "talk therapies" (A1-S3) inside a somatic guide's Layer 3, because S2 says why (you can feel your body and still slip away) and S4 and S8 bring the body back in. No contradictions found between A3 and A1 or IC1. A2 was not in the draft file, so nothing was checked against it.
