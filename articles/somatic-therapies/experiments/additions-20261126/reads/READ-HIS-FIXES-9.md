- A1-S3: 18, 10 — a nine-word line standing alone between longer ones, and "exactly that" could mean slipping away or agreeing with the therapist. His way: join it to S4 and name what it points at ("A few talk therapies are built for people who slip away like this, and Intensive Short-Term Dynamic Psychotherapy is one: ...").
- A1-S4: 1 — three actions (points out each slip, helps you turn back, watches your body) plus a parenthetical list, all in one sentence. His way: the therapist's part in one sentence, then a second one for the body signs ("They also watch for signs the anxiety is too much, like tense muscles, sighing or a foggy head").
- A1-S5: 4, 10 — "Most people who go" (go where? to this therapy?), "a slower version of it" (it = ISTDP or the whole slipping-away problem?), and "more help settling down" says no act. "Most people" is also a claim Joel never gave. His way: name who and what ("Most people who see a therapist trained in this get a slower version of it, where the therapist stops to help you settle when the anxiety climbs"), or cut the "most people" if he can't source it.

His way: name them ("I'd start with Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy ... and otherwise with Pain Reprocessing Therapy"), with the reason: no head-to-head trial, so I'd pick by what your pain seems tied to.
