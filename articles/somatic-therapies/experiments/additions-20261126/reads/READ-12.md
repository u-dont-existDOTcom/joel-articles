# Reads of draft 12 (the changed sentences): against his fixes; needed, fit, contradictions

## Against his fixes
MARKED:
- A3-S4: 14, 11 — It tells someone in chronic pain to pay close attention to the pain, which they already do all day and which sounds like it would make it worse, and it never says how this watching differs. It also opens on the means ("Part of how you learn it is by"). His way gives the natural worry first, then what is different. Rewrite: "You already notice the pain all day, but here you practice watching how it feels in your body calmly, without bracing against it, and that's part of how you learn it's a false alarm." ("calmly, without bracing" is not among the checked facts; check it before using.)
- A1-S4: 4 — "anxiety" with no object. Someone who explains instead of feeling often doesn't think of themselves as anxious, and could take it for general anxiety. Rewrite: "It's usually anxiety about the feeling that makes you slip away, so the therapist also watches for its signs, like tense muscles, sighing, or your thinking going foggy, to tell when it's getting to be too much for you."

## Needed, fit, contradictions
1. Contradiction, IC2-S1 against A3-S2 and A3-S3, and against the inner child guide. IC2-S1 offers the therapies "while the investigation goes on", after a section that says a cause can be missed by the best doctors ("like your house is moldy") and "don't let amelioration of symptoms be the end of the investigation". A3-S2 offers the same two therapies once a doctor "has checked and found no injury or disease", and A3-S3 says flatly there is no danger ("where there isn't any"). A reader who follows IC2-S3 to the somatic guide is told to keep hunting for a cause, then to learn there is none. "Both treat the pain as real" answers the "imaginary" worry, not this one. Rewrite A3-S3 so it rests on what A3-S2 already said: "In Pain Reprocessing Therapy, you learn to treat the pain, though real, as a false alarm, your brain signaling danger where the doctors haven't found any." And say in IC2-S2 that the search doesn't have to stop: "Both treat the pain as real, and you can keep looking for a cause while you try them; in trials they've helped people with mild to moderate chronic back pain and with other long-lasting muscle and joint pain." (The middle clause is his call to make; it isn't a checked fact.)
2. Fit, A3-S4: ANYWAY yes, a person with chronic pain already attends to it constantly, so as written the line tells them nothing new. MISREAD yes, it sounds like dwelling on the pain. Same rewrite as above.
