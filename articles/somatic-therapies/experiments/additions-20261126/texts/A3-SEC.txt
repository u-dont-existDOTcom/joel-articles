Maybe pain is part of what brought you to this guide. If yours has lasted months, and a doctor has checked and found no injury or disease to explain it, two therapies are worth knowing about.

In Pain Reprocessing Therapy, you learn that the pain, though real, is a false alarm, your brain signaling danger where there isn't any, partly by paying close attention to how it feels in your body. Then, a little at a time, you go back to things you've been avoiding because of the pain, like going for walks.

Pain Reprocessing Therapy's first big trial was only in mild to moderate chronic back pain. There, 66% of the people who got four weeks of the therapy rated their pain 0 or 1 out of 10 afterward, compared to 20% of those given a placebo shot, and the gains mostly held a year later.

The second therapy is for pain that seems bound up with stress and feelings about the people in your life. Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy goes straight at those feelings. Much of the therapy is letting out anger and other feelings you've held back, and rehearsing in role-play what you'd say to someone you've never stood up to.

In a 2024 trial of veterans aged 60 and up, this therapy beat CBT: 63% had their pain drop by at least 30% afterward, compared to 17% with CBT. A 30% drop is a lower bar than 0 or 1 out of 10, so don't compare the two trials' numbers.

I haven't found a trial comparing the two. So if your pain seems tied to stress or to people in your life, I'd start with Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy, and otherwise with Pain Reprocessing Therapy. I'd also ask a therapist where they trained in the one you pick, and keep track of how it's going the way I describe in the last section, How I Judge Whether Any of This Is Helping.
