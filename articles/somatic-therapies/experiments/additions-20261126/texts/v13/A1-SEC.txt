Water therapy and Brainspotting let a feeling come up on its own. But some people can feel their body just fine and still slip away from a feeling the moment it shows up in a session, explaining it instead of feeling it, or agreeing with whatever the therapist says. If that's you, look into talk therapies like Intensive Short-Term Dynamic Psychotherapy, where the therapist points out each time you slip away and helps you turn back toward the feeling.

It's usually anxiety about the feeling that makes you slip away, so the therapist also watches for its signs, like tense muscles, sighing, or your thinking going foggy, to tell when it's getting to be too much for you. If you can only handle a little of that anxiety, like most people who try this therapy, you'd get a slower version, where the therapist stops to help you settle whenever it climbs.

If it's one feeling in particular that scares you, like grief, Affect Phobia Therapy treats that fear a lot like a phobia, getting you used to the feeling bit by bit, at a pace your anxiety can handle.

And if what you can't let in is kindness, when someone cares about you and it just bounces off, there's Accelerated Experiential Dynamic Psychotherapy. The therapist works to keep you from feeling alone with whatever comes up, and when some of their care does get in, they ask what that's like and where you feel it in your body.

As with water therapy, I'd ask before booking: "What do you do when I start slipping away from a feeling, or get overwhelmed? And what if I think you've read me wrong?" If the answer leaves no room for you to slow down or to disagree, keep looking.
