By now you might feel your body just fine, and still, the moment a real feeling comes up in a session, you slide away from it. Maybe you start explaining the feeling instead of feeling it, or you agree with whatever the therapist says so you don't have to stay in it. What helps then is a therapist who catches you sliding away and brings you back to the feeling, and a few talk therapies are built around doing exactly that.

In Intensive Short-Term Dynamic Psychotherapy, the therapist calls out each slide right as it happens and keeps turning you back toward the feeling. All the while they watch your body for signs of anxiety (tense muscles, sighing, a foggy head), to see how much you can take. Most people who come to it would find the full version too much, so they usually get a slower one, where the therapist helps them settle whenever the anxiety climbs too high.

If it's one feeling in particular that scares you, like grief, Affect Phobia Therapy treats that fear a lot like a phobia, getting you used to the feeling bit by bit, at a pace your anxiety can handle. Accelerated Experiential Dynamic Psychotherapy is for when you can't let kindness in, when someone cares about you and it just bounces off. The therapist first makes sure you don't feel alone with the feelings that come up, then follows how they feel in your body.

As with water therapy, I'd ask before booking: "What do you do when I start moving away from a feeling, and what if I get overwhelmed?" If the answer leaves no room for you to slow down, or to disagree with them about what you're feeling, keep looking.
