If one scene from your childhood still leaves you feeling small and helpless when it comes back, imagery rescripting lets you walk back into it, this time with backup. You picture the scene again, and somebody steps in to protect you or to give you what you needed back then, first the therapist and later you as an adult. Rescripting is a lot like the way I re-do trauma memories in my inner child reparenting guide.

Does it work? For adults whose PTSD started in childhood, about as well as EMDR. A trial gave 155 of them one or the other, and their PTSD symptoms came down about equally. The new version won't tell you what really happened, since you know you're imagining it; what changes is how the memory feels afterward. Because rescripting takes you back into the memory like EMDR does, the same care goes for it: stop when it's too much, and ground yourself afterward.
