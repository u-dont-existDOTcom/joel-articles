## Trauma-Sensitive / Restorative Yoga

Yoga helps release tension and build tone. I also like it for body awareness, flexibility, enjoyment, and agency, and it can help with relaxation, posture, meditation, and fitness too.

- Good options include trauma-sensitive yoga, restorative yoga, gentle somatic yoga, and very gentle yin when it is propped, short, and easy to leave.
- “You might try…” is different from “stay there.” Keep your eyes open if you want. Come out whenever you want. Notice whether the pose feels useful, neutral, or too much. There can be different correct versions of a posture; it is not true that everything is correct.
- I would avoid power yoga, hot yoga, intense stretching, long passive holds, forced breathwork or breath retention, aggressive hip opening, or instructions to push through sensation or “open your hips and release your trauma.” Hands-on adjustments require clear consent.

## Gentle Shaking / [TRE](https://treprovider.com/)

Here I mean a little shaking, not an attempt to blast trauma out of my body. Very light TRE or neurogenic tremoring can be enough. So can gentle standing shaking or a little playful movement. I want to be able to stop easily. If it starts feeling compulsive, frightening, dissociative, or difficult to stop, I have gone past what I mean by gentle.

**Neck safety note:** If tremoring reaches your neck while you're lying down, don't let an unsupported head whip around. Stop or reduce the movement, support your head, or get experienced help.

If I am so flooded that I am basically the child-state, there may not be much adult perspective left online to comfort or protect that child. In my inner-child work I call those functions the Nurturer and Protector.

Sometimes the first thing available is only a neutral witness, or what I call borrowed adulthood: taking the stance of a steady adult when you cannot yet find that steadiness inside yourself. Even one ordinary protective action can matter.

Gentle self-hypnosis can help too, but if imagery starts pulling me away from the room, time, or body I am actually in, I keep my eyes open and stay present instead of going deeper into memories or child dialogue.
