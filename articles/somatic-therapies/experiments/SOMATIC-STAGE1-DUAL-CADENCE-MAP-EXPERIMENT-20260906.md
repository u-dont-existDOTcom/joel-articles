## Literal candidate

Stage 1 sounds almost too obvious: if you can't stay there, you can't really choose what to do with what comes up. This is where Somatic Experiencing makes sense to me, because it doesn't ask you to solve the trauma yet or even explain it very well; maybe your chest goes hard, your eyes want to look away, you suddenly want to run, and you stay with enough of that to see what happens without having to make the whole memory happen at once. Then you come back. Look around the room. You might go toward it again in a minute. Or not.

SE has names for this stuff—titration, pendulation, orientation—but they make more sense after you feel the back-and-forth than before. What I don't want is to turn “building capacity” into months of getting ready for therapy. If you already have one clear target and can stay with it, what exactly are you waiting for? Sometimes, though, getting near the memory wipes out so much adult perspective that this question barely applies; there may be no Nurturer or Protector online, no real sense that the room is safer than the memory, and before doing anything deeper you may just need enough of yourself back in the present that somebody is there to choose.
