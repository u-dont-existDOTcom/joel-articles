## 1. Opening paragraph

`How much of a difficult reaction can you stay with before it starts making the decisions for you?`

The question largely restates the already-visible Stage-1 map (`stay present ... choose what comes next`) rather than adding a new SE-specific relation.

`You can be willing to face a subject and still find that once the body gets going, willingness isn't doing much of the steering anymore.`

This mostly explains/rephrases the first sentence. It also repeats the same agency metaphor in a second form (`making the decisions` -> `steering`). The pair is a polished setup-and-clarification unit, not a genuine second move.

## 2. Titration paragraph

`Levine calls working with a smaller amount at once titration.`

This is compact textbook naming. More importantly, it begins the recurring `experience -> technical label` cycle.

`The amount isn't something you can decide from the name of the trauma. You find it while you're in the work.`

These sentences expand beyond the minimal inherited source. They may be plausible, but the current source packet does not require the stronger rule that trauma-name/category cannot inform dose or that dose is specifically discovered only during the work. The prose should not invent a generalized clinical rule to make titration vivid.

`Something that sounds manageable in conversation can feel very different once attention lands on the part that is actually charged...`

Again plausible, but it introduces a specific contrast (`conversation` versus `charged part`) that was not supplied as the local fact. It reads like synthetic concreteness.

`...and the change may happen before you have a neat explanation for it.`

This also drifts backward into the already-completed upstream sensation/meaning discussion. It is article-wide redundant even if locally coherent.

`For a while you are still there with the reaction. Then there is less room around it.`

This is vague synthetic phenomenology. `less room around it` sounds evocative but does not identify a sourced or clearly observable distinction.

## 3. Pendulation paragraph

`Sometimes attention moves toward a place that is easier to stay with and, after a while, returns to the difficult part. SE calls that pendulation.`

The passage repeats the same didactic unit used for titration: practical description -> technical name. That recurrence is the strongest cadence finding in the full boundary.

`When you come back, is the body actually coming with you, or is the first reaction still running even though your attention has moved?`

The final question is rhetorically neat but semantically muddy. It risks conflating pendulation with a split between cognitive attention and bodily activation rather than simply preserving the inherited approach/return function. It also gives the whole passage an engineered question-at-both-ends frame.

## Stronger causal diagnosis

The current model can stop serializing the **whole preservation inventory** and still serialize **one technical concept at a time**.

Call this **concept-by-concept didactic cycling** or **terminology laddering**:

`practical setup -> term -> explanation/operationalization -> next practical setup -> next term -> explanation/landing`

This is a finer-grained version of the same generative prior: the model wants each concept to receive a complete teaching unit.

Question symmetry, repeated `Levine/SE calls...`, and mini-definitions are surface symptoms. The deeper issue is that terminology still controls progression.

## Revised strategy — pre-terminology natural movement, then source reconciliation

### Stage A — generate without terminology obligations

Generate an adequately sized natural movement (default roughly 180–250 words for this experiment) from **one plain-language practical phenomenon only**.

For the next SE test, the phenomenon is bounded approach/return: the person engages only as much charged material as can be stayed with, can back away, and can later approach again.

The writer should not receive `titration`, `pendulation`, and `orientation` as items it must cover. Technical naming is optional during generation. Orientation stays outside this boundary.

The writer should also not see Joel's direct readiness sentence or the rejected model prose during the scored generation test. That prevents owner-style borrowing and rejected-prose anchoring.

### Stage B — audit natural prose before terminology insertion

Cold-audit the complete generated movement for:

- reader-state subtraction (does it add beyond the already-known Stage-1 choice premise?);
- recurring mini-lessons or glossary rhythm;
- explanatory aftercare;
- synthetic concreteness or invented clinical rules;
- fake phenomenology;
- tidy opening/closing symmetry;
- true stopping point.

If the prose fails here, do **not** rescue it by adding or moving technical labels.

### Stage C — minimal source reconciliation after a prose pass

Only after a movement survives the prose audit, map the already-described phenomena back to inherited source terminology.

Insert a technical label only where it names something the prose has already made intelligible. The insertion should not trigger a new definition/explanation unit.

This stage is supervisor/editor work, not an obligation on the original generator. `titration`, `pendulation`, and `orientation` remain preservation requirements at the Stage-1 level, but the generator does not consume them as a sequence.
