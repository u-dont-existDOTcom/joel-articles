## Why it fails

### 1. Procedural staircase reappears locally

The seed ends with `what to do next`. The new sentence answers by declaring `The next step...`.

That converts an unresolved situation into a procedural step immediately. The sentence-chain experiment was meant to allow a thought to remain locally alive without using each sentence as a new state transition. `The next step` reinstates exactly that state-transition framing, even though it arose from a genuinely fresh writer context.

This is not evidence that the writer saw the old staircase diagnosis. It is evidence that procedural completion is a strong generic generation prior.

### 2. Article-wide cross-boundary repetition

The immediately preceding accepted trauma-memory/SIBAM ending already tells the reader that meaning/narrative reevaluation can remain useful while bottom-up process is honored first or concurrently.

The clause:

`without deciding yet what it means`

returns to substantially the same relation: bodily noticing may precede explicit meaning-making.

The only genuinely new local action is `notice that response`. The meaning clause mostly uses already-established article material to explain what noticing does not require.

A repeated premise can survive when necessary to establish a new practical relation, but here the clause is not necessary enough to justify the cross-boundary repetition.

### 3. Negated-requirement structure

`without deciding yet what it means` is another negative qualification: notice X without having to do Y.

Unlike the earlier paragraph-level failures, this is only one clause and not by itself a mini-essay. But combined with `The next step`, the sentence becomes a tidy instructional unit:

`state unresolved -> prescribe next step -> say what that step does not require`.

That is too complete for the locally live movement.

## Fidelity / role-strength

No hard unexplained factual delta is required for rejection.

- noticing bodily response is compatible with the current Stage-1 / Somatic Experiencing working function;
- deferring explicit meaning-making is compatible with the accepted bottom-up relation;
- `may be` avoids turning the action into a universal requirement.

The sentence fails editorially rather than because of a clear unsupported mechanism or chronology claim.
