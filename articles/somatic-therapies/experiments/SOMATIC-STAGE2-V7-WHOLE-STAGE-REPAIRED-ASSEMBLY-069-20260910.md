**Shaking Qigong — reference notes**

- **Regular practice:** 10–45 minutes.
- **Use contexts:** non-verbal emotional processing; chronic freeze patterns; stress accumulation between therapy sessions; post-therapy discharge when activation remains in the body.
- **Possible non-qigong explanations:** rhythmic movement; reduced muscular armoring; greater awareness of bodily sensation; a possible shift from fight-or-flight toward a calmer state; tremor-mediated release of chronic tension; completion of interrupted defensive impulses.
- **Qigong explanation:** in qigong, *qi* means vital energy. From that perspective: removing energetic stagnation; increasing qi circulation; dissolving stored emotional charge; reconnecting breath, body, and movement.

These are different explanations for the same practice; one does not prove the other.

**Yin yoga caveat:** Yin yoga can be helpful, but only if it is gentle, propped, and non-coercive. In trauma work, long passive holds can become too evocative. ## EFT / Tapping

EFT tapping has been useful for me as a regulation practice, and for me it partly works by diverting attention while I am activated. I also notice different changes at different tapping points, and some of it feels a little like massage. I sometimes get an even bigger calming effect from shaving my head and massaging it.

It's quick and private enough to use before a difficult conversation, after a trigger, or around a therapy session, and to repeat if the activation comes back. For severe trauma I would still want more than EFT, and I would not use tapping to avoid deeper work that still needs doing.

## Solar Plexus / Heart-Chakra Love

You do not have to use that opening to go straight into pain. Bring to mind a pet, a baby, a friend, a partner, or anybody you naturally love.
