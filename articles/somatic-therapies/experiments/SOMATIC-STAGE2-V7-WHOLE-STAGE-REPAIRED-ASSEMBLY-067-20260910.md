# Stage 2: Regulate or Discharge Accumulated Activation Energy

## [**Shaking Qigong**](http://shakingclass.innersignalselfhypnosis.com/) / Shaking Medicine

> Shaking Qigong is basically shaking plus qigong. I’m linking to a $10-per-month Skool class my friend Louka took because he had already tried many different healing and somatic modalities, including basic TRE, without getting much help. This was the one that finally helped him.

Somatic Experiencing can already include letting the body's trauma response come to completion, and sometimes that includes shaking. It can help you feel your body and what it's saying, and sometimes that process itself moves into discharge. If you want a more active way of moving what you found, or you aren't feeling much from SE in the first place, you can try TRE or Shaking Qigong. You should still be able to stop, orient, and settle afterward.

**For stronger shaking — not ideal as:**

- Standalone treatment for severe PTSD
- The primary trauma-processing modality
- A catharsis-chasing practice
- A replacement for skilled therapy when you are highly dissociative or unstable

**Shaking Qigong — reference notes**

- **Regular practice:** 10–45 minutes.
- **Use contexts:** non-verbal emotional processing; chronic freeze patterns; stress accumulation between therapy sessions; post-therapy discharge when activation remains in the body.
- **Possible non-qigong explanations:** rhythmic movement; reduced muscular armoring; greater awareness of bodily sensation; a possible shift from fight-or-flight toward a calmer state; tremor-mediated release of chronic tension; completion of interrupted defensive impulses.
- **Qigong explanation:** in qigong, *qi* means vital energy. From that perspective: removing energetic stagnation; increasing qi circulation; dissolving stored emotional charge; reconnecting breath, body, and movement.

These are different explanations for the same practice; one does not prove the other.

After shaking, EFT, massage, restorative yoga, or gentle yin yoga could help with grounding as well as a soft physical exploration of the body.

Massage is a more passive option here for bodily contact and muscular release. Consent means being able to change the pressure or body area, or stop or redirect the touch at any point.

**Yin yoga caveat:** Yin yoga can be helpful, but only if it is gentle, propped, and non-coercive. In trauma work, long passive holds can become too evocative. Early on, shorter holds are usually better than ambitious five-minute shapes. You should never be encouraged to endure sensation for the sake of “release.”

## EFT / Tapping

EFT tapping has been useful for me as a regulation practice, and for me it partly works by diverting attention while I am activated. I also notice different changes at different tapping points, and some of it feels a little like massage. I sometimes get an even bigger calming effect from shaving my head and massaging it.

EFT is also easy to use while real life is happening. You can tap in private before a difficult conversation, after getting triggered, or around a therapy session, and repeat it if the activation comes back. I would not make it the only treatment for severe trauma or use it to avoid deeper work that still needs doing.

## Solar Plexus / Heart-Chakra Love

In my experience, EFT, Somatic Experiencing, trauma-sensitive yoga, gentle shaking or qigong, non-forced longer exhales, and gentle Buteyko-style breathing can all help the body loosen a little. I especially notice this around the solar plexus or abdomen. When that area softens, heart-chakra love can become easier for me to access, which can make the inner-child work much easier.

You do not have to use that opening to go straight into pain. Bring to mind a pet, a baby, a friend, a partner, or anybody you naturally love. Feel the love that is already there and see whether a little of it can move from the heart toward the younger part around the solar plexus, then back up again in a loop.
