## Evidence leads recovered 2026-09-08

### Aquatic bodywork / WATSU

- Schitter et al. 2020 systematic review/meta-analysis, PMID **32168328**: WATSU uses warm water and combines myofascial stretching, joint mobilization, massage and shiatsu. Meta-analyses suggested benefits for pain, physical function and mental outcomes; authors rated the evidence **low-to-moderate at best** and called for higher-quality RCTs.
- Danner et al. 2024 randomized controlled study, PMID **39096547**: 60 volunteers; one 20-minute WATSU session produced greater immediate improvements in measured body awareness, pain and mood than a lecture control.
- Rutledge & Berg 2025 retrospective PTSD study, PMID **40266503**: 111 active-duty/veteran participants with diagnosed PTSD entered up to eight trauma-informed aquatic sessions; among 86 completing 4+ sessions, mean PCL-M improvement was 14.4 points. This is preliminary single-arm evidence, not causal proof.

### Emotional opening / practitioner support

WaterDance/Aquatic Bodywork training material explicitly acknowledges that substantial thoughts, emotions and perceptions may arise and says a trained practitioner should be able to provide appropriate support. This supports the plausibility and clinical relevance of Hale's warning without establishing how common practitioner under-recognition is.

### Massage

Reader-facing safety disposition:
