## [Shaking Qigong](http://shakingclass.innersignalselfhypnosis.com/) / Shaking Medicine

My friend Louka is why I'm linking this particular class. I haven't tried the class myself. He'd tried many different healing and somatic modalities, including basic TRE. TRE itself did nothing for him, and nothing helped him much until he got into this class, which costs $10 per month on Skool.

The class is much more than just TRE.

By “blockage” I mean what energy is stuck in you. I often have the kind of natural shaking that animals have, rather than noticing much benefit from intentional therapeutic techniques. Especially on psychedelics, it's like my body knows exactly which part to shake and how. So it's not just “shaking = mental health.” When the right thing is shaken the right way, things loosen up as they should and the energy flows.

TRE is a system people are trying to teach en masse, so they had to distill it down to what seems to work most of the time for most people. But obviously, if you could get more angles at it, you'd get more chance of success—and less chance of being called an expert. “Just try lots of stuff till it works” is how my expertise runs on this.

I have done qigong and found it very helpful for moving energy, so I can understand how the two would naturally go together. It also seems like a middle ground between the fully predictable TRE structure and totally unstructured shaking. That probably suits more people who feel like they need some guidance but aren't able to benefit from standard TRE. It's also social, and I think that can work better than individual practice for many people when they see others getting results.

This is a different dose from the little exploratory shakes above. I could imagine 10–45 minutes as a regular practice for chronic freeze, non-verbal emotional processing, stress that keeps accumulating between therapy sessions, or activation left over after deeper work. If I can't stop, orient, and settle afterward, I've gone too far.

I don't know exactly what deserves the credit when shaking helps. Maybe the movement lets muscles and defensive impulses do something they never got to finish. Maybe moving while paying close attention to the body changes arousal. Qigong would say stuck qi is moving again. Those don't have to be the same explanation.

I wouldn't turn shaking into a catharsis contest or treat it as a complete treatment for severe PTSD. If somebody is highly dissociative or unstable, I wouldn't substitute experimentation for skilled help.
