## Exposure terminology — current owner position

Joel uses `exposure` in a broad functional sense: deliberately bringing avoided traumatic material — memory, bodily feeling, traumatic meaning, cues, or associated thought — into therapeutic attention rather than continuing avoidance. Under this broad sense, PE, EMDR, CPT/trauma-focused cognitive work, and SE can all contain exposure.

The editorial distinction therefore must **not** be `exposure vs. no exposure`. SE itself explicitly approaches trauma-associated sensations bit by bit through titration.

The more useful unresolved comparison is what each method is trying to make happen **after traumatic material is activated**:

- PE / extinction-based exposure: new learning reduces or inhibits expression of conditioned fear; modern accounts emphasize inhibitory learning and expectancy violation, with habituation/desensitization as only part of the picture.
- CPT: engages trauma-related meanings / stuck points and uses cognitive restructuring; current standard CPT does not require the written trauma account, even though it still confronts avoided trauma-related cognition and can reasonably be described as exposure in Joel's broad functional sense.
- EMDR: activates a trauma target and its image/cognition/affect/body components, then uses bilateral stimulation within an Adaptive Information Processing framework; exact mechanism remains debated.
- SE: approaches stress-associated sensations through titration and gives causal/therapeutic primacy to autonomic regulation, procedural/sensorimotor organization, subtle motor impulses, discharge, and biological completion of incomplete defensive responses.

## Suppression wording — not yet owner-final

Joel argues that PE/EMDR bring feelings up in order to extinguish/desensitize the response and therefore sees `suppression` as a legitimate description of what happens to the conditioned response.

Evidence distinction to preserve:

- extinction-learning literature does explicitly describe new inhibitory learning as suppressing/inhibiting expression of the original fear memory/response rather than erasing it;
- this supports wording such as `suppress/inhibit the old conditioned fear response` for extinction-based models;
- it does **not automatically establish** that PE, EMDR, and CPT all work by `suppressing feelings`, because PE intentionally evokes emotion during treatment, CPT's main mechanism is cognitive restructuring, and EMDR's AIP model describes adaptive memory reprocessing rather than extinction as its official mechanism.

Do not silently weaken Joel's critique into `these therapies merely differ from SE`. The active question is how to phrase the reduction/inhibition/desensitization critique accurately enough to survive expert scrutiny while preserving the owner's argument.
