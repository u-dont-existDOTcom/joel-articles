## Strong / supportable as currently argued

- The new heading concept `The Dominant Trauma-Memory Therapies Are Missing Something` accurately avoids forcing EMDR into a purely top-down category while preserving the article's methodological critique.
- A broad functional use of `exposure` — deliberate therapeutic contact with avoided traumatic memories, meanings, cues, feelings, or sensations — can reasonably encompass PE, EMDR, CPT, and also SE. Therefore `exposure` cannot itself be the main somatic/non-somatic distinction.
- Modern exposure/extinction models support the statement that the original fear association can remain intact while new inhibitory/safety learning inhibits its expression. This supports a carefully scoped `suppression/inhibition of the old conditioned fear response` claim for exposure/extinction models.
- Payne/Levine/Crane-Godreau (2015) supports the SE contrast: incomplete defensive/procedural responses, interoceptive/proprioceptive tracking, autonomic regulation, discharge, and biological completion.
- The owner EMDR/SE distinction is directionally strong: EMDR centers reprocessing of a trauma-memory network that includes bodily sensations, while SE gives special causal/therapeutic primacy to sensorimotor/autonomic defensive organization and completion.
- The owner claim that cognitive meaning can often reorganize after autonomic/kinesthetic discharge is supported by Payne/Levine/Crane-Godreau, while explicit cognitive restructuring remains useful in some cases.

## Local repairs required before calling this section factually final

1. **Heading typo:** remove accidental `retrau` from `# The Dominant Trauma-Memory Therapies Are Missing Somethingretrau`.
2. **EMDR name:** standard name is `Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR)`; current draft omits `and`.
3. **Shared-protocol overstatement:** `Their logic is simple: slowly increase your exposure to painful memories until you can fully re-live the experience without overwhelming your nervous system` is not a literal shared protocol for PE, CPT, and EMDR. PE uses repeated imaginal/in-vivo exposure; CPT primarily identifies/restructures trauma-related stuck points and may omit a detailed trauma account; EMDR activates a target memory during bilateral stimulation while tracking associations, affect, cognition, and body sensations. Owner may retain the broader exposure framing, but should not describe all three as one graded-re-living protocol.
4. **CBT sequencing:** `The CBT trauma therapies then go further after that into regular CBT` is inaccurate for CPT, where cognitive restructuring is central from the beginning rather than a later phase after exposure. The article can still characterize CPT broadly as exposure/desensitization if Joel wishes; the correction concerns sequencing/mechanism, not the label.
5. **Suppression scope:** modern exposure/extinction theory supports new inhibitory learning suppressing/inhibiting expression of an older conditioned fear association, but this should not be asserted as the single established mechanism of CPT and EMDR. If `suppress` is retained, scope it to exposure/extinction learning or frame the broader cross-modality claim at the level of changing the response/meaning/processing of activated trauma rather than claiming one mechanism.
6. **Re-traumatization evidence wording:** Payne/Levine/Crane-Godreau explicitly warn about flooding and potential re-traumatization. Dropout is studied, and trauma-focused treatment has shown higher pooled dropout in some meta-analyses, but reasons for termination/adverse events are incompletely characterized. Therefore use `may be under-captured` or equivalent rather than claiming re-traumatization categorically `isn't captured` by positive evidence.
7. **Outcome specificity:** mainstream trauma-focused therapies have strong evidence for reducing overall PTSD symptoms; hyperarousal/arousal improves in relevant analyses. Dissociation can also improve, but is less consistently targeted/measured and a 2026 meta-analysis found only a small pooled effect on dissociation. `reducing dissociation and hyper-arousal` is directionally defensible but `reducing PTSD symptoms, including hyper-arousal, and sometimes dissociation` would be more precise if owner wants maximal accuracy.
8. **SIBAM sourcing:** the 2015 Frontiers SE paper does not itself introduce/name SIBAM. Add a separate source for the formal SIBAM five-channel model (Levine 2010 / Rothschild 2000 or another direct/academic source). A 2025 peer-reviewed art-therapy paper explicitly summarizes SIBAM as Sensation, Image, Behavior, Affect, Meaning and discusses overcoupling/undercoupling.
9. **Mechanical fixes:** change malformed `(explained)[[URL](URL)]` to normal Markdown link syntax; fix `hyper-arrousal` to `hyperarousal`; fix comma splice `..., They` after `response to it`.
