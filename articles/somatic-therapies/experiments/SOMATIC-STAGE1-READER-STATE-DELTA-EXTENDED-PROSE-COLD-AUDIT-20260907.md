## Verdict

The prior audit was too generous. The paragraph advances beyond the preceding SIBAM discussion, but it contains two semantic defects and a deeper generation-architecture failure:

## Cold audit — pass 1: semantic sanity and role fidelity

### Sentence 1

`I put Somatic Experiencing in Stage 1 because sometimes activation is already running away with you before you've had a chance to decide what to do.`

The underlying relation is authorized: SE is an early Stage-1 example when activation outruns choice.

Residual weakness: `I put Somatic Experiencing in Stage 1 because...` is editorial-classification scaffolding. The article's Introduction has already presented the five-stage map, and the reader is now literally under the Stage-1 / SE headings. The sentence spends rhetorical energy explaining the author's filing decision rather than directly entering the problem. This is not the strongest rejection reason, but the map should not require a placement-justification sentence.

### Sentence 2

`Can you stay present enough to choose what happens next while that reaction is still happening?`

This is semantically coherent in isolation, but it is extremely close to Joel's already-visible Stage-1 map wording:

`Stay present so you can stay with what happens and choose what comes next.`

The earlier map's cross-boundary subtraction looked only at the immediately preceding trauma-memory/SIBAM section. It did **not** subtract reader knowledge established earlier in the Introduction. As a result, an internal interpretive question derived from the owner map was allowed to become a near-paraphrase of the owner map in the prose.

This sentence may still perform a useful reader-facing act, but the current map cannot distinguish genuine social questioning from supervisor-prompt echo.

### Sentence 3

`The reaction does not have to be finished first; if you can still tell that you are in the room, you can still decide whether you want to stay with what is coming up.`

First clause: source-compatible. The Stage-1 function does not require activation to disappear before choice can exist.

Second clause: **semantic overclaim**.

`can still tell that you are in the room` is a present-orientation marker/action. The source supports room orientation as a small present-focused move when somebody is flooded or highly identified with the child-state. It does **not** establish:

`room orientation -> sufficient decision capacity`.

A person may know exactly where they are and still be too activated, compulsive, dissociated in another sense, or otherwise unable to make the relevant voluntary choice. Conversely, retained choice need not require a literal moment of checking the room.

The sentence therefore converts an authorized **example / support / marker** into a **criterion and sufficient condition**. That is a role and certainty escalation even though every noun in the sentence came from authorized Stage-1 material.

Reverse traceability is not clean. This is an unexplained substantive delta.

### Sentence 4

`If you can't, I would stop there and care more about whether you can look around the room and notice that you're here now than about getting farther into the trauma.`

Problems:

1. `If you can't` has an unstable antecedent. It can mean:
   - cannot tell you are in the room;
   - cannot decide whether to stay with what is coming up;
   - cannot remain present enough to choose.
   These are not equivalent states.
2. The sentence inherits the false threshold from sentence 3 and supplies the negative branch of the same binary.
3. `getting farther into the trauma` reintroduces the chronology defect found in earlier rejected probes. The paragraph begins with activation already occurring before a decision has been made. That does **not** establish that the person has begun deliberate trauma processing or is moving `farther into` trauma. A spontaneous reaction and intentional processing remain distinct.
4. The `if you can / if you can't` pair is rhetorically complete in a way the thought does not require. It turns a live problem into a two-branch decision card.

## Strongest objection / counterexample test

### Defense of the room criterion

Strongest defense: knowing that one is in the present room is a concrete way to operationalize `stay present`, and the article already routes room orientation to Stage 1.

Counterexample: a person can be fully aware of the room yet still have little voluntary choice about whether to continue engaging the material. Therefore room awareness cannot carry the sufficiency claim made by sentence 3.

Conclusion: retain room orientation later as an example or support if naturally warranted, but do not use it as the definition or threshold for choice unless a source/owner explicitly authorizes that stronger relation.

### Defense of the positive/negative pair

Strongest defense: to explain retained choice, the reader needs to know both what it looks like when capacity is present and what to do when it is absent.

Counterpoint: both branches do not have to be completed in the same movement. The negative branch becomes especially suspect because it was added only after Joel asked for a longer sample. The earlier two-sentence movement had reached a genuine pause. Completing the opposite case is one way a model fills requested length without discovering a genuinely new thought.

Conclusion: the contrast may eventually be necessary, but the current map did not prove that the negative branch was live **at this boundary**, rather than supplied for formal completeness.

Hidden rhetorical architecture:

`classification -> restated Stage-1 question -> positive threshold -> negative threshold`

or more compactly:

`why SE is here -> can choice coexist? -> if yes -> if no`.

This is a new version of the old checklist problem. The list is no longer a preservation inventory; it is a **binary completion structure** generated from one internal question.

The paragraph also leaks supervisory architecture:

- sentence 2 publishes nearly that question verbatim.

The internal reader question should guide evaluation. It should not automatically become reader-visible prose.

The existing map distinguishes `ALREADY ESTABLISHED IMMEDIATELY UPSTREAM` from `NEW SECTION CONTRACT`.

That is insufficient.

The reader has also already encountered the owner five-stage map in the Introduction, including the exact Stage-1 sentence. The current map treated that sentence as a new contract to restate rather than as **already-visible article-wide reader knowledge whose implications now need development**.

Therefore future subtraction must operate at two scales:

1. immediate preceding boundary;
2. earlier load-bearing article knowledge, especially explicit map/heading promises already shown to the reader.

## Causal diagnosis of the map failure

### Failure mechanism 1 — internal-question leakage

The map constructs a synthesized `OPEN READER QUESTION` and permits the writer to see it. The model can satisfy the architecture cheaply by repeating that question as prose.

Repair: keep the synthesized reader question on the **supervisor/evaluator plane only**. The local writer should not receive its wording.

### Failure mechanism 2 — length request caused case completion

The two-sentence probe had reached a natural pause. Joel then asked for more. The map extended the same movement until it became paragraph-sized, which created pressure to answer the open question more completely.

The easiest completion was:

`if capacity present -> ... / if absent -> ...`.

Repair: when a natural movement has ended but a larger sample is required, do **not** stretch that movement. Open a **new movement boundary** and run a fresh supervisory reader-state differential. A paragraph or section may contain multiple natural movements; one movement should not be forced to become exhaustive merely to satisfy length.

### Failure mechanism 3 — source authorization mistaken for relation authorization

Because room orientation is an authorized Stage-1 function, the audit accepted its emergence as natural. But source authorization of an item does not authorize every logical role assigned to it.

Repair: add a blocking **role/entailment-strength gate**. For every substantive relation, distinguish:

- example;
- marker;
- support/action;
- necessary condition;
- sufficient condition;
- definition/criterion;
- cause/mechanism;
- outcome.

Reject any promotion in logical strength unless the source/owner supports it.

### Failure mechanism 4 — binary completion not tested

The rhythm gate notices matched opposites but allowed S3/S4 because the distinction looked logically relevant.

Repair: add a **binary-completion gate**. When prose creates `if X / if not X`, positive/negative, problem/exception, or permission/prohibition branches, require evidence that both branches are independently live at the current boundary. Logical availability is not enough.

### Failure mechanism 5 — chronology guard too narrow

The map checked that sentence 1 did not equate activation with deliberate trauma processing, but sentence 4 later introduced `getting farther into the trauma`.

Repair: chronology/agency checks must run over the **whole movement**, not only the initiating relation. Preserve the invariant:

`activation already happening` does not imply `intentional trauma processing already underway`.
