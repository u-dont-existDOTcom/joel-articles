## Cold audit 1 — literal prose

### Sentence 1

`Your body can already be reacting before you've decided what to do, but that reaction is not, by itself, a decision about what to do next.`

The first clause is useful and source-compatible. The second clause is logically true but rhetorically tidy: it converts the situation into a neat `reaction / decision` distinction and partially explains an inference the Stage-1 heading/map has already made available.

This is not a factual error. It is a mild overcompletion / conceptual-diagnosis risk.

### Sentence 2

`In Somatic Experiencing, you do not have to wait until all the activation is gone before working with what is happening.`

Source-compatible, but the sentence introduces a hypothetical prerequisite (`wait until all the activation is gone`) that no visible reader has asserted. The prose then negates it.

That is a familiar model move: invent a possible misconception so the paragraph has something reassuring to correct. `working with what is happening` is also vague compared with the concrete movement that follows.

### Sentence 3

`You can stay with a small part of it and then come back, without treating the fact that you paid attention to it as a reason you have to keep going.`

The first half is the strongest prose in the sample. It carries a source-grounded approach/return relation without terminology packaging.

The second half is the strongest remaining defect. Nothing in the visible article has suggested that paying attention creates an obligation to continue. The sentence manufactures that obligation and then reassures the reader against it. This is explanatory aftercare, not the next necessary move.

The standing choice whether to continue is a protected Stage-1 function, but protection of the function does not justify this particular invented-objection realization.

### Sentences 4–5

`Coming back might be as simple as letting your attention include the room again. Looking around is one way to do that.`

The role-strength problem from the prior probe is fixed: looking around is now only an option/example, not a criterion for choice.

But the pair is still overpackaged. Sentence 4 gives an abstract realization of return; sentence 5 immediately gives the concrete action. The reader gains little from having both. `letting your attention include the room again` is the more model-written abstraction; `Looking around` does the actual work.

This is a smaller form of `abstraction -> example` mini-unit closure.

## Blocking architecture audit — v2 writer isolation was not real

The experiment records:

> first-movement semantic custody only: SE is an early example when activation outruns deliberate choice.

and says the writer did not receive the full Stage-1 preservation inventory.

Yet movement 1 produced:

- `stay with a small part` -> titration/small-dose function;
- `then come back` -> approach/return function;
- `you ... don't have to keep going` -> standing voluntary stop/continue function.

Those were not in the declared writer-plane input. They were, however, already present in the same chat/model's GitHub-recovered context.

Therefore at least one of the following is true:

1. the writer plane was not informationally isolated from prior context; or
2. the writer independently generated precisely the hidden protected relations.

Within the same saturated context there is no discriminating evidence for (2). Claiming the bank was sealed is therefore unjustified.

This matters because movement 2 then selected another protected function — orientation — and the same-context supervisor explained that it arose from the phrase `come back` rather than from the preservation inventory. That explanation is not independent evidence. A supervisor that already knows the inventory can rationalize a required item as locally natural after the fact.

### Strongest counterargument

Counterargument: `small part`, return, voluntary stopping, and room orientation all follow naturally from ordinary reasoning about staying present with activation; the inventory may simply contain the same sensible ideas.

Response: plausible, but that is exactly why actual isolation is needed. When the same model already knows the hidden targets, observing target-aligned generation cannot distinguish genuine local emergence from contextual leakage. The experiment can still produce useful prose, but it cannot validate the sealing architecture.

## Durable method correction

For future experiments, distinguish:

- **conceptual separation**: one model is instructed to ignore information it has already seen;
- **informational isolation**: the writer context genuinely does not contain that information.

Only the second can support a claim that future preservation obligations did not influence generation.

If a physically/separately isolated writer context is unavailable, the experiment must be labeled **non-isolated** and must not treat target-aligned emergence as evidence that the sealed-bank mechanism worked.

A real isolated writer should receive only the literal writer-plane packet. The supervisor may then audit the returned prose against full authority afterward. Same-context self-explanations about why a hidden item `arose naturally` remain hypotheses, not proof.

## Map implications

V2 needs at least two new blocking rules before another claimed-isolation test:

1. **Actual-context isolation gate** — do not say the writer `did not receive` information merely because the prompt says to ignore it. Record whether the information existed anywhere in the writer's actual context.
2. **Invented-objection / defensive-aftercare gate** — flag sentences whose main job is to negate a prerequisite, obligation, misconception, or implication that the literal reader state never raised.

The earned-presence gate should also compare abstraction/example pairs, not only paraphrases. If one sentence exists mainly to abstract the concrete action supplied immediately after it, test deletion aggressively.
