## Independent conception snapshot

- The just-accepted five-stage architecture explains **when** various practices may fit, but it does not explain the deeper reason somatic approaches are interesting or how they differ methodologically from cognitive/exposure approaches.
- CBT is currently buried under Stage 5 as `Light CBT / Narrative Integration`, which implies cognitive reality-testing belongs only after somatic processing. The newly supplied owner direction explicitly rejects that implication: CBT can be genuinely useful for testing beliefs and avoidance much earlier.
- The article risks becoming a modality catalogue unless the reader understands that different approaches treat different aspects of experience as information and run different kinds of therapeutic experiments.

Candidate mechanism:

- Keep the five stages as the **practical second half** of the article.
- Add one substantial methodological section before the five-stage overview, using CBT/exposure as the foil that makes the somatic project intelligible.
- Treat CBT and bodily methods as complementary investigative tools rather than opposing doctrines.
- Reframe Stage 5 so cognitive/narrative **integration** belongs there, while CBT/reality-testing itself remains cross-cutting.

Candidate central distinction:

> bodily signals deserve investigation, not automatic obedience; cognition deserves reality-testing authority, not automatic sovereignty.

The concise owner-supplied formulation `listen before correcting; then test what needs testing` is the strongest current conceptual core, but is not yet owner-final publication wording.

## Bounded existing-work scan

### CBT / cognitive therapy

Current PTSD cognitive-therapy literature supports the useful side of the foil:

- behavioral experiments are used to test beliefs/predictions, reveal effects of avoidance/safety behaviors, and learn new information;
- cognitive restructuring addresses problematic appraisals;
- exposure approaches deliberately confront trauma memories/reminders and avoidance;
- these approaches are strongly evidence-supported and should not be caricatured as mere verbal disputation.

### Somatic Experiencing

Payne, Levine, and Crane-Godreau (2015) explicitly describe SE as:

- developed by Levine from 1977 onward;
- primarily bottom-up through interoceptive/proprioceptive/kinesthetic attention rather than primarily cognitive experience;
- not direct/intense exposure, instead approaching charged memories indirectly and gradually;
- a supplement to cognitive and exposure therapies.

This strongly supports a deliberate methodological contrast. It does **not alone establish** the stronger historical-causal claim that Levine invented SE *because* CBT/exposure was inadequate.

### TRE

Berceli founder-history material supports:

- war-zone experience and terror-related tremors/fetal response as formative;
- later influence from Alexander Lowen/Bioenergetic Analysis;
- later separation of the tremor mechanism from psychodynamics in Israel/Palestinian work;
- community/self-help application.

It does not currently support an equally strong anti-CBT/exposure origin claim.

### Adjacent embodied approaches

- Focusing/Gendlin explicitly treats a bodily `felt sense` as implicitly meaningful information that may not yet be differentiated into words.
- Sensorimotor Psychotherapy explicitly works with posture, sensation, movement, impulse, protective adaptations, and new action possibilities.
- Alexander Technique works with habitual/unnecessary effort and `inhibition` of automatic response rather than simply adding corrective effort.
- 5Rhythms explicitly organizes practice around different movement qualities/states. Its claims/evidence are not equivalent to established trauma psychotherapies and should be presented as an adjacent embodied practice if used.

### Institutional/evidence-system critique

- CBT/manualized therapies fit controlled research particularly well because they can be specified and reproduced; a systematic review found no convincing evidence that manualized psychotherapy is inherently superior to nonmanualized psychotherapy, despite its research/standardization advantages.
- A 2026 American Journal of Psychiatry article argues that functioning and quality of life need a clearer/core role in psychiatric diagnosis, supporting a nuanced critique that symptom/function endpoints are important but not necessarily a complete account of healing.
- Do not claim DSM defines disorder mainly by ability to work or imply a conspiracy-shaped institutional motive.

## Choice after scan

**Composition/adaptation.** Do not replace the five-stage map with a new bespoke taxonomy. Compose:

1. established cognitive/exposure methods and their valid epistemic function;
2. established somatic/experiential methods and their different information channels/experiments;
3. Joel's owner-developed flexible five-stage practical map.

The genuinely article-specific remainder is the synthesis and sequencing, not invention of the underlying therapy traditions.

# Proposed governing movement after reorganization

1. **The body carries and may resolve trauma in ways cognition alone does not capture.** (owner-final Introduction)
2. **CBT/exposure are valuable precisely because they test cognition, prediction, and avoidance—but they are not the only way to investigate experience.**
3. **Somatic and embodied approaches ask other questions of bodily sensation, impulse, movement, tremor, posture, and felt meaning.**
4. **Neither body nor cognition gets automatic veto power: investigate first, then test.**
5. **The five stages are Joel's practical map for choosing what kind of work seems useful now.**
6. **The modality sections show the map behaving in practice, including overlap and exceptions.**
7. **Outcome is judged by actual life, symptoms, function, quality of life, and whether the practice increases capacity rather than merely intensity.**
