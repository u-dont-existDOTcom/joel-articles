## Governing correction

The five-stage map still explains **when** different kinds of work may fit, but it no longer carries the article's whole intellectual thesis. CBT/exposure becomes the major comparison case that explains why somatic approaches exist and what they add.

CBT/exposure are not being dismissed. They offer reality testing, behavioral experiments, work with avoidance, prediction testing, exposure, and cognitive/narrative updating. The accepted argument is that these methods were **inadequate as a complete account and treatment of trauma**, which is why alternative/somatic approaches were developed.

For Somatic Experiencing, the Payne/Levine/Crane-Godreau account supports this interpretation: it describes SE as developed by Levine from 1977 onward; explicitly contrasts SE's bottom-up method with primarily cognitive intervention and direct/intense exposure; calls the simple stimulus/response conditioning model inadequate for complex behavior; identifies a drawback in talk therapy for trauma; and describes the relevant regulatory system as relatively unaffected by rational thought. Do not weaken this to mere neutral difference. Also do not misstate it as `CBT is useless`.

TRE genealogy remains separately sourced. Do not automatically give every embodied practice the exact same anti-CBT origin story.

### Different therapies ask different questions

Use a compact comparative movement, not seven equal cards:

- CBT — test belief/prediction/avoidance;
- SE — investigate what the organism is doing beneath explicit story;
- TRE — allow/observe tremor organization with reduced voluntary control;
- Focusing — attend to bodily felt meaning before it becomes explicit language;
- Sensorimotor/Ogden — experiment with posture, movement, protective action/impulse;
- Alexander — notice/inhibit habitual unnecessary effort;
- 5Rhythms — explore accessible/avoided movement qualities.

Do not imply evidence equivalence among these approaches.

# Accepted governing movement

1. The body carries and may resolve trauma in ways cognition alone does not capture.
2. CBT/exposure are valuable tools for testing cognition, prediction, and avoidance, but were inadequate as the complete answer to trauma.
3. Somatic and embodied approaches arose to investigate/process channels and mechanisms those approaches missed.
4. Neither body nor cognition gets automatic sovereignty: investigate bodily information, then test what needs testing.
5. The five stages are Joel's practical map for choosing what kind of work seems useful now.
6. The modality sections show the map behaving in practice, including overlap and exceptions.
7. Judge success by actual life, symptoms, functioning, quality of life, and increased capacity rather than session intensity alone.
