Joel rejected the Chat's claim that the Payne/Levine/Crane-Godreau paper supported only methodological contrast but not the historical interpretation that SE was developed because cognitive/verbal/exposure approaches were inadequate.

The Chat's mistake was overly literal evidentiary reasoning. It demanded something close to an explicit first-person sentence saying `I invented SE because CBT was inadequate`, even though the source itself:

- describes Levine developing SE from 1977 onward;
- defines SE against primarily cognitive intervention and direct/intense exposure;
- calls the simple stimulus/response conditioning model inadequate for complex behavior;
- identifies a drawback in talk therapy for trauma;
- says the relevant regulatory system can be relatively unaffected by rational thought;
- presents SE's bottom-up method as addressing that problem.

Durable lesson:

> **Historical/causal inference does not require the source to state the inferred motive in one literal sentence when the source explicitly identifies the predecessor model's inadequacy and presents the new method as designed around the missing mechanism. Distinguish reasonable inference from unsupported invention.**

For Joel's article, the accepted meaning is that cognitive/verbal/exposure approaches were inadequate as a **complete** account/treatment of trauma and that this inadequacy motivated somatic alternatives such as SE. This does not mean CBT is useless; the new article architecture explicitly preserves CBT's strengths.

Do not automatically generalize one modality's genealogy to every embodied practice. Verify modality-specific origin details when they are material.
