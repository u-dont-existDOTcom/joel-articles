## Literal candidate audited

> Somatic Experiencing doesn't require you to start with a detailed trauma story. Sometimes the first thing you notice is just that something is happening in the body and you have no idea yet what it has to do with anything. That's not a very tidy starting point. The sensation can be quite clear while everything you would say about it is still vague. And sometimes you can keep paying attention without the explanation showing up. You are still there with something happening in the body, but it has not arranged itself into a story.

## Verdict

**Reject as current Stage-1 prose.**

The continuous-field architecture successfully removed the prior procedural staircase, but the literal prose exposes a different model-shaped failure: **semantic stasis disguised as varied rhetorical lingering**.

## Cold audit — pass 1: semantic movement

Sentence 1 establishes one supported relation: Somatic Experiencing need not begin with a detailed trauma narrative.

Sentence 2 moves to a possible pre-narrative bodily experience. This is broadly compatible with the upstream SIBAM/body-versus-meaning context, although `the first thing you notice` adds a mildly stronger temporal framing than the source itself requires.

After that, the paragraph mostly stops moving:

- `That's not a very tidy starting point.` evaluates the same situation without changing the reader's understanding.
- `The sensation can be quite clear while everything you would say about it is still vague.` repackages the same sensation-versus-meaning distinction as a polished antithesis.
- `you can keep paying attention without the explanation showing up` restates that meaning/explanation may remain absent.
- `it has not arranged itself into a story` restates the same relation again through a metaphorical closing image.

The passage therefore does not suffer from too many conceptual jobs. It suffers from **too few**: one relation is repeatedly reformulated as though sustained attention itself justified additional sentences.

Continuous attention is not the same as semantic repetition.

## Cold audit — pass 2: acoustic / model shape

The sentence-length contour is superficially varied, but the rhetorical packaging is conspicuous:

1. direct proposition;
2. longer experiential elaboration;
3. short evaluative aside;
4. balanced `clear / vague` contrast;
5. another explanatory reformulation;
6. metaphorical restatement that also acts as the paragraph's landing.

Specific model-shaped features:

- `That's not a very tidy starting point.` reads like manufactured epistemic friction. It tells the reader that the situation is messy instead of letting the actual uncertainty provide that friction.
- `quite clear / still vague` is a neat matched antithesis. The contrast is semantically plausible, but its realization is unusually polished and symmetric.
- `the explanation showing up` and `arranged itself into a story` personify/animate abstraction to keep the same thought sounding fresh.
- The final sentence is not formally a takeaway, but it functions as rhetorical closure by restating the paragraph's governing contrast in its most polished form.

So acoustic irregularity did not solve the problem. The prose replaced sequential mini-jobs with **sequential rephrasings**.

## Fidelity / reverse traceability

Sentence 1 is source-grounded.

Sentence 2 is mostly source-compatible, but `the first thing you notice` is a stronger chronology than required by the current source. Treat as a mild realization risk rather than the main rejection reason.

Sentences 3–6 add no major new factual claims, but several substantive realizations are not directly source-required:

- the judgment that this is `not a very tidy starting point`;
- the broad `everything you would say about it is still vague` formulation;
- `the explanation showing up`;
- `arranged itself into a story`.

These are rhetorical/interpretive inventions rather than clinical facts. They are not automatically forbidden, but because they are doing most of the paragraph's work while adding little semantic value, they do not earn their insertion into production prose.

Preservation status for promotion: **FAIL / not eligible**, because the candidate is editorially rejected and reverse traceability is not clean enough to justify the added rhetorical realizations.

## Heading fit

The paragraph names Somatic Experiencing and distinguishes it from starting with a detailed trauma narrative. That is a legitimate heading relation.

However, it still does not give the reader much that is specifically characteristic of Somatic Experiencing beyond body-before-story attention. As a short opening movement this could be acceptable, but the added four sentences do not improve heading fulfillment proportionally.

Heading fit: **partial**.

## Stopping point

The paragraph should have stopped earlier or genuinely discovered something else inside the same field. Instead it continues until it can produce a polished version of the same thought.

The final sentence is therefore a rhetorical stop, not evidence that the attention naturally released.

## Method diagnosis: continuous field became stylized stasis

The previous correction was necessary: a sentence should not be forced to create a new conceptual state merely to justify its existence.

But the new implementation overcorrected. Because the model was explicitly allowed to `linger`, `hesitate`, and remain in one field, it filled the available space with devices that simulate continued human attention:

- evaluative aside;
- antithesis;
- paraphrase;
- abstract personification;
- polished restatement.

This creates **stylized stasis**: the prose sounds as though thought is continuing, while the reader's semantic/experiential position barely changes.

### Provisional correction

Keep the continuous-field architecture, but add a retrospective **anti-paraphrase / earned-presence gate**:

1. A sentence does **not** need to change the world state.
2. But a cluster of sentences cannot survive merely because each phrases the same state differently.
3. After a natural draft exists, compress each sentence to its semantic/pragmatic contribution. If several adjacent sentences collapse to the same contribution, reject or remove the redundant realizations.
4. A sentence may remain in the same field when it contributes real specificity, evidence, lived friction, uncertainty, relation to the reader, or another genuine function already supported by the source/context. Do not expose these as a generation checklist.
5. Do not manufacture `messiness`, hesitation, metaphor, or evaluative asides merely to prove that the prose is lingering like a human.
6. The continuous field should be seeded as much as possible from **literal source relations and visible article context**, not an abstract model-written summary of what the field is `about`; otherwise the model may circle its own abstraction.

This gate is retrospective. It must not become a new per-sentence state machine or affordance list.
