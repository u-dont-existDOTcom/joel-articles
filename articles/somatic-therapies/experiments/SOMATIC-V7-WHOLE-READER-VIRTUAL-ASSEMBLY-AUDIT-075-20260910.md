```markdown
# Stage 1: Stay Present and Keep Choice

## [Somatic Experiencing](http://traumahealing.org/)

Somatic Experiencing can work with only a small part of a difficult reaction at a time (titration), with room to move away and come back later (pendulation).

To know how ready you are for focused trauma work, ask yourself how stable you are, and whether you need to work on something specific, or a more broad feeling or pattern. If you are already stable enough to work on the trauma itself, spending more and more time on preparation can become another way of avoiding it.

[EXISTING SOMATIC EXPERIENCING YOUTUBE NATIVE OBJECT — PRESERVE EXACT LIVE-SOURCE IDENTITY/ANCHOR]

## Trauma-Sensitive / Restorative Yoga

Yoga helps release tension and build tone. I also like it for body awareness, flexibility, enjoyment, and agency, and it can help with relaxation, posture, meditation, and fitness too.

- Good options include trauma-sensitive yoga, restorative yoga, gentle somatic yoga, and very gentle yin when it is propped, short, and easy to leave.
- “You might try…” is different from “stay there.” Keep your eyes open if you want. Come out whenever you want. Notice whether the pose feels useful, neutral, or too much. There can be different correct versions of a posture; it is not true that everything is correct.
- I would avoid power yoga, hot yoga, intense stretching, long passive holds, forced breathwork or breath retention, aggressive hip opening, or instructions to push through sensation or “open your hips and release your trauma.” Hands-on adjustments require clear consent.

## Gentle Shaking / [TRE](https://traumaprevention.com/frequently-asked-questions/)

Here I mean a little shaking, not an attempt to blast trauma out of my body. Very light TRE or neurogenic tremoring can be enough. So can gentle standing shaking or a little playful movement. I want to be able to stop easily. If it starts feeling compulsive, frightening, dissociative, or difficult to stop, I have gone past what I mean by gentle.

[EXISTING TRE/SHAKING YOUTUBE NATIVE OBJECT — PRESERVE EXACT LIVE-SOURCE IDENTITY/ANCHOR]

**Neck safety note:** aggressive full-body shaking can over-strain the neck, especially if you have whiplash, instability, or hypermobility. If your head is whipping around, support it or stop. Do not turn "release" into an injury.

## Adult Perspective and the Child-State

If I am so flooded that I am basically the child-state, there may not be much adult perspective left online to comfort or protect that child. In my inner-child work I call those functions the Nurturer and Protector.

Sometimes the first thing available is only a neutral witness, or what I call borrowed adulthood: taking the stance of a steady adult when you cannot yet find that steadiness inside yourself. Even one ordinary protective action can matter.

Gentle self-hypnosis can help too, but if imagery starts pulling me away from the room, time, or body I am actually in, I keep my eyes open and stay present instead of going deeper into memories or child dialogue.
```

## Stage-3 virtual boundary after 045 + 074

```markdown
# Stage 3: Work With Trauma Feelings Before the Story Comes In

Some trauma gives you a scene you can point to. Some of it gives you a body reaction, an impulse, an emotion, an image, a fragment, or just the sense that something enormous is happening before there is a coherent story attached to it.

## Aquatic Bodywork / Water Therapy

Hale is trained in aquatic bodywork, and she finds the water especially effective for bringing people back toward their inner child. Sometimes people get so young in the water that they feel as if they're back in the womb. There's also a physical side to it that I find interesting: floating lets somebody stretch and move you through positions that would be difficult or impossible to get into the same way on land.

Some people are just prone to ear infections, even with clean water and earplugs, and for them this may not be worth doing very often.

Hale says water therapy can open someone up emotionally a lot more than expected. Before the water, she likes to do some inner-child work and see what the person is feeling and what they want to work on. After the water, Hale uses massage as a grounding step. I would ask a water therapist about this before booking: if a lot gets opened up, are you still available afterward if I need more help with it?

## Brainspotting

Brainspotting interests me especially for diffuse developmental trauma, pre-verbal or body-held material, trauma that has not responded well to purely cognitive approaches, and those knots where the body seems to know more than the story.

The method uses gaze position, focused attention, bodily awareness, and therapist attunement to stay with emotionally charged material that may be difficult to narrate.

I do not necessarily need to know exactly what I am targeting before the session starts moving. That is also where it connects naturally with inner-child reparenting: something younger can show up through the body before I have a neat memory to narrate, and the Nurturer or Protector can join the work when it does.

Brainspotting can also prepare for inner-child reparenting or run alongside it, depending on how it is used. A resource-oriented or carefully titrated session is different from using Brainspotting to enter highly charged developmental material.

[EXISTING BRAINSPOTTING YOUTUBE NATIVE OBJECT — PRESERVE EXACT LIVE-SOURCE IDENTITY/ANCHOR]

**Further reading:** [D’Antoni et al., 2022 — Comparative study of EMDR, Brainspotting, and related methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8835026/)

I would resist the urge to turn the rest of the day into another processing marathon. If spontaneous shaking comes, let it happen gently. If the body still wants discharge, use gentle [**Shaking Qigong**](http://shakingclass.innersignalselfhypnosis.com/). If you feel floaty or far away, walking meditation may be better. Restorative, gentle yin, or somatic yoga can help if what you want is rest, stretching, toning, or reconnection. EFT can help if emotional activation is still high.

EMDR may fit better after some basic inner-child reparenting for people with diffuse developmental or relational trauma. Having at least some access to a Nurturer and Protector can make it easier to hold what memory work opens. That is not a universal rule: a stable person working with one discrete traumatic event may be ready for EMDR without completing a long preliminary sequence.
```

```markdown
# Stage 4: Work With Memories You Can Actually Point To

This is the part of the map I think of as targeted memory work or reconsolidation. The problem is less “my whole body seems to carry something I cannot quite name” and more “this event, this flashback, this trigger network is still alive.”

## EMDR

If one event is obviously the problem and I can touch it without losing myself, I may go straight to EMDR. That could be an accident or assault, military trauma, a distinct flashback, or a residual trigger that remains after other somatic work. If touching trauma makes me vanish, flood, or spend the rest of the day wrecked, I am going to start more gently.

[EXISTING EMDR YOUTUBE NATIVE OBJECT — PRESERVE EXACT LIVE-SOURCE IDENTITY/ANCHOR]

## Deep Hypnosis and Other Deep Memory Work

I would save deep hypnosis, immersive child dialogue, suggestive exploration, and other deep memory work for the point where you can stay present, stop voluntarily, and return to ordinary life afterward.

## Neurological De-Armoring Support

Sleep, substance use, medication effects, pain, nutrition, and general physiological stability can all change how much therapy you can tolerate. I have a separate [sleep guide here](https://sleep.u-dont-exist.com/).

This is also one place where neurological de-armoring support may matter. EMDR directly engages memory reprocessing, so anything that changes fear learning, inflammation, dissociation, glutamate/GABA balance, or autonomic tone may change what reconsolidation feels like. I go into that separately in my [de-armoring supplement guide](http://dearmor.u-dont-exist.com/).

## After EMDR

Afterward I would keep things simple: walk, eat, hydrate, sleep early if you are tired, do restorative yoga, journal only if it grounds you, maybe use a little EFT if activation is still there.

I would not casually stack Brainspotting, EMDR, intense shaking, and deep yin on the same day unless you already know your system handles that much and you have skilled support. More processing can simply be more processing. Sometimes the missing piece is integration.
```
