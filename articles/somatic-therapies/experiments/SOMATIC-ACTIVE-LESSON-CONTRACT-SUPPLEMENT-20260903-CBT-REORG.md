## A16 — do not erase supported causal inference through hyper-literal sourcing

**Trigger:** the article makes a historical/causal interpretation from a source that explicitly identifies an inadequacy in an earlier model and describes a newer method built around the missing mechanism.

**Required behavior:** distinguish three things:

1. unsupported invented motive;
2. reasonable causal/historical inference from the source's explicit problem critique plus method design;
3. literal first-person motive language.

Do not require (3) before allowing (2).

For the current Somatic CBT/SE section, Joel has directly accepted the interpretation that prevailing cognitive/verbal/exposure approaches were inadequate as a complete account/treatment of trauma and that Somatic Experiencing was developed to address those inadequacies. Payne/Levine/Crane-Godreau (2015) supports that interpretation through its account of SE's development, explicit bottom-up contrast, critique of direct/intense exposure, statement that the simple conditioning model is inadequate for complex behavior, identification of a drawback in talk therapy for trauma, and description of the targeted regulatory system as relatively unaffected by rational thought.

**FAIL if:** model prose or editorial review silently weakens this accepted meaning to `SE merely happens to be different from CBT/exposure` solely because the source does not contain a single literal sentence stating Levine's motive.

**Also FAIL if:** the article exaggerates the accepted inference into `CBT is useless`, `CBT never works for trauma`, or `every somatic method was invented for exactly the same anti-CBT reason`.

## A17 — CBT must not be confined to Stage 5

**Trigger:** any article architecture or prose describing cognitive/reality-testing work.

**Required behavior:** preserve CBT/reality testing as cross-cutting. Stage 5 is specifically about narrative/cognitive integration, meaning, identity, values, and life-building after or alongside other changes.

**FAIL if:** prose implies that thinking, testing beliefs, or behavioral experiments only become legitimate after Stages 1–4.

## A18 — methodological comparison must not become equalized modality cards

**Trigger:** `The Elephant in the Therapy Room: CBT and Exposure` and any compact comparison of CBT, SE, TRE, Focusing, Sensorimotor, Alexander Technique, and 5Rhythms.

**Required behavior:** let the comparison arise inside one developing argument. Use only the modalities needed for the live thought and preserve evidence/role differences.

**FAIL if:** the section becomes a repeated template (`X asks...`, `Y asks...`, `Z asks...`) with similar rhetorical weight, a seven-item taxonomy disguised as prose, or an evidence-equivalence list.

## A19 — no generic placeholder action language

**Trigger:** model-authored Joel-byline prose describing what someone actually does in therapy or in a behavioral experiment.

**Required behavior:** name the action at the level needed for the thought. If the point is testing a feared prediction, dropping a safety behavior, approaching a feared situation, observing what happened, or comparing outcome with prediction, say that rather than substituting generic model shorthand.

**FAIL if:** generated prose uses `go do the thing`, `do the thing`, or a close vague placeholder where the concrete action is doing argumentative work. Joel explicitly identified `go do the thing` as lazy, overused, and strongly AI-shaped on 2026-09-03.

This is an owner style correction, not Pangram evidence and not a universal ban on the ordinary noun `thing` when it is genuinely the exact word.

## A21 — section-local cadence attack after the failed first CBT attempt

The first attempt failed the general cadence gate by packaging the architecture into consecutive rhetorical beats: credit CBT -> `awkward case` -> body/cognition premise -> SE evidence -> `what if body wrong?` -> parallel possibilities -> synthesis -> modality sweep -> slogan.

**Required behavior:** follow one unresolved thought for as long as it actually remains live. A paragraph may contain credit, limitation, evidence, and consequence if that is how the thought develops; these do not each deserve their own rhetorical beat.
