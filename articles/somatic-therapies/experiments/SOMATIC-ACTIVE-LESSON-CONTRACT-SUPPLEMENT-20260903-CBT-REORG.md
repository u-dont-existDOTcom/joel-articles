## A16 — do not erase supported causal inference through hyper-literal sourcing

**Trigger:** the article makes a historical/causal interpretation from a source that explicitly identifies an inadequacy in an earlier model and describes a newer method built around the missing mechanism.

**Required behavior:** distinguish three things:

1. unsupported invented motive;
2. reasonable causal/historical inference from the source's explicit problem critique plus method design;
3. literal first-person motive language.

Do not require (3) before allowing (2).

For the current Somatic CBT/SE section, Joel has directly accepted the interpretation that prevailing cognitive/verbal/exposure approaches were inadequate as a complete account/treatment of trauma and that Somatic Experiencing was developed to address those inadequacies. Payne/Levine/Crane-Godreau (2015) supports that interpretation through its account of SE's development, explicit bottom-up contrast, critique of direct/intense exposure, statement that the simple conditioning model is inadequate for complex behavior, identification of a drawback in talk therapy for trauma, and description of the targeted regulatory system as relatively unaffected by rational thought.

**FAIL if:** model prose or editorial review silently weakens this accepted meaning to `SE merely happens to be different from CBT/exposure` solely because the source does not contain a single literal sentence stating Levine's motive.

**Also FAIL if:** the article exaggerates the accepted inference into `CBT is useless`, `CBT never works for trauma`, or `every somatic method was invented for exactly the same anti-CBT reason`.
