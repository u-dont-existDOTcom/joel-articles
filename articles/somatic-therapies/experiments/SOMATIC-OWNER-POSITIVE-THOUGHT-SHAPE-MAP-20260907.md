## Source audit 1 — owner-accepted Somatic Introduction

### Actual movement

Opening movement:

`direct bodily claim -> named source/origin -> concrete consequence`

The first paragraph does not begin by listing somatic concepts. It states the claim, follows ordinary curiosity to Levine's source story, then gives the consequential detail about captured animals.

Next movement:

`practical reversal -> concrete human variation -> analogy -> social/humorous aside -> practical judgment`

`But discovering the body's programming...` changes the epistemic mode: a book/source cannot complete the practical knowing. The prose stays with that pressure through concrete bodily variation, then an analogy, then the `Bike Expert` aside, and only then lands on `feel it out and see what works.`

Next movement:

`qualification of the practical openness -> rough owner map -> reason the map is only a map -> personal destination`

`Still...` does not provide a symmetrical counterargument. It changes scale: despite individual variation, Joel sees a broad sequence. The five stages then appear as the object being asserted, not as five explanatory cards. The article explains why modality placement is approximate, acknowledges evidence heterogeneity, states that the map is Joel's, and then connects the map to deeper inner-child work.

### Positive structural lessons

- Begin from the **claim or pressure**, not the terminology inventory.
- Let a source enter because the preceding claim naturally raises `where did this come from?`, not because the paragraph needs evidence as a separate job.
- Let consequences do explanatory work; do not add a sentence afterward explaining why the consequence matters when it already shows it.
- A `but` or `still` turn changes the actual thought-state; it is not balance machinery.
- Lists can appear when the thought/source really is a list, but surrounding prose does not convert each list item into an explanatory card.
- Analogy/humor may arise after the concrete thought is already alive; they are not opening devices or required humanization features.

## Source audit 3 — natural cancer research prose

This sample is especially useful for research-conversational thought duration.

Common movement observed across the opening and later research passages:

`current/personal stake -> story or odd observation -> consequence/curiosity -> another relevant case -> explicit shift of scale -> why the article/research exists`

The prose often stays with an event long enough for its oddness or consequence to become clear. It then uses an ordinary connective (`Anyway`, `But`, `That being said`, etc.) when the mind genuinely changes level. The connector itself is not the style; the **state change it marks** is.

Research passages frequently use:

`what I thought -> what I checked/encountered -> what changed -> what remains uncertain -> what I currently recommend or want the reader to notice`

This is not a polished expert card. Epistemic history remains visible when it performs a reader-facing function.

### Positive structural lessons

- Concrete stake can legitimately precede abstraction by a long distance.
- Paragraph duration is uneven because stories, claims, and caveats take different amounts of thought-time.
- Parentheses/asides can sit inside a continuing thought rather than becoming separate micro-sections.
- Open-ended accumulations occur (`etc.`, extra cases, side observations) when the set is genuinely open; closure is not manufactured by choosing three representative items.
- The prose is comfortable making a judgment and then continuing into unresolved uncertainty rather than resolving every tension immediately.

## Cross-sample positive invariants

These are the strongest reusable structural findings from the source set.

### 1. Concrete pressure precedes terminology

Joel commonly establishes the thing that is happening, the practical question, the real-world situation, or the odd observation before introducing the conceptual vocabulary used to explain it.

Generation consequence: **do not open a fresh paragraph by serializing the terminology that must eventually be preserved unless the terminology itself is the reader's live question.**

### 2. Thought follows causality/curiosity, not inventory order

Common transitions are:

- claim -> where did that come from? -> source/observation;
- observation -> what happened because of it? -> consequence;
- practical uncertainty -> what would I actually look at? -> question/test;
- existing framework -> what does it miss? -> alternative frame;
- surprising result -> does this happen elsewhere? -> another case;
- current belief -> what changed it? -> evidence/revision.

These relations are generative routes. They are not required sequences.

### 3. State changes are explicit but earned

`But`, `Still`, `Anyway`, `However`, and similar turns appear when the thought actually changes scale, direction, source, or practical implication. They are not sprinkled in to make prose conversational.

### 4. Unevenness comes from thought duration

One movement may be one sentence; another may run for a paragraph. A source story can take longer than its abstract conclusion. A caveat can be shorter than the claim it modifies. There is no expectation that neighboring functions receive equal rhetorical space.

### 5. Lists are content-dependent

Owner prose does contain lists. The difference is functional:

- a list may be the actual external model (SIBAM, five stages, FINDSEX);
- an open-ended set may illustrate variation;
- a recommendation inventory may genuinely need navigation.

What is not characteristic as a default thought engine is repeated model-selected compact closure: three terms -> three actions, three symptoms -> three fixes, paragraph 1 forms -> paragraph 2 cues -> paragraph 3 warnings.

### 6. The next sentence often makes a new move rather than explaining the prior move

Joel's prose frequently trusts a source detail, example, consequence, or judgment to do its own work. Explanatory aftercare is used when genuinely needed, not as an automatic landing function.

### 7. Authorial position is provenance, not significance staging

First person often answers `what do I think / what did I see / what changed my mind / what am I doing?` It is less often used merely to announce that the next generic recommendation matters (`I like X because...`) when the concrete judgment can simply be stated.

## Positive map library for generation

Choose **one** only when it fits the live local pressure. Do not walk through the whole library.

### Map A — claim / provenance / consequence

`live claim -> source, observation, or event that made it credible -> consequence that changes the reader's understanding -> stop or continue only if a new question is alive`

Useful when a claim feels generic until its origin or consequence is shown.

### Map B — practical uncertainty / discriminating question

`practical problem -> what the person would actually look at or ask -> distinction that changes the decision -> stop`

Useful for Stage-1 readiness and other guide decisions. Joel's current readiness sentence is an owner example of this type.

### Map C — real situation / mechanism / limitation / alternative

`reader's actual situation -> what the existing approach does -> what remains unresolved -> alternative frame that becomes necessary -> detail only after the alternative has a job`

Useful for comparative/modality argument, not for every modality introduction.

### Map D — odd observation / curiosity / extension

`odd or concrete observation -> question it creates -> another relevant case/source or mechanism -> practical/current interpretation -> stop before abstract taxonomy unless the taxonomy is genuinely needed`

Useful when a lived/source anomaly is what generated the idea.

### Map E — continuing thought with an earned turn

`thought continues through uneven clauses/sentences -> actual complication changes state -> explicit turn (`but/still/anyway/etc.` if natural) -> new scale/direction`

Useful when the model is tempted to close one mini-essay and start another even though Joel's thought is still one continuous movement.
