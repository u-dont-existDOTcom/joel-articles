## Owner-directed conceptual direction

The supplied material establishes the following direction for the reorganization:

- CBT belongs in the article precisely because it is not another somatic modality; it is the major comparison case that can make the somatic project intelligible.
- Give CBT genuine credit for reality testing, challenging interpretations, behavioral experiments, changing avoidance, and testing feared predictions.
- Reject both extremes:
  - bodily signals are not automatically infallible truths that should be obeyed;
  - bodily signals are not automatically irrational noise merely because explicit cognition says the environment is safe.
- Central proposed synthesis: **listen before correcting; then test what needs testing.**
- The bodily-intelligence thesis is epistemic: do not discard information merely because it arrives somatically rather than propositionally.
- Premature cognitive restructuring can reproduce a gaslighting-like failure mode when the therapist decides what a bodily reaction means before investigating it. Do not make the stronger claim that CBT itself is gaslighting.
- A body reaction may reflect a conditioned false alarm, subtle real-world information not yet conceptualized, or some mixture; inquiry should precede premature certainty.
- The social/institutional critique should be stated defensibly: CBT fits unusually well with systems organized around diagnosable symptoms, measurable functioning, standardized interventions, and randomized trials. Do not reduce DSM impairment to employment or claim a conspiracy-shaped motive.
- `CBT Dad` is a candidate joke/device, not owner-final prose: direct reality-testing can be exactly useful at some moments and can also arrive too early.
- Larger methodological thesis candidate: **the body is not infallible; neither is the thinking mind.** Healing becomes more interesting when neither is appointed dictator.

## Therapy-question comparison supplied for consideration

The owner-supplied conceptual source proposes comparing modalities by the question or experiment they bring to experience:

- CBT: Is this thought/belief/fear accurate, and what happens if I behave differently?
- Somatic Experiencing: What is the organism doing underneath the story, and what happens when the interrupted physiological process gets enough space to change?
- TRE: What happens if voluntary control gets out of the way enough for tremoring to organize itself?
- Focusing: Is there implicit bodily knowing here that has not differentiated into words yet?
- Sensorimotor Psychotherapy / Ogden: What action, movement, posture, or protective organization is trying to happen, and what changes when it is experimentally altered?
- 5Rhythms: Which qualities of movement are accessible or habitually avoided?
- Alexander Technique: What habitual/unnecessary effort is being added that might be stopped rather than corrected by doing more?

These are conceptual source-pool formulations, not automatically publication-ready claims or evidence-equivalent recommendations. New modalities must be distinguished from the article's existing detailed modality recommendations unless Joel separately accepts their fuller inclusion.

## Genealogy/evidence distinctions to preserve

- Somatic Experiencing: the 2015 Payne/Levine/Crane-Godreau paper describes SE as developed by Levine from 1977 onward, explicitly contrasts its bottom-up interoceptive/proprioceptive focus with primarily cognitive therapies, and explicitly distinguishes its gradual/indirect approach from direct/intense exposure. This supports deliberate methodological contrast. It does **not by itself prove** the stronger historical-causal claim that Levine invented SE *because* CBT/exposure was inadequate.
- TRE: Berceli's founder-history materials describe war experience, terror tremors/fetal response, later study with Alexander Lowen/Bioenergetic Analysis, and eventually separating the tremor mechanism from psychodynamics. Do not attribute an anti-CBT origin without a stronger direct Berceli source.
- Focusing/Gendlin and Sensorimotor/Ogden provide established adjacent examples of bodily/implicit information and posture/movement/action as therapeutic data.
- Alexander Technique and 5Rhythms may serve as adjacent embodied-method examples, but should not be silently presented as equivalently evidenced trauma therapies.
