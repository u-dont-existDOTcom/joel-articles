## Verdict

**Reject as current Stage-1 prose.**

The longer boundary reveals a model-shaped procedural staircase that was difficult to diagnose confidently in the earlier two-sentence probe. The prior provisional PASS was too charitable.

## Cold audit — pass 1: sentence necessity / semantic movement

### Sentence 1

`Somatic Experiencing doesn't require you to start with a detailed trauma story.`

This is source-grounded and has good heading fit. It inherits the immediately preceding bottom-up-versus-narrative discussion and does not itself create a fidelity problem.

### Sentence 2

`Sometimes all you have at first is a sensation, and you haven't decided what, if anything, you want to do next.`

This keeps the narrative-vs-bodily-material pressure open. `Sometimes` prevents it from becoming a universal definition of SE, although `all you have` remains somewhat stronger than the source strictly requires.

### Sentence 3

`You can leave that undecided.`

This is the clearest unnecessary sentence. Sentence 2 already established that the decision is unresolved. Sentence 3 adds generic therapeutic permission rather than changing time, case, premise, causal state, consequence, or live question. It is explanatory/reassuring aftercare.

### Sentence 4

`The body is already doing something, but noticing it does not commit you to following it into a memory.`

The first clause is vague restatement. The second introduces a new figurative/procedural relation: a sensation is something one might `follow ... into a memory`. The current source supports not forcing a detailed trauma narrative, but it does not establish that attending to a sensation naturally leads along a path into a memory. This is an unnecessary causal metaphor and therefore at least a reverse-traceability concern.

### Sentences 5–7

`You can pay attention to the sensation and still look around the room.`

`If the reaction starts carrying you farther than you meant to go, stop there.`

`Whether you go back to the sensation after that is still a choice.`

These are not merely clauses inside one indivisible event. They perform distinct procedural jobs:

1. divide/shift attention between sensation and room;
2. detect excessive movement and stop;
3. decide whether to return.

Together they recreate the approach/return preservation sequence as a compact procedure. The fact that the sequence arose from local reader continuity rather than an exposed checklist does not make the resulting cognitive cadence non-list-like.

`carrying you farther than you meant to go` also introduces an imprecise movement metaphor: farther in attention, activation, memory depth, distress, or processing is left unspecified.

## Cold audit — pass 2: cadence / thought shape

The paragraph's hidden architecture is approximately:

`no story required -> sensation may be present -> permission to remain undecided -> noticing is noncommittal -> orient elsewhere -> stop threshold -> optional return`

This is a staircase. Each sentence advances the reader to the next compact conceptual/action state. The final four sentences have similar rhetorical weight and close cleanly after one job each.

This is exactly the sort of equalized conceptual bar-length and hidden procedural sequencing that the active cadence protocol warns about. The defect is larger than sentence length. Removing or combining individual sentences without repairing the governing mechanism would likely reproduce the same shape.

The ending is also more closed than the prior audit admitted. `Whether you go back ... is still a choice` restates the Stage-1 thesis in a final form after the preceding actions have demonstrated it. It functions as a takeaway/closure sentence.

The room-orientation, stopping, and possible return functions are source-compatible in isolation. Their problem here is primarily realization/architecture: they have been serialized into a neat procedure.

## Heading fit

## Stopping point

The final sentence creates rhetorical closure rather than demonstrating that the thought naturally stopped. It restates choice after a sequence already designed around choice.

A natural stop may occur earlier or later, but this exact stop is generated by the procedure's completion: after `return?`, the state machine has nowhere else to go inside the local loop, so it produces a thesis-like ending.

## Method diagnosis: sentence-by-sentence state transition is itself creating AI cadence

The veto-only correction successfully removed the preservation inventory from the next-pressure selector. That was a real improvement. However, the remaining **state-machine requirement** still biases generation toward model-shaped prose.

The current microworld method asks after every sentence:

- what changed?
- what is the strongest unresolved pressure?
- what next action/state follows?

That implicitly rewards every sentence for performing a discrete transition. Over several sentences, the result is a staircase even when no hidden checklist is present.

Natural prose does not require every sentence to advance to a new conceptual state. A necessary sentence may:

- remain inside the same perception;
- hesitate or qualify without balancing it;
- elaborate one image or relation without completing a new job;
- preserve a spoken connective across clauses;
- perform a social/pragmatic act toward the reader;
- dwell because the thought itself has not moved yet.

The existing canonical skill already requires each sentence to answer, complicate, or naturally redirect live curiosity; it does **not** require each sentence to create a distinct state transition.

### Provisional method correction

For the next experiment:

1. Keep preservation knowledge out of generation-state selection; retain it as veto/validation only.
2. Retire the requirement that every sentence must produce a named state change.
3. Track the **continuous field of attention/pressure** across an irregular stretch of prose rather than discrete sentence states.
4. Allow several sentences or clauses to inhabit the same unresolved state without being penalized as `no progress`, provided each remains locally necessary.
5. Evaluate state change only when the thought actually changes, not after every period.
6. Do not ask `what comes next?` after each sentence. Ask instead whether the current thought is still alive, and if so what the writer is still attending to inside it.
7. After the bounded movement exists, use the preservation inventory and cold audit to test fidelity and hidden sequencing.

This is a stronger approximation of continuous human thought than the current Markov-like sentence/action loop.
