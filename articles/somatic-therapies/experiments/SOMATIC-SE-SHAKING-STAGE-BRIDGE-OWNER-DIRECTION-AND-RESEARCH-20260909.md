## Source verification — is shaking part of Somatic Experiencing?

### Conclusion

**Yes, spontaneous shaking/trembling can be part of Somatic Experiencing, but SE is not the same thing as a deliberate shaking protocol such as TRE or Shaking Medicine.**

Primary source:

Peter Payne, Peter A. Levine, Mardi A. Crane-Godreau. “Somatic experiencing: using interoception and proprioception as core elements of trauma therapy.” *Frontiers in Psychology* 6 (2015): 93. doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00093.

Relevant source claims:

- SE is described as using interoceptive, proprioceptive, and kinesthetic attention to support completion of thwarted defensive responses and regulation/discharge of excess autonomic arousal.
- The paper explicitly describes spontaneous gentle shaking, trembling, tears, heat, and related phenomena as possible autonomic discharge during SE.
- It states that an SE therapist may treat trembling/shivering as an opportunity for therapeutic intervention and encourage the movement to develop while maintaining titration and capacity.
- Biological completion/discharge is described as occurring in controlled steps while attention follows visceral sensation or **subtle motor impulses associated with incomplete defensive responses**.
- The authors explicitly caution, based on their clinical model, that “not just any muscular activity will do”; they argue the important movement may be the specific defensive action that was interrupted.

Secondary Levine/Ergos description:

- Levine’s public SE description says freeze/collapse may discharge through spontaneous gentle inner shaking and trembling and presents SE as helping this incomplete process run its course.

### Animal-evidence boundary

Do **not** state as established comparative physiology that wild animals must shake out trauma.

The 2015 Payne/Levine/Crane-Godreau paper itself says the wild-animal recovery observations involving involuntary movement, breathing changes, yawning, shaking, and trembling came from Levine’s long naturalistic observation and anecdotal reports from animal professionals, and that the authors could not find significant peer-reviewed treatment of those phenomena.

Reader-facing implication:

- It is accurate to say that **Levine’s model was partly inspired by observations that wild animals often tremble/shake after extreme threat and that he interprets this as discharge/completion**.
- It is not accurate to present the animal-shaking mechanism as settled experimental evidence.

## Architecture consequence

### Stages are functional jobs, not modality silos

A modality can legitimately perform more than one stage function.

Somatic Experiencing itself can perform:

- Stage-1 functions: sensing/interoception, orientation, titration, pendulation, staying present, maintaining choice;
- Stage-2 functions: biological completion and autonomic discharge, sometimes including spontaneous trembling/shaking or specific defensive movements.

Therefore the article must **not** imply:

`SE finds the energy -> shaking is required to release what SE cannot release`.

The more accurate owner route is:

`SE may help someone feel/track the body and stay with activation -> sometimes SE itself already permits completion/discharge -> when a more active or sustained discharge practice seems useful, or when subtle SE work is not getting much moving, deliberate shaking can be tried as a Stage-2-biased tool.`

This is intentionally non-exclusive and non-mandatory.

## Shaking Qigong / TRE distinction

Treat deliberate Shaking Qigong / Shaking Medicine / TRE as **separate practices that overlap conceptually with one SE mechanism** rather than as components of the SE method.

Useful distinction:

- SE: follows sensations, impulses, and defensive responses; spontaneous tremor may emerge; movement may be very subtle and ideally corresponds to what the body is trying to complete.
- TRE / deliberate shaking practices: intentionally evoke or sustain tremoring/shaking as the practice itself.

Do not overstate the empirical equivalence of these mechanisms.
