Introduction

Physical trauma reactions can continue after the danger is over, even when you know you're safe.

With complex or developmental trauma, going straight for the deepest memory can be overwhelming. Instead of diving off the deep end heroically, take it easy, one step at a time. As Buddhist nun Pema Chodron says, "feather touch" is all it takes to gently open up.  Peter Levine, the founder of Somatic Experiencing, calls this gentle work around the edges, "pendulation."

In inner-child therapy, you can know exactly what the younger part needs and still get pulled so far into the child-state that there isn't enough adult perspective left to give it.

Your Physical State Can Change What Therapy Does

Whether a somatic therapy works well can depend a lot on what else is going on in your body. Keep in mind that poor sleep, pain, inflammation, dissociation, medications, and so forth can all change how you respond. If the therapy suddenly stops doing much, that doesn't necessarily mean you need a different therapy. Sometimes the physical problem is the thing you need to deal with first.

I have seen friends get very little from these therapies until they addressed physical neuroinflammation with things like low-dose naltrexone, phosphatidylserine, DMSO, and so on. In my own experience, I cannot do real healing work when I feel like I have toxins in my gut. Ozonated zeolite helps that a lot. Ozonated charcoal water also helps me when I am surrounded by chaotic energy fields, whether EMF or auric.

I think of that as neurological de-armoring: not another trauma technique, but part of the physical ground the techniques are working on. I have separate guides on sleep and neurological de-armoring if that seems relevant to you.

Somatic Work and Inner-Child Reparenting

It's not that I can't access the love, but the love seems unsafe. My inner child is sitting there observing its grown-up self and saying, “Yeah, that doesn't look like things went well for us.” Then I find myself getting resentful at the child and saying, “Well, you're the one who was the child and you have the opportunity to grow up.” I end up at war with the younger version who didn't do what they were supposed to do, while the older version is trying to make this vow of protection and nurturing. And the child is sitting there thinking, “Big fuckity whoopty doo. What are you gonna do for me?” Relaxation hasn't really affected that awkward, difficult time with the inner child for me.

In my inner-child work I call the adult roles the Nurturer and Protector.

The Protector creates real-world safety. The Protector cleans the room.

I also use gentle self-hypnosis for reparenting and present safety. I do not use it to force trauma memories.

I notice that I can be really tense in my solar plexus, and that may be blocking me from coming up into the heart chakra. When the body feels too tight, or there's too much emotional tension to access the heart, I use a mantra or an unforced slower exhale. If I use Buteyko-style breathing, the exhale is passive, relaxed, and unforced.

If I can't feel the love, I imagine something real or someone real that I love to feel that feeling, then redirect it.

There’s one very simple technique you can do at any time to feel a deeper connection to your inner child. Place one hand on the center of your chest (heart chakra), to feel a connection to love. Place the other hand on the center of your solar plexus just under your chest (solar plexus chakra) to feel a connection to your inner child. The solar plexus is the most vulnerable part of the torso. One slap there hurts quite a lot, and so it’s natural to feel the vulnerable inner child living in this region. Now imagine sending love from the heart to the inner child, then receiving love from inner child and sending back to heart, in an infinite beautiful loop!

Starting Gently

If Higher Self, the universal heart, or any sacred language feels unavailable or borrowed from somebody else, begin with plain witnessing instead. Notice the room, the body, and the younger state. The fact that something can notice is enough adult presence for the first step.

Somatic Experiencing

Somatic Experiencing is probably the first thing on this list that I would look at when even brushing the edge of the trauma is too much.

Trauma-Sensitive / Restorative Yoga

When I say yoga here, I do not mean power yoga, hot yoga, or somebody announcing, “Open your hips and release your trauma.” I mean trauma-sensitive yoga, restorative yoga, gentle somatic yoga, or very gentle yin.

With yin I would use props and keep the holds short. Can I keep my eyes open? Can I come out whenever I want? Is the sensation useful, neutral, or too much? A posture can have more than one correct version; choose one that fits your body.

I would avoid intense stretching, forced breathwork, breath retention, aggressive hip openers, long passive holds that you have to endure, and hands-on adjustments without clear consent. Pushing through is not the point.

Gentle Shaking / TRE

Here I mean a little shaking, not trying to blast trauma out of the body. Basic TRE, very light neurogenic tremoring, gentle standing shaking, or short playful movement can be enough. If it becomes frightening, dissociative, compulsive, or hard to stop, stop.

If tremoring reaches your neck while you are lying down, do not let your head whip around unsupported. Reduce or stop the movement, support the head, and get guidance before continuing. Forceful, rapid back-and-forth neck movement can injure the neck.

What I Use Between Deeper Sessions

EFT / Tapping

EFT is very nice for regulation. Part of it is distraction, because my hands and attention have something else to do while I am triggered. You can also literally activate different parts of the brain by tapping different parts, so it is interesting to notice how you feel as you move through the tapping points. And it is a little bit of a massage.

Actually, I mentioned earlier that shaving my head and massaging it works really well for me. Sometimes I feel like that works even better than EFT.

I use it before a hard conversation, after somebody triggers me, or when my mind and body are both stuck in the same loop. It can take the pressure down. That does not make it the only treatment for severe trauma.

Shaking Qigong / Shaking Medicine

My friend Louka is why I am linking this particular class. He had tried many different healing and somatic modalities, including basic TRE. TRE itself did nothing for him, and nothing helped him much until he got into this class, which costs $10 per month on Skool.

The class is much more than just TRE.

By blockage I mean what energy is stuck in you. I often have the kind of natural shaking that animals have, rather than noticing much benefit from intentional therapeutic techniques. Especially on psychedelics, it is like my body knows exactly which part to shake and how. So it is not just shaking = mental health. When the right thing is shaken the right way, things loosen up as they should and the energy flows.

TRE is a system they are trying to teach en masse, so they had to distill the method down to what seems to work most of the time for most people. Obviously, if you could get more angles at it, you would get more chance of success—and less chance of being called an expert. “Just try lots of stuff till it works” is how my expertise runs on this.

I did not try the Shaking Qigong class, but I have done qigong and found it very helpful for moving energy. The class is a middle ground between a predictable TRE structure and totally unstructured shaking, with other people there too. That might help somebody who needs guidance but gets nowhere with standard TRE.

I could imagine 10–45 minutes as a regular practice for chronic freeze, non-verbal emotional processing, accumulated stress, or activation left over after deeper work. I still need to be able to stop and settle afterward.

I do not know exactly what deserves the credit when shaking helps. Maybe movement lets muscles or defensive impulses do something they never got to finish. Maybe it changes arousal because you are moving and paying attention to the body. Qigong says stuck qi is moving again. These are different explanations, and one does not prove the other.

I also do not use the size of the research literature as a ranking of what my own nervous system should try first. Claims about releasing decades of trauma, awakening kundalini, clearing stored emotion, or rapidly healing the whole nervous system still have to be tested against life. I pay attention to people whose patterns resemble mine, change one thing at a time when I can, and ask whether I am actually doing better.

One combination I like is:

1. Shaking Qigong / TRE — let some activation move.
2. Restorative or gentle yin yoga — settle and soften.
3. EFT tapping — use it if emotional activation is still high.

Yoga can also come before shaking or after deeper processing. This is not a law.

When the Body Knows More Than the Story

Brainspotting

I used my finger to direct my gaze slowly around my field of vision and noticed that different memories felt different, or different memories came up, at different angles. That was basically how I figured out very quickly that I could do a simple version myself.

I had heard that looking up-left means remembering and up-right means constructing or thinking. That general NLP rule did not hold up when I checked it, so I do not use it to read other people's eyes. I do notice the pattern in myself, and gaze and eye movement interact with memory retrieval more broadly.

What I like is the edging. I can go toward the uncomfortable place, feather-touch the boundary, then come back out instead of diving into the worst of it. It feels more effective to me. I also give Brainspotting points for being democratic like EFT: you can experiment with a simple version yourself, although a formal session adds therapist attunement, focused attention, and bodily awareness around the gaze position.

I would not use the fact that I can experiment myself as a reason to dive into the deepest trauma alone. If I cannot tell when I am overwhelmed, stay present, or stop, that is the limit.

Brainspotting interests me for diffuse developmental trauma, pre-verbal or body-held material, and anything where the body seems to know more than the story. A younger part can show up through the body before there is a neat memory, and the Nurturer or Protector can join the work then. For further reading, see D’Antoni et al., 2022, a comparative study involving EMDR, Brainspotting, and related methods.

Afterward, if the body still wants gentle discharge, Shaking Qigong can fit. If I feel floaty or far away, I would rather walk and get oriented to ordinary life.

EMDR

EMDR is more targeted toward specific memories or traumas, with recall procedures and a clearer thing you are working on. Brainspotting you can feel out, and it does not require knowing exactly what you are targeting. That is the main difference in my mind.

An accident, assault memory, military trauma, distinct flashback, or specific trigger network gives EMDR a fairly clear target. Somebody who floods every time they get near the material may spend three to six months building capacity first, and severe developmental trauma can take longer. A stable person with one clear event does not need months of body work first just because a sequence looks tidy on paper.

I become more cautious when the trauma is diffuse or developmental, mainly bodily or pre-verbal, relational, or the nervous system is still unstable. I would want some access to a Nurturer and Protector before opening very charged material, and Brainspotting may fit earlier.

After EMDR I want the rest of the day to be almost boring. Walk. Eat. Hydrate. Sleep early if I am tired. Maybe do restorative yoga or a little EFT. I would not casually stack Brainspotting, EMDR, intense shaking, and deep yin on the same day unless I already knew my system could handle that much and I had skilled support.

I check the wake too: sleep that night, irritability the next day, dissociation, pain, compulsive processing, and whether I am treating people better or worse. Sometimes the useful thing after processing is to stop processing.

Making Sense of What Changed

Light CBT / Narrative Integration

Once my body is no longer insisting “this is happening now,” talking to the belief makes more sense. Then I can revisit old beliefs, build a more coherent life story, separate past danger from present reality, clarify boundaries, and return to inner-child reparenting.

If the adult voices still do not feel available, I may borrow one sentence, boundary, or direction from somebody safe. The borrowed figure stays beside me while I choose the words and carry one small part myself. I call that borrowed adulthood.

How I Know Whether It Actually Helped

My outcome measure is boring: does the thing still hit as hard later? By restimulation I mean what happens when I remember the event again. Does the same charge come back into the body and mind, or not?

While I was working on this article I was still upset about an argument with my housemate. I had been washing her moldy clothes for her, one of the dresses got discolored, and she yelled at me about it. I was still stewing over how she treated me.

So I did a quick bit of self-Brainspotting. I moved my finger and eyes slowly around while thinking about the event. When my gaze went farther to either side I noticed blissful tingles as I moved into more peripheral vision. I do not take that as proof of the simple claim that “peripheral vision activates the parasympathetic nervous system”; the research is more complicated than that.

Subjectively, I could move from the more settled side with the blissful tingles to the distressed side from moment to moment and kind of move the bliss into the distress while I was thinking about the event. Afterward it felt as though the event held less negative energy, or less blocking energy, in my mind.

If one hour later I am still stewing, perhaps the shift was temporary. If I can remember the event and still be less restimulated by it, then it worked more durably. One treatment of EMDR is often not enough either.

Sky Hypnosis and Vagal Blitz

Deep memory work, immersive child dialogue, deep hypnosis, and suggestive exploration are things I save for when I can stay present, stop voluntarily, and come back afterward.

Sky Hypnosis and Vagal Blitz are intense state-shifters rather than ordinary regulation practices. They are not prerequisites for healing, although a stable person may still find one useful before doing the therapies above.

Sky Hypnosis can make you lightheaded. Vagal Blitz is a lying-down-only practice with cardiovascular and mental-health cautions. Read the complete guide, begin with the least intense version that gives a useful shift, and stop if balance, orientation, or recovery becomes unreliable. Fainting and loss of consciousness are not the goal.

I do not count “wow” as an outcome. A huge release, bliss, or an altered state can feel profound while the actual problem stays basically untouched. When I remember the event later, am I less restimulated? Am I doing any better with pain, boundaries, conflict, and ordinary life? If the practice has become a way to avoid grief, conflict, practical problems, or deeper relational healing, the extraordinary state does not rescue it.
