## Cold audit 1 — literal prose

### Sentence 1

`Sometimes the body is already responding before there is a clear sense of what to do next.`

This is the strongest sentence.

- It directly realizes the supplied local fact.
- It does not explain section placement.
- It does not reopen the previous meaning/narrative discussion.
- It introduces no hidden Stage-1 technique.
- `clear sense of what to do next` is slightly softer than `deliberate choice`, but does not materially change the relation.

Potential risk: `body is already responding` remains generic, but no concrete symptom should be invented merely to make it vivid.

Disposition: **candidate seed worth preserving for further experiment; not owner-final prose.**

### Sentence 2

`In that moment, choice does not have to mean making the activation disappear.`

This repeats the invented-prerequisite pattern seen in prior same-context prose, now arising independently in the isolated writer.

The visible packet did not say or imply:

`choice = making activation disappear`.

The sentence creates that possible misconception so it can negate it. It is source-compatible as a non-claim, but rhetorically defensive and not the next necessary move.

Disposition: **FAIL — invented prerequisite / defensive aftercare.**

### Sentence 3

`It can begin more simply: noticing what is happening without immediately having to follow it, change it, or decide what it means.`

The positive core — `noticing what is happening` — is source-compatible with the working SE relation that attention can stay with activation.

But the sentence packages the thought as a neat three-part negative list:

- do not immediately follow it;
- do not immediately change it;
- do not immediately decide what it means.

Problems:

1. `follow it` is semantically unclear: follow the activation, an impulse, a behavior, or the material?
2. `change it` introduces another imagined demand not established by the reader state.
3. `decide what it means` partially reopens meaning/narrative material already established in the preceding article section, despite the writer not having seen that section in v2. The fact that it arose independently makes it a generic model association, but article-wide subtraction still rejects it as duplicate work at this placement.
4. The three negatives create hidden list-thinking and make the sentence feel comprehensive rather than locally necessary.

Disposition: **FAIL as written. The bare `noticing what is happening` relation is the only clearly useful component.**

### Sentence 4

`That small distinction can leave room between bodily activation and the next action.`

This is a polished abstract landing.

- `That small distinction` is significance-staging / retrospective diagnosis.
- `leave room between` converts the preceding thought into a metaphorical conceptual takeaway.
- The sentence mostly explains why the prior sentence mattered rather than advancing the thought.
- It also makes a weak causal claim (`distinction -> room between activation and action`) that is plausible but stronger than the sparse packet itself established.

Disposition: **FAIL — explanatory landing / abstraction after the thought.**
