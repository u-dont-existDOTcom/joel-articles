## Why this map exists

The prior microworld variants removed increasingly obvious outlines but still produced model-shaped cadence because they treated every sentence as a state transition:

`sentence -> what changed? -> next pressure -> next sentence`

That architecture rewards each sentence for having a discrete conceptual job. In the extended veto-only probe, the result became a procedural staircase even though the preservation inventory was hidden.

The correction is to model **continuous attention**, not sequential sentence states.

A sentence is not automatically an event in the world model. Several sentences may occur while the same governing attention, uncertainty, and pressure remain active. The map changes only when the literal prose actually changes the governing thought.

## What the local writer can see

The local writer may see only:

- the literal visible article context immediately before the target;
- the current heading/subheading;
- the literal prose generated so far;
- the continuous field above in its **current** state.

The local writer does **not** receive:

- unsatisfied Stage-1 preservation obligations;
- a list of available techniques or concepts;
- a next-pressure question after each sentence;
- a target sentence count or word count;
- a prescribed cadence contour;
- a required conclusion, takeaway, or stopping sentence.

## The field is descriptive, not generative instructions

The field records what remains live; it does not tell the writer what move to make.

In particular, do not turn `governing attention`, `live uncertainty`, `reader position`, and `field pressure` into four rhetorical jobs. They are simultaneous properties of one field.

A sentence may deepen, linger, hesitate, specify, address the reader, or simply continue the same attention without the world model declaring a new state. Those are not required operations and are not presented to the writer as an affordance menu.

## Field-change gate

Do **not** run this gate after every sentence as a mandatory transition step. Run it only when there is a credible reason to think the governing thought may have shifted, or when the writer naturally pauses.

A field change requires something substantial such as:

- the object of attention genuinely changes;
- a new causal premise becomes necessary;
- the live uncertainty is actually displaced by a different one;
- the reader is now in a materially different practical/intellectual situation;
- the existing field genuinely releases and another thought begins.

These are diagnostics, not destinations.

A new period, a new example, a connective, or a slightly different wording is not by itself a field change.

## Veto wall — outside the field

Source and preservation knowledge remain outside generation and act only as a veto layer.

The veto layer may reject literal prose for the smallest sufficient reason, for example:

- unsupported chronology;
- invented symptom / vignette / mechanism / timeframe;
- changed actor or agency;
- changed certainty;
- substantive metaphor with no source/owner basis;
- contradiction with accepted article context;
- restatement with no useful reader function;
- hidden procedural/checklist packaging;
- artificial summary or closure.

The veto must **not** respond by naming what protected content should be inserted next.

## Stopping rule

Do not stop because:

- a paragraph is long enough;
- a sentence supplied a nice landing;
- one protected function has been covered;
- the prose has reached a familiar `problem -> response -> choice` shape;
- the current field can be summarized.

Stop when the governing attention actually releases or changes and there is no remaining live pressure inside this field that the writer is still genuinely attending to.

The stop may be open, partial, abrupt, or grammatically ordinary. It does not need to announce that the reader still has choice.

## Acoustic cadence

Do not prescribe sentence lengths.

After a complete natural movement exists, inspect the acoustic trace only retrospectively:

- did several sentences receive suspiciously equal rhetorical weight?
- did short/long alternation appear engineered?
- did each sentence become a separate instruction or verdict?
- did periods repeatedly coincide with conceptual job completion?

If so, repair the governing movement or sentence relations. Do not impose a replacement meter.
