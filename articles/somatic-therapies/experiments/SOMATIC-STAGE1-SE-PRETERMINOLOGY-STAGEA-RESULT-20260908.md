## Stage A long probe — exact model-generated bytes

Going farther is not automatically better here. You can decide that you want to work on something difficult and then find that the reaction itself sets a different limit. At some point you're no longer choosing how much of it to stay with; you're just in it. That's where backing away becomes useful.

The work doesn't have to keep advancing simply because it has begun. You can move away from the difficult material for a while and later come back to it. Sometimes the return is easy enough that you can stay a little longer. Other times you come back and quickly realize that nothing has really changed, so you leave it alone again.

That back-and-forth changes the question from “Can I make myself face this?” to “How much of this can I actually stay with?” Willingness matters, but it doesn't answer that second question for you. The only way the work stays voluntary is if moving away remains a real option.

This may make a session look less dramatic. You might spend very little time with the difficult material itself. But if the alternative is getting pulled so far into it that you no longer have much say in what happens next, more intensity isn't necessarily more work. You can come back another time.
