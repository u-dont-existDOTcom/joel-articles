## 1. `top-left = remembered / top-right = constructed/thinking`

**Conclusion: do not state this fixed directional map as established fact.**

That directional code is the familiar NLP `eye accessing cues` claim. Controlled work has failed to support the NLP mapping. Most directly:

- Wiseman R, Watt C, ten Brinke L, Porter S, Couper S-L, Rankin C. *The Eyes Don't Have It: Lie Detection and Neuro-Linguistic Programming.* PLoS One. 2012;7(7):e40259. PMID 22808128. The paper also reviews earlier work on the proposed eye-movement/thought-modality mapping and reports that the claimed patterns were not supported.
- Sharpley CF. *Predicate Matching in NLP: A Review of Research on the Preferred Representational System.* J Couns Psychol. 1984;31(2):238–248. Review of 15 studies; little supportive evidence and considerable contrary evidence.

**But the broader eye-position/memory connection is real enough to mention cautiously.** Experimental memory research shows that gaze position and eye movements can interact with retrieval:

- Johansson R, Johansson M. *Look here, eye movements play a functional role in memory retrieval.* Psychol Sci. 2014;25(1):236–242. PMID 24166856. Manipulating gaze during retrieval affected spatial-memory performance, supporting a functional role for gaze position.
- Scholz A et al. *Gaze direction affects visuo-spatial short-term memory.* Exp Brain Res. 2014. PMID 24998909. Leftward gaze enhanced some visual-spatial short-term memory tasks; the result is task-specific, not an NLP lookup table.
- Martarelli CS et al. *Time in the eye of the beholder...* Mem Cognit. 2017. PMID 27443321. Spontaneous gaze position covaried with past/future representations and retrieval.
- 2024 work (PMID 38658592) found gaze biases toward locations associated with retrieved memories and evidence that naturalistic gaze patterns track retrieval/selection.

**Article-safe realization:** Joel can say that eye position and eye movements really do interact with memory/processing, while explicitly discarding the simplistic NLP `look this direction = this exact mental operation` map.

## 2. `edging` around the uncomfortable spot

**Best established terms: `titration` and `pendulation`, especially in Somatic Experiencing.**

- Titration = approaching traumatic activation in small, manageable increments rather than flooding the system.
- Pendulation = moving attention between more activated/dysregulated sensations and more regulated/resourceful sensations.

The Somatic Experiencing RCT by Brom et al. (*Somatic Experiencing for Posttraumatic Stress Disorder: A Randomized Controlled Outcome Study*, 2017) explicitly includes titration and pendulation in its protocol.

A 2026 study of graduated vs standard imaginal exposure in PTSD (PMID 42177712) found lower peak subjective distress when participants progressed through less-distressing traumatic events before the most distressing event. That supports the narrow statement that graduated exposure can reduce peak distress. It does **not** establish that titration/pendulation or gradual exposure is universally more effective than more intensive exposure; other PTSD trials show intensive/immediate trauma-focused approaches can also work very well.

**Article-safe realization:** Joel's `feather touch the boundary` description maps naturally to titration/pendulation. Keep his felt preference; do not convert it into a broad superiority claim.

## 3. `peripheral vision activates the PNS`

**Conclusion: the direct broad claim is stronger than the evidence located.**

There is extensive evidence that visual attention, gaze, visual flow, pupil behavior, and autonomic arousal interact, and some popular neuroscience teaching describes panoramic vision as parasympathetic/relaxing. However, I did not find strong primary evidence establishing a simple general rule that deliberately entering peripheral/panoramic vision directly `activates the parasympathetic nervous system` in the way the sentence implies.

Relevant evidence is more qualified/mixed:

- Bradley et al. and related psychophysiology work show pupil changes during emotional viewing primarily track sympathetic arousal; visual state and autonomic state are coupled, but not via a simple peripheral=PNS law.
- Farmer et al. 2014, PMID 24792503, meta-analysis of visually induced motion sickness, found visual stimulation can alter parasympathetic tone; again the direction depends on the visual condition.
- A 2026 Frontiers case report by Schiffer reported rapid differences in anxiety and pulse during left-vs-right lateral visual-field viewing, plus uncontrolled clinical observations across patients. This supports the plausibility that visual-field manipulation can change state/autonomic arousal, but is not proof of a general PNS mechanism.
- Wide-field moving visual stimulation can instead increase sympathetic/autonomic activation or inhibit vagal activity in some experimental settings, showing that `wider/peripheral visual input` by itself is not synonymous with parasympathetic activation.

**Article-safe realization:** preserve Joel's direct experience: moving gaze toward the periphery produced `piti`, a tingling/blissful shift, while holding the upsetting event in mind. If a mechanism is mentioned, write it as a plausible interpretation (`this felt like moving between a more settled and a more activated state`) rather than `we already know peripheral vision activates the PNS`.
