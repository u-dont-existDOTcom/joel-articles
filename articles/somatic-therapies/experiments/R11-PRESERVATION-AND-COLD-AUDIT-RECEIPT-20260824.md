## Eye-position evidence used for the bounded correction

- Wiseman et al. (2012), PLOS ONE, summarizes the classic NLP visual-accessing claim and reports that controlled testing did not support the upper-left/upper-right pattern as a general lie/truth rule; its introduction also notes that 1980s studies testing eye direction against visual/auditory recall consistently failed to support NLP's modality mapping.
- Contemporary autobiographical-memory research supports a broader relationship between memory retrieval and gaze/eye movements: free gaze can support richer autobiographical retrieval, retrieval changes fixation/saccade behavior, and recent reviews conclude that eye movements can reflect or support reconstruction of remembered visual content.

Therefore the article distinguishes an **idiosyncratic owner pattern that may be real for Joel** from an **unsupported universal decoder claim**.
