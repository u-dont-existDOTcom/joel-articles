## Accepted trauma-memory / somatic section

`The Dominant Trauma-Memory Therapies Are Missing Something` remains accepted owner prose. Preserve its thought movement and cadence; do not reopen specialist protocol distinctions unless Joel asks or materially new contradictory evidence changes the reader-relevant argument.

The accepted argument remains:

- mainstream trauma-memory therapies can reduce PTSD symptoms and deserve genuine credit;
- they focus treatment on reactivating traumatic material and changing its meaning, processing, and/or conditioned response;
- Levine/SE critiques this as incomplete because current autonomic/sensorimotor organization and incomplete defensive responses may not be exhausted by changing memory, meaning, or conditioned response;
- Joel intentionally uses `suppress` as an argumentative/theoretical characterization of new learning inhibiting old conditioned fear responses;
- EMDR includes bodily processing but remains principally organized around distress encoded in trauma-memory networks, whereas SE gives greater therapeutic primacy to current sensorimotor/autonomic defensive organization and completion of blocked action;
- cognitive/narrative re-evaluation can still be useful.

SIBAM formal naming still needs a direct Levine/*In an Unspoken Voice* or other suitable citation during citation cleanup.
