## Accepted trauma-memory / somatic argument

The accepted section makes the following owner argument:

- mainstream trauma-memory therapies can reduce PTSD symptoms and deserve genuine credit;
- they focus treatment on reactivating traumatic material and changing its meaning, processing, and/or conditioned response;
- Levine/SE critiques this as incomplete because the body may contain current autonomic/sensorimotor organization and incomplete defensive responses that are not exhausted by changing memory, meaning, or conditioned response;
- exposure/desensitization can, in Levine's critique, risk re-traumatization;
- Joel intentionally characterizes new cognitive-behavioral learning as suppressing old conditioned fear responses; do not reopen `suppress` merely because specialists would distinguish mechanisms unless the sentence becomes materially false in context;
- SIBAM is used to explain undercoupling/dissociation and overcoupling/hyperarousal across Sensation, Image, Behavior, Affect, and Meaning;
- Joel contrasts EMDR's body involvement inside trauma-memory reprocessing with SE's greater therapeutic primacy on bodily completion of blocked defensive action;
- cognitive/narrative re-evaluation remains useful and can be used concurrently or after bottom-up change.
