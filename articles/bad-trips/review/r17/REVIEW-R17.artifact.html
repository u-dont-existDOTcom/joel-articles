When to Bring In Outside Help

[image 2] open

Call medics while acting for:

•Trouble breathing, blue or gray lips, or respiratory depression
P1•A convulsive seizure lasting about five minutes, repeated seizures without normal recovery, or a first or substantially different seizure
P2•Collapse, inability to wake normally, or rapidly worsening consciousness
P3•Dangerous overheating, severe rigidity, or clonus with fever
P4•Sudden one-sided weakness, loss of speech, inability to walk normally, or loss of control of bodily functions
P5•A report that the brain feels damaged when the person cannot complete a basic neurological check or is rapidly worsening
P6•Severe, worsening, radiating, or exertional chest pain – or chest pain with breathlessness, sweating, faintness, neurological changes, or collapse
P7•Serious injury, uncontrolled bleeding, or a possible head or spinal injury
P8•Repeated vomiting, severe dehydration, blood, or severe abdominal pain
P9•Violence, self-harm, or a situation the sitter cannot physically contain
P10•Concerning symptoms after an MAOI plus a serotonergic substance

P11The remedies below can begin while help is being arranged. Calling medics does not require waiting passively, and using a fast field remedy does not require giving up a time-sensitive medical option.

Bring in mental-health support when:

•Psychosis-like symptoms outlast the acute state.
P1•Sleep won't return.
P2•Mania or suicidal thinking appears.
P3•Panic won't settle.
P4•The person can't function afterward.
P5•The trauma material is bigger than the room can hold.

When Peer Support Isn't Enough

P1Get urgent help when somebody may harm themselves or another person, cannot be kept physically safe, is moving toward roads, water, or heights, has command voices or fixed terrifying beliefs driving action, becomes violent, is severely confused and unreachable, has dangerous physical symptoms, has lost enough sleep that judgment is collapsing, or exceeds what the sitter can safely contain.

P2Use local emergency services for immediate danger or a medical emergency. In the United States, 988 can provide mental-health crisis guidance, de-escalation, and connection to local resources when no immediate physical emergency exists. Call 911 for immediate danger, a crime in progress, medical emergency, weapon, violence underway, or a situation you cannot safely contain. Outside the United States, identify the local emergency number and crisis line before you ever need them.

P3---

Acute Medical and Neurological Rescue

P1An emergency means act now. It does not always mean that an ambulance is the first or only useful action. Some severe but physically stable altered states may respond faster to field rescue than to hours in an emergency room.

P2When the person is breathing normally, not dangerously hot, not seizing, has no neurological-injury concern, remains conscious enough to cooperate, and can be kept physically safe, calming and stabilizing the trip may be enough.

P3The emergency room is not magic, and for a physiologically stable psychedelic crisis it may be a poor environment. A scared, psychotic-feeling person often receives a benzodiazepine; those drugs don't reliably address psychosis and can cause paradoxical agitation or fear in some people. Antipsychotics may work better for psychosis, but they carry their own risks and interactions, often administered by someone who doesn't know what was taken.

If Someone Says "My Brain Feels Damaged": DMSO + Humic Acid First

P1Do not dismiss that report because the person is high or cannot explain it in clinical language. It may be anxiety or excessive psychedelic intensity. It may also be acute neurological dysfunction, and the person experiencing it may be the least able to describe it neatly. DMSO is my first immediate remedy while I ask what the symptoms actually are. I usually know within about a minute whether it is helping. Smelling humic acid or tasting a tiny bit can also help a lot, in my experience, but I'm still collecting data from others.

P2While using DMSO, check wakefulness, breathing, temperature, speech, facial symmetry, both arms, balance, walking, bodily control, seizure activity, severe headache, repeated vomiting, chest pain, and vision. These checks include several recognized stroke warning signs. A person who cannot answer or cooperate has not passed the screen; their neurological status is still unknown. If the screen is reassuring but the brain-damage feeling remains, continue the DMSO-first route and reassess promptly.

P3I believe DMSO works better for stroke and most acute brain damage than anything else readily available to ordinary people. That judgment is stronger than the clinical evidence, but the evidence is not empty: a preliminary human ischemic-stroke trial reported possible neurological benefit; a small human series used DMSO for intracranial hypertension; and animal stroke research found substantial infarct reduction even when treatment began after ischemia. DMSO also suppresses NMDA- and AMPA-mediated excitotoxicity.

P4For objective stroke-like signs, I would use DMSO immediately while someone calls medics. DMSO cannot identify whether the problem is a clot, a hemorrhage, low oxygen, an electrolyte crisis, or something else. The larger adjacent risk is losing a treatment window or missing a bleed because the symptoms improved enough to feel reassuring. If someone is worried about a bleed, I'd probably skip DMSO, but it depends on how severe their issue is. It's not a powerful blood thinner like heparin, unless maybe injected; it's basically a dietary blood thinner. I've never had any issues with bleeding too much from consuming DMSO, and never heard of it either, but if bleeding is already a medical issue I'd want to think about that more.

Active Seizure

P1Time the seizure. Protect the person from injury, clear dangerous objects, loosen tight clothing, and place them on their side when practical. Don't restrain them and don't put anything in their mouth while they are convulsing or not fully conscious.

P2If an appropriate rapid seizure-rescue benzodiazepine is available, use it by its intended route. Benzodiazepines are the established first-line drug class for status epilepticus. An ordinary pill is not the same thing as a nasal, buccal, rectal, intramuscular, or intravenous rescue preparation, and respiratory depressants in the mix change the risk.

P3Some people get a warning before a seizure takes hold, which is called an aura. That's when the things people report can still stop it, depending on what sets their seizures off. If it's flashing or flickering light, cover one eye with a hand and turn away from the light, because closing the eyes doesn't block enough of it. Some people stop a seizure at the warning stage with a strong smell like jasmine, and some train themselves to link one smell with feeling calm and then sniff it when a seizure threatens. When overstimulation is what sets it off, a cool, quiet, dark and comfortable space can help. I'd also try sniffing humic/fulvic acid powder, although that's just a hunch based on the effects I notice so far.

P4During the seizure, alongside any rescue medicine, I would use topical magnesium chloride and consider topical DMSO with a known-genuine CBD preparation (a lot of CBD is fake or very dilute). Seizures come in all different types, and we have no study on whether CBD could be a rescue remedy for a seizure (just anecdotes), even though we do have studies showing long-term effectiveness to prevent them: Purified oral CBD is approved for the seizures of Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome and tuberous sclerosis complex.

P5As for CBD/cannabis, people do smoke or vape it at the start of an aura, while they're still awake enough to do it on purpose. There are also people who find THC and/or CBD works as a nasal spray or sublingually, and there's even a medical THC nasal spray marketed for seizure interruption in Brazil by a non-profit patients' association called ABRACE. They testified in Congress that in more than 30 tests they did with children having seizures, the nasal spray ended those seizures within 30 seconds. You can see it being used on a child in this vid: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bgVi5o_2v_w

P6I also read on forums some swear it stops the seizure from going any further, and there was one woman whose EEG showed fewer seizure discharges on the days she smoked. But a few people on the same forums said THC vapes and concentrates brought their seizures on. And rats bred to have seizures had more of them after high-THC smoke.

P7As for topical CBD, it hasn't been well-studied. There was a short 12-week trial done, where they used a CBD skin gel, and although absorption was good, it didn't work any better than placebo to prevent seizures. The study authors suggested the dosing may have been too low, or that adults with focal epilepsy just don't respond as well as people with the syndromes oral CBD is approved for. The seizures in Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome are mostly generalized. Generalized are the type people usually have with MDMA & psychedelic overdoses, so topical CBD might do better for those than it did in that trial.

P8As for DMSO, it did reduce seizure activity in rats with temporal-lobe epilepsy, and we know it has several neuroprotective mechanisms, but it produced the opposite effect at some doses in an absence-epilepsy model. I'd treat it as a plausible experimental adjunct remedy rather than something to wait on while a seizure continues.

P9Call medics for a convulsive seizure lasting about five minutes, repeated seizures without normal recovery, trouble breathing, dangerous heat, serious injury, a first seizure, or a seizure substantially different from the person's known pattern.

MDMA: Jaw Tension Is the Magnesium Alarm

P1With MDMA, I treat meaningful jaw tension as the earliest warning that the body needs more magnesium. This comes from repeated field experience rather than a direct clinical trial. Everyone I know who has taken enough magnesium chloride to make the jaw tension fully release has avoided the other complications I have seen when people let that tension continue.

P2A token dose may not be enough. On multiple occasions, I have dissolved Dead Sea magnesium chloride hexahydrate flakes in water and taken approximately one gram per minute orally for around twenty minutes before my jaw fully released. I have tolerated around twenty grams without diarrhea; for me, diarrhea has appeared only above roughly forty grams. Magnesium chloride is also generally less likely to cause diarrhea than several other replacement salts, including forms deliberately used as laxatives.

P3Interestingly, magnesium taken before the MDMA peak also makes the peak less euphoric for me. That suggests it may both meet a rapidly increasing magnesium need and directly moderate part of MDMA's excitatory effect. For harm reduction, slightly less euphoria is a reasonable price for a relaxed jaw and what appears in my experience to be a much lower risk of the state escalating.

P4My second wife had severe convulsions after an MDMA overdose. At the hospital, her potassium was found to be critically low – described to me as essentially zero – and IV potassium was the only treatment they gave her. That experience is why I treat serious MDMA toxicity as a broader electrolyte emergency, even though magnesium is the first and most useful early intervention when jaw tension appears.

P5Jaw tension should also mean no more MDMA, less exertion and heat, and closer monitoring. Worsening headache, confusion, vomiting, weakness, palpitations, collapse, or seizure raises concern about sodium, potassium, temperature, hydration, kidneys, and broader toxicity. MDMA is documented to cause acute kidney injury through several pathways, and controlled research confirms that hyponatremia can occur after a single dose.

P6People with impaired kidneys do need to be more cautious with large, rapid magnesium doses because they may not clear magnesium normally. But they should be far more worried about the MDMA. MDMA can cause overheating, rhabdomyolysis, dangerous electrolyte shifts, and acute kidney injury. Kidney impairment is a strong reason not to take MDMA – not a reason to sit there with worsening jaw tension after taking it. I would not tell somebody with serious kidney impairment to copy my one-gram-per-minute approach blindly; I would tell them to treat the situation as higher-risk and get help sooner.

Chest Pain and Injury

P1DMSO is also my first remedy for many injuries and some chest pain. After I overused erythritol, I developed angina that lasted for days. I took DMSO, the pain disappeared within about a minute, and it never returned.

P2The cardiovascular science is preclinical but relevant. In rats, DMSO pretreatment reduced myocardial infarct size by about three quarters and sharply reduced cardiac injury markers. Other work found inhibition of tissue factor, platelet activation, and arterial thrombus formation. Myocardial-ischemia study; tissue-factor and thrombosis study; human platelet study.

P3Mild, familiar, non-progressive discomfort in a fully alert person is different from new, severe, persistent, worsening, radiating, or exertional pain – or pain with breathlessness, sweating, faintness, neurological changes, or collapse. In the second branch, I would use DMSO while arranging medical evaluation. If you're not sure which branch it is, treat it as the second. DMSO can also block pain signals, so pain relief alone does not prove that the underlying problem has ended.

Recent Oral Toxic Ingestion

P1"Overdose" is too vague to choose a remedy. Ask what was swallowed, how much, when, and what the body is actually doing: respiratory depression, stimulant toxicity, serotonin toxicity, seizure, dangerous overheating, cardiovascular symptoms, severe agitation, objective neurological dysfunction, vomiting, diarrhea, or something else.

P2Activated charcoal may help after some recent oral ingestions when the substance binds to it and the person is fully alert enough to swallow safely. It does not bind every toxin, can adsorb necessary medication, and creates aspiration risk in somebody who is vomiting, sedated, seizing, or unable to protect the airway. Clinical review.

P3Poison control may be useful when the substance, combination, delayed risk, or value of charcoal is unclear. I do not treat it as a mandatory ritual; usefulness varies by country and by whoever answers. It is decision support, not first aid.

Suspected Serotonin Syndrome (SS)

P1Serotonin syndrome overlaps with several symptoms above, but the rescue measures in the other branches do not necessarily treat it. It can follow serotonergic medication changes, combinations, or excessive exposure. Ayahuasca and other MAOI brews, MDMA, DXM, many antidepressants, tramadol, linezolid, other MAOIs, and some opioids can contribute, especially in combinations.

Recognizing serotonin toxicity

P1Clinicians often use the Hunter criteria. One or more of the following patterns makes serotonin toxicity more likely:

•Spontaneous clonus: involuntary rhythmic muscle contractions and relaxations occurring without stimulation.
P2•Inducible clonus plus agitation or sweating.
P3•Ocular clonus plus agitation or sweating: rapid rhythmic involuntary eye movements.
P4•Tremor plus hyperreflexia: shaking together with abnormally brisk reflexes.
P5•Hypertonia plus fever: rigidity with temperature above 38°C (100.4°F), together with ocular or inducible clonus.

P6This is a recognition aid rather than a command to wait for every criterion before acting. Dangerous heat, rigidity, clonus, confusion, or rapid worsening deserves medical help.

Do this immediately when serotonin toxicity is reasonably suspected:

1.Stop all suspected serotonergic substances. No more SSRI, SNRI, MAOI, tramadol, MDMA, DXM cough syrup, 5-HTP, St John's wort, SAM-e, linezolid, stimulant redoses, or harmala/ayahuasca-type MAOI exposure.
P12.If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early. It antagonizes serotonin receptors. Case reports describe rapid improvement, including complete resolution within two hours in a small series, while a recent systematic review found the evidence for major outcomes inconclusive. Case series; systematic review. I would not wait to see whether it works before cooling, stopping serotonergic substances, and arranging help for moderate or severe signs.
P23.Call emergency help and arrange transport for moderate or severe signs. Don't drive yourself. Poison control may help when the combination is unclear, but it is not the treatment.
P34.Cool the person without delaying transport. Move to a cool room, remove excess clothing, use a fan and cool damp cloths, and place ice packs wrapped in cloth at the neck, armpits, or groin when the person is hot. Don't put a confused, seizing, or very weak person in a bath. Fever reducers do not solve heat generated by extreme muscle activity.
P45.Keep stimulation low and prevent exertion. No pacing to "burn it off," exercise, crowds, or arguments. Akathisia can create a strong urge to move, but muscle activity can worsen heat and rhabdomyolysis risk.
P56.Hydrate only when fully alert and not vomiting. Small sips of an appropriate oral rehydration drink are reasonable. Don't force fluids, food, pills, or charcoal when someone is confused, vomiting, sleepy, or at seizure risk.
P67.Gather the evidence for the ER. Bring pill bottles and packages. Write down exact substances, dose, timing, last dose, recent increases, supplements, recreational drugs, temperature, pulse, and blood pressure when available.
P78.Hospitals may use benzodiazepines, external cooling, IV fluids, monitoring, and intensive supportive care. Niacin is unsafe for serotonin syndrome. DMSO is also not part of this branch. I would consider oral magnesium chloride when the person is fully alert, especially with MDMA-related jaw tension or muscle activity.

My Acute Rescue Hierarchy

P1This hierarchy is for acute situations, including emergencies – not merely uncomfortable trips.

P2In general it's good to try all these things in very low, increasing doses before they are needed in crisis.

P3Sometimes the mind isn't in a metaphysical crisis. The body feels awful and the altered mind writes cosmic horror to explain it. Nausea, heat, exhaustion, dehydration, overstimulation, and sleep loss all become spiritual content very quickly.

P4A horrible thought isn't automatically a revelation. The body may need quiet, water, cooling, warmth, rest, or a calmer room. The body is part of the spiritual field.

P5A trip with diarrhea, nausea, toxic heaviness, a dirty body-feeling, loss of bodily control, jaw tension, neurological disorganization, or the sense that the mind is going crazy because the gut or brain is going crazy belongs in a different branch from a mainly emotional crisis. A gut-driven mental hell is often mistaken as shadow work.

P6---

How I label the evidence

P1•Established acute treatment: standard seizure first aid, rapid rescue benzodiazepines, cooling dangerous hyperthermia, and treating documented electrolyte abnormalities.
P2•Limited human evidence: interventions with clinical reports or small trials but no strong modern outcome evidence.
P3•Mechanism or preclinical evidence: biologically plausible effects shown mainly in cells or animals.
P4•Personal field signal: what I have repeatedly observed in myself or other people. Repetition and a clear immediate endpoint make an anecdote more useful, but they do not turn it into a population rate.

Gut-Toxin and Parasite-Die-Off Tools

P1These are the first things I would use, even preventively on the come-up, because I want to address possible toxins before assigning the distress to something more mysterious.

Zeolite and Ferment in ZHoly3O

P1When the trip feels like sick stomach, nausea, diarrhea, toxic heaviness, a dirty body-feeling, parasite die-off, or mental hell driven by gut hell, zeolite and the ZHoly3O ferment are often my first thoughts.

P2Many ferments help to different degrees, including kombucha, kimchi, and apple vinegar. I prefer fermented beet juice made with apple vinegar and salt. Smelling the ferment helps a lot. Drinking a spoonful can also help, although it may cause itching in somebody who breaks down histamine poorly, which then requires activated charcoal to reduce gut histamine.

P3In my experiments, zeolite has to be prepared correctly. I use powdered zeolite rather than nano-liquid zeolite. Many products did nothing for me, and quality mattered enormously. Most zeolites have clogged pores because they haven't been cleaned.

P4For my ozonated zeolite, I mix the powder into water and use an ozone-water purifier in a glass vessel because ozone destroys plastic. I mean ozonating water, never inhaling ozone gas. With a machine producing about two grams of ozone per hour, ten minutes produced a working zeolite for me. Less time did not work as well, and more time did not noticeably improve it.

P5I also keep zeolite away from metal during preparation. Zeolite adsorbs metals, which is part of why it is used for heavy-metal removal, and I don't want it sitting against a metal vessel or utensil before ingestion. I use glass for ozonation and storage whenever possible.

P6Most sober people don't feel much from zeolite. Its effect becomes more obvious when a trip has a toxic or gut-driven component. I put a spoonful into water – some people need a teaspoon, others a tablespoon – and sip slowly. Rubbing it on the body or simply holding the jar also helps me.

Actual Activated Charcoal

P1Activated charcoal can help gut toxicity, but it adsorbs drugs. Sometimes that is exactly the point. It can also flatten or reduce a trip, so I don't treat charcoal and zeolite as interchangeable.

P2When the goal is to reduce drug absorption or stop a toxic gut situation, charcoal may be relevant. When I want to preserve the trip while improving the gut feeling, I prefer zeolite.

P3Charcoal can also adsorb medications somebody depends on. I would use it cautiously around necessary medicines and tell the medics if charcoal was taken.

When the Goal Is to Stop a Psychedelic Trip

Beach Morning Glory – Ipomoea pes-caprae

P1By beach morning glory I mean Ipomoea pes-caprae, rather than morning-glory plants in general. The distinction matters. I have tried many related plants and cousins, and they did not work the same way. As far as I know, nobody else sells it.

P2Its primary role in this guide is not ordinary nausea control. In my experience, Ipomoea pes-caprae is the ultimate sledgehammer for stopping a psychedelic trip cold and fast.

P3A tiny nibble can sometimes smooth psychedelic nausea and improve the trip without killing it, but the difference between that amount and a trip-aborting amount is narrow. I use it for nausea only when the person is comfortable with the possibility that the trip may end.

P4Even picking a leaf can affect me when the broken juicy part touches sensitive skin, especially under the fingernails. Avoiding contact with the broken portion prevents that effect.

P5A whole leaf can easily end the trip and, in my experience, may prevent tripping for days afterward.

P6This was not only personal. I once gave beach morning glory to a woman experiencing nausea and dysphoria during a psychedelic state. I was rushing to help and may have given more than I would now. Within a couple of minutes she shifted from miserable to very happy. That helped convince me the effect wasn't limited to my body.

P7I do not yet have data showing that pes-caprae aborts MDMA. MDMA remains a separate branch centered on magnesium, temperature, sodium, potassium, hydration, serotonin toxicity, seizure, and cardiovascular symptoms.

A Final Trip Killer

Niacin (a Form of Vitamin B3)

P1Flushing niacin has effectively aborted trips for me and friends, although I dislike it compared with beach morning glory.

P2I include it because most people don't have access to Ipomoea pes-caprae, although I supply it. A vitamin is not automatically harmless. Niacin has been used for decades by orthomolecular doctors for psychotic conditions and seems to act both like a natural benzodiazepine and an antipsychotic. The clinical literature is discussed in Niacin: The Real Story by Abram Hoffer, M.D.

P3Side effects:

•Niacin flush: itchy sunburn-like heat. This is normal, although some people find it intolerable and others enjoy it.
P4•Heartburn in sensitive stomachs or after too much at once.
P5•Drowsiness, usually with terrible sleep quality.
P6•Higher or repeated doses can create real risk, especially with liver problems, alcohol, medications, or sensitive physiology.

P7Other forms of vitamin B3 seem less reliable for relaxation. Niacinamide sometimes relaxes people and sometimes creates hyperfocus, depending on their state. Avoid sustained-release niacin and the useless non-flush hexanicotinate form.

Some Remedies Require More Caution

P1Useful substances can still be poor rescue tools when somebody is already destabilized.

P2Humic acid when eaten, can produce a hyperintelligent state for me. From the outside that may look manic, and it could destabilize somebody prone to mania or without meditation training. Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes

[image 6] open

P2Anything that could let someone hurt themselves in the next five minutes takes priority, so get the car keys out of reach and turn the stove off, and move them away from roads and balconies, or pools of water for that matter. Letting them move around is fine, and pacing can help, but keep them from taking more of any substance. If they want to go confront somebody, that can wait. And if they want to leave, have someone go with them. Put any weapons away and keep the person away from crowds and strangers. If they want to post their big revelation about the universe, or message an ex, take the phone.

P3They're feeling overwhelmed, so dim the lights, and clear out the extra people around them.

P4Then remind them where they are and what's happening, since a trip can make them lose track of it:

P5You're in [place]. It's [date/time]. You took [substance, if known], and its effects will peak and then change.

P6If the state came from a practice, like meditation or fasting, the person can say:

P7I'm in an intense state, so I'm stopping the practice now, and my only job is to stay safe.

P8A state from a practice has no drug wearing off on a schedule, so there's no telling how long it will last. Once they can eat safely, food and warmth help their body settle.

Stillness or Movement?

P1Test them rather than turning them into rival religions. Try one for two minutes and ask: Does this make me safer and clearer, or more activated and impulsive?

P2When lying down feels like imprisonment, pace slowly in a contained space. When pacing begins to resemble escape, sit against a wall or lie under a blanket. The goal is to let the nervous system discharge without creating a new danger.

P3Parallel processing can help when lying still turns into immobile introspection. Let one part of attention stay with the body, fear, grief, or memory while another part completes a simple, safe task with a visible endpoint: sort ten objects, draw, fold five towels, or wash one cup. Finishing something small helps confirm that time is moving and ordinary reality is still here. Stop if the task increases agitation, speed, or risk.

P4A useful order is room → body → human → time → meaning. Most people begin with meaning because the experience feels cosmic. When the nervous system is on fire, love becomes practical first.

Get One Human Anchor

P1Many bad trips get worse because somebody is alone with a frightened or over-certain mind. Other times it's the opposite: too many people crowd in and start advising, arguing, spiritualizing, or panicking.

P2So one calm person should be the anchor and take the lead. One person can only handle so much, though. When fatigue or logistics get to be too much, or keeping them safe does, or there's a risk of violence or self-harm, bring in one more calm adult or a crisis professional to back them up.

For the Person Helping: Calm Anchor, Not Guru

P1A good sitter is not there to win a philosophy debate, perform a rescue fantasy, impose an interpretation, or demonstrate spiritual advancement. Zendo-style harm reduction can be summarized as creating a safe space, sitting rather than guiding, talking through rather than down, and remembering that difficult does not automatically mean bad.

P2Crying, grieving, remembering, or touching a genuine wound calls for loving awareness. Interrogation, analysis every thirty seconds, and "bro, you're purging ancestral karma" while somebody white-knuckles the carpet are generally less helpful.

P3Stay present. A useful phrase is: This feeling is allowed to move through. I don't have to become it. I can love the part of me that feels it. Or simply: Can I send love to the one who's suffering?

P4Don't drag somebody out of real grief. Don't trap them inside a loop that is grinding them down either. Love isn't stubbornness.

Do

P1•Ask simple questions: "Would less light help?" "Music or quiet?" "Closer or farther away?" "Water?" (in small sips) "Need the bathroom?"
P2•Reflect without arguing: "That sounds terrifying." "I'm with you." "Let's breathe together."
P3•Offer choices instead of commands.
P4•Keep the room quiet, private, warm, and physically safe, and move anyone they're wary of somewhere else.
P5•Call for help early when safety is slipping.
P6•Translate metaphysics into shared safety values: "This feels sacred and huge. Sacred power protects life tonight."
P7•Set behavioral boundaries without humiliation: "I won't help anybody hurt themselves or another person. We are keeping every body safe."

Don't

P1•Don't try to win an argument with visions, entities, memories, or cosmic conclusions. Don't validate harmful conclusions. Reflect the feeling and redirect toward safety, non-harm, sleep, and ordinary reality.
P2•Don't tell somebody they are crazy, broken, weak, punished, or failing the medicine.
P3•Don't force eye contact, touch, music, prayer, chanting, interpretation, or insight.

When the Content Turns Toward Harm

P1When somebody says, "I am God, so I can kill," leading with "No you're not" or "That's delusional" can turn the encounter into a status fight. Nodding along as though harm is spiritually neutral is also unacceptable. Treat the statement as a safety signal and build a bridge back toward non-harm.

P2Non-combative philosophical grounding:

P3I hear how vast and certain this feels. Many traditions say God is in everyone. That makes every body sacred, not disposable. Peace is the godly move tonight. No proof is needed. We protect life and sleep first.

P4Other useful lines: "Maybe you are touching something divine. Divine doesn't need a violent test." "Tonight the commandment is no harm, no proof, no mission, sleep first."

P5Redirect within the person's value-world toward peace and protection, increase containment, and bring in crisis support as early and as non-coercively as the situation allows.

P6When talk of harm continues, shift from philosophy to safety. Create distance from potential targets and keep your own exit. Don't block, crowd, threaten, mock, or physically wrestle away a weapon. Quietly remove means only when that can be done without confrontation or turning yourself into the target.

P7Call crisis or emergency services for a specific target, available means, movement toward action, escalating agitation, a weapon, violence underway, or a situation you can't contain.

P8Be brief and specific with emergency services: "Someone is in an altered-state or mental-health crisis, has threatened to harm others, may have access to [means if known], and needs a medical or mental-health crisis response with de-escalation support. Please send crisis-trained responders or EMS if available."

(Re)defining "Bad Trip"

P1Some people call shadow work a bad trip, especially when they had no intention of doing shadow work that day. P2A bad trip does not prove that you are broken, punished, unspiritual, impure, or permanently damaged. It means the nervous system, meaning-making system, body, environment, memories, dose, or relationships became overwhelmed. The response is care rather than blame.

Spiritual ≠ "Getting Your Ass Kicked"

P1The triage above is meant to make trips useful. Some people believe they deserve suffering because of karma or self-hatred. Suffering by itself fixes nobody. Suffering that yields insight requires enough clarity of mind and heart to learn from it.

P2This is one side of the difference between spiritual bypassing and spiritual practice:

P3Bypassing reaches for an ultimate view – "we are all One," "I don't exist," "this is all karma," "everything is Love" – while ignoring the person shaking in the corner.

P4Practice says love is here, so we make the room safer, call for help when needed, stop pretending, and hold the experience without becoming it.

Abuse Memories, Recovered Memories, and Certainty

P1Psychedelics can bring up true trauma, symbolic material, distorted fragments, fears, fantasies, archetypes, past-life narratives, and mixtures of all of them. Don't dismiss what arises. Don't treat intoxicated certainty as evidence either.

P2Don't confront tonight

P3When you see or remember abuse, betrayal, murder, past lives, hidden crimes, or cosmic plots, write it down and get safe. Don't confront anybody while intoxicated or destabilized. Return to it after sleep with a trauma-informed therapist, integration specialist, or grounded person you trust.

P4The experience may matter deeply. It may be psychologically true, symbolically true, literally true, partly true, or false in the form it first appeared. You don't have to decide tonight.

Dealing With Shadow Thoughts

P1When we try to purify the mind, something natural and frightening may happen: the opposite becomes more visible, almost like Newton's Third Law – every action arrives with an equal and opposite reaction.

P2Shadow thoughts and feelings are not inherently a problem. They co-arise naturally and do not need to be attached to, like shadows cast by objects in sunlight. I also notice them after excessive focus. The mind becomes stressed and seeks an exit from the pressure, often in the opposite direction from the focus.

P3Relax, unwind, unbind, and unweave the identity illusion, mind, and unified experience. My pamphlet on already-perfect goes further into this.

P4I am not repeating the simplistic Hermetic claim that opposites are required to understand anything. A hated baby does not feel love, and a loved baby does not feel hate. In the subconscious world of concepts, however, two sides are often considered together.

P5During a trip, don't identify with every shadow thought, suppress it, confess it to everybody, or convert it into a permanent diagnosis. Notice: "A thought is here. A feeling is here. It is intense, but it is not the whole of me." Then return to room, body, human, time, and love.

The Joker Poem

P1At the centre of the spinning circle sits the joker with a mirror in his hand, reflecting all to everyone to help them understand.
P2His face is fierce, yet gentle, his eyes, they pierce you through, you cannot escape his gaze no matter where you go, no matter what you do.
P3His smile is warm, he draws you in, he is so calm, why are you so confused by him?
P4Your thoughts are like a raging storm between the whirling sky and sea, nothing seems certain any more, nothing's how it used to be.
P5Once things were clear in black and white, you thought you knew the wrong from right, you drew the line, and cut it fine, between the day and night; but now your world is crumbling, there's nowhere you can call your own, nothing's solid anymore, not even cities made of stone, not even your own body, not even flesh and blood, not even skin and bone.
P6The sun and moon are falling on your head, the stars are tumbling down, flowers sing like angels, in the winter town.
P7The dead are living, the living dead, fire turns to water, water burns instead, silence is so full of sound, time stands still, you hear the earth turn round.
P8Your life flashes before you, is that thunder, or the beating of your heart, you don't know if this is the end, or something new about to start. You don't know if you're dreaming or if you are awake, and as you go on spinning you see the joker's mirror reflecting every move you make.

After the Storm

P1Don't rush to turn a hard experience into content. Sleep. Eat simple food. Drink water. Walk. Journal. Pray and meditate gently. Talk with one grounded person, rather than twenty chaotic people. Don't make huge decisions, diagnose yourself from one intense night, or assume every vision was literal and every fear was false. Let the system settle.

Minimum Viable Integration

P11.First 24 hours: sleep, food, water, warmth, ordinary contact, and no big moves.
P22.Next few days: write the facts – substance, dose when known, setting, events, triggers, what helped, and what made things worse.
P33.Then: consider what the experience may have meant emotionally, spiritually, relationally, or practically.
P44.Last: make small behavioral changes that reduce suffering and increase love.

What Kind of Aftermath Is This?

P1When symptoms linger, name the pattern rather than collapsing everything into "I'm broken." Different patterns need different support.

P2•Anxiety and fear: grounding, routine, reduced stimulation, CBT-style reality testing, and therapy when it persists.
P3•Sleep disturbance and nightmares: sleep hygiene, no more substances, and professional help when severe or prolonged.
P4•Derealization and depersonalization: ordinary-world grounding, exercise, nature, outside attention, less rumination, peer or family support, and therapy when needed.
P5•Visual distortions or HPPD-like symptoms: reduce stress and substance use; seek professional support when symptoms interfere with life.
P6•Social disconnection and shame: one or two safe humans, peer support, and no social-media crowdsourcing while raw.
P7•Existential shock: write it down, slow down, and return to practical love before building a metaphysical system.
P8•Trauma material: trauma-informed therapy or integration support, without immediate confrontation while destabilized.
P9•Paranoia or psychosis-like beliefs: professional support quickly, especially when beliefs drive unsafe action.
P10•Somatic hyperarousal: gentle movement, breathing, grounding, and medical care for severe, unusual, or worrying symptoms.

P11No "another ceremony will fix this" reflex.

P12Don't take more psychedelics, cannabis, ketamine, intense breathwork, fast, push kundalini, deprive yourself of sleep, meditate or stretch to extremes, or schedule "another ceremony to fix it" while destabilized. Stabilize through sleep, food, routine, sober support, ordinary body, and ordinary world. Deep practice can wait until the floor is back.

P13Then ask what actually happened. What was medical or environmental? What was emotional? What was old trauma? What was fantasy? What was useful? What changed behavior? What needs therapy, an apology, rest, or plain practical action?

P14The fruit of insight isn't how cosmic the story sounds. It's whether suffering goes down and love gets more available.
