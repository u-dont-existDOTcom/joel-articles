**Why:** Your questions: no more electrolyte drinks once those signs show, since they carry far less salt than blood and still thin it (the wilderness-medicine guidelines name water and sports drinks as the cause of low sodium from overdrinking, and restrict fluids once it's suspected); and yes to something hypertonic: the same guidelines treat mild low sodium with salt by mouth, a very strong broth or salty food, when the person is alert and can keep it down, and MDMA harm-reduction advice says the same (salty snacks, less water, get ready to get help). Hospitals use a concentrated salt drip. "Too late for an easy fix" read as "nothing can be done", and a fresh reader of my first wording couldn't tell whether to call or to feed them, so it now says "while you wait for medics". And the seizure line: low sodium and low magnesium both bring seizures, and low magnesium often brings low potassium with it and keeps potassium from coming back up, which fits your magnesium-first experience; very low potassium brings weakness and heart rhythm trouble.
