7. Is the trip merely too intense, with no brain-damage concern?

P1Use this calming branch only after the quick neurological screen is reassuring and the person wants to preserve the trip. If they say their brain feels damaged, return to the DMSO-first branch instead.

P2Try grounding before chemically or forcefully changing the state:

•One hand on heart and one on belly.
P3•Longer-exhale breathing.
P4•Gentle Buteyko-style reduced breathing: breathe softly and slowly through the nose, taking in slightly less air than you want until there is only faint air hunger. No strain, no long breath-holds while intoxicated, and stop if it spikes fear.
P5•Silence or one calm track on repeat.
P6•Low light, a blanket, and simple reassurance.
P7•Slow pacing or gentle movement when that reduces pressure.

P8Some people over-breathe during panic. The resulting low CO<sub>2</sub> can produce tingling, lightheadedness, and feelings of unreality that make the trip seem even more threatening. Gentle breathing reduction is meant to soften that loop, rather than become a new breathing project. Source: Johns Hopkins on hyperventilation.

Active Seizure

P1Time the seizure. Protect the person from injury, clear dangerous objects, loosen tight clothing, and place them on their side when practical. Don’t restrain them and don’t put anything in their mouth while they are convulsing or not fully conscious.

P2If an appropriate rapid seizure-rescue benzodiazepine is available, use it by its intended route. Benzodiazepines are the established first-line drug class for status epilepticus. P3I would also use topical magnesium chloride and consider topical DMSO with a known CBD preparation as adjuncts. CBD has established antiseizure activity in several epilepsy syndromes, and transdermal CBD reaches systemic circulation, although it has not been validated as rapid seizure rescue. MDMA: Jaw Tension Is the Magnesium Alarm

P1With MDMA, I treat meaningful jaw tension as the earliest warning that the body needs more magnesium. This comes from repeated field experience rather than a direct clinical trial. Everyone I know who has taken enough magnesium chloride to make the jaw tension fully release has avoided the other complications I have seen when people let that tension continue.

P2A token dose may not be enough. On multiple occasions, I have dissolved Dead Sea magnesium chloride hexahydrate flakes in water and taken approximately one gram per minute orally for around twenty minutes before my jaw fully released. I have tolerated around twenty grams without diarrhea; for me, diarrhea has appeared only above roughly forty grams. Magnesium chloride is also generally less likely to cause diarrhea than several other replacement salts, including forms deliberately used as laxatives.

P3Interestingly, magnesium taken before the MDMA peak also makes the peak less euphoric for me. That suggests it may both meet a rapidly increasing magnesium need and directly moderate part of MDMA’s excitatory effect. For harm reduction, slightly less euphoria is a reasonable price for a relaxed jaw and what appears in my experience to be a much lower risk of the state escalating.

P4My second wife had severe convulsions after an MDMA overdose. At the hospital, her potassium was found to be critically low—described to me as essentially zero—and IV potassium was the only treatment they gave her. That experience is why I treat serious MDMA toxicity as a broader electrolyte emergency, even though magnesium is the first and most useful early intervention when jaw tension appears.

P5Jaw tension should also mean no more MDMA, less exertion and heat, and closer monitoring. Worsening headache, confusion, vomiting, weakness, palpitations, collapse, or seizure raises concern about sodium, potassium, temperature, hydration, kidneys, and broader toxicity. MDMA is documented to cause acute kidney injury through several pathways, and controlled research confirms that hyponatremia can occur after a single dose.

P6People with impaired kidneys do need to be more cautious with large, rapid magnesium doses because they may not clear magnesium normally. But they should be far more worried about the MDMA. MDMA can cause overheating, rhabdomyolysis, dangerous electrolyte shifts, and acute kidney injury. Kidney impairment is a strong reason not to take MDMA—not a reason to sit there with worsening jaw tension after taking it. I would not tell somebody with serious kidney impairment to copy my one-gram-per-minute approach blindly; I would tell them to treat the situation as higher-risk and get help sooner.

Suspected Serotonin Syndrome (SS)

P1Serotonin syndrome overlaps with several symptoms above, but the rescue measures in the other branches do not necessarily treat it. Psychedelics, MDMA, DXM, many antidepressants, tramadol, linezolid, MAOIs, and some opioids can contribute, especially in combinations.

P1Clinicians often use the Hunter criteria. One or more of the following patterns makes serotonin toxicity more likely:

•Spontaneous clonus: involuntary rhythmic muscle contractions and relaxations occurring without stimulation.
P2•Inducible clonus plus agitation or sweating.
P3•Ocular clonus plus agitation or sweating: rapid rhythmic involuntary eye movements.
P4•Tremor plus hyperreflexia: shaking together with abnormally brisk reflexes.
P5•Hypertonia plus fever: rigidity with temperature above 38°C (100.4°F), together with ocular or inducible clonus.

How I label the evidence

P1•Established acute treatment: standard seizure first aid, rapid rescue benzodiazepines, cooling dangerous hyperthermia, and treating documented electrolyte abnormalities.

P1•Don’t try to win an argument with visions, entities, memories, or cosmic conclusions. Don’t validate harmful conclusions. Reflect the feeling and redirect toward safety, non-harm, sleep, and ordinary reality.
P2•Don’t tell somebody they are crazy, broken, weak, punished, or failing the medicine.
P3•Don’t force eye contact, touch, music, prayer, chanting, interpretation, or insight.

When the Content Turns Toward Harm

P1When somebody says, “I am God, so I can kill,” leading with “No you’re not” or “That’s delusional” can turn the encounter into a status fight. Nodding along as though harm is spiritually neutral is also unacceptable. Treat the statement as a safety signal and build a bridge back toward non-harm.

P2Non-combative philosophical grounding:

P3I hear how vast and certain this feels. Many traditions say God is in everyone. That makes every body sacred, not disposable. Peace is the godly move tonight. No proof is needed. We protect life and sleep first.

P4Other useful lines: “Maybe you are touching something divine. Divine doesn’t need a violent test.” “Tonight the commandment is no harm, no proof, no mission, sleep first.”

P5Redirect within the person’s value-world toward peace and protection, increase containment, and bring in crisis support as early and as non-coercively as the situation allows.

P6When talk of harm continues, shift from philosophy to safety. Create distance from potential targets and keep your own exit. Don’t block, crowd, threaten, mock, or physically wrestle away a weapon. Quietly remove means only when that can be done without confrontation or turning yourself into the target.

P7When you are the only helper and safety is slipping, recruit one additional calm adult or crisis professional. Call crisis or emergency services for a specific target, available means, movement toward action, escalating agitation, a weapon, violence underway, or a situation you can’t contain.

P8Be brief and specific with emergency services: “Someone is in an altered-state or mental-health crisis, has threatened to harm others, may have access to [means if known], and needs a medical or mental-health crisis response with de-escalation support. Please send crisis-trained responders or EMS if available.”

(Re)defining “Bad Trip”

P1Some people call shadow work a bad trip, especially when they had no intention of doing shadow work that day. It still makes sense to reduce present-moment trauma so we can address what was already there.

P2A bad trip does not prove that you are broken, punished, unspiritual, impure, or permanently damaged. It means the nervous system, meaning-making system, body, environment, memories, dose, or relationships became overwhelmed. The response is care rather than blame.

Spiritual ≠ “Getting Your Ass Kicked”

P1The triage above is meant to make trips useful. Some people believe they deserve suffering because of karma or self-hatred. Suffering by itself fixes nobody. Suffering that yields insight requires enough clarity of mind and heart to learn from it.

P2This is one side of the difference between spiritual bypassing and spiritual practice:

P3Bypassing reaches for an ultimate view—“we are all One,” “I don’t exist,” “this is all karma,” “everything is Love”—while ignoring the person shaking in the corner.

P4Practice says love is here, so we make the room safer, call for help when needed, stop pretending, and hold the experience without becoming it.

P5That is tripsitting, shadow work, and the path becoming practical.

The Other Bypass: Feeling Good Forever

P1A well-rounded trip should offer practical lessons and contact with suffering as well as freedom from suffering, lest we become “so spiritually high that we are no earthly good.” “Life is full of suffering” is Buddha’s First Noble Truth. P2Many people meditate or take psychedelics to feel good. I am definitely one of them, and Buddha also said meditation should generally feel good. My friend Eshwar, however, found that shadow work made up about 99% of his path to heart-chakra awakening.

P3Modern people carry so much trauma—and some people did even in Buddha’s time—that shadow work and inner-child reparenting may need to come first, even before authentic liberation from the identity illusion.

P4When shadow work is bypassed in meditation and therapy, it may appear in ordinary life without explaining itself. I encountered a lot of it in my first two marriages, to my bewilderment. Dealing With Sorrow: Missing Versus Sending Love

What Kind of Aftermath Is This?

P1When symptoms linger, name the pattern rather than collapsing everything into “I’m broken.” Different patterns need different support.

P2•Anxiety and fear: grounding, routine, reduced stimulation, CBT-style reality testing, and therapy when it persists.
P3•Sleep disturbance and nightmares: sleep hygiene, no more substances, and professional help when severe or prolonged.
P4•Derealization and depersonalization: ordinary-world grounding, exercise, nature, outside attention, less rumination, peer or family support, and therapy when needed.
P5•Visual distortions or HPPD-like symptoms: reduce stress and substance use; seek professional support when symptoms interfere with life.
P6•Social disconnection and shame: one or two safe humans, peer support, and no social-media crowdsourcing while raw.
P7•Existential shock: write it down, slow down, and return to practical love before building a metaphysical system.
P8•Trauma material: trauma-informed therapy or integration support, without immediate confrontation while destabilized.
P9•Paranoia or psychosis-like beliefs: professional support quickly, especially when beliefs drive unsafe action.
P10•Somatic hyperarousal: gentle movement, breathing, grounding, and medical care for severe, unusual, or worrying symptoms.

P11No “another ceremony will fix this” reflex.

P12Don’t take more psychedelics, cannabis, ketamine, intense breathwork, fast, push kundalini, deprive yourself of sleep, meditate or stretch to extremes, or schedule “another ceremony to fix it” while destabilized. Stabilize through sleep, food, routine, sober support, ordinary body, and ordinary world. Deep practice can wait until the floor is back.

P13Then ask what actually happened. What was medical or environmental? What was emotional? What was old trauma? What was fantasy? What was useful? What changed behavior? What needs therapy, an apology, rest, or plain practical action?

P14The fruit of insight isn’t how cosmic the story sounds. It’s whether suffering goes down and love gets more available.
