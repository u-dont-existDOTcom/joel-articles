## SS-DRINK: 4. Step 6: your hypertonic drink in bold, and two things in it (Your call)

*Act Now on SS, P6*

**Question:** Your answer (b): "a hypertonic drink could be as simple as throwing in a bit extra salt, potassium and magnesium, or even just a salty potato soup with some epsom salt thrown in", with the bold you asked for. The signs it is for are in your MDMA: Magnesium Alarm (confusion or headache that worsens, vomiting that won't stop, muscles getting weaker), so step 6 points there instead of saying them again. Two things in it I'd change, and one I'd leave:  
a) Potassium. Serotonin syndrome can break muscle down into the blood (your step 5), broken muscle releases potassium, and too much of it can stop the heart ([StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448168/), [a review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19046720/)). It can also run low after MDMA, as your MDMA section says, and only a blood test tells which. I'd add none and leave it to the ER.  
b) Epsom salt is magnesium sulfate, the laxative kind your MDMA section steers away from ("less likely to cause diarrhea than ... forms deliberately used as laxatives"), and diarrhea dries them out more. Potatoes are also high in potassium. I'd use your magnesium chloride flakes, in broth.  
c) Magnesium is then in step 6's drink and in step 8; the drink's is a little, so I'd leave both.

1) As you put it:  
"Overstimulating also dehydrates them, but we can't give fluids unless they're fully alert, able to swallow and not vomiting, and then just small sips of an electrolyte drink. If they still show the signs of low electrolytes from [MDMA: Magnesium Alarm](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/mdma-magnesium-alarm) above, they may need **a hypertonic electrolyte drink with more magnesium and potassium than a normal one**. That can be as simple as an ordinary one with a bit of each thrown in, plus extra salt, or even a salty potato soup with some Epsom salt."

2) Mine, without potassium or Epsom salt (quiz 2, S2 to S4):  
"Overstimulating also dehydrates them, but we can't give fluids unless they're fully alert, able to swallow and not vomiting, and then just small sips of an electrolyte drink.
