**How it would read:**

> I would also use topical magnesium chloride and consider topical DMSO with a known CBD preparation as adjuncts. Purified oral CBD is approved for the seizures of Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome and tuberous sclerosis complex. For focal seizures outside those syndromes, the evidence is mixed. In a 12-week trial, a CBD skin gel reached the blood at levels like oral CBD's but did no better than placebo. In a 2025 study of high-dose oral CBD, around 23 mg/kg a day (about 1,400 mg for a 60 kg adult), focal seizures fell by about half to three quarters over nearly three years, but it had no placebo group to compare with. I treat it as a plausible experimental adjunct rather than something to wait on while a seizure continues.

**Why:** You sent back a summary of the evidence on CBD for focal seizures, and each point checks out. In the 2025 study (PubMed: [Patel et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.18496)), 140 people with treatment-resistant focal epilepsy took purified oral CBD (Epidiolex) in an open program. In the 107 without tuberous sclerosis, the median dose was 23 mg/kg a day, and focal seizures fell by a median of 46 to 75% through 144 weeks. 96% had side effects, nobody got a placebo, and five of its nine authors work for the maker. The FDA label approves it for the seizures of Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome and tuberous sclerosis complex, from age 1 ([FDA label, May 2026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/210365s024s029s030lbl.pdf)). The skin-gel trial is the one the guide links (PubMed: [O'Brien et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.20189)). So high-dose oral CBD shows a signal for focal seizures, and the skin gel, which is closest to your DMSO-with-CBD plan, didn't beat placebo.

**What stays:** Your plan of topical magnesium chloride and DMSO with a known CBD preparation, and the DMSO seizure lines.

**For you to weigh:** The label also lists two interactions for people already on seizure drugs. CBD raises the active form of clobazam (sleepiness in 46% of those on clobazam, against 16% of the rest), and with valproate it raises liver enzymes more often. Revise to add a line if your readers may be on those. The question at the end of what you sent, about which kind of focal seizure, was the tool's question to you, and it doesn't change the paragraph.

**My advice:** Approve, or Revise.
