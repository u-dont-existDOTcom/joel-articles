**The paragraph now:**

> I would also use topical magnesium chloride and consider topical DMSO with a known CBD preparation as adjuncts. Purified oral CBD is approved for the seizures of Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome and tuberous sclerosis complex. For focal seizures outside those syndromes, the evidence is mixed. In a 12-week trial, a CBD skin gel reached the blood at levels like oral CBD's but did no better than placebo. In a 2025 study of high-dose oral CBD, around 23 mg/kg a day (about 1,400 mg for a 60 kg adult), focal seizures fell by about half to three quarters over nearly three years, but it had no placebo group to compare with. None of it tested CBD as rapid seizure rescue. DMSO reduced seizure activity in a rat temporal-lobe epilepsy model and has several neuroprotective mechanisms, but it produced the opposite effect at some doses in an absence-epilepsy model. I treat it as a plausible experimental adjunct rather than something to wait on while a seizure continues.
