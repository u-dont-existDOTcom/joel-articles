When to Bring In Outside Help

P1

Call medics while acting for:

  • Trouble breathing, blue or gray lips, or respiratory depression
  • A convulsive seizure lasting about five minutes, repeated seizures without normal recovery, or a first or substantially different seizure
  • Collapse, inability to wake normally, or rapidly worsening consciousness
  • Dangerous overheating, severe rigidity, or clonus with fever
  • Sudden one-sided weakness, loss of speech, inability to walk normally, or loss of control of bodily functions
  • A report that the brain feels damaged when the person cannot complete a basic neurological check or is rapidly worsening
  • Severe, worsening, radiating, or exertional chest pain – or chest pain with breathlessness, sweating, faintness, neurological changes, or collapse
  • Serious injury, uncontrolled bleeding, or a possible head or spinal injury
  • Repeated vomiting, severe dehydration, blood, or severe abdominal pain
  • Violence, self-harm, or a situation the sitter cannot physically contain
  • Concerning symptoms after an MAOI plus a serotonergic substance

P1 The remedies below can begin while help is being arranged. Calling medics does not require waiting passively, and using a fast field remedy does not require giving up a time-sensitive medical option.

Bring in mental-health support when:

  • Psychosis-like symptoms outlast the acute state.
  • Sleep won't return.
  • Mania or suicidal thinking appears.
  • Panic won't settle.
  • The person can't function afterward.
  • The trauma material is bigger than the room can hold.

When Peer Support Isn't Enough

P1 Get urgent help when somebody may harm themselves or another person, cannot be kept physically safe, is moving toward roads, water, or heights, has command voices or fixed terrifying beliefs driving action, becomes violent, is severely confused and unreachable, has dangerous physical symptoms, has lost enough sleep that judgment is collapsing, or exceeds what the sitter can safely contain.

P2 Use local emergency services for immediate danger or a medical emergency. In the United States, 988 can provide mental-health crisis guidance, de-escalation, and connection to local resources when no immediate physical emergency exists. Acute Medical and Neurological Rescue

P1 An emergency means act now. It does not always mean that an ambulance is the first or only useful action. Some severe but physically stable altered states may respond faster to field rescue than to hours in an emergency room.

P2 When the person is breathing normally, not dangerously hot, not seizing, has no neurological-injury concern, remains conscious enough to cooperate, and can be kept physically safe, calming and stabilizing the trip may be enough.

P3 The emergency room is not magic, and for a physiologically stable psychedelic crisis it may be a poor environment. Antipsychotics may work better for psychosis, but they carry their own risks and interactions, often administered by someone who doesn't know what was taken.

Active Seizure

P1 Time the seizure. Protect the person from injury, clear dangerous objects, loosen tight clothing, and place them on their side when practical. Don't restrain them and don't put anything in their mouth while they are convulsing or not fully conscious.

P2 If an appropriate rapid seizure-rescue benzodiazepine is available, use it by its intended route. Benzodiazepines are the established first-line drug class for status epilepticus. An ordinary pill is not the same thing as a nasal, buccal, rectal, intramuscular, or intravenous rescue preparation, and respiratory depressants in the mix change the risk.

P3 I would also use topical magnesium chloride and consider topical DMSO with a known CBD preparation as adjuncts. CBD has established antiseizure activity in several epilepsy syndromes, and transdermal CBD reaches systemic circulation, although it has not been validated as rapid seizure rescue. DMSO reduced seizure activity in a rat temporal-lobe epilepsy model and has several neuroprotective mechanisms, but it produced the opposite effect at some doses in an absence-epilepsy model. I treat it as a plausible experimental adjunct rather than something to wait on while a seizure continues.

P4 Call medics for a convulsive seizure lasting about five minutes, repeated seizures without normal recovery, trouble breathing, dangerous heat, serious injury, a first seizure, or a seizure substantially different from the person's known pattern.

MDMA: Jaw Tension Is the Magnesium Alarm

P1 With MDMA, I treat meaningful jaw tension as the earliest warning that the body needs more magnesium. This comes from repeated field experience rather than a direct clinical trial. Everyone I know who has taken enough magnesium chloride to make the jaw tension fully release has avoided the other complications I have seen when people let that tension continue.

P2 A token dose may not be enough. On multiple occasions, I have dissolved Dead Sea magnesium chloride hexahydrate flakes in water and taken approximately one gram per minute orally for around twenty minutes before my jaw fully released. I have tolerated around twenty grams without diarrhea; for me, diarrhea has appeared only above roughly forty grams. Magnesium chloride is also generally less likely to cause diarrhea than several other replacement salts, including forms deliberately used as laxatives.

P3 Interestingly, magnesium taken before the MDMA peak also makes the peak less euphoric for me. That suggests it may both meet a rapidly increasing magnesium need and directly moderate part of MDMA's excitatory effect. For harm reduction, slightly less euphoria is a reasonable price for a relaxed jaw and what appears in my experience to be a much lower risk of the state escalating.

P4 My second wife had severe convulsions after an MDMA overdose. At the hospital, her potassium was found to be critically low – described to me as essentially zero – and IV potassium was the only treatment they gave her. That experience is why I treat serious MDMA toxicity as a broader electrolyte emergency, even though magnesium is the first and most useful early intervention when jaw tension appears.

P5 Jaw tension should also mean no more MDMA, less exertion and heat, and closer monitoring. Worsening headache, confusion, vomiting, weakness, palpitations, collapse, or seizure raises concern about sodium, potassium, temperature, hydration, kidneys, and broader toxicity. MDMA is documented to cause acute kidney injury through several pathways, and controlled research confirms that hyponatremia can occur after a single dose.

P6 People with impaired kidneys do need to be more cautious with large, rapid magnesium doses because they may not clear magnesium normally. But they should be far more worried about the MDMA. MDMA can cause overheating, rhabdomyolysis, dangerous electrolyte shifts, and acute kidney injury. Kidney impairment is a strong reason not to take MDMA – not a reason to sit there with worsening jaw tension after taking it. I would not tell somebody with serious kidney impairment to copy my one-gram-per-minute approach blindly; I would tell them to treat the situation as higher-risk and get help sooner.

Chest Pain and Injury

P1 DMSO is also my first remedy for many injuries and some chest pain. After I overused erythritol, I developed angina that lasted for days. I took DMSO, the pain disappeared within about a minute, and it never returned.

P2 The cardiovascular science is preclinical but relevant. In rats, DMSO pretreatment reduced myocardial infarct size by about three quarters and sharply reduced cardiac injury markers. Other work found inhibition of tissue factor, platelet activation, and arterial thrombus formation. Myocardial-ischemia study; tissue-factor and thrombosis study; human platelet study.

P3 Mild, familiar, non-progressive discomfort in a fully alert person is different from new, severe, persistent, worsening, radiating, or exertional pain – or pain with breathlessness, sweating, faintness, neurological changes, or collapse. In the second branch, I would use DMSO while arranging medical evaluation. DMSO can also block pain signals, so pain relief alone does not prove that the underlying problem has ended.

Recent Oral Toxic Ingestion

P1 "Overdose" is too vague to choose a remedy. Ask what was swallowed, how much, when, and what the body is actually doing: respiratory depression, stimulant toxicity, serotonin toxicity, seizure, dangerous overheating, cardiovascular symptoms, severe agitation, objective neurological dysfunction, vomiting, diarrhea, or something else.

P2 Activated charcoal may help after some recent oral ingestions when the substance binds to it and the person is fully alert enough to swallow safely. It does not bind every toxin, can adsorb necessary medication, and creates aspiration risk in somebody who is vomiting, sedated, seizing, or unable to protect the airway. Clinical review.

P3 Poison control may be useful when the substance, combination, delayed risk, or value of charcoal is unclear. I do not treat it as a mandatory ritual; usefulness varies by country and by whoever answers. It is decision support, not first aid.

Suspected Serotonin Syndrome (SS)

P1 Serotonin syndrome overlaps with several symptoms above, but the rescue measures in the other branches do not necessarily treat it. It can follow serotonergic medication changes, combinations, or excessive exposure. Psychedelics, MDMA, DXM, many antidepressants, tramadol, linezolid, MAOIs, and some opioids can contribute, especially in combinations.

Recognizing serotonin toxicity

P1 Clinicians often use the Hunter criteria. One or more of the following patterns makes serotonin toxicity more likely:

  • Spontaneous clonus: involuntary rhythmic muscle contractions and relaxations occurring without stimulation.
  • Inducible clonus plus agitation or sweating.
  • Ocular clonus plus agitation or sweating: rapid rhythmic involuntary eye movements.
  • Tremor plus hyperreflexia: shaking together with abnormally brisk reflexes.
  • Hypertonia plus fever: rigidity with temperature above 38°C (100.4°F), together with ocular or inducible clonus.

P2 This is a recognition aid rather than a command to wait for every criterion before acting. Dangerous heat, rigidity, clonus, confusion, or rapid worsening deserves medical help.

Do this immediately when serotonin toxicity is reasonably suspected:

  1. Stop all suspected serotonergic substances. No more SSRI, SNRI, MAOI, tramadol, MDMA, DXM cough syrup, 5-HTP, St John's wort, SAM-e, linezolid, stimulant redoses, or harmala/ayahuasca-type MAOI exposure.
  2. If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early. It antagonizes serotonin receptors. Case reports describe rapid improvement, including complete resolution within two hours in a small series, while a recent systematic review found the evidence for major outcomes inconclusive. Case series; systematic review. I would not wait to see whether it works before cooling, stopping serotonergic substances, and arranging help for moderate or severe signs.
  3. Call emergency help and arrange transport for moderate or severe signs. Don't drive yourself. Poison control may help when the combination is unclear, but it is not the treatment.
  4. Cool the person without delaying transport. Move to a cool room, remove excess clothing, use a fan and cool damp cloths, and place ice packs wrapped in cloth at the neck, armpits, or groin when the person is hot. Don't put a confused, seizing, or very weak person in a bath. Fever reducers do not solve heat generated by extreme muscle activity.
  5. Keep stimulation low and prevent exertion. No pacing to "burn it off," exercise, crowds, or arguments. Akathisia can create a strong urge to move, but muscle activity can worsen heat and rhabdomyolysis risk.
  6. Hydrate only when fully alert and not vomiting. Small sips of an appropriate oral rehydration drink are reasonable. Don't force fluids, food, pills, or charcoal when someone is confused, vomiting, sleepy, or at seizure risk.
  7. Gather the evidence for the ER. Bring pill bottles and packages. Write down exact substances, dose, timing, last dose, recent increases, supplements, recreational drugs, temperature, pulse, and blood pressure when available.
  8. Hospitals may use benzodiazepines, external cooling, IV fluids, monitoring, and intensive supportive care. Niacin is unsafe for serotonin syndrome. DMSO is also not part of this branch. I would consider oral magnesium chloride when the person is fully alert, especially with MDMA-related jaw tension or muscle activity.

P1 This hierarchy is for acute situations, including emergencies – not merely uncomfortable trips.

P2 In general it's good to try all these things in very low, increasing doses before they are needed in crisis.

P6 ---

How I label the evidence

  • P1 Established acute treatment: standard seizure first aid, rapid rescue benzodiazepines, cooling dangerous hyperthermia, and treating documented electrolyte abnormalities.
  • Limited human evidence: interventions with clinical reports or small trials but no strong modern outcome evidence.
  • Mechanism or preclinical evidence: biologically plausible effects shown mainly in cells or animals.
  • Personal field signal: what I have repeatedly observed in myself or other people. Repetition and a clear immediate endpoint make an anecdote more useful, but they do not turn it into a population rate.

Leaving 100% DMSO on the skin can crenate it, and the skin may take a week to look plump again. DMSO can also burn dry mouth tissue, so the mouth should be moist first.
  • Nausea: when drinking DMSO, dilute it in water, juice, or milk and sip gradually. Sensitivity varies.
  • Reduced sleep: DMSO reduces how long I sleep when I take too much, take it too late, or use it over too many consecutive days. It builds up past a threshold that seems to differ between people. This is proven in rats. Some people sleep better with DMSO because it reduces whatever symptom had been keeping them awake.
  • Body odor: above a certain dose you may smell like sulfur. I don't smell when I put it into milk.

P5 Interactions:

  • P6 Don't put it on dirty skin; it can carry chemicals inward.
  • Don't nebulize too much DMSO when your lungs are full of smoking-related tar, because it can carry those substances into the blood too quickly. Weakness is an early symptom.
  • Don't take it with drugs that should not absorb rapidly, including corticosteroids and NSAIDs. With harmalas, don't put the harmala in the DMSO or take them at exactly the same time; taking them a bit apart is totally fine, and the point is to take DMSO if you've already absorbed the harmalas and the aya is too crazy.
  • Don't combine it with cholinergic drugs because DMSO is also cholinergic, although I haven't found anecdotes of this causing a real problem. Supplement extra choline because DMSO depletes it.

P7 Contraindications:

  • P8 DMSO has produced both anticonvulsant and proconvulsant effects depending on dose and seizure model. I consider it a plausible adjunct for excitotoxic or temporal-lobe-type seizure activity, not a proven universal seizure rescue.
  • It may worsen a cluster headache, even though rubbing DMSO – especially with magnesium chloride – on the head can alleviate tension headaches.
  • It may release histamine in some people. Use caution with MCAS.

A Final Trip Killer

Niacin (a Form of Vitamin B3)

P1 Flushing niacin has effectively aborted trips for me and friends, although I dislike it compared with beach morning glory.

P2 I include it because most people don't have access to Ipomoea pes-caprae, although I supply it. A vitamin is not automatically harmless. Niacin has been used for decades by orthomolecular doctors for psychotic conditions and seems to act both like a natural benzodiazepine and an antipsychotic. The clinical literature is discussed in Niacin: The Real Story by Abram Hoffer, M.D.

P3 Side effects:

  • P4 Niacin flush: itchy sunburn-like heat. This is normal, although some people find it intolerable and others enjoy it.
  • Heartburn in sensitive stomachs or after too much at once.
  • Drowsiness, usually with terrible sleep quality.
  • Higher or repeated doses can create real risk, especially with liver problems, alcohol, medications, or sensitive physiology.

P5 Other forms of vitamin B3 seem less reliable for relaxation. Niacinamide sometimes relaxes people and sometimes creates hyperfocus, depending on their state. Avoid sustained-release niacin and the useless non-flush hexanicotinate form.

Some Remedies Require More Caution

P1 Useful substances can still be poor rescue tools when somebody is already destabilized.

P2 Humic acid when eaten, can produce a hyperintelligent state for me. From the outside that may look manic, and it could destabilize somebody prone to mania or without meditation training. Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes

P1 The checklist above gives the order. This section is about carrying it out without becoming one more source of pressure.

P2

P3 Begin with whatever could hurt somebody in the next five minutes. Put away the car keys. Turn off the stove. Move away from roads, balconies, water, weapons, crowds, and strangers. Take the phone when somebody is preparing to publish the complete revelation of the universe or message an ex.

P4 Movement is allowed. Unsafe movement is not. No driving, redosing, confrontations, or leaving alone.

P5 Then look at the room. Is the light too bright? Is the music horrible? Are five concerned people talking at once? Is somebody touching the person when touch is unwelcome? Are they too hot, too cold, desperate for the bathroom, or trapped beside somebody they don't trust?

P6 A huge amount of what gets called profound psychedelic crisis is a frightened nervous system stuck in an annoying room.

P7 Dim the lights. Lower the sound. Clear out the extra people. Offer water without forcing it. Sip rather than chug. Ask before touching. Speak slowly and use fewer words. Please don't explain the universe at somebody who is already frightened by it.

P8 Ask "music or quiet?" If a track starts pulling the experience somewhere unwanted, change it. This is a bad time to defend the playlist.

P9 Give them ordinary reality:

P10 You're in [place]. It's [date/time]. You took [substance, if known]. These effects rise, peak, and change. This is intense, but it will change. We don't need to solve your life tonight.

P11 When the state came from meditation, breathwork, fasting, kundalini-style practice, extreme stretching, ritual, or a retreat, say:

P12 I am in an intense state. I am stopping the practice now. My job is ordinary safety.

P13 From there, choose one safe body experiment and one calm human. The next two sections explain those choices. Protect sleep, especially when the person is euphoric or grandiose, and let meaning wait until the nervous system has somewhere to stand.

P14 A non-drug altered state may have a less predictable clock than a substance. The practical move remains simple: stop the practice, eat something ordinary when safe, let the body settle, keep warm, and sleep. An open portal does not obligate you to keep pushing through it.

Stillness or Movement?

P1 Test them rather than turning them into rival religions. Try one for two minutes and ask: Does this make me safer and clearer, or more activated and impulsive?

P2 When lying down feels like imprisonment, pace slowly in a contained space. When pacing begins to resemble escape, sit against a wall or lie under a blanket. The goal is to let the nervous system discharge without creating a new danger.

P3 Parallel processing can help when lying still turns into immobile introspection. Let one part of attention stay with the body, fear, grief, or memory while another part completes a simple, safe task with a visible endpoint: sort ten objects, draw, fold five towels, or wash one cup. Finishing something small helps confirm that time is moving and ordinary reality is still here. Stop if the task increases agitation, speed, or risk.

P4 A useful order is room → body → human → time → meaning. Most people begin with meaning because the experience feels cosmic. When the nervous system is on fire, love becomes practical first.

P2 Use one calm human anchor as the lead. When safety, fatigue, logistics, violence, or self-harm risk exceeds what one person can hold, bring in one additional calm adult or crisis professional. That is backup. A committee of frantic people rarely improves anyone's nervous system.

P1 The quick checklist above contains the stripped-down 3 a.m. plan. Once the lamp is on, the keys are away, and "No big actions until morning" is visible, use the phone only to reach a calm person or support service.

For the Person Helping: Calm Anchor, Not Guru

P1 A good sitter is not there to win a philosophy debate, perform a rescue fantasy, impose an interpretation, or demonstrate spiritual advancement. Zendo-style harm reduction can be summarized as creating a safe space, sitting rather than guiding, talking through rather than down, and remembering that difficult does not automatically mean bad.

P2 Crying, grieving, remembering, or touching a genuine wound calls for loving awareness. Interrogation, analysis every thirty seconds, and "bro, you're purging ancestral karma" while somebody white-knuckles the carpet are generally less helpful.

P3 Stay present. A useful phrase is: This feeling is allowed to move through. I don't have to become it. I can love the part of me that feels it. Or simply: Can I send love to the one who's suffering?

P4 Don't drag somebody out of real grief. Don't trap them inside a loop that is grinding them down either. Love isn't stubbornness.

Do

  • P1 Ask simple questions: "Would less light help?" "Music or quiet?" "Closer or farther away?" "Water?"
  • Reflect without arguing: "That sounds terrifying." "I'm with you." "Let's breathe together."
  • Offer choices instead of commands.
  • Keep the room quiet, private, warm, and physically safe.
  • Call for help early when safety is slipping.
  • Translate metaphysics into shared safety values: "This feels sacred and huge. Sacred power protects life tonight."
  • Set behavioral boundaries without humiliation: "I won't help anybody hurt themselves or another person. We are keeping every body safe."

Don't

  • P1 Don't try to win an argument with visions, entities, memories, or cosmic conclusions. Don't validate harmful conclusions. Reflect the feeling and redirect toward safety, non-harm, sleep, and ordinary reality.
  • Don't tell somebody they are crazy, broken, weak, punished, or failing the medicine.
  • Don't force eye contact, touch, music, prayer, chanting, interpretation, or insight.

When the Content Turns Toward Harm

P1 When somebody says, "I am God, so I can kill," leading with "No you're not" or "That's delusional" can turn the encounter into a status fight. Nodding along as though harm is spiritually neutral is also unacceptable. Treat the statement as a safety signal and build a bridge back toward non-harm.

P2 Non-combative philosophical grounding:

P3 I hear how vast and certain this feels. Many traditions say God is in everyone. That makes every body sacred, not disposable. Peace is the godly move tonight. No proof is needed. We protect life and sleep first.

P4 Other useful lines: "Maybe you are touching something divine. Divine doesn't need a violent test." "Tonight the commandment is no harm, no proof, no mission, sleep first."

P5 Redirect within the person's value-world toward peace and protection, increase containment, and bring in crisis support as early and as non-coercively as the situation allows.

P6 When talk of harm continues, shift from philosophy to safety. Create distance from potential targets and keep your own exit. Don't block, crowd, threaten, mock, or physically wrestle away a weapon. Quietly remove means only when that can be done without confrontation or turning yourself into the target.

P7 When you are the only helper and safety is slipping, recruit one additional calm adult or crisis professional. Call crisis or emergency services for a specific target, available means, movement toward action, escalating agitation, a weapon, violence underway, or a situation you can't contain.

P8 Be brief and specific with emergency services: "Someone is in an altered-state or mental-health crisis, has threatened to harm others, may have access to [means if known], and needs a medical or mental-health crisis response with de-escalation support. Please send crisis-trained responders or EMS if available."

P1 Some people call shadow work a bad trip, especially when they had no intention of doing shadow work that day. The Other Bypass: Feeling Good Forever

P1 A well-rounded trip should offer practical lessons and contact with suffering as well as freedom from suffering, lest we become "so spiritually high that we are no earthly good." Give the Session a Beginning and an End

P1

P2 For planned altered-state work, think through the beginning, middle, and end before starting. A lot of prevention is boring: being rested, starting before everyone is exhausted, leaving time afterward, having food ready, and knowing how the session ends.

P3 When you have a choice:

Touch, Consent, and Compassion

P1 Self-touch is different from somebody else touching you. Compassion does not mean grabbing, hugging, restraining, stroking, or crowding. Ask first: "Would touch help, or would you rather have space?" Good support may be silent presence from six feet away.

P2 When the person says stop, freezes, pulls away, or seems unsure, stop. Touch must remain nonsexual, consensual, and unrelated to the helper's needs.

Demons, Jinn, Entities, and Shadow Forces

P1 I have faced demons and jinn during psychedelic and exorcism-type situations. The most important thing I discovered was that fighting them did not help. Sending love did.

P2 In one especially dramatic situation involving another person, the entity seemed to attack both of us psychically. I sent it love, and within a few seconds it left. The other person could not do that immediately. I had to show her, and it took hours of practice before it worked because she had never gone deeply into "Love Thy Enemy."

P3 I don't have to settle whether these things are objectively real or internal formations because the response is mostly the same.

P4 Hatred, fear, shame, pride, and rage make hooks. Love removes hooks.

P5 The common mistake is fighting the terrifying thing with the energy it already consumes. Panic fights panic, hatred fights hatred, pride fights pride, and suddenly a small war is running inside consciousness.

P6 Love is a better move, although it is difficult advice. Almost nobody can authentically love what terrifies them while they are terrified. Train before the emergency: send love to a frightening image, a hated person without approving what they did, your shame, your body, sickness, aging, fear, death, and the inner child. Practice being the space rather than the storm.

P7 This is not passivity. It is becoming unhookable. A demon can fight hatred, feed on fear, argue with pride, and seduce grandiosity. Love gives it nothing to grab.

P8 Safety rule

P9 Even a completely real-feeling entity does not make decisions for your body. No entity may tell you to hurt yourself or another person, drive, jump, flee, accuse somebody, cross a sexual boundary, or make a life decision tonight.

P10 When somebody is medically, physically, or psychologically too overwhelmed to reach love, stabilize first. Don't teach nondual demonology to somebody who needs the lights down and a doctor.

Abuse Memories, Recovered Memories, and Certainty

P1 Psychedelics can bring up true trauma, symbolic material, distorted fragments, fears, fantasies, archetypes, past-life narratives, and mixtures of all of them. Don't dismiss what arises. Don't treat intoxicated certainty as evidence either.

P2 Don't confront tonight

P3 When you see or remember abuse, betrayal, murder, past lives, hidden crimes, or cosmic plots, write it down and get safe. Don't confront anybody while intoxicated or destabilized. Return to it after sleep with a trauma-informed therapist, integration specialist, or grounded person you trust.

P4 The experience may matter deeply. It may be psychologically true, symbolically true, literally true, partly true, or false in the form it first appeared. You don't have to decide tonight.

Dealing With Shadow Thoughts

P1 When we try to purify the mind, something natural and frightening may happen: the opposite becomes more visible, almost like Newton's Third Law – every action arrives with an equal and opposite reaction.

P2 Shadow thoughts and feelings are not inherently a problem. They co-arise naturally and do not need to be attached to, like shadows cast by objects in sunlight. I also notice them after excessive focus. The mind becomes stressed and seeks an exit from the pressure, often in the opposite direction from the focus.

P3 Relax, unwind, unbind, and unweave the identity illusion, mind, and unified experience. My pamphlet on already-perfect goes further into this.

P4 I am not repeating the simplistic Hermetic claim that opposites are required to understand anything. A hated baby does not feel love, and a loved baby does not feel hate. In the subconscious world of concepts, however, two sides are often considered together.

P5 During a trip, don't identify with every shadow thought, suppress it, confess it to everybody, or convert it into a permanent diagnosis. Notice: "A thought is here. A feeling is here. It is intense, but it is not the whole of me." Then return to room, body, human, time, and love.

The Joker Poem

P1 At the centre of the spinning circle sits the joker with a mirror in his hand, reflecting all to everyone to help them understand.

His face is fierce, yet gentle, his eyes, they pierce you through, you cannot escape his gaze no matter where you go, no matter what you do.

His smile is warm, he draws you in, he is so calm, why are you so confused by him?

Your thoughts are like a raging storm between the whirling sky and sea, nothing seems certain any more, nothing's how it used to be.

Once things were clear in black and white, you thought you knew the wrong from right, you drew the line, and cut it fine, between the day and night; but now your world is crumbling, there's nowhere you can call your own, nothing's solid anymore, not even cities made of stone, not even your own body, not even flesh and blood, not even skin and bone.

Your life flashes before you, is that thunder, or the beating of your heart, you don't know if this is the end, or something new about to start. You don't know if you're dreaming or if you are awake, and as you go on spinning you see the joker's mirror reflecting every move you make.

After the Storm

P1 Don't rush to turn a hard experience into content. Sleep. Eat simple food. Drink water. Walk. Journal. Pray and meditate gently. Talk with one grounded person, rather than twenty chaotic people. Don't make huge decisions, diagnose yourself from one intense night, or assume every vision was literal and every fear was false. Let the system settle.

P2

Minimum Viable Integration

  1. P1 First 24 hours: sleep, food, water, warmth, ordinary contact, and no big moves.
  2. Next few days: write the facts – substance, dose when known, setting, events, triggers, what helped, and what made things worse.
  3. Then: consider what the experience may have meant emotionally, spiritually, relationally, or practically.
  4. Last: make small behavioral changes that reduce suffering and increase love.

What Kind of Aftermath Is This?

P1 When symptoms linger, name the pattern rather than collapsing everything into "I'm broken." Different patterns need different support.

  • P2 Anxiety and fear: grounding, routine, reduced stimulation, CBT-style reality testing, and therapy when it persists.
  • Sleep disturbance and nightmares: sleep hygiene, no more substances, and professional help when severe or prolonged.
  • Derealization and depersonalization: ordinary-world grounding, exercise, nature, outside attention, less rumination, peer or family support, and therapy when needed.
  • Visual distortions or HPPD-like symptoms: reduce stress and substance use; seek professional support when symptoms interfere with life.
  • Social disconnection and shame: one or two safe humans, peer support, and no social-media crowdsourcing while raw.
  • Existential shock: write it down, slow down, and return to practical love before building a metaphysical system.
  • Trauma material: trauma-informed therapy or integration support, without immediate confrontation while destabilized.
  • Paranoia or psychosis-like beliefs: professional support quickly, especially when beliefs drive unsafe action.
  • Somatic hyperarousal: gentle movement, breathing, grounding, and medical care for severe, unusual, or worrying symptoms.

P3 No "another ceremony will fix this" reflex.

P4 Don't take more psychedelics, cannabis, ketamine, intense breathwork, fast, push kundalini, deprive yourself of sleep, meditate or stretch to extremes, or schedule "another ceremony to fix it" while destabilized. Stabilize through sleep, food, routine, sober support, ordinary body, and ordinary world. Deep practice can wait until the floor is back.

P5 Then ask what actually happened. What was medical or environmental? What was emotional? What was old trauma? What was fantasy? What was useful? What changed behavior? What needs therapy, an apology, rest, or plain practical action?

P6 The fruit of insight isn't how cosmic the story sounds. It's whether suffering goes down and love gets more available.
