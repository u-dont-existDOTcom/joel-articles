When to Bring In Outside Help

P1

Call medics for:

  • Trouble breathing, blue or gray lips, or respiratory depression
  • A convulsive seizure lasting about five minutes, repeated seizures without normal recovery, or a first or substantially different seizure
  • Collapse, inability to wake normally, or rapidly worsening consciousness
  • Dangerous overheating, severe rigidity, or clonus with fever
  • Sudden one-sided weakness, loss of speech, inability to walk normally, or loss of control of bodily functions
  • A report that the brain feels damaged when the person cannot complete a basic neurological check or is rapidly worsening
  • Severe, worsening, radiating, or exertional chest pain--or chest pain with breathlessness, sweating, faintness, neurological changes, or collapse

  • Concerning symptoms after an MAOI, which slows the breakdown of serotonin (some older antidepressants, and the harmalas in ayahuasca and Syrian rue seeds), plus a serotonergic substance, one that raises serotonin, like MDMA or most antidepressants

Acute Medical and Neurological Rescue

P1 An emergency means act now. It does not always mean that an ambulance is the first or only useful action. Some severe but physically stable altered states may respond faster to field rescue than to hours in an emergency room.

P2 When the person is breathing normally, not dangerously hot, not seizing, has no neurological-injury concern, remains conscious enough to cooperate, and can be kept physically safe, calming and stabilizing the trip may be enough.

P3 The emergency room is not magic, and for a physiologically stable psychedelic or practice-induced crisis it may be a poor environment. Antipsychotics may work better for psychosis, but they carry their own risks and interactions, often administered by someone who doesn't know what was taken, if anything.

Active Seizure

P1 Time the seizure. Protect the person from injury, clear dangerous objects, loosen tight clothing, and place them on their side when practical. Don't restrain them and don't put anything in their mouth while they are convulsing or not fully conscious.

P2 If an appropriate rapid seizure-rescue benzodiazepine is available, use it by its intended route. Benzodiazepines are the established first-line drug class for status epilepticus. An ordinary pill is not the same thing as a nasal, buccal, rectal, intramuscular, or intravenous rescue preparation, and respiratory depressants in the mix change the risk.

P3 Call medics for a convulsive seizure lasting about five minutes, repeated seizures without normal recovery, trouble breathing, dangerous heat, serious injury, a first seizure, or a seizure substantially different from the person's known pattern.

What Else May Help: At the Aura, and Alongside Rescue Medicine

P1 Some people get a warning before a seizure takes hold, which is called an aura. That's when the things people report can still stop it, depending on what sets their seizures off. If it's flashing or flickering light, cover one eye with a hand and turn away from the light, because closing the eyes doesn't block enough of it. Some people stop a seizure at the warning stage with a strong smell like jasmine, and some train themselves to link one smell with feeling calm and then sniff it when a seizure threatens. When overstimulation is what sets it off, a cool, quiet, dark and comfortable space can help. I'd also try sniffing humic/fulvic acid powder, although that's just a hunch based on the effects I notice so far.

P2 During the seizure, alongside any rescue medicine, I would use topical magnesium chloride and consider topical DMSO with a known-genuine CBD preparation (a lot of CBD is fake or very dilute). Seizures come in all different types, and we have no study on whether CBD could be a rescue remedy for a seizure (just anecdotes), even though we do have studies showing long-term effectiveness to prevent them: Purified oral CBD is approved for the seizures of Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome and tuberous sclerosis complex.

P3 As for CBD/cannabis, people do smoke or vape it at the start of an aura, while they're still awake enough to do it on purpose. There are also people who find THC and/or CBD works as a nasal spray or sublingually, and there's even a medical THC nasal spray marketed for seizure interruption in Brazil by a non-profit patients' association called ABRACE. They testified in Congress that in more than 30 tests they did with children having seizures, the nasal spray ended those seizures within 30 seconds. You can see it being used on a child in this vid: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bgVi5o_2v_w

P4 I also read on forums some swear it stops the seizure from going any further, and there was one woman whose EEG showed fewer seizure discharges on the days she smoked. But a few people on the same forums said THC vapes and concentrates brought their seizures on. And rats bred to have seizures had more of them after high-THC smoke.

P5 As for topical CBD, it hasn't been well-studied. There was a short 12-week trial done, where they used a CBD skin gel, and although absorption was good, it didn't work any better than placebo to prevent seizures. The study authors suggested the dosing may have been too low, or that adults with focal epilepsy just don't respond as well as people with the syndromes oral CBD is approved for. The seizures in Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome are mostly generalized. Generalized are the type people usually have with MDMA & psychedelic overdoses, so topical CBD might do better for those than it did in that trial.

P6 As for DMSO, it did reduce seizure activity in rats with temporal-lobe epilepsy, and we know it has several neuroprotective mechanisms, but it produced the opposite effect at some doses in an absence-epilepsy model. I'd treat it as a plausible experimental adjunct remedy rather than something to wait on while a seizure continues.

MDMA: Jaw Tension Is the Magnesium Alarm

P1 With MDMA, I treat meaningful jaw tension as the earliest warning that the body needs more magnesium. This comes from repeated field experience rather than a direct clinical trial. Everyone I know who has taken enough magnesium chloride to make the jaw tension fully release has avoided the other complications I have seen when people let that tension continue.

P2 A token dose may not be enough. On multiple occasions, I have dissolved Dead Sea magnesium chloride hexahydrate flakes in water and taken approximately one gram per minute orally for around twenty minutes before my jaw fully released. I have tolerated around twenty grams without diarrhea; for me, diarrhea has appeared only above roughly forty grams. Magnesium chloride is also generally less likely to cause diarrhea than several other replacement salts, including forms deliberately used as laxatives.

P3 Interestingly, magnesium taken before the MDMA peak also makes the peak less euphoric for me. That suggests it may both meet a rapidly increasing magnesium need and directly moderate part of MDMA's excitatory effect. For harm reduction, slightly less euphoria is a reasonable price for a relaxed jaw and what appears in my experience to be a much lower risk of the state escalating.

P4 My second wife had severe convulsions after an MDMA overdose. At the hospital, her potassium was found to be critically low--described to me as essentially zero--and IV potassium was the only treatment they gave her. That experience is why I treat serious MDMA toxicity as a broader electrolyte emergency, even though magnesium is the first and most useful early intervention when jaw tension appears.

P5 Jaw tension should also mean no more MDMA, less exertion and heat, and closer monitoring. Worsening headache, confusion, vomiting, weakness, palpitations, collapse, or seizure raises concern about sodium, potassium, temperature, hydration, kidneys, and broader toxicity. MDMA is documented to cause acute kidney injury through several pathways, and controlled research confirms that hyponatremia can occur after a single dose.

P6 People with impaired kidneys do need to be more cautious with large, rapid magnesium doses because they may not clear magnesium normally. But they should be far more worried about the MDMA. MDMA can cause overheating, rhabdomyolysis, dangerous electrolyte shifts, and acute kidney injury. Kidney impairment is a strong reason not to take MDMA--not a reason to sit there with worsening jaw tension after taking it. I would not tell somebody with serious kidney impairment to copy my one-gram-per-minute approach blindly; I would tell them to treat the situation as higher-risk and get help sooner.

Chest Pain and Injury

P1 DMSO is also my first remedy for many injuries and some chest pain. After I overused erythritol, I developed angina that lasted for days. I took DMSO, the pain disappeared within about a minute, and it never returned.

P2 The cardiovascular science is preclinical but relevant. In rats, DMSO pretreatment reduced myocardial infarct size by about three quarters and sharply reduced cardiac injury markers. Other work found inhibition of tissue factor, platelet activation, and arterial thrombus formation. Myocardial-ischemia study; tissue-factor and thrombosis study; human platelet study.

P3 Mild, familiar, non-progressive discomfort in a fully alert person is different from new, severe, persistent, worsening, radiating, or exertional pain--or pain with breathlessness, sweating, faintness, neurological changes, or collapse. In the second branch, I would use DMSO while arranging medical evaluation. If you're not sure which branch it is, treat it as the second. DMSO can also block pain signals, so pain relief alone does not prove that the underlying problem has ended.

Recent Oral Toxic Ingestion

P1 "Overdose" is too vague to choose a remedy. Ask what was swallowed, how much, when, and what the body is actually doing: respiratory depression, stimulant toxicity, serotonin toxicity, seizure, dangerous overheating, cardiovascular symptoms, severe agitation, objective neurological dysfunction, vomiting, diarrhea, or something else.

P2 Activated charcoal may help after some recent oral ingestions when the substance binds to it and the person is fully alert enough to swallow safely. It does not bind every toxin, can adsorb necessary medication, and creates aspiration risk in somebody who is vomiting, sedated, seizing, or unable to protect the airway. Clinical review.

P3 Poison control may be useful when the substance, combination, delayed risk, or value of charcoal is unclear. I do not treat it as a mandatory ritual; usefulness varies by country and by whoever answers. It is decision support, not first aid.

Suspected Serotonin Syndrome (SS)

P1 Serotonin syndrome overlaps with several symptoms above, but the rescue measures in the other branches do not necessarily treat it. It can follow serotonergic medication changes, combinations, or excessive exposure. Ayahuasca and other MAOI brews, MDMA, DXM, many antidepressants, tramadol, linezolid, other MAOIs, and some opioids can contribute, especially in combinations.

Recognizing serotonin toxicity

P1 Clinicians often use the Hunter criteria. One or more of the following patterns makes serotonin toxicity more likely:

  • Spontaneous clonus: involuntary rhythmic muscle contractions and relaxations occurring without stimulation.
  • Inducible clonus plus agitation or sweating.
  • Ocular clonus plus agitation or sweating: rapid rhythmic involuntary eye movements.
  • Tremor plus hyperreflexia: shaking together with abnormally brisk reflexes.
  • Hypertonia plus fever: rigidity with temperature above 38°C (100.4°F), together with ocular or inducible clonus.

P2 This is a recognition aid rather than a command to wait for every criterion before acting. Dangerous heat, rigidity, clonus, confusion, or rapid worsening deserves medical help.

Do this immediately when serotonin toxicity is reasonably suspected:

  1. Stop all suspected serotonergic substances. No more SSRI, SNRI, MAOI, tramadol, MDMA, DXM cough syrup, 5-HTP, St John's wort, SAM-e, linezolid, stimulant redoses, or harmala/ayahuasca-type MAOI exposure.
  2. If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early. It antagonizes serotonin receptors. Case reports describe rapid improvement, including complete resolution within two hours in a small series, while a recent systematic review found the evidence for major outcomes inconclusive. Case series; systematic review. I would not wait to see whether it works before cooling, stopping serotonergic substances, and arranging help for moderate or severe signs.
  3. Call emergency help and arrange transport for moderate or severe signs. Don't drive yourself. Poison control may help when the combination is unclear, but it is not the treatment.
  4. Cool the person without delaying transport. Move to a cool room, remove excess clothing, use a fan and cool damp cloths, and place ice packs wrapped in cloth at the neck, armpits, or groin when the person is hot. Don't put a confused, seizing, or very weak person in a bath. Fever reducers do not solve heat generated by extreme muscle activity.
  5. Keep stimulation low and prevent exertion. No pacing to "burn it off," exercise, crowds, or arguments. Akathisia can create a strong urge to move, but muscle activity can worsen heat and rhabdomyolysis risk.
  6. Hydrate only when fully alert and not vomiting. Small sips of an appropriate oral rehydration drink are reasonable. Don't force fluids, food, pills, or charcoal when someone is confused, vomiting, sleepy, or at seizure risk.
  7. Gather the evidence for the ER. Bring pill bottles and packages. Write down exact substances, dose, timing, last dose, recent increases, supplements, recreational drugs, temperature, pulse, and blood pressure when available.
  8. Hospitals may use benzodiazepines, external cooling, IV fluids, monitoring, and intensive supportive care. Niacin is unsafe for serotonin syndrome. DMSO is also not part of this branch. I would consider oral magnesium chloride when the person is fully alert, especially with MDMA-related jaw tension or muscle activity.

Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes

P1

P2 Anything that could let someone hurt themselves in the next five minutes takes priority, so get the car keys out of reach and turn the stove off, and move them away from roads and balconies, or pools of water for that matter. Letting them move around is fine, and pacing can help, but keep them from taking more of any substance. If they want to go confront somebody, that can wait. And if they want to leave, have someone go with them. Put any weapons away and keep the person away from crowds and strangers. If they want to post their big revelation about the universe, or message an ex, take the phone.

P3 They're feeling overwhelmed, so dim the lights, and clear out the extra people around them.

P4 Sometimes it can be helpful to remind them where they are and what's happening, since a trip can make them lose track of the fact that they took a substance which has temporary effects.

P5 If a practice like meditation or fasting brought this on, have them stop it now, since doing more of it only makes this stronger. There's no drug wearing off here, so it could be over soon or take a while.

P6 As the person helping, you want to play the role of the calm, caring anchor to the safe present moment, not the witch doctor guru who knows magic spells. So don't force eye contact, touch, music, prayer, chanting on them, just offer gently if it might help. After they've slept, they may wish to talk more about their experience.

P7 But if they want to talk about it right now, don't try to win an argument with their visions, the entities they spoke to, the memories that surfaced, or whatever cosmic conclusions they reached ten minutes ago... But don't let silence validate anything harmful either, like a plan that ends with somebody getting hurt.

P8 Sometimes people get stuck in a panic loop, where they make interpretations to try to solve a feeling of panic, but then those create another kind of panic. It's helpful to remind them that sometimes feelings just need to be felt, so they can move and release, and then thinking can be more clear afterwards.

P9

P10 If they're being attacked by a demon, let your care help them connect to love, which cancels fear. Hopefully they've been practicing that prior. P11 I actually get something like this regularly when I take a high dose of something (or meditate too much), where I feel like I'm at risk of ruining my life if I don't stay away from sex and money. P1 An intense altered state can make everything feel final, cosmic, and urgent, and it might feel like you have to act right now. But more likely the urgency is just part of the state, and acting on it isn't safe.

P2 Put yourself on a 24–72 hour action lock.

P3 Until you have slept and eaten, maybe don't drive, travel, break up, make up, quit your job, confess everything, confront or accuse that list of people you've been meaning to... P4 Write it down, get some sleep, and go back over it with one grounded person once your head is clear.

P5 Treat trip content first as experience-content, rather than fact-content. Because you saw it, it might feel like you have to act on it, and because it felt cosmic, obeying it might seem wise. But more likely you had an experience, and it can wait until the state has passed.

P6 No revelation makes murder, assault, coercion, sexual boundary-crossing, reckless driving, or self-harm safe tonight. Anything requiring violated consent or harm belongs in the notebook until you can review it with a clear head.

P1 A bad trip is one of those terms thrown around very loosely in the psychedelic space. It can refer to various things which may be intertwined.

P2 When too much hits you at once, your nervous system can become flooded, and nothing makes sense, even as the brain still tries to derive meanings that might create unhelpful feedback loops. After the Storm

P1 Don't make huge decisions or diagnose yourself from an intense night. You don't need to assume every vision was literal, nor that anything you realized is actually true (or false). Ask your body what it needs from you now. I'm betting it will tell you: Give me sleep, natural food, pure water, a nice nature walk. It helps to integrate the experience via journaling your feelings, and talking with someone grounded (not the 20 chaotic people who are still coming down). To prevent your mind from giving spirituality a negative connotation, engage in some calm prayer or gentle meditation.

P2 No "another ceremony will fix this" reflex.

P3 Don't take more psychedelics, cannabis or ketamine, do intense breathwork, fast, push kundalini, deprive yourself of sleep, meditate or stretch to extremes, or schedule "another ceremony to fix it" while destabilized. Deep practice can wait until the floor is back.

P5 It can feel like the more cosmic the story sounds, the bigger the insight. But the fruit of an insight is whether suffering goes down and love gets more available.

What Kind of Aftermath Is This?

P1 When symptoms linger, people too often put everything into the "I'm broken" bucket, which solves nothing. While a professional can be called in to help at any point, self-diagnostics and basic fix strategies may obviate the need for one. Try to find the underlying psychological and/or physiological patterns driving the symptoms:

  • Anxiety and fear: with this pattern, it helps to first figure out what is providing the stimulation for the anxiety. CBT-style reality testing, checking the frightening thought against what's actually happening, is one way to do that which could be explored with a therapist if it persists, but can be done without one to start with. In general, it can help to have grounding/relaxing routines (like morning or bedtime yin yoga, meeting a friend for lunch).
  • Sleep disturbance and nightmares: I'd first ensure no more substances are being used, including "normal" ones like coffee, which some people become extra sensitive to after certain psychedelics (especially iboga). Pay attention to sleep hygiene, especially what you eat at what time, and what you might not be processing during the day which then grabs attention at night.
  • Derealization and depersonalization (when the world, or you yourself, feel unreal): Is it due to isolation? Rumination (which often goes along with isolation)? Peer and family support are crucial to combat those. Some things that tend to help are related to reconnecting with the feelings in your body: taking walks in nature (with bare feet for grounding to the Earth), doing gentle yin yoga, and Somatic Experiencing, a therapy that works through body sensations.
  • Visual distortions/Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD)-like symptoms: These can be long-lasting, and people often end up just getting used to it, but it helps to reduce stress and avoid any further use of psychedelics. I'd also try DMSO, niacin, and other neuroprotective/restorative treatments.
  • Social disconnection and shame: The biggest thing here is if you can find one or two trusted people who won't judge you. Get whatever it is off your chest. If you can't find someone like that, talk to a therapist. You'd be surprised how many people have gone through something like this and come out the other side realizing it wasn't what they made it out to be, myself included. Check my guide on shame for more info.
  • Existential shock: This one's pretty normal if someone has a deep journey, especially one they weren't prepared for. It helps to go back one step to focus on loving kindness and sending love to the inner child before getting lost in a philosophical ocean. Finding others who've been through a similar experience could be helpful, or harmful, depending on which rabbit hole it was you fell into. I find reading the Buddhist suttas supremely helpful.
  • Paranoia or psychosis-like beliefs: Here we gotta be careful because this can spiral out of control and get dangerous quickly. I'd seek professional support while utilizing the relatively safe and natural antipsychotics mentioned in this guide, like DMSO, syrian rue OIL, and niacin.
  • Somatic hyperarousal: This is not rumination, but more like akathisia, bodily restlessness. Try gentle movements (that yin yoga again!), slower breathing, grounding, and medical care if the symptoms are severe, unusual or longer-lasting than expected. I'd still go for my trusty relaxing herbs first, like beach morning glory, Syrian rue oil or the Metta NMT sniffer, and natural antipsychotics like DMSO and niacin.

Bring in mental-health support when:

  • Psychosis-like symptoms outlast the acute state.
  • Sleep won't return.
  • Mania or suicidal thinking appears.
  • Panic won't settle.
  • The person can't function afterward.
  • The trauma material is bigger than the room can hold.

If the trip is so crazy that you can't believe it a few days later, chances are, like me, you'll end up taking that drug again and again to understand it.

P3 With healthy drugs like Loveyhuasca, which has literally saved my life on numerous occasions from unknown infections, there's less of an argument for "Hang up the phone" as Alan Watts famously said you should do "once you get the message." But what we shouldn't be doing is feeling like we need to use these substances because we can't integrate them.

P7 Here's a reddit poster who talks about going through tremendous pain with MDMA as she uncovered childhood sex abuse memories. It took her dozens of sessions that felt harmful before she finally had one that fully got her to accept what happened to her (and a huge somatic release which looked like a re-enactment of the abuse). So there are some people who argue that if MDMA opened up your wounds, just keep taking more MDMA until it's fixed. Although on this thread, there are some who disagree with that simplistic advice. I know a few people who quit because they simply couldn't keep going through the pain that MDMA brought up for them. And as the commenters on the first thread note, very few people would go thru that dozens of times hoping for something different to happen.

P8 My perspective is that these people are not correctly preparing for the inner child reparenting that MDMA suddenly provides experiential access to. Because they are unprepared to make the best of the experience, and unprepared to integrate it after, it's sort of like being thrown into the deep end of a swimming pool before you learn to swim. You flail around for a bit then terrified, you go back to holding on to the wall and begging to get out. It really will take a long time to learn to swim that way, for most folks. On the other hand, without MDMA or loveyhuasca, many people are unable to really do inner child therapy, because it sounds fake to them, just as ego death sounded fake to me before I experienced it. So I think these approaches complement each other greatly.

Planning a Trip or Ceremony? Give It a Beginning and an End

P1

P2 If you're planning a session, think through beforehand how it will start and end, and what happens in between. Rest and timing come first: don't begin already depleted, and ideally start earlier in the day, so there's time afterward for food, some normal company and most importantly, sleep.

P3 Set up the space you'll be in, and decide ahead of time what would make you call for outside help. Go in curious about what might come up, along with gentle intentions, not harboring demands for revelations, buried memories, good cries or spiritual breakthroughs. And train before you need it, sending love to a frightening image, to someone you hate (without approving of what they did), and to the inner child. Sometimes people find that when they prepare to purify themselves and open up to love, they don't even end up needing the experience itself. The work is already done.

P4 One woman I met in Kenya was going to take shrooms to fix her tobacco and marijuana addictions. Before she took the shrooms, she simply realized she was addicted to those substances because she was trying to escape anxiety. So instead, she let the anxiety come, and accepted the feeling until it left. Her addictions vanished, no shroom trip required.

P5 If you're taking a psychoactive drug, though, know what you're taking and roughly how much, and look up what it mixes badly with and any medical concerns. If you've taken this amount before, that's a good starting place to understand how it could affect you this time, although people's sensitivities can vary from one session to the next, sometimes quite dramatically. If you've never taken this dose or done this type of meditation this intensely before, make sure you're not jumping into the deep end all at once.

P6 During the experience, the sitter (the sober person with you, not literally someone sitting on your face!) keeps you safe and offers you choices if relevant.

P7 As the experience winds down, give it some closure. Stop adding stimulation (I really don't like the MDMA protocols that include redosing, as it amplifies the neurotoxicity concerns). Check where you are and what day it is. Eat and drink a bit to replenish your electrolytes.

P8 Jot down anything you want to remember before you forget, and let your body know the session is over with some gentle massage. Keep the rest of the day free, and the next morning too if possible, since your brain is still in a vulnerable, flexible, "plastic" state, and the goal is not to cement in worse new patterns than what you had before!

P9 Use this sequence for planned sessions. Once somebody is already in trouble, return to the triage app instead of trying to improve the ceremony plan.
