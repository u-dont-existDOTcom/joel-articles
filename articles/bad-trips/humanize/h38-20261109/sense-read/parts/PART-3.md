## The part to read

[248] ## Spiritual ≠ "Getting Your Ass Kicked"

[249] The triage above is meant to make trips useful. Some people believe they deserve suffering because of karma or self-hatred. Suffering by itself fixes nobody. Suffering that yields insight requires enough clarity of mind and heart to learn from it.

[250] This is one side of the difference between spiritual bypassing and spiritual practice:

[251] Bypassing reaches for an ultimate view--"we are all One," "I don't exist," "this is all karma," "everything is Love"--while ignoring the person shaking in the corner.

[252] Practice says love is here, so we make the room safer, call for help when needed, stop pretending, and hold the experience without becoming it.

[253] That is tripsitting, shadow work, and the path becoming practical.

[254] ## The Other Bypass: Feeling Good Forever

[255] A well-rounded trip should offer practical lessons and contact with suffering as well as freedom from suffering, lest we become "so spiritually high that we are no earthly good." "Life is full of suffering" is Buddha's First Noble Truth. Ignoring that side of life becomes dissociation or spiritual bypassing in the opposite direction, followed eventually by statements like "Everything is Love, including violence."

[256] Many people meditate or take psychedelics to feel good. I am definitely one of them, and Buddha also said meditation should generally feel good. My friend Eshwar, however, found that shadow work made up about 99% of his path to heart-chakra awakening.

[257] Modern people carry so much trauma--and some people did even in Buddha's time--that shadow work and inner-child reparenting may need to come first, even before authentic liberation from the identity illusion.

[258] When shadow work is bypassed in meditation and therapy, it may appear in ordinary life without explaining itself. I encountered a lot of it in my first two marriages, to my bewilderment. I wish everybody could keep shadow work inside the safer rooms of meditation, therapy, journaling, prayer, and honest conversation.

[259] Shadow work to me is neither BDSM nor "feeding your demons." It means bringing loving awareness to the parts of the mind built in fear, pain, humiliation, rejection, and survival. Inner-child reparenting is central. Most negative mind-states aren't some error in your philosophy, they're just young parts of you that are still waiting for love and protection and permission, and for the explanation nobody ever gave them.

[260] Begin with my Inner Child Therapy guide and app, and my Self-Hypnosis guide and app.

[261] ## Hang up the phone, get off the Healing Treadmill

[262] ![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/ccdad771-dde3-4618-ba38-830eb35b4e59_1254x1254.png)

[263] If you use psychedelics (or MDMA) to achieve a goal like healing or spiritual liberation, you need to already be on the same path so that they aren't radically changing your ordinary understanding of things. If the trip is so crazy that you can't believe it a few days later, chances are, like me, you'll end up taking that drug again and again to understand it.

[264] With healthy drugs like Loveyhuasca, which has literally saved my life on numerous occasions from unknown infections, there's less of an argument for "Hang up the phone" as Alan Watts famously said you should do "once you get the message." But what we shouldn't be doing is feeling like we need to use these substances because we can't integrate them.

[265] YouTube video 9yp0lvYKK6Q

[266] That happened to me at first when I started with ayahuasca. The ego deaths were so far beyond my ordinary understanding of reality that I dismissed them each time within a week. Then I felt a compulsion to have another ego death soon after. Once I learned to meditate into the experience (and beyond) without ayahuasca, that compulsion was gone. If I had learned about Buddhism and if I could have accepted that my view of reality was limited prior to ayahuasca, then probably just one or two ego deaths would have been enough inspiration for me.

[267] With toxic drugs like MDMA & ketamine, that can be a recipe for long-term harm to your physical, and/or mental health. Ketamine is super easy to abuse because it's a dissociative psychedelic. All your problems are magically **gone, **and if you take enough, you get some crazy trips that you can barely remember later. 5-MeO-DMT is the more psychedelic version of ketamine. See my guide on that, because it shares a lot of the same warnings, altho I do think it has more potential to be helpful when used correctly.

[268] Here's a reddit poster who talks about going through tremendous pain with MDMA as she uncovered childhood sex abuse memories. It took her dozens of sessions that felt harmful before she finally had one that fully got her to accept what happened to her (and a huge somatic release which looked like a re-enactment of the abuse). So there are some people who argue that if MDMA opened up your wounds, just keep taking more MDMA until it's fixed. Although on this thread, there are some who disagree with that simplistic advice. I know a few people who quit because they simply couldn't keep going through the pain that MDMA brought up for them. And as the commenters on the first thread note, very few people would go thru that dozens of times hoping for something different to happen.

[269] My perspective is that these people are not correctly preparing for the inner child reparenting that MDMA suddenly provides experiential access to. Because they are unprepared to make the best of the experience, and unprepared to integrate it after, it's sort of like being thrown into the deep end of a swimming pool before you learn to swim. You flail around for a bit then terrified, you go back to holding on to the wall and begging to get out. It really will take a long time to learn to swim that way, for most folks. On the other hand, without MDMA or loveyhuasca, many people are unable to really do inner child therapy, because it sounds fake to them, just as ego death sounded fake to me before I experienced it. So I think these approaches complement each other greatly.

[270] ## Give the Session a Beginning and an End

[271] ![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/5ae3cffc-d155-41ef-b1af-c547efa8f52f_1122x1402.png)

[272] If you're planning a session, think through beforehand how it will start and end, and what happens in between. Rest and timing come first: don't begin already depleted, and ideally start earlier in the day, so there's time afterward for food, some normal company and most importantly, sleep.

[273] Set up the space you'll be in, and decide ahead of time what would make you call for outside help. Go in curious about what might come up, along with gentle intentions, not harboring demands for revelations, buried memories, good cries or spiritual breakthroughs. Sometimes people find that when they prepare to purify themselves and open up to love, they don't even end up needing the experience itself. The work is already done.

[274] One woman I met in Kenya was going to take shrooms to fix her tobacco and marijuana addictions. Before she took the shrooms, she simply realized she was addicted to those substances because she was trying to escape anxiety. So instead, she let the anxiety come, and accepted the feeling until it left. Her addictions vanished, no shroom trip required.

[275] If you're taking a psychoactive drug, though, know what you're taking and roughly how much, and look up what it mixes badly with and any medical concerns. If you've taken this amount before, that's a good starting place to understand how it could affect you this time, although people's sensitivities can vary from one session to the next, sometimes quiet dramatically. If you've never taken this dose or done this type of meditation this intensely before, make sure you're not jumping into the deep end all at once.

[276] During the experience, the sitter (the sober person with you, not literally someone sitting on your face!) keeps you safe and offers you choices if relevant.

[277] As the experience winds down, give it some closure. Stop adding stimulation (I really don't like the MDMA protocols that include redosing, as it amplifies the neurotoxicity concerns). Check where you are and what day it is. Eat and drink a bit to replenish your electrolytes.

[278] Jot down anything you want to remember before you forget, and let your body know the session is over with some gentle massage. Keep the rest of the day free, and the next morning too if possible, since your brain is still in a vulnerable, flexible, "plastic" state, and the goal is not to cement in worse new patterns than what you had before!

[279] Use this sequence for planned sessions. Once somebody is already in trouble, return to the triage app instead of trying to improve the ceremony plan.

[280] ## The Fear/Panic Loop

[281] A common loop is fear trying to solve fear. Terror appears, and the mind answers it with metaphysics, diagnosis, conspiracy, spiritual certainty, or desperate action. That usually feeds the terror.

[282] ![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/633de218-0966-491c-aac4-6b4a366195ce_1122x1402.png)

[283] **Loop breaker:**

[284] **Name it:** "This is fear trying to solve fear." 2. **Stop debating:** no metaphysics, diagnosis, or life decisions at the peak. 3. **Move down the ladder:** room → body → human → time. 4. **Repeat:** "This is a wave. I don't have to solve it at the peak."

[285] ## Dealing With Sorrow: Missing Versus Sending Love

[286] I once found a pamphlet helpful on sorrow, although it was too "ultimate" and Advaita for my taste. I now think focusing on love is more helpful then focusing on missing somebody or something.

[287] Missing hurts. Sending love feels good.

[288] This sounds almost embarrassingly obvious, but it took me months of daily loveyhuasca after my ex-wife Bee left to understand it.

[289] Missing someone is a contraction. There's hunger and ache in it, it keeps circling back to you, and there's the painful fantasy that loving them means needing them to come back. Sending love is different. Sending love is pleasure in itself, even when the person is gone. Love can bless without grabbing.

[290] The difference is enormous.

[291] The mistake people often make in love states is simply enjoying the love--or sending heart emojis to everybody. That can feel like the whole point, and it's nice.

[292] But the deepest utility is sending love to the places where love is missing.

[293] Send love to the person you miss without demanding their return. - Send love to the part of you still waiting for them. - Send love to the inner child who believed somebody else had to rescue him. - Send love to the ashamed, angry, lonely, terrified, and exiled parts.

[294] ## Hand on Heart and Gratitude 🫶🏿

[295] A hand on the heart can change the direction of a trip, especially when the problem is emotional contraction, grief, fear, or loneliness. There's no need to manufacture loving thoughts, you're just giving the body one simple signal: love is here.

[296] Put one hand on the heart and the other on the solar plexus just below it, sending love to the inner child. Sometimes that shifts the whole field. Once love reaches the inner child, send it toward all beings suffering now. Recognizing that you are not the only one suffering can open awareness in a liberating way, with real heart magic.

[297] Gratitude comes like icing on the cake. Being thankful for every lesson, difficult experience, reprieve, heart opening, and moment of compassion can create a deep connection with the sacred. Never force gratitude. Let it bubble up gently.

[299] ### Touch, Consent, and Compassion

[300] Self-touch is different from somebody else touching you. Compassion does not mean grabbing, hugging, restraining, stroking, or crowding. Ask first: **"Would touch help, or would you rather have space?"** Good support may be silent presence from six feet away.

[301] When the person says stop, freezes, pulls away, or seems unsure, stop. Touch must remain nonsexual, consensual, and unrelated to the helper's needs.

[303] ## Demons, Jinn, Entities, and Shadow Forces

[304] I have faced demons and jinn during psychedelic and exorcism-type situations. The most important thing I discovered was that fighting them did not help. Sending love did.

[305] In one especially dramatic situation involving another person, the entity seemed to attack both of us psychically. I sent it love, and within a few seconds it left. The other person could not do that immediately. I had to show her, and it took hours of practice before it worked because she had never gone deeply into "Love Thy Enemy."

[306] I don't have to settle whether these things are objectively real or internal formations because the response is mostly the same.

[307] Hatred, fear, shame, pride, and rage make hooks. Love removes hooks.

[308] The common mistake is fighting the terrifying thing with the energy it already consumes. Panic fights panic, hatred fights hatred, pride fights pride, and suddenly a small war is running inside consciousness.

[309] Love is a better move, although it is difficult advice. Almost nobody can authentically love what terrifies them while they are terrified. So train before you need it, sending love to a frightening image, to someone you hate (without approving of what they did), to your shame and your body, to sickness and aging and fear and death, and to the inner child. Practice being the space rather than the storm.

[310] It might look passive, but this is how you become unhookable. A demon can fight your hatred, feed on your fear, argue with your pride or seduce your grandiosity, but there's nothing in love for it to grab.

[311] When somebody is medically, physically, or psychologically too overwhelmed to reach love, stabilize first. Don't teach nondual demonology to somebody who needs the lights down and a doctor.

[312] ## Abuse Memories, Recovered Memories, and Certainty

[313] Psychedelics and intense practices can bring up true trauma, symbolic material, distorted fragments, fears, fantasies, archetypes, past-life narratives, and mixtures of all of them. Don't dismiss what arises. Don't treat the certainty you feel in that state as evidence either.

[314] **Don't confront tonight**

[315] When you see or remember abuse, betrayal, murder, past lives, hidden crimes, or cosmic plots, write it down and get safe. Don't confront anybody while intoxicated or destabilized. Return to it after sleep with a trauma-informed therapist, integration specialist, or grounded person you trust.

[316] The experience may matter deeply. It could be true psychologically, or symbolically, or literally, or only partly true, or false in the form it first took. You don't have to decide tonight.

[317] ## Dealing With Shadow Thoughts

[318] When we try to purify the mind, something natural and frightening may happen: the opposite becomes more visible, almost like Newton's Third Law--every action arrives with an equal and opposite reaction.

[319] Shadow thoughts and feelings are not inherently a problem. They co-arise naturally and do not need to be attached to, like shadows cast by objects in sunlight. I also notice them after excessive focus. The mind becomes stressed and seeks an exit from the pressure, often in the opposite direction from the focus.

[320] Relax, unwind, unbind, and unweave the identity illusion, mind, and unified experience. My pamphlet on already-perfect goes further into this.

[321] I am not repeating the simplistic Hermetic claim that opposites are required to understand anything. A hated baby does **not** feel love, and a loved baby does **not** feel hate. In the subconscious world of concepts, however, two sides are often considered together.

[322] During a trip, don't identify with every shadow thought, suppress it, confess it to everybody, or convert it into a permanent diagnosis. Notice: **"A thought is here. A feeling is here. It is intense, but it is not the whole of me."** Then return to room, body, human, time, and love.

[326] ## After the Storm

[327] Don't make huge decisions or diagnose yourself from an intense night. You don't need to assume every vision was literal, nor that anything you realized is actually true (or false). Ask your body what it needs from you now. I'm betting it will tell you: Give me sleep, natural food, pure water, a nice nature walk. It helps to integrate the experience via journaling your feelings, and talking with someone grounded (not the 20 chaotic people who are still coming down). To prevent your mind from giving spirituality a negative connotation, engage in some calm prayer or gentle meditation.

[329] ### What Kind of Aftermath Is This?

[330] When symptoms linger, people too often put everything into the "I'm broken" bucket, which solves nothing. While a professional can be called in to help at any point, self-diagnostics and basic fix strategies may obviate the need for one. Try to find the underlying psychological and/or physiological patterns driving the symptoms:

[331] **Anxiety and fear:** with this pattern, it helps to first figure out what is providing the stimulation for the anxiety. CBT-style reality testing is one way to do that which could be explored with a therapist if it persists, but can be done without one to start with. In general, it can help to have grounding/relaxing routines (like morning or bedtime yin yoga, meeting a friend for lunch). - **Sleep disturbance and nightmares:** I'd first ensure no more substances are being used, including "normal" ones like coffee, which some people become extra sensitive to after certain psychedelics (especially iboga). Pay attention to sleep hygiene, especially what you eat at what time, and what you might not be processing during the day which then grabs attention at night. - **Derealization and depersonalization:** Is it due to isolation? Rumination (which often goes along with isolation)? Peer and family support are crucial to combat those. Some things that tend to help are related to reconnecting with the feelings in your body: taking walks in nature (with bare feet for grounding to the Earth), doing gentle yin yoga, and Somatic Experiencing therapy. - **Visual distortions/Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD)-like symptoms:** These can be long-lasting, and people often end up just getting used to it, but it helps to reduce stress and avoid any further use of psychedelics. I'd also try DMSO, niacin, and other neuroprotective/restorative treatments. - **Social disconnection and shame:** The biggest thing here is if you can find one or two trusted people who won't judge you. Get whatever it is off your chest. If you can't find someone like that, talk to a therapist. You'd be surprised how many people have gone through something like this and come out the other side realizing it wasn't what they made it out to be, myself included. Check my guide on shame for more info. - **Existential shock:** This one's pretty normal if someone has a deep journey, especially one they weren't prepared for. It helps to go back one step to focus on loving kindness and sending love to the inner child before getting lost in a philosophical ocean. Finding others who've been through a similar experience could be helpful, or harmful, depending on which rabbit hole it was you fell into. I find reading the Buddhist suttas supremely helpful. - **Paranoia or psychosis-like beliefs:** Here we gotta be careful because this can spiral out of control and get dangerous quickly. I'd seek professional support while utilizing the relatively safe and natural antipsychotics mentioned in this guide, like DMSO, syrian rue OIL, and niacin. - **Somatic hyperarousal:** This is not rumination, but more like akathisia, bodily restlessness. Try gentle movements (that yin yoga again!), slower breathing, grounding, and medical care if the symptoms are severe, unusual or longer-lasting than expected. I'd still go for my trusty relaxing herbs and natural antipsychotics listed above first.

[332] **No "another ceremony will fix this" reflex.**

[333] Don't take more psychedelics, cannabis, ketamine, intense breathwork, fast, push kundalini, deprive yourself of sleep, meditate or stretch to extremes, or schedule "another ceremony to fix it" while destabilized. Deep practice can wait until the floor is back.

[334] Then ask what actually happened. What was medical or environmental? What was emotional? What was old trauma? What was fantasy? What was useful? What changed behavior? What needs therapy, an apology, rest, or plain practical action?

[335] It can feel like the more cosmic the story sounds, the bigger the insight. But the fruit of an insight is whether suffering goes down and love gets more available.
