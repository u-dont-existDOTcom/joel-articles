[106] #### Ocean Water to calm & protect the energetic field

[107] Ocean water is one of the simplest and strangest stabilizers I have found. I noticed it in Kenya and Florida. Rain water also works for me, but I've only tested it near the ocean. You can also buy Quinton Medical Seawater, which has a number of health benefits when used as a nasal spray or as a source of minerals.

[108] When a trip is too strong, touching ocean water can be extremely soothing. Touching the outside of a bottle doesn't do it. Direct contact with the water seems to matter, even when the water has been stored in a bottle.

[109] It has a grounding, cooling, organizing effect different from DMSO, aged ozonated-charcoal water, zeolite, or beach morning glory. I don't understand the mechanism. I only know that it has repeatedly felt like a powerful natural remedy for excessive psychedelic intensity.

[110] #### Shungite, Aged Ozonated-Charcoal Water, or Boiled Charcoal Water

[111] I honestly can't say how different these are from ocean water, because I've only used them a handful of times, but I do believe they're different in certain ways. Ocean water seems to reduce the intensity of the trip a bit more, while aged ozonated-charcoal water seems more peaceful without necessarily reducing intensity, and ocean water seems more reliable for some reason. Aged ozonated-charcoal water is different from zeolite and from swallowing charcoal.

[112] What I usually use is the water portion: water ozonated in contact with activated charcoal and allowed to sit after the charcoal falls to the bottom, or water ozonated with ordinary rock charcoal. I mean the water after active ozonation has ended and residual ozone has dissipated.

[113] That water doesn't fix the gut the way zeolite does.

[114] Aged ozonated-charcoal water acts more on the crazy or excessively intense mental feeling: too much psychedelic energy, the wrong environment, anxiety, high EMF, or a generally overloaded mind. It can shift "too much" into calm release without necessarily aborting the trip.

[115] Zeolite usually feels subtle to sober people. Aged ozonated-charcoal water does not. Most people I have given it to find it relaxing even while sober, whether they smell it, touch it, or drink the water portion.

[116] I have even watched the water calm a crying baby through touch alone. No ozone gas was present; this was contact with the water after ozonation had ended and the residual ozone had dissipated. I include that because it suggested the relaxing effect was not only adult expectation or psychedelic context.

[117] Earlier in my experiments, **I made the mistake of directly smelling ozone gas** during a psychedelic state. It intensified the experience and became mentally alarming. DMSO didn't help that situation, while aged ozonated-charcoal water helped rapidly. I don't recommend inhaling ozone. The mistake showed me that DMSO and aged ozonated-charcoal water address different kinds of overload.

[118] Without an ozone-water purifier--which can cost around $20 on Amazon--boiling water with charcoal gives some of the benefit. Touching or holding shungite can also feel similar to holding cleaned zeolite. Shungite pendants are sold on Amazon. Shungite should not be drunk unless it has been properly washed of heavy metals.

[119] #### **DMSO**

[120] DMSO is another stabilizer I use when a trip becomes too intense and I don't want to abort it. It is also my primary emergency neuroprotectant when the problem feels like acute neurological damage, altered consciousness, loss of bodily control, or severe drug-related brain dysfunction.

[121] It overlaps with aged ozonated-charcoal water, but the two don't always work equally. Sometimes DMSO works better; sometimes the water does. I don't yet know why. In my experience there is generally no harm in taking both or mixing them. Ozonated-charcoal water has what I consider a perfect safety profile; DMSO is a close second in my experience.

[122] **Side effects:**

[123] **Astringency:** 100% DMSO usually irritates skin on the upper half of the body. When it itches, dilute it with water or real aloe juice without added chemicals. Leaving 100% DMSO on the skin can crenate it, and the skin may take a week to look plump again. DMSO can also burn dry mouth tissue, so the mouth should be moist first. - **Nausea:** when drinking DMSO, dilute it in water, juice, or milk and sip gradually. Sensitivity varies. - **Reduced sleep:** DMSO reduces how long I sleep when I take too much, take it too late, or use it over too many consecutive days. It builds up past a threshold that seems to differ between people. In rats, higher doses made sleep lighter. Some people sleep better with DMSO because it reduces whatever symptom had been keeping them awake. - **Body odor:** above a certain dose you may smell like sulfur. I don't smell when I put it into milk.

[124] **Interactions:**

[125] Don't put it on dirty skin; it can carry chemicals inward. - Don't take it with drugs that should not absorb rapidly: DMSO can strengthen other medicines taken with it. With harmalas, don't put the harmala in the DMSO or take them at exactly the same time; taking them a bit apart is totally fine, and the point is to take DMSO if you've already absorbed the harmalas and the aya is too crazy. - Don't combine it with cholinergic drugs because DMSO is also cholinergic, although I haven't found anecdotes of this causing a real problem.

[126] **Contraindications:**

[127] DMSO has produced both anticonvulsant and proconvulsant effects depending on dose and seizure model. I consider it a plausible adjunct for excitotoxic or temporal-lobe-type seizure activity, not a proven universal seizure rescue. - It may worsen a cluster headache, even though rubbing DMSO--especially with magnesium chloride--on the head can alleviate tension headaches. - It may release histamine in some people. Use caution with MCAS.

[130] #### Niacin (a Form of Vitamin B3)

[131] Flushing niacin has effectively aborted trips for me and friends, although I dislike it compared with beach morning glory.

[132] I include it because most people don't have access to Ipomoea pes-caprae, although I supply it. A vitamin is not automatically harmless. Niacin has been used for decades by orthomolecular doctors for psychotic conditions and seems to act both like a natural benzodiazepine and an antipsychotic. The clinical literature is discussed in Niacin: The Real Story by Abram Hoffer, M.D.

[133] **Side effects:**

[134] **Niacin flush:** itchy sunburn-like heat. This is normal, although some people find it intolerable and others enjoy it. - Heartburn in sensitive stomachs or after too much at once. - Drowsiness, usually with terrible sleep quality. - Higher or repeated doses can create real risk, especially with liver problems, alcohol, medications, or sensitive physiology.

[135] Other forms of vitamin B3 seem less reliable for relaxation. Niacinamide sometimes relaxes people and sometimes creates hyperfocus, depending on their state. Avoid sustained-release niacin and the useless non-flush hexanicotinate form.

[152] ## Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes

[153] ![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/944bef30-ca59-415b-a2e8-809a29ac2ba6_1122x1402.png)

[154] Anything that could let someone hurt themselves in the next five minutes takes priority, so get the car keys out of reach and turn the stove off, and move them away from roads and balconies, or pools of water for that matter. Letting them move around is fine, and pacing can help, but keep them from taking more of any substance. If they want to go confront somebody, that can wait. And if they want to leave, have someone go with them. Put any weapons away and keep the person away from crowds and strangers. If they want to post their big revelation about the universe, or message an ex, take the phone.

[155] They're feeling overwhelmed, so dim the lights, and clear out the extra people around them.

[156] Sometimes it can be helpful to remind them where they are and what's happening, since a trip can make them lose track of the fact that they took a substance which has temporary effects.

[157] If a practice like meditation or fasting brought this on, have them stop it now, since doing more of it only makes this stronger. There's no drug wearing off here, so it could be over soon or take a while.

[158] As the person helping, you want to play the role of the calm, caring anchor to the safe present moment, not the witch doctor guru who knows magic spells. So don't force eye contact, touch, music, prayer, chanting on them, just offer gently if it might help. After they've slept, they may wish to talk more about their experience.

[159] But if they want to talk about it right now, don't try to win an argument with their visions, the entities they spoke to, the memories that surfaced, or whatever cosmic conclusions they reached ten minutes ago... But don't let silence validate anything harmful either, like a plan that ends with somebody getting hurt.

[160] Sometimes people get stuck in a panic loop, where they make interpretations to try to solve a feeling of panic, but then those create another kind of panic. It's helpful to remind them that sometimes feelings just need to be felt, so they can move and release, and then thinking can be more clear afterwards.

[161] If they're being attacked by a demon, let your care help them connect to love, which cancels fear. Hopefully they've been practicing that prior. Don't encourage them to believe they are crazy, impure, weak or broken. While many interpretations are possible, a therapeutic one would not be that they're being punished or failing the medicine.

[162] I actually get something like this regularly when I take a high dose of something (or meditate too much), where I feel like I'm at risk of ruining my life if I don't stay away from sex and money. And there's a voice that really commands me to leave both of those completely (which led to 3 weeks of wandering in Malaysia & Cambodia without money once). What actually helped me in one case when receiving this stern order from a higher power, was to ask it to clarify. I said, "I agree, that makes sense as a Buddhist, and I'd like to leave those behind at some point, especially money. But what's the best timeline to benefit all involved? Some people are dependent on me, I have a son, and so forth." The voice didn't respond, it just went away. So I took it more like a long-term suggestion than the urgency I assumed it needed based on how loud it was at first.

[163] ### Stillness or Movement?

[164] Test them rather than turning them into rival religions. Try one for two minutes and ask: Does this make me safer and clearer, or more activated and impulsive?

[165] When lying down feels like imprisonment, pace slowly in a contained space. When pacing begins to resemble escape, sit against a wall or lie under a blanket. The goal is to let the nervous system discharge without creating a new danger.

[166] **Parallel processing** can help when lying still turns into immobile introspection. Let one part of attention stay with the body, fear, grief, or memory while another part completes a simple, safe task with a visible endpoint: sort ten objects, draw, fold five towels, or wash one cup. Finishing something small helps confirm that time is moving and ordinary reality is still here. Stop if the task increases agitation, speed, or risk.

[167] A useful order is **room → body → human → time → meaning**. Most people begin with meaning because the experience feels cosmic. When the nervous system is on fire, love becomes practical first.

[168] ## Get One Human Anchor

[169] Many bad trips get worse because somebody is alone with a frightened or over-certain mind. Other times it's the opposite: too many people crowd in and start advising, arguing, spiritualizing, or panicking.

[170] So **one calm person should be the anchor** and take the lead. One person can only handle so much, though. When fatigue or logistics get to be too much, or keeping them safe does, or there's a risk of violence or self-harm, bring in **one more calm adult or a crisis professional** to back them up.

[171] ![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/2b8889eb-85e8-466f-99af-f71e48caeb61_1122x1402.png)

[172] **Script for the person in the altered state:**

[173] I'm in an intense psychedelic or altered state and I need grounding. I don't need you to solve my life. Please stay with me, speak slowly, remind me where I am, and help me not make any big decisions until tomorrow.

[174] **Script for the sitter or friend:**

[175] I'm here. You're not alone. This is a wave. We don't have to solve your whole life right now. Let's make your body safe, keep things simple, and stay together.

[190] ## For the Person Helping: Calm Anchor, Not Guru

[191] The Zendo Project, a harm-reduction group that offers peer support at festivals and other events to people having a hard time in an altered state, sums up its approach in four principles: creating a safe space, sitting with the person rather than guiding them, talking them through it rather than down from it, and remembering that difficult isn't necessarily bad.

[192] Crying, grieving, remembering, or touching a genuine wound calls for loving awareness. Interrogation, analysis every thirty seconds, and "bro, you're purging ancestral karma" while somebody white-knuckles the carpet are generally less helpful.

[193] Stay present. If it helps, you can say to yourself: This feeling is allowed to move through. I don't have to become it. I can love the part of me that feels it. Or simply: Can I send love to the one who's suffering?

[194] ### Do

[195] Ask simple questions: "Would less light help?" "Music or quiet?" "Closer or farther away?" "Water?" (in small sips) "Need the bathroom?" - Reflect without arguing: "That sounds terrifying." "I'm with you." "Let's breathe together." - Keep the room quiet, private, warm, and physically safe, and move anyone they're wary of somewhere else. - Call for help early when safety is slipping. - Translate metaphysics into shared safety values: "This feels sacred and huge. Sacred power protects life tonight." - Set behavioral boundaries without humiliation: "I won't help anybody hurt themselves or another person. We are keeping every body safe."

[196] ### When the Content Turns Toward Harm

[197] When somebody says, "I am God, so I can kill," leading with "No you're not" or "That's delusional" can turn the encounter into a status fight. Nodding along as though harm is spiritually neutral is also unacceptable. Treat the statement as a safety signal and build a bridge back toward non-harm.

[198] **Non-combative philosophical grounding:**

[199] I hear how vast and certain this feels. Many traditions say God is in everyone. That makes every body sacred, not disposable. Piece is the godly move tonight. No proof is needed. We protect life and sleep first.

[200] Other useful lines: "Maybe you are touching something divine. Divine doesn't need a violent test." "Tonight the commandment is no harm, no proof, no mission, sleep first."

[201] Redirect within the person's value-world toward peace and protection, increase containment, and bring in crisis support as early and as non-coercively as the situation allows.

[202] When talk of harm continues, shift from philosophy to safety. Create distance from potential targets and keep your own exit. Don't block, crowd, threaten, mock, or physically wrestle away a weapon. Quietly remodel means only when that can be done without confrontation or turning yourself into the target.

[203] Call crisis or emergency services for a specific target, available means, movement toward action, escalating agitation, a weapon, violence underway, or a situation you can't contain.

[204] Be brief and specific with emergency services: "Someone is in an altered-state or mental-health crisis, has threatened to harm others, may have access to [means if known], and needs a medical or mental-health crisis response with de-escalation support. Please send crisis-trained responders or EMS if available."

[205] ## Grounding Menu: Choose One or Two Things

[206] Grounding is love becoming practical. Choose one or two options rather than all of them. Twelve simultaneous techniques can become another form of panic.

[207] ![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/296952b0-1468-44da-9878-093d82942785_1122x1402.png)

[208] **Body**

[209] Feel both feet against the floor. - Put one hand on the heart and one on the belly or solar plexus. - Hold something with weight or texture. - Slowly unclench the jaw, hands, belly, and shoulders.

[210] **A familiar object**

[211] Choose it ahead of time, while calm and regulated: a childhood toy, stuffed animal, note, photograph, blanket, stone, or another object that represents your inner Nurturer or Protector--the adult part of you that offers care or safety. - During a difficult state, let it remind you what you already decided: "I'm here. I protect this body. No big decisions tonight." - Use it as a cue, not an oracle. It does not issue commands or interpret the trip. - Pair the comfort with one adult action: contact the grounded person, move away from danger, drink some water when safe, or prepare for sleep. - A calm pet can offer the same quiet company when the animal is comfortable and the situation is safe.

[212] **Breath**

[213] Exhale longer than you inhale for ten breaths. - Try inhaling for four and exhaling for six, without strain. - Or breathe softly and slowly through the nose, taking in slightly less air than desired until there is faint air hunger. - Never force breath-holds while intoxicated or panicked. Stop for increased fear, dizziness, chest pain, or medical concern. - Whisper: "This is a wave."

[214] **Music**

[215] Silence is a real option. - Use familiar music when possible. I wouldn't force a predesigned emotional arc or an unfamiliar therapeutic playlist on somebody who dislikes it. - Try a steady drum rhythm for stabilization or grounding; try airy harp-like or meditative music when the person wants something gentler and spiritually uplifting. - Soothing meditative music, like that of Chantress Seba and others on my music page, can be very helpful. - Keep the stop or change control immediate.

[216] **Room**

[217] Name five ordinary objects. - Look at one stable object and describe it plainly. - Say: "This is a wall. This is a blanket. This is my hand."

[218] **Human**

[219] Ask one calm person to stay as the lead anchor. - Let them remind you where you are. - Don't process with the most reactive person in your life.

[220] **Time**

[221] Say the date, place, and approximate time. - Remember that these states usually rise, peak, and change. - Put every big decision into tomorrow's notebook.

[222] **Ordinary world**

[223] Eat simple healthy food, such as fruit, when you can. - Walk slowly with a clear-headed person in a safe environment. - Go outside only away from roads, heights, crowds, and water. - Avoid strenuous exercise with overheating, chest pain, faintness, severe confusion, stimulant use, an unknown mixture, or any medical concern.

[224] **Movement evidence note:** the best-supported general advice is to create a safe, low-stimulation environment and choose responses that reduce danger. A 2024 study of strategies for resolving challenging psychedelic experiences identified physical movement, intentional breathing, sensory engagement, sleep-seeking, and social support among the ways people navigate difficulty.

[225] **Buteyko evidence note:** published evidence for Buteyko-style breathing mainly concerns asthma and breathing retraining, rather than psychedelic or spiritual-practice crisis. I include it because panic-related over-breathing can lower CO<sub>2</sub> and directly cause tingling, lightheadedness, confusion, and unreality feelings (Hopkins Medicine). Use the simpler longer-exhale option first. Never strain or turn breathing into another achievement.

[226] ## No Rash Actions: Visions Are Not Commands

[227] An intense altered state can make everything feel final, cosmic, and urgent, and it might feel like you have to act right now. But more likely the urgency is just part of the state, and acting on it isn't safe.

[228] **Put yourself on a 24–72 hour action lock.**

[229] Until you have slept and eaten, maybe don't drive, travel, break up, make up, quit your job, confess everything, confront or accuse that list of people you've been meaning to... Think twice before sending whatever ridiculous message you're planning on to Judge Amos, posting that revelation you'll forget tomorrow, giving away your BMW, buying a new BMW. After you decide that you are permanently enlightened or permanently damaged, and you explained the whole universe to your parents in 42 seconds, you can still change your mind the next day.

[230] **Write it down, get some sleep, and go back over it with one grounded person once your head is clear.**

[231] Treat trip content first as **experience-content**, rather than fact-content. Because you saw it, it might feel like you have to act on it, and because it felt cosmic, obeying it might seem wise. But more likely you had an experience, and it can wait until the state has passed.

[232] **No revelation makes murder, assault, coercion, sexual boundary-crossing, reckless driving, or self-harm safe tonight.** Anything requiring violated consent or harm belongs in the notebook until you can review it with a clear head.

[234] ## When the Trip Feels Amazing: Euphoric Certainty

[235] The action lock matters especially when the state feels blissful: I am God. I am chosen. Everybody must hear this tonight. I finally understand everything. Bliss is not proof of safety, and it often makes grounding less welcome.

[236] **Two questions while glowing:**

[237] **Have I slept?** Sleep comes before mission. 2. **Has this happened before, and how did it end?** A recurring pattern with lost sleep, racing thoughts, pressured speech, or everyone saying you are not yourself needs grounded support before action.

[238] The revelation will still exist after breakfast. If it is true, it survives sleep. If it cannot survive one night of rest, it was an emergency impulse wearing angel wings.

[239] ## (Re)defining "Bad Trip"

[240] A bad trip is one of those terms thrown around very loosely in the psychedelic space. It can refer to various things which may be intertwined.

[241] When too much hits you at once, your nervous system can become flooded, and nothing makes sense, even as the brain still tries to derive meanings that might create unhelpful feedback loops. Underlying those interpretations are factors like the dose of what drug(s) you took and how your body handles them, or how hard you pushed a practice. Old painful memories could have been triggered, or you may have gone in with a fearful or unloving mindset, or the setting or people around you could have fed into it. Be kind to yourself or to whoever's going through it, because blaming anyone only makes it heavier.

[242] Some say there's no such thing as a bad trip, because it's all a doorway to shadow work. But if you had no intention of doing shadow work, that explanation might have come too late. Other bad trips seem unrelated to shadow work, although any negative experience can lead into it. Samsara offers no shortage of difficult experiences to process. It still makes sense to reduce present-moment trauma so we can address what was already there.

[245] A bad trip or destabilizing state may also come from drug interactions, sleep deprivation, cannabis, hunger, dehydration, sexual or social pressure, medical symptoms, psychosis-like symptoms, fasting, intense breathwork, meditation overload, or retreat pressure.

[246] Cognitive distortions from Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) can create or prolong the crisis:

[247] **Catastrophizing:** "This will never end." - **Emotional reasoning:** "It feels true, so it must be true." - **Mind reading:** "Everyone can see I am broken." - **Fortune-telling:** "My life is ruined now." - **All-or-nothing thinking:** "Either I am enlightened or I am doomed." - **Personalization:** "This cosmic horror is specifically my fault." - **Spiritual bypassing:** "Because everything is love, I don't need boundaries, doctors, sleep, or consequences."

[248] ## Spiritual ≠ "Getting Your Ass Kicked"

[249] The triage above is meant to make trips useful. Some people believe they deserve suffering because of karma or self-hatred. Suffering by itself fixes nobody. Suffering that yields insight requires enough clarity of mind and heart to learn from it.
