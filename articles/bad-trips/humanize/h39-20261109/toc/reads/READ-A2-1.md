## 2. What gets in the way

**The same question is answered in more than one place**
- Harm: "Risk of Harm: When Peer Support Isn't Enough" (under When to Bring In Outside Help) and "When What They Say Turns Toward Harm" (under For the Person Helping). Same situation, two doors, and the headings don't say how they differ.
- When to get help: "Call medics for:", "Who to Contact, and When", "Risk of Harm...", and later "Bring in mental-health support when:". Four places for "should I call someone". The medical-emergency ones also overlap with the whole of Acute Medical and Neurological Rescue (seizure, chest pain, serotonin syndrome are all call-medics cases).
- DMSO and humic acid: the "Brain Feels Damaged: DMSO + Humic Acid First" heading, the H3 "Humic Acid Brings Brain to Optimal Function" and the H3 "DMSO" (431 words) all appear. I can't tell which holds the dose and caution and which is the short version.
- Charcoal: "Recent Oral Toxic Ingestion", "Actual Activated Charcoal", and "Shungite, Aged Ozonated-Charcoal Water, or Boiled Charcoal Water". Three charcoal places in different branches.
- Certainty: "No Rash Actions: Visions Are Not Commands" and "When the Trip Feels Amazing: Euphoric Certainty" sit under Tripsitting; "Abuse Memories, Recovered Memories, and Certainty" sits under Shadow Work. Same idea (don't act on what feels certain), split across two parents.
- Feeling good: "When the Trip Feels Amazing: Euphoric Certainty" (Tripsitting) and "The Other Bypass: Feeling Good Forever" (Shadow Work) look like the same topic.
- Shadow: "Demons, Jinn, Entities, and Shadow Forces" and "Dealing With Shadow Thoughts" overlap on "something dark is happening to me". For situation 8 I'd have to open both.
- Anchor: "Get One Human Anchor" (the tripper finds a person) and "For the Person Helping: Calm Anchor, Not Guru" (the helper is the anchor). Same word, two roles, and a reader could take either as theirs.
- Calming: the only "calm it down" headings before the greeting are in the remedies part ("Mental Stabilizers..."), while "Psychological First Aid" and "Grounding Menu" do the same job in the Tripsitting part. A person in situation 4 or 6 has two plausible homes.

**Headings that don't say what's under them**
- "Inner Signal App to the Rescue!": I can't tell it's a triage tool or what it triages.
- "When to Bring In Outside Help" (1 word): a parent that is only a doorway to three children. Fine, but it reads as if the real answer is missing.
- "My Acute Rescue Hierarchy of Remedies" and "Acute Medical and Neurological Rescue": both "Acute ... Rescue", so I can't tell which holds the medical emergencies and which holds the substances. The second doesn't say "remedies" are mostly supplements and non-medical tools.
- "Some Remedies Require More Caution": which remedies? Is it a list, a warning or a gate? Reads two ways (cautions about use, or a set of riskier remedies).
- "Gut-Toxin and Parasite-Die-Off Tools": a reader in a bad trip doesn't know why parasites or gut toxins come up. Its child "Zeolite and Ferment in ZHoly3O" is a brand name with no hint what it's for.
- "Metta NMT Sniffer Makes the Trip Chill & Lovey!" and "Syrian Rue Oil Makes the Trip Gentle & Sweet": playful but opaque; I can't tell what they are (a smell, an oil, a tea).
- "Beach Morning Glory--Ipomoea pes-caprae": a plant name only; I wouldn't guess it's a trip-ender without the parent heading.
- "Suspected Serotonin Syndrome (SS)": uses "SS" and its child says "serotonin toxicity". Two names for one thing. The second child ("Do this immediately when serotonin toxicity is reasonably suspected:") is a lead-in sentence, not a heading.
- "Do" under the helper section: with no "Don't" sibling it tells me nothing.
- "Stillness or Movement?": a question; I can't tell if it's advice for the helper or the tripper.
- "Hand on Heart and Gratitude" (with the emoji) and its child "Touch, Consent, and Compassion": the first sounds like a practice, the second like a rule for helpers. Not clear whether it is for the tripper or the sitter.
- "Dealing With Sorrow: Missing Versus Sending Love": missing whom? I can't tell if this is about a lost person, a loved one, or the person's own feelings.
- "The Joker Poem" (493 words) under "Dealing With Shadow Thoughts": a poem's title says nothing about the method it shows.
- "(Re)defining "Bad Trip"" and "Spiritual ≠ "Getting Your Ass Kicked"": framing arguments, not instructions. "The Other Bypass": "other" refers to a bypass I haven't met, and "bypass" is jargon.
- "Bring in mental-health support when:" (38 words): a colon lead-in, the same pattern as "Call medics for:". It reads as the first line of a list, not a section.
- "Tripsitting Yourself or Others": it doesn't say the section is the psychological side. "Tripsitting" is also spelled differently from the title's "Trip-Sitting".

**Possibly in the wrong place, level or parent**
- "If Someone Says "My Brain Feels Damaged": DMSO + Humic Acid First" is first under Acute Medical and Neurological Rescue, ahead of seizure, chest pain and serotonin syndrome, which are far more urgent. Also it opens with a remedy, so it may be a remedy section sitting in the medical one.
- "Active Seizure" is 657 words and sits fourth-ish behind a remedy; for situation 1 that's the first place I look and I'd have to find it among the others.
- "Mental Stabilizers That Don't Abort the Trip" and "When the Goal Is to Stop a Psychedelic Trip" are 0-word parents, with their children carrying everything. "Nausea Smoothers When the Person Wants to Preserve the Trip" has only 23 words and one child, so it's mostly a doorway too.
- "Shadow Work" is an H1 sibling of "Tripsitting Yourself or Others", yet its contents (grief, entities, abuse memories, bad-trip definition) are all psychological, so nothing shows the two sit in the same part. It's also a 0-word H1.
- Within Shadow Work, "Hand on Heart and Gratitude" and "Touch, Consent, and Compassion" read like grounding or helper practice and would fit better beside "Grounding Menu" and "For the Person Helping".
- "(Re)defining "Bad Trip"", "Spiritual ≠ ..." and "The Other Bypass" are at the end of Shadow Work, after the practical headings; I'd expect the definition near the start of the guide.
- "When What They Say Turns Toward Harm" is a child of "For the Person Helping", so it's easy to miss for situation 14.
- "Planning a Trip or Ceremony? Give It a Beginning and an End" is an H1 with no children, at the same level as the entire medical section, and comes after "After the Storm". I can't tell if it belongs with the aftermath (prevention) or stands alone.
- "Trip After Trip? Hang up the phone, get off the Healing Treadmill" sits under "After the Storm" but is about a pattern across ceremonies, not recovery from one.
- "Alone at 3 a.m." is a child of "Get One Human Anchor", so a reader who's alone and has no one may skip past a heading about getting a person.
