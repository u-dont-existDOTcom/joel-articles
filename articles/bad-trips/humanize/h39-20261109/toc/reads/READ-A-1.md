## 2. What gets in the way

**Headings that don't say what's under them, or read two ways**
- `Inner Signal App to the Rescue!`: names a product and doesn't say what it does (a triage tool, a symptom checker, a hotline?). It is also a full top-level section of 68 words that reads like an ad. I'd skim past it in an emergency.
- `When to Bring In Outside Help` (1 word): it's an empty parent, but fine as one. The problem is that the same question, when to get help, is answered in many places (see duplicates).
- `My Acute Rescue Hierarchy of Remedies`: "Acute Rescue" repeats the previous section's "Acute Medical ... Rescue", so I can't tell the two apart. "Hierarchy" doesn't say what is ranked (by strength? by risk? by order of use?). "Remedies" doesn't say these are supplements and substances rather than actions. It's also the only "My" heading in the guide.
- `Some Remedies Require More Caution`: it comes before the remedies it warns about, and "some" doesn't say which. A reader going straight to a remedy won't see the warning.
- `Gut-Toxin and Parasite-Die-Off Tools` (28 words): I can't tell why a gut or parasite section is in a bad-trip guide, or what situation it serves. `Zeolite and Ferment in ZHoly3O` contains a brand or product name I don't know. `Actual Activated Charcoal` ("Actual") only makes sense against some other charcoal, and there are charcoal items in `Shungite, Aged Ozonated-Charcoal Water, or Boiled Charcoal Water` too.
- `Coffee Cherry for Nausea and Brain Recharge`: two jobs in one heading, and the "Brain Recharge" half doesn't belong under nausea.
- `Metta NMT Sniffer Makes the Trip Chill & Lovey!`: playful on purpose, and I'd keep it, but "Metta NMT Sniffer" doesn't say what it is. `Syrian Rue Oil Makes the Trip Gentle & Sweet` is the same kind of heading. Both are fine to find only if you already know what the thing is.
- `Authentic Calming Essential Oils (or a Lime Peel)`: fine, but "Authentic" is an unexplained claim.
- `Ocean Water to calm & protect the energetic field`: unclear whether this is literal (drink, swim) or symbolic.
- `Tripsitting Yourself or Others`: sounds like the whole guide, and the title uses "Trip-Sitting" with a hyphen while this uses "Tripsitting". The heading says nothing about the psychological, non-medical, non-remedy nature of the part.
- `Get One Human Anchor` and `For the Person Helping: Calm Anchor, Not Guru`: two "anchor" headings that sound like the same thing. One is for the person having the trip and one is for the helper.
- `Do` (H3): says nothing. There is no "Don't" next to it, so I can't tell what it is a "do" of.
- `Dealing With Sorrow`, `Dealing With Shadow Thoughts`: "Dealing With" twice, and neither says which side of the trip it is for. `Dealing With Sorrow: Missing Versus Sending Love` is hard to parse.
- `The Joker Poem` (493 words, H3): a poem is hiding in the middle of a section, and the heading doesn't say what it does for the reader.
- `(Re)defining "Bad Trip"`: a concept heading in the middle of a list of practical sections. It reads like something I should have read first.
- `Spiritual ≠ "Getting Your Ass Kicked"` and `The Other Bypass: Feeling Good Forever`: "The Other Bypass" depends on a first bypass I haven't seen. Another heading probably names it, but none says "bypass".
- `Bring in mental-health support when:` (38 words): a bare fragment with a colon, same level as 600-word sections.
- `Hang up the phone, get off the Healing Treadmill`: playful on purpose, and I'd keep it, but "hang up the phone" could mean quit calling a hotline, a friend, a guru, or a coach.
- `Give the Session a Beginning and an End`: "session" is never defined in the contents, and the heading could be about preparing, running, or closing.

**Sections that look like they are in the wrong place, level, or parent**
- `Give the Session a Beginning and an End` is a top-level section after `After the Storm`. If it's about preparing or framing a ceremony, it belongs before the trip. If it's closing the session, it could be under `Tripsitting Yourself or Others` or `After the Storm`. Either way, a H1 at the end next to the Appendix looks tacked on.
- `Shadow Work` is a sibling of `Tripsitting Yourself or Others`, but both are the psychological side. The first-aid, helper and shadow sections run together, and `Shadow Work` is 0 words under its H1 with nine children.
- `When the Content Turns Toward Harm` sits under `For the Person Helping`, but harm is an emergency question and also a shadow-content question. It is in neither of those places, and the emergency and outside-help parts are separate.
- `Touch, Consent, and Compassion` is under `Hand on Heart and Gratitude`. Touch and consent are about the helper's conduct, so they would fit better under `For the Person Helping`.
- `When the Trip Feels Amazing: Euphoric Certainty` (under first aid) and `The Other Bypass: Feeling Good Forever` (under Shadow Work) are close to the same subject in two different H1s. `No Rash Actions: Visions Are Not Commands` sits between them and is also about certainty.
- `Alone at 3 a.m.` is a child of `Get One Human Anchor`. It is the answer to a different situation (nobody is there), so it is hidden one level down, and the parent is about getting a person.
- `Suspected Serotonin Syndrome (SS)` has 51 words and its two H3s have the content, while `Active Seizure` is 657 words at the same H2 level. Sizes differ by a factor of 13 among siblings; that's fine, but the heading `Do this immediately when serotonin toxicity is reasonably suspected:` is a whole sentence used as a heading.
- `Bring in mental-health support when:` is a tiny stub between two large sections, in `After the Storm`.

**Things that look like they're in two places**
- When to get help: `When to Bring In Outside Help` (`Call medics for:`, `When Peer Support Isn't Enough`, `Who to Contact, and When`), the medical section, `When the Content Turns Toward Harm`, and `Bring in mental-health support when:`. That is five or six places for one question.
- DMSO and humic acid: `If Someone Says "My Brain Feels Damaged": DMSO + Humic Acid First` in the medical part, and `Humic Acid Brings Brain to Optimal Function` and `DMSO` (431 words) in the remedies part.
- Seizure: `Active Seizure` and `Call medics for:` (likely lists seizure).
- Charcoal: `Actual Activated Charcoal` and `Shungite, Aged Ozonated-Charcoal Water, or Boiled Charcoal Water`.
- Anchor: `Get One Human Anchor` and `For the Person Helping: Calm Anchor, Not Guru`.
- Certainty/bypass: the three sections named above.
- "Trip" definition: `(Re)defining "Bad Trip"` and `Spiritual ≠ "Getting Your Ass Kicked"` look like they argue the same point.
