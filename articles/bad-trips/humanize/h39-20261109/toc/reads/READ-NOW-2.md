## 2. What gets in the way

### Headings that don't say what's under them, or read two ways

- `My Acute Rescue Hierarchy` comes straight after `Acute Medical and Neurological Rescue`, and both say "acute rescue". From the heading I'd expect a ranked emergency drill, more of the medical part. Only the headings under it show that it's the remedies, and the word "remedies" first appears in its last section, `Some Remedies Require More Caution`.
- `Some Remedies Require More Caution` reads two ways: warnings about the remedies just listed, or a further set of riskier remedies. It matters because it comes after all of them. If it's warnings, a reader who has already picked a remedy never gets there.
- `If Someone Says "My Brain Feels Damaged": DMSO + Humic Acid First`: "First" can mean "try these first for this complaint" or, since this is the medical part's first section, "before anything else". Someone whose friend is seizing reads "DMSO + Humic Acid First" before reaching `Active Seizure`.
- `Inner Signal App to the Rescue!` doesn't say what the app does, or whether it's the triage web app linked just above it. If it is, the heading hides that; if it isn't, a reader meets two apps before any advice and can't tell which to open. It's also a top-level heading, level with the guide's main parts, for 68 words.
- `Give the Session a Beginning and an End`: which session, the trip, the night of sitting, or a healing session later on? Between the treadmill and sorrow, I can't tell whether it's advice for before a ceremony (where I tried it for situation 12) or something to do tonight.
- `The Other Bypass: Feeling Good Forever`: "other" points back to a first bypass that no heading names, and "bypass" (spiritual bypassing) is a word many readers won't know. "Feeling Good Forever" carries it, but only while it stays right after `Spiritual ≠ "Getting Your Ass Kicked"`.
- Smaller ones. `Suspected Serotonin Syndrome (SS)` becomes "serotonin toxicity" in both its subheadings, so a reader may wonder whether there are two conditions. `Do` stands alone, and a reader looks for the "Don't" that usually comes with it. In `Gut-Toxin and Parasite-Die-Off Tools`, "die-off" is jargon, and the contents don't show why gut tools head the remedies for a bad trip. "ZHoly3O" in `Zeolite and Ferment in **ZHoly3O**` doesn't say what it is (its parent does), and it and `**DMSO**` are the only headings with bold markup in them.

### In the wrong place, at the wrong level, or under the wrong parent

- `How I label the evidence` (85 words) is the parent of all five remedy groups: `Gut-Toxin and Parasite-Die-Off Tools`, `Nausea Smoothers When the Person Wants to Preserve the Trip`, `When the Goal Is to Stop a Psychedelic Trip`, `Mental Stabilizers That Don't Abort the Trip` and `A Final Trip Killer`. They aren't part of the evidence key; they are the remedies. In the contents, about 2,000 words of remedies look like a footnote to an 85-word key.
- `A Final Trip Killer` is cut off from `When the Goal Is to Stop a Psychedelic Trip` by the whole `Mental Stabilizers That Don't Abort the Trip` group, so a "don't abort" group sits between two "stop" groups. For situation 7 I'd have read Beach Morning Glory and stopped; niacin is eight headings further down, under another parent.
- `When the Content Turns Toward Harm` looks like the guide's only place for someone who might hurt themselves or others, and it's a third-level heading inside the helper section, about 6,800 words below `When to Bring In Outside Help`, which is where I started for situation 14. "Content" also leaves it open whether it's about dark imagery or real danger.
- `If Someone Says "My Brain Feels Damaged": DMSO + Humic Acid First` leads the medical part, ahead of seizure, chest pain, poisoning and serotonin syndrome.
- The psychological part is 17 sections at one level, under a heading that names two things (`Shadow Work and Tripsitting Yourself or Others`). It runs: what to do now (`Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes` to `When the Trip Feels Amazing: Euphoric Certainty`), then five reflective pieces (`(Re)defining "Bad Trip"` to `Give the Session a Beginning and an End`, about 1,750 words), then the specific hard content (sorrow, demons, abuse memories, shadow thoughts), then the aftermath. Someone mid-trip with "something evil" attacking has to get past the reflection to reach `Demons, Jinn, Entities, and Shadow Forces`.

- `Touch, Consent, and Compassion`: a helper wondering whether to hold their friend's hand (situation 16) looks under `For the Person Helping: Calm Anchor, Not Guru`, not under `Hand on Heart and Gratitude 🫶🏿`. I've left it where it is, because it reads as the consent note for that practice.
