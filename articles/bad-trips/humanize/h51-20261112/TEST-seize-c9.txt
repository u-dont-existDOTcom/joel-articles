Active Seizure

Time the seizure, and while it lasts:
- Move any dangerous objects away from them, especially anything hard or sharp.
- Don't restrain them, since that can cause injuries, but do put pillows where appropriate, especially under the head.
- Loosen any tight clothing.
- Turn them gently onto their side, which helps them breathe.
- Keep everything out of their mouth while they're convulsing or not fully conscious.

Call medics if any of these happen:
- They have a seizure after taking too much, or you don't know how much they took.
- A seizure on a normal dose goes on for over 5 minutes.
- Their breathing gets slow and shallow.
- Their lips turn blue or gray.
- Their forehead feels very hot (not just sweaty).
- They injure their head, break a bone, or bleeding won't stop.

Waiting the 5 minutes is only for a seizure on a normal dose. Psychedelics can lower the seizure threshold even then, so someone with epilepsy or who's otherwise prone to seizures might have a seizure, and it's most likely less dangerous than an actual drug overdose seizure.

If they have a rescue medicine for epilepsy seizures with them, give it as the label instructs. These medicines are benzodiazepines in nasal spray or rectal gel, or sometimes an intramuscular shot (pills can't be swallowed). After giving it, keep a close eye on their breathing, since a benzodiazepine adds to the risk of it getting slow and shallow, especially if they've also taken other respiratory depressants like opioids or alcohol.
