## Part 1. His beliefs as the guide states them

### B1. Love cancels fear; fighting an entity feeds it; love removes its hooks
Section "Demons, Jinn, Entities, and Shadow Forces":
- "I have faced demons and jinn during psychedelic and exorcism-type situations. The most important thing I discovered was that fighting them did not help. Sending love did."
- "In one especially dramatic situation involving another person, the entity seemed to attack both of us psychically. I sent it love, and within a few seconds it left. The other person could not do that immediately. I had to show her, and it took hours of practice before it worked because she had never gone deeply into "Love Thy Enemy.""
- "Hatred, fear, shame, pride, and rage make hooks. Love removes hooks."
- "The common mistake is fighting the terrifying thing with the energy it already consumes. Panic fights panic, hatred fights hatred, pride fights pride, and suddenly a small war is running inside consciousness."
- "Love is a better move, although it is difficult advice. Almost nobody can authentically love what terrifies them while they are terrified. So train before you need it, sending love to a frightening image, to someone you hate (without approving of what they did), to your shame and your body, to sickness and aging and fear and death, and to the inner child. Practice being the space rather than the storm."
- "It might look passive, but this is how you become unhookable."

Section "For the Person Helping: Calm Anchor, Not Guru", under "Don't" (the line he says he had to change):
- "If they're being attacked by a demon, let your care help them connect to love, which cancels fear. Hopefully they've been practicing that prior."

### B2. Agnostic about whether entities are real
"Demons, Jinn, Entities, and Shadow Forces": "I don't have to settle whether these things are objectively real or internal formations because the response is mostly the same."

### B3. Stabilize the body first, then love
"Demons, Jinn, Entities, and Shadow Forces": "When somebody is medically, physically, or psychologically too overwhelmed to reach love, stabilize first. Don't teach nondual demonology to somebody who needs the lights down and a doctor."
"Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes": "A useful order is **room → body → human → time → meaning**. Most people begin with meaning because the experience feels cosmic. When the nervous system is on fire, love becomes practical first."

### B4. Missing hurts, sending love feels good; send love where love is missing
Section "Dealing With Sorrow: Missing Versus Sending Love":
- "I now think focusing on love is more helpful than focusing on missing somebody or something."
- "*Missing hurts. Sending love feels good.*"
- "This sounds almost embarrassingly obvious, but it took me months of daily loveyhuasca after my ex-wife Bee left to understand it."
- "Missing someone is a contraction. There's hunger and ache in it, it keeps circling back to you, and there's the painful fantasy that loving them means needing them to come back. Sending love is different. Sending love is pleasure in itself, even when the person is gone. Love can bless without grabbing."
- "But the deepest utility is sending love to the places where love is missing."
- "Send love to the part of you still waiting for them." / "Send love to the inner child who believed somebody else had to rescue him." / "Send love to the ashamed, angry, lonely, terrified, and exiled parts."

### B5. Sending love to the inner child, with a hand on the heart; love as a signal to the body
Section "Hand on Heart and Gratitude":
- "A hand on the heart can change the direction of a trip, especially when the problem is emotional contraction, grief, fear, or loneliness. There's no need to manufacture loving thoughts, you're just giving the body one simple signal: love is here."
- "Put one hand on the heart and the other on the solar plexus just below it, sending love to the inner child. Sometimes that shifts the whole field. Once love reaches the inner child, send it toward all beings suffering now."
- "Recognizing that you are not the only one suffering can open awareness in a liberating way, with real heart magic."
- "Gratitude comes like icing on the cake. ... Never force gratitude. Let it bubble up gently."

### B6. Self-talk that loves the suffering part
"For the Person Helping": "Stay present. If it helps, you can say to yourself: *This feeling is allowed to move through. I don't have to become it. I can love the part of me that feels it.* Or simply: *Can I send love to the one who's suffering?*"
Same section, opening: "Crying, grieving, remembering, or touching a genuine wound calls for loving awareness."

### B7. Inner-child reparenting is the core of shadow work; negative mind-states are young parts, not philosophical errors
Section "The Other Bypass: Feeling Good Forever":
- "Modern people carry so much trauma--and some people did even in Buddha's time--that shadow work and inner-child reparenting may need to come first, even before authentic liberation from the identity illusion."
- "Shadow work to me is neither BDSM nor "feeding your demons." It means bringing loving awareness to the parts of the mind built in fear, pain, humiliation, rejection, and survival. Inner-child reparenting is central. Most negative mind-states aren't some error in your philosophy, they're just young parts of you that are still waiting for love and protection and permission, and for the explanation nobody ever gave them."
- "When shadow work is bypassed in meditation and therapy, it may appear in ordinary life without explaining itself. I encountered a lot of it in my first two marriages, to my bewilderment."
- "My friend Eshwar, however, found that shadow work made up about 99% of his path to heart-chakra awakening."
Section "Grounding Menu", "A familiar object": "an object that represents your inner Nurturer or Protector--the adult part of you that offers care or safety."

### B8. Bypassing runs both ways; practice versus bypassing
- "Spiritual ≠ 'Getting Your Ass Kicked'": "Suffering by itself fixes nobody. Suffering that yields insight requires enough clarity of mind and heart to learn from it." / "Practice says love is here, so we make the room safer, call for help when needed, stop pretending, and hold the experience without becoming it."
- "The Other Bypass": "A well-rounded trip should offer practical lessons and contact with suffering as well as freedom from suffering, lest we become "so spiritually high that we are no earthly good.""
- "After the Storm": "It can feel like the more cosmic the story sounds, the bigger the insight. But the fruit of an insight is whether suffering goes down and love gets more available."

### B9. A gut- or body-driven mental hell is mistaken for shadow work; the body is part of the spiritual field
Section "My Acute Rescue Hierarchy":
- "Sometimes the mind isn't in a metaphysical crisis. The body feels awful and the altered mind writes cosmic horror to explain it. Nausea, heat, exhaustion, dehydration, overstimulation, and sleep loss all become spiritual content very quickly."
- "A horrible thought isn't automatically a revelation. The body may need quiet, water, cooling, warmth, rest, or a calmer room. The body is part of the spiritual field."
- "A gut-driven mental hell is often mistaken as shadow work."
- Under "Gut-Toxin and Parasite-Die-Off Tools": "These are the first things I would use, even preventively on the come-up, because I want to address possible toxins before assigning the distress to something more mysterious."
- Under "Zeolite and Ferment": "When the trip feels like sick stomach, nausea, diarrhea, toxic heaviness, a dirty body-feeling, parasite die-off, or mental hell driven by gut hell, zeolite and the ZHoly3O ferment are often my first thoughts."

### B11. Distrust of the emergency room as the default
"Acute Medical and Neurological Rescue": "The emergency room is not magic, and for a physiologically stable psychedelic or practice-induced crisis it may be a poor environment."

### B12. Healing treadmill: integrate rather than re-dose; MDMA gives access to reparenting people are unprepared for
Section "Hang up the phone, get off the Healing Treadmill":
- "But what we shouldn't be doing is feeling like we need to use these substances *because we can't integrate them.*"
- "Once I learned to meditate into the experience (and beyond) without ayahuasca, that compulsion was gone."
- "With healthy drugs like Loveyhuasca, which has literally saved my life on numerous occasions from unknown infections ..."
- "My perspective is that these people are not correctly preparing for the inner child reparenting that MDMA suddenly provides experiential access to."
- "On the other hand, without MDMA or loveyhuasca, many people are unable to really do inner child therapy, because it sounds fake to them, just as ego death sounded fake to me before I experienced it. So I think these approaches complement each other greatly."
- "With toxic drugs like MDMA & ketamine, that can be a recipe for long-term harm to your physical, and/or mental health."

### B13. Preparing by purifying and opening to love can make the experience unnecessary; accepting a feeling lets it leave
"Give the Session a Beginning and an End": "Sometimes people find that when they prepare to purify themselves and open up to love, they don't even end up needing the experience itself. The work is already done." and the Kenya woman: "So instead, she let the anxiety come, and accepted the feeling until it left. Her addictions vanished, no shroom trip required."
"(Re)defining "Bad Trip"": "you may have gone in with a fearful or unloving mindset"; "Be kind to yourself or to whoever's going through it, because blaming anyone only makes it heavier."

### B14. Shadow thoughts co-arise; do not attach, suppress or confess; opposites in the mind
"Dealing With Shadow Thoughts":
- "Shadow thoughts and feelings are not inherently a problem. They co-arise naturally and do not need to be attached to, like shadows cast by objects in sunlight. I also notice them after excessive focus. The mind becomes stressed and seeks an exit from the pressure, often in the opposite direction from the focus."
- "Relax, unwind, unbind, and unweave the identity illusion, mind, and unified experience."
- "I am not repeating the simplistic Hermetic claim that opposites are required to understand anything. A hated baby does **not** feel love, and a loved baby does **not** feel hate."
- "Notice: **"A thought is here. A feeling is here. It is intense, but it is not the whole of me."** Then return to room, body, human, time, and love."

### B15. The helper is not a guru, but is a source of loving presence
"For the Person Helping": the heading ("Calm Anchor, Not Guru"), "As the person helping, you want to play the role of the calm, caring anchor to the safe present moment, not the witch doctor guru who knows magic spells." and the Zendo summary "sitting with the person rather than guiding them, talking them through it rather than down from it, and remembering that difficult isn't necessarily bad." Joel says the guide's guru line needed his change; in the demon section he writes "I had to show her", so he did teach.

### B16. Open-ended "no": experience-content vs fact-content; a recovered memory may be true "psychologically, or symbolically, or literally"
"No Rash Actions": "Treat trip content first as **experience-content**, rather than fact-content." "Abuse Memories, Recovered Memories, and Certainty": "The experience may matter deeply. It could be true psychologically, or symbolically, or literally, or only partly true, or false in the form it first took." (This one is close to standard advice; listed for completeness, not counted below.)

## Places where a belief would change what the reader or helper does

**P1. "Get One Human Anchor": the script for the sitter**
- Generic line: "I'm here. You're not alone. This is a wave. We don't have to solve your whole life right now. Let's make your body safe, keep things simple, and stay together."
- Belief (B1): "let your care help them connect to love, which cancels fear."
- Change: the sitter's job grows from reassuring and making the body safe to being the source of love the person can connect to, which is what cancels the fear in his account.

**P2. "Alone at 3 a.m."**
- Generic lines: "Once the lamp is on, the keys are away, and "No big actions until morning" is visible, use the phone only to reach a calm person or support service." and "When the body still needs movement, keep it contained and boring: brush your teeth, stretch gently, take a calm shower, or do a few slow wall push-ups. Then protect sleep."
- Beliefs (B1, B5, B6): "Practice being the space rather than the storm."; "Put one hand on the heart and the other on the solar plexus just below it, sending love to the inner child."; "Can I send love to the one who's suffering?"
- Change: with no sitter at all, the person's own action while frightened is the plan; the generic list has them wait it out, and his belief gives them something to do with the fear itself (send love to it, hand on heart). This is the "tripsitting yourself" half of the section's title.

**P3. "For the Person Helping", Do list**
- Generic lines: "Reflect without arguing: "That sounds terrifying." "I'm with you." "Let's breathe together."" and "Translate metaphysics into shared safety values: "This feels sacred and huge. Sacred power protects life tonight.""
- Belief (B1, B5): "let your care help them connect to love, which cancels fear."; "Once love reaches the inner child, send it toward all beings suffering now."
- Change: the helper's tools when the content is frightening entities are only to mirror and to reframe values; his belief adds that the helper models love (and can lead the person to send it) rather than only reflecting the terror back.

**P4. "For the Person Helping", Don't: the demon sentence and the guru line**
- Generic lines: "As the person helping, you want to play the role of the calm, caring anchor to the safe present moment, not the witch doctor guru who knows magic spells." and "So don't try to win an argument with their visions, the entities they spoke to, the memories that surfaced, or whatever cosmic conclusions they reached ten minutes ago..."
- Beliefs (B1, B15): "The most important thing I discovered was that fighting them did not help. Sending love did." and "I had to show her, and it took hours of practice before it worked because she had never gone deeply into "Love Thy Enemy.""
- Change: the helper's job when a demon attacks. The guide's frame ("anchor, not guru", "don't argue") is a list of things not to do, and the single belief sentence ("let your care help them connect to love, which cancels fear") sits inside it as an aside, with "Hopefully they've been practicing that prior." In his own account the helper does model love and teach it, over hours if needed, so the helper's role is active, not just the absence of the guru. This is the place Joel named.

**P5. "Grounding Menu", Body: the hand on the heart**
- Generic line: "Put one hand on the heart and one on the belly or solar plexus."
- Belief (B5): "Put one hand on the heart and the other on the solar plexus just below it, sending love to the inner child. Sometimes that shifts the whole field."
- Change: the reader does the same posture but with a purpose, sending love to the inner child; the menu lists it as one body cue among four, with the same level of importance as "Hold something with weight or texture."

**P6. "Grounding Menu", Breath: the whispered phrase**
- Generic line: "Whisper: "This is a wave.""
- Belief (B6): "I can love the part of me that feels it." / "Can I send love to the one who's suffering?"
- Change: the self-talk during the breathing is passing-it-out only; his belief gives self-talk that turns toward the frightened part, not just toward waiting.

**P7. "(Re)defining 'Bad Trip'", the CBT distortions list**
- Generic lines: "Cognitive distortions from Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) can create or prolong the crisis:" with "**Catastrophizing:** "This will never end."", "**Mind reading:** "Everyone can see I am broken."", "**Personalization:** "This cosmic horror is specifically my fault.""
- Belief (B7): "Most negative mind-states aren't some error in your philosophy, they're just young parts of you that are still waiting for love and protection and permission, and for the explanation nobody ever gave them."
- Change: the list treats these thoughts as thinking errors to be corrected; his belief says they are young parts to be loved, which changes what the reader does with each thought (reparent, not reality-test). The two framings are not the same advice.

**P8. "Give the Session a Beginning and an End": preparation**
- Generic lines: "Set up the space you'll be in, and decide ahead of time what would make you call for outside help." and "Go in curious about what might come up, along with gentle intentions, not harboring demands for revelations, buried memories, good cries or spiritual breakthroughs."

The planning list prepares space, sitter, dose, and exit rules, but leaves out the practice his demon account depends on, training love-sending beforehand so it is available when fear arrives. **P9. "The Fear/Panic Loop" (and the ladder in "Psychological First Aid")**

**P10. "Abuse Memories, Recovered Memories, and Certainty"**

- Beliefs (B4, B7, B12): "Send love to the inner child who believed somebody else had to rescue him." and "My perspective is that these people are not correctly preparing for the inner child reparenting that MDMA suddenly provides experiential access to."
- Change: what to do with the memory after sleep. The generic step is to hand it to a professional or a grounded person; his belief adds that the child who holds the memory is to be reparented and sent love by the person, not only discussed.

**P11. "Dealing With Shadow Thoughts": the in-trip move**
- Generic line: "During a trip, don't identify with every shadow thought, suppress it, confess it to everybody, or convert it into a permanent diagnosis. Notice: **"A thought is here. A feeling is here. It is intense, but it is not the whole of me."**"
- Belief (B4, B7): "Shadow work to me is neither BDSM nor "feeding your demons." It means bringing loving awareness to the parts of the mind built in fear, pain, humiliation, rejection, and survival." and "Send love to the ashamed, angry, lonely, terrified, and exiled parts."
- Change: noticing and not identifying is the standard mindfulness move; his shadow-work belief says the thought is a part to be met with loving awareness, which is what the reader would do with it once noticed.

**P12. "After the Storm" / aftermath: Anxiety and fear**
- Generic line: "**Anxiety and fear:** grounding, routine, reduced stimulation, CBT-style reality testing, and therapy when it persists."
- Beliefs (B4, B7, B13): "Send love to the part of you still waiting for them."; "Most negative mind-states aren't some error in your philosophy ..."; "So instead, she let the anxiety come, and accepted the feeling until it left."
- Change: what to do in the days after. Reality-testing and routine are replaced or joined by sending love to the frightened part and accepting the feeling until it leaves.

**P13. "After the Storm" / aftermath: Social disconnection and shame**
- Generic line: "**Social disconnection and shame:** one or two safe humans, peer support, and no social-media crowdsourcing while raw."
- Belief (B4, B7): "Send love to the ashamed, angry, lonely, terrified, and exiled parts." and "Send love to your shame and your body."
- Change: shame after a trip is a part to send love to, not only something to be eased by company.

**P14. "After the Storm" / aftermath: Sleep disturbance and nightmares**
- Generic line: "**Sleep disturbance and nightmares:** sleep hygiene, no more substances or intense practice, and professional help when severe or prolonged."
- Belief (B1): "sending love to a frightening image" and "Love removes hooks."
- Change: for nightmares that return after a demon or frightening-image encounter, the reader's step becomes sending love to that image while awake; sleep hygiene stays.

**P15. "After the Storm": the review questions**
- Generic line: "Then ask what actually happened. What was medical or environmental? What was emotional? What was old trauma? What was fantasy? What was useful? What changed behavior? What needs therapy, an apology, rest, or plain practical action?"
- Belief (B9): "A gut-driven mental hell is often mistaken as shadow work." and "I want to address possible toxins before assigning the distress to something more mysterious."
- Change: the review has "medical or environmental" but no question about the gut or toxins, so the reader could still take a gut-driven hell for old trauma or shadow work, which is the mistake he names. Only partly generic, because "medical" is there.

Considered and not counted: the generic "Euphoric Certainty" and "No Rash Actions" sections (his B16 matches standard advice); "When the Content Turns Toward Harm" (see item 8 above); the Touch, Consent, and Compassion subsection; the hotline lists; the appendix.

## Summary

Sections that carry his beliefs: "My Acute Rescue Hierarchy" (B9, B10), "Spiritual ≠ Getting Your Ass Kicked", "The Other Bypass", "Hang up the phone, get off the Healing Treadmill", "Dealing With Sorrow", "Hand on Heart and Gratitude", "Demons, Jinn, Entities, and Shadow Forces", and parts of the rescue remedies in "Acute Medical and Neurological Rescue". Generic or mostly generic: "When to Bring In Outside Help", "Psychological First Aid", "Get One Human Anchor" and its contacts, the "Grounding Menu", "No Rash Actions", "When the Trip Feels Amazing", "The Fear/Panic Loop", "Abuse Memories", "After the Storm" and the appendix. Mixed: "For the Person Helping", "(Re)defining Bad Trip", "Give the Session a Beginning and an End", "Dealing With Shadow Thoughts". The beliefs are concentrated in the middle of the guide (demons, sorrow, hand on heart, bypasses) and in the remedies, while the sections people read when someone is actually in trouble (helper, scripts, fear loop, grounding, aftermath) are the generic ones, so the guide's love-based method (B1, B4, B5) has no presence at the moments it would be used.

15 places in all. The five that would change the guide most:
1. **P4**, the helper's Don't paragraph: the helper's job when a demon attacks (model love, as he did and taught) in place of "anchor, not guru" as a list of don'ts.
2. **P9**, the Fear/Panic Loop: the self-talk when fear loops, and the ladder that omits love.
3. **P8**, preparation in "Give the Session a Beginning and an End": training love-sending before the session, which his own account says is the condition for it working.
4. **P2**, "Alone at 3 a.m.": what a person with no sitter does with the fear.
5. **P12**, the aftermath row for anxiety and fear (with P13 to P15 beside it): what to do in the days after.
Runner-up: P7, the CBT distortions list, which treats as errors what he treats as young parts.
