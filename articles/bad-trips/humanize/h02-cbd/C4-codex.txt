I would also use topical magnesium chloride and consider topical DMSO with a known CBD preparation as adjuncts. If the seizure keeps going, don't wait for DMSO. I think DMSO could help as an experimental extra. It has several ways of protecting nerve cells and reduced seizure activity in a rat temporal-lobe epilepsy model, though some doses made seizure activity worse in an absence-epilepsy model. Purified oral CBD is approved for the seizures of Lennox-Gastaut and Dravet syndromes. That approval covers tuberous sclerosis complex too. For focal seizures outside those syndromes, the evidence goes both ways, and the CBD studies here didn't test it as a fast rescue during a seizure. People in a 2025 study had about half to three quarters fewer focal seizures over nearly three years on high-dose oral CBD. They were taking around 23 mg/kg a day, about 1,400 mg for a 60 kg adult, and there was no placebo group to compare them with. In the 12-week trial, a CBD skin gel reached blood levels similar to oral CBD's and still did no better than placebo.
