Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes

The triage app gives you the order to do things in, and this section is about doing them in a way that takes pressure off the person.

Anything that could let someone hurt themselves in the next five minutes takes priority, so get the car keys out of reach and turn the stove off, and move them away from roads and balconies, or pools of water for that matter. Letting them move around is fine, and pacing can help, but keep them from taking more of any substance. If they want to go confront somebody, that can wait. And if they want to leave, have someone go with them. Put any weapons away and keep the person away from crowds and strangers. If they want to post their big revelation about the universe, or message an ex, take the phone.

They're feeling overwhelmed, so dim the lights, and clear out the extra people around them.

Then remind them where they are and what's happening, since a trip can make them lose track of both:

You're in [place], and it's [date/time]. You took [substance, if known], and the effects will peak and then ease off. It's intense right now, but it'll pass, and we can sort out the rest of your life tomorrow.

If a practice like meditation or fasting brought this on, have them stop now, since doing more of it only makes this stronger. Nobody can say how long it'll last, with no drug wearing off on a schedule. So look after their body in the meantime: once they're alert and not throwing up, food and warmth help it calm down, especially after fasting.
