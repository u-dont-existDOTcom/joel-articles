If there's a rescue medicine for seizures on hand, like one they carry for epilepsy, follow its label for when and how to give it. It's a benzodiazepine, which is what doctors reach for first when a seizure won't stop. Because a pill is no use while someone's seizing, it comes as a nasal spray or a rectal gel, or sometimes an intramuscular shot. After giving it, keep watching their breathing, since a benzodiazepine adds to the risk of it getting slow and shallow, especially if they've also taken respiratory depressants like opioids or alcohol.
