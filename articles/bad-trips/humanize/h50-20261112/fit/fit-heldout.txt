The text below is from a guide for people helping a friend through a bad trip or a drug emergency. The friend may be on a
psychedelic or another drug and may have taken too much; the helper is a friend with no medical training.

THE TEXT:
S1: Call medics if any of these happen:
S2: - Their pupils are very dilated.
S3: - They can't be woken up.
S4: - Their heart is pounding fast.
S5: - They're seeing things that aren't there.
S6: - Their temperature keeps climbing even after you cool them.
S7: - They can't say what year it is or who the president is.
S8: - Check whether their pulse is irregular.
S9: - Their lips turn blue or gray.
S10: - They vomit more than twice.
S11: - They have trouble standing up straight.
S12: - One side of their face droops when they smile.

For each line that tells the helper what to look for or what to do, ask three questions:

- ANYWAY: Would this already be true of someone tripping or overdosing when nothing is going wrong beyond the drug
  itself (their senses, their thinking, their mood, their body)? Then, as written, it can't tell the helper anything.
- TELL: Can a friend with no training actually see, feel or find this out, right there and then? If not, what couldn't
  they tell?
- FROM: Is this a rule or a sign from another setting (a general health manual, a stroke or epilepsy clinic, a hospital
  ward) that doesn't hold for someone tripping or overdosing? Which setting?

Answer in this shape, listing only the lines with a "yes" to at least one question:

- S3: ANYWAY yes, [one line why] / TELL - / FROM -
- (or "none")
