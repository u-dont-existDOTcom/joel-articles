"Overdose" is too vague to choose a remedy. Figure out how much of what was swallowed and how long ago, then see what the body is doing: are they suffering from [slow and shallow breathing](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/call-medics-for) (respiratory depression, from opioids, benzos and alcohol)? Or is it rapid breathing, heart rate, and agitation, signs of stimulant toxicity? Do you see symptoms of [serotonin toxicity](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/how-to-check-for-ss), like seizure, dangerous overheating, tremor? Do they have vomiting or diarrhea beyond what they can handle? Some of these symptoms are also normal, like vomiting and diarrhea, which are helpful in undoing an overdose, up to a point. Slow breathing is normal with iboga, but if cardiac irregularities come along with it, that's an extra danger sign.

[Activated charcoal](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/activated-charcoal) is safe and can help with many drug overdoses (and toxins in the gut). It only helps with what binds to it, which leaves out alcohol and lithium among other things, and it can also adsorb medication they need. [The usual advice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6620762/) says it's only useful within an hour of the drug overdose, but that's not true for many plant medicines which can take hours to digest. Obviously don't give anything by mouth to someone who can't swallow, as they could aspirate it into their lungs and die. And don't give any fine powders to anyone directly, mix in water first to prevent choking.

If you're unsure about the interactions between the drugs taken, it could be helpful to check with AI & Poison Control. They might have important info, especially if the drugs are well-known. But I wouldn't ask them about charcoal: someone I know did, and they said they aren't allowed to tell people to take it ([their line](https://www.poison.org/articles/2017-jun/activated-charcoal) is that it's not for home use), which left them very confused. They can also help you with standard first aid instructions.
