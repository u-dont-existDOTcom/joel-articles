THE TEXT:
S1: "Overdose" is too vague to choose a remedy.
S2: Figure out how much of what was swallowed and how long ago, then see what the body is doing: are they suffering from [slow and shallow breathing](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/call-medics-for) (respiratory depression, from opioids, benzos and alcohol)?
S3: Or is it rapid breathing, heart rate, and agitation, signs of stimulant toxicity?
S4: Do you see symptoms of [serotonin toxicity](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/how-to-check-for-ss), like seizure, dangerous overheating, tremor?
S5: Do they have vomiting or diarrhea beyond what they can handle?
S6: Some of these symptoms are also normal, like vomiting and diarrhea, which are helpful in undoing an overdose, up to a point.
S7: Slow breathing is normal with iboga, but if cardiac irregularities come along with it, that's an extra danger sign.
S8: [Activated charcoal](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/activated-charcoal) is safe and can help with many drug overdoses (and toxins in the gut).
S9: It only helps with what binds to it, which leaves out alcohol and lithium among other things, and it can also adsorb medication they need.
S10: [The usual advice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6620762/) says it's only useful within an hour of the drug overdose, but that's not true for many plant medicines which can take hours to digest.
S11: Obviously don't give anything by mouth to someone who can't swallow, as they could aspirate it into their lungs and die.
S12: And don't give any fine powders to anyone directly, mix in water first to prevent choking.
S13: If you're unsure about the interactions between the drugs taken, it could be helpful to check with AI & Poison Control.
S14: They might have important info, especially if the drugs are well-known.
S15: But I wouldn't ask them about charcoal: someone I know did, and they said they aren't allowed to tell people to take it ([their line](https://www.poison.org/articles/2017-jun/activated-charcoal) is that it's not for home use), which left them very confused.
S16: They can also help you with standard first aid instructions.
