NEEDS:
S1: Tells the reader the list is for acute trouble and emergencies, not ordinary discomfort, so they know when to reach for it.
S2: Tells them to rehearse the remedies in low doses beforehand; useful advice, but it says how to prepare, not what the list is for.
S3: Opens the distinction: a crisis is not always a metaphysical or emotional one.
S4: Shows the mechanism: a bad body state gets turned into cosmic horror by the altered mind, so the horror may be a symptom.
S5: Gives concrete body causes (nausea, heat, exhaustion, dehydration, overstimulation, sleep loss) to check for, and says they turn into spiritual content fast.
S6: Implies: do not take a horrible thought as truth; check the body first.
S7: Says what to do for a body-driven crisis: quiet, water, cooling, warmth, rest, calmer room.
S8: Nothing; it restates that the body matters without adding a sign to look for or an act.
S9: Nothing; it explains where the section sits in the article, not when to use the list.
S10: Defines the body/gut/brain branch by concrete signs, so the reader can tell it apart from a mainly emotional crisis.
S11: Implies: do not treat a gut-driven mental hell as shadow work; treat the gut first.

- S2: In general it's good to try all these things in very low, increasing doses before they are needed in crisis. — It is about preparing in advance. The reader can lose it and still learn what the list is for (body, gut or brain trouble versus emotional crisis).
- S8: The body is part of the spiritual field. — It adds no sign or act beyond S7. It also shifts to a different picture ("spiritual field") from the practical body-trouble frame around it.
- S9: The hierarchy appears before the mystical sections because love doesn't require us to ignore the body. — It explains the section's placement, not when to use it. The "because love…" reason does not follow from the placement, so the words don't fit together.
