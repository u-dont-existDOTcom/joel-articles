S1: With MDMA, I treat meaningful jaw tension as the earliest warning that the body needs more magnesium.
S2: That's from what I've seen again and again, with no clinical trial of it that I know of: everyone I know who took enough magnesium chloride for the jaw tension to fully release avoided the other complications I've seen in people who let it keep going.
S3: A token dose may not be enough: more than once, I've dissolved **Dead Sea magnesium chloride hexahydrate flakes** in water and taken about **one gram per minute by mouth for around twenty minutes** before my jaw fully released.
S4: Around twenty grams hasn't given me diarrhea, and I've only had it above about forty grams.
S5: Magnesium chloride is also [less likely to cause diarrhea than several other kinds of magnesium](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10509448/), including the ones people take on purpose as laxatives.
S6: Magnesium taken before the MDMA peak also makes the peak less euphoric for me, which makes me think it calms part of what MDMA does on top of filling a need that's rising fast.
S7: I'll take slightly less euphoria for a relaxed jaw, since in my experience the risk of things getting out of hand is much lower then.
S8: My second wife had severe convulsions after an MDMA overdose.
S9: At the hospital they found her potassium critically low, described to me as essentially zero, and IV potassium was the only treatment they gave her.
S10: Because of that, I treat serious MDMA toxicity as a broader electrolyte emergency, even though magnesium is still the first and most useful intervention once the jaw tenses.
S11: Once their jaw tenses, that should be their last dose of MDMA, so get them to slow down and cool off.
S12: If they drink a lot of plain water, [even a single normal MDMA dose](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39546312/) raises the risk of their sodium dropping too low, so have them sip an electrolyte drink when they're thirsty instead.
S13: Plain water is the risk because the drug keeps them from peeing out the extra, so it thins out the salt in their blood.
S14: Women are most at risk of this, based on the results we have: at [a rave in the Netherlands](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23476039/), sodium was mildly low in 1 in 4 of the women on ecstasy vs only 3% of the men.
S15: [California's poison control](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17084942/) found the same pattern in the MDMA cases it got, with women at four times the odds.
S16: The following indicators would justify calling medics:
S17: If their confusion or headache worsens, or vomiting won't stop (sodium might be too low by then for an electrolyte drink to fix; while you wait for medics, if they're alert and not vomiting, give them something very salty instead, like strong broth).
S18: That has to be a lot of salt, more than a snack gives:
S19: [guidelines for hikers who drank too much water](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2021/0215/p252.html) use three or four bouillon cubes in half a cup of water.
S20: If their muscles keep getting weaker or their heart palpitations keep getting stronger, that could be from dangerously low potassium, like my second wife had.
S21: A [seizure](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/active-seizure) or collapse can result from low sodium, magnesium or potassium, and can also come from overheating or MDMA toxicity.
S22: That's why I'd tell everyone not to dance on MDMA at all, however good the music is, since dancing makes the heat and the sweat that drain their salt.
S23: And really, what's the point of MDMA if someone wants to dance?

S25: Real MDMA, the kind that's therapeutic and helps people access very early childhood trauma, possibly better than San Pedro can in some cases, isn't the kind people want to dance on, which is why you don't see people in the MAPS trials of MDMA therapy saying "I NEED TO DANCE!"
S26: No, they're sitting there doing the therapy, and even if the science isn't clear on this yet, I and everyone I know are totally clear about what the real thing feels like.
S27: It's not like "I don't feel like dancing", it's like "I can barely move", couch lock.
S28: And in the same exact setting, the pills have gone both ways on me: some had me wanting to dance, and some left me in that couch lock.
S29: So if a pill makes someone feel like dancing, I'm highly suspicious that it contains amphetamines, or that it isn't real MDMA at all, maybe 6-APB, a similar drug.

S34: So kidney trouble is a strong reason not to take MDMA at all, and once someone has taken it, their jaw tension still needs treating.
