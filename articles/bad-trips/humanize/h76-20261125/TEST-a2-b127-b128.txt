If they swallowed it [within the last hour or so](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6620762/) (slow-release pills leave a few hours more), [activated charcoal](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/activated-charcoal) may help, since it binds to a lot of drugs in the gut before the body can absorb them. It only helps with what binds to it, which leaves out alcohol and lithium among other things, and it can also adsorb medication they need. I'd only give it to somebody who's fully alert and able to swallow, because anybody who is vomiting, sedated, seizing or otherwise unable to protect their airway could breathe it into their lungs (aspiration).

If you're unsure charcoal would be of any value to them, poison control may be useful. They're also who to ask when you can't tell what the substance is or what it does in combination with something else they took, or when there may be a delayed risk (the worst coming hours later). I don't treat calling poison control as a mandatory ritual, though, since how useful they are varies by country and by whoever answers. And what they give you is help deciding, so go on with any first aid while you talk to them.
