HEADING: Swallowed Too Much?

PARAGRAPH:
S1: "Overdose" is too vague to choose a remedy.
S2: Figure out how much of what was swallowed and how long ago, then see what the body is doing: are they suffering from [slow and shallow breathing](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/call-medics-for) (respiratory depression, from opioids, benzos and alcohol)?
S3: Or is it rapid breathing, heart rate, and agitation, signs of stimulant toxicity?
S4: Do you see symptoms of [serotonin toxicity](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/how-to-check-for-ss), like seizure, dangerous overheating, tremor?
S5: Do they have vomiting or diarrhea beyond what they can handle?
S6: Some of these symptoms are also normal, like vomiting and diarrhea, which are helpful in undoing an overdose, up to a point.
S7: Slow breathing is normal with iboga, but if cardiac irregularities come along with it, that's an extra danger sign.
S8: If they swallowed it [within the last hour or so](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6620762/) (slow-release pills leave a few hours more), [activated charcoal](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/activated-charcoal) may help, since it binds to a lot of drugs in the gut before the body can absorb them.
S9: But it doesn't bind every toxin (not alcohol or lithium, for example), and it can also adsorb medication they need.
S10: I'd only give it to somebody who's fully alert and able to swallow safely, because anybody who is vomiting, sedated, seizing or otherwise unable to protect their airway could breathe it into their lungs (aspiration).
S11: If the value of charcoal still isn't clear, poison control may be useful, the same as when you're unsure about the substance, the combination, or a delayed risk (when the worst comes hours later).
S12: I don't treat calling them as a mandatory ritual, though, since how useful they are varies by country and by whoever answers.
S13: And what they give you is help deciding what to do, so the first aid is still up to you.
