I would also use topical magnesium chloride and consider topical DMSO with a known-genuine CBD preparation as adjuncts (a lot of CBD is fake or very dilute). Seizures come in all different types, and we have no study on whether CBD could be a rescue remedy for a seizure (just anecdotes), even though we do have studies showing long-term effectiveness to prevent them: Purified oral CBD is approved for the seizures of Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome and tuberous sclerosis complex. For focal seizures outside those syndromes, the evidence is mixed, but still long-term.

And topical CBD hasn't been well-studied. There was a short 12-week trial done, where they used a CBD skin gel, and it did show that transdermal absorption works like oral CBD's but the dosing was too low, and transdermal CBD also doesn't get converted to the potentially more active form (7-OH-CBD) by the liver.

As for DMSO, it did reduce seizure activity in rats with temporal-lobe epilepsy, and we know it has several neuroprotective mechanisms, but it produced the opposite effect at some doses in an absence-epilepsy model. I'd treat it as a plausible experimental adjunct remedy rather than something to wait on while a seizure continues.
