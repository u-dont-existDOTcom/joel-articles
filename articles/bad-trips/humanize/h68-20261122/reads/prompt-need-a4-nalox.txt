You are reading one paragraph of an article for the person it is written for. Here is what the paragraph is for:

WHAT IT IS FOR: the list of signs that mean calling an ambulance, then what to do while help comes; the list and the last line are the writer's own and stay as they are: read the paragraph after the list (three sentences)

HEADING: Call Medics For: (under: When to Get Outside Help; the list is followed by a short paragraph, then "The remedies below can begin while help is being arranged.")

PARAGRAPH:
S1: - Trouble breathing, blue or gray lips, or respiratory depression
S2: - A seizure after taking too much (or an unknown amount), or one on a normal dose lasting over 5 minutes
S3: - Collapse, inability to wake normally, or rapidly worsening consciousness
S4: - Dangerous overheating (not just regular sweating), severe rigidity, or clonus with fever
S5: - Severe insomnia
S6: - Sudden one-sided weakness, loss of speech, inability to walk normally, or loss of control of bodily functions
S7: - A report that the brain feels damaged when the person cannot complete a basic neurological check or is rapidly worsening
S8: - Severe, worsening, radiating, or exertional chest pain--or chest pain with breathlessness, sweating, faintness, neurological changes, or collapse
S9: - Serious injury, uncontrolled bleeding, or a possible head or spinal injury
S10: - Repeated vomiting, severe dehydration, blood, or severe abdominal pain
S11: - Concerning symptoms after an MAOI, which slows the breakdown of serotonin (some older antidepressants, and the harmalas in ayahuasca and Syrian rue seeds), plus a serotonergic substance, one that raises serotonin, like MDMA or most antidepressants
S12: If their breathing gets slow and shallow, give them naloxone (the Narcan nasal spray) if you have it.
S13: It doesn't matter if they think they only took MDMA, since fentanyl, an opioid, has turned up in pills sold as MDMA, and naloxone does no harm when there's no opioid.
S14: It wears off in 30 to 90 minutes, sooner than some opioids do, so call medics either way.
S15: The remedies below can begin while help is being arranged.

For each sentence, answer in one line: what does the reader need this sentence for, given what the paragraph is for?
Then list the sentences the reader doesn't need.

A sentence the reader doesn't need is one they could lose and still do what the paragraph is for, with nothing
missing. These are not reasons to keep a sentence: it is true; it has a source; it is interesting; it is a careful
hypothesis or a testable guess; it explains a disease or a case the reader doesn't have; it qualifies another claim the
reader didn't need either; it was already in the text. Read also for sense: a sentence whose words don't make sense
together, or that mixes pictures (a dial, a door, a floor and a leak for one thing), goes on the list too, with why.

Before you list a sentence for giving the reader nothing to do, ask what it means they should do. A caution, a risk
or a difference between people often implies an act the sentence doesn't say ("Sensitivity varies" means: start
with less): that sentence is needed, and its NEEDS line says the act it implies, so the writer can add it.
