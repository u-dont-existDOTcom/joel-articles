S9: ## "My Brain Feels Damaged"
S10: Don't dismiss someone who says their brain feels damaged just because they're high or can't explain what they feel in medical language, since something could really be going wrong in their brain.
S11: DMSO is my first immediate remedy while I ask what the symptoms actually are.
S12: I usually know within about a minute whether it is helping.
S13: Smelling humic acid or tasting a tiny bit can also help a lot, in my experience, but I'm still collecting data from others.
S14: I give electrolytes to drink also, because magnesium (and then potassium) can be depleted by some drugs, especially MDMA and iboga, although I don't think just electrolytes would be sufficient to reverse damage already underway.
S15: While the DMSO is working, check for stroke warning signs that would trigger a call to medics:
S16: - First, are they unconscious or breathing abnormally?
S17: - Feel whether they're very hot.
S18: - Have them talk and smile.
S19: Is their speech slurred, or does one side of their face droop?
S20: - Ask them to hold both arms out in front of them, and see if one drifts down.
S21: - If you can safely hold them as they try, have them stand and walk a few steps to see if they keep their balance.
S22: - Wetting or soiling themselves is another sign to watch for.
S23: - Watch for a seizure, and for vomiting that keeps coming back.
S24: - Ask about a really bad headache or chest pain.
S25: When they're too out of it to help you do the checks, or if the brain-damage feeling stays despite sufficient treatment, then you've done what you can do, and it's time to call for help.
S26: I believe, based on my experience and those of others I've heard from, as well as the books I've read on this, that DMSO works better for most acute brain damage than anything else readily available.
S27: The clinical evidence is incomplete, but it leans this way.
S28: In animals, when given after a stroke, it shrank the area of the brain that died (which is a good thing).
S29: Some patients whose stroke came from a clot seemed to recover better on DMSO in a human trial, and doctors have used it in a handful of patients to bring down dangerous pressure inside the skull.
S30: But more relevant to the drug overdose issue this article deals with, DMSO calms NMDA- and AMPA-mediated excitotoxicity that kills brain cells, at least in rat brain cells in a dish.
S31: If someone is worried about a bleed, I'd probably skip DMSO, but it depends on how severe their issue is.
S32: It's not a powerful blood thinner like heparin, unless maybe injected; it's basically a dietary blood thinner.
S33: I've never had any issues with bleeding too much from consuming DMSO, and never heard of it either, but if bleeding is already a medical issue I'd want to think about that more.
S34: They can feel better on DMSO with a clot or a bleed still there.
S35: So if any of the checks above fail, call medics right away, before the window for treating a stroke closes.

S51: ## Active Seizure
S52: Time the seizure, and while it lasts:
S53: - Move any dangerous objects away from them, especially anything hard or sharp.
S54: - Don't restrain them, since that can cause injuries, but do put pillows where appropriate, especially under the head.
S55: - Loosen any tight clothing.
S56: - Turn them gently onto their side, which helps them breathe.
S57: - Keep everything out of their mouth while they're convulsing or not fully conscious.
S58: Call medics if any of these happen:
S59: - They have a seizure after taking too much, or you don't know how much they took
S60: - Note that psychedelics do reduce the seizure threshold, so if someone is prone to seizures, a normal dose could produce a seizure for them which doesn't indicate brain damage, but if it goes on over 5 minutes, that's too much
S61: - Their breathing gets slow and shallow
S62: - Their lips turn blue or gray
S63: - When you touch their forehead, it's very hot (not just sweaty)
S64: - They injure their head, break a bone, or bleeding won't stop
S65: If they have a rescue medicine for epilepsy seizures with them, give it as the label instructs.
S66: These medicines are benzodiazepines in nasal spray or rectal gel, or sometimes an intramuscular shot (pills can't be swallowed).
S67: After giving it, keep a close eye on their breathing, since a benzodiazepine adds to the risk of it getting slow and shallow, especially if they've also taken other respiratory depressants like opioids or alcohol.
S68: ### Seizure:
S69: What Else Helps
S70: Some people get a warning before a seizure takes hold, which is called an aura.
S71: That's when the things people report can still stop it, depending on what sets their seizures off.
S72: If it's flashing or flickering light, cover one eye with a hand and turn away from the light, because closing the eyes doesn't block enough of it.
S73: Some people stop a seizure at the warning stage with a strong smell like jasmine, and some train themselves to link one smell with feeling calm and then sniff it when a seizure threatens.
S74: When overstimulation is what sets it off, a cool, quiet, dark and comfortable space can help.
S75: I'd also try sniffing humic/fulvic acid powder, although that's just a hunch based on the effects I notice so far.
S76: During the seizure, alongside any rescue medicine, I would use topical magnesium chloride and consider topical DMSO with a known-genuine CBD preparation (a lot of CBD is fake or very dilute).
S77: Seizures come in all different types, and we have no study on whether CBD could be a rescue remedy for a seizure (just anecdotes), even though we do have studies showing long-term effectiveness to prevent them:
S78: Purified oral CBD is approved for the seizures of Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome and tuberous sclerosis complex.
S79: As for CBD/cannabis, people do smoke or vape it at the start of an aura, while they're still awake enough to do it on purpose.
S80: There are also people who find THC and/or CBD works as a nasal spray or sublingually, and there's even a medical THC nasal spray marketed for seizure interruption in Brazil by a non-profit patients' association called ABRACE.
S81: They testified in Congress that in more than 30 tests they did with children having seizures, the nasal spray ended those seizures within 30 seconds.
S82: You can see it being used on a child in this vid: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bgVi5o_2v_w
S83: I also read on forums some swear it stops the seizure from going any further, and there was one woman whose EEG showed fewer seizure discharges on the days she smoked.
S84: But a few people on the same forums said THC vapes and concentrates brought their seizures on.
S85: And rats bred to have seizures had more of them after high-THC smoke.
S86: As for topical CBD, it hasn't been well-studied.
S87: There was a short 12-week trial done, where they used a CBD skin gel, and although absorption was good, it didn't work any better than placebo to prevent seizures.
S88: The study authors suggested the dosing may have been too low, or that adults with focal epilepsy just don't respond as well as people with the syndromes oral CBD is approved for.
S89: The seizures in Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome are mostly generalized.
S90: Generalized are the type people usually have with MDMA & psychedelic overdoses, so topical CBD might do better for those than it did in that trial.
S91: As for DMSO, it did reduce seizure activity in rats with temporal-lobe epilepsy, and we know it has several neuroprotective mechanisms, but it produced the opposite effect at some doses in an absence-epilepsy model.
S92: While a seizure continues, the rescue medicine comes first, and I'd still consider DMSO alongside it, although that's experimental.
