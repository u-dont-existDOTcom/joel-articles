S2: Magnesium Alarm
S3: With MDMA, I treat meaningful jaw tension as the earliest warning that the body needs more magnesium.
S4: This comes from repeated field experience rather than a direct clinical trial.
S5: Everyone I know who has taken enough magnesium chloride to make the jaw tension fully release has avoided the other complications I have seen when people let that tension continue.
S6: A token dose may not be enough.
S7: On multiple occasions, I have dissolved Dead Sea magnesium chloride hexahydrate flakes in water and taken approximately one gram per minute orally for around twenty minutes before my jaw fully released.
S8: I have tolerated around twenty grams without diarrhea; for me, diarrhea has appeared only above roughly forty grams.
S9: Magnesium chloride is also generally less likely to cause diarrhea than several other replacement salts, including forms deliberately used as laxatives.
S10: Interestingly, magnesium taken before the MDMA peak also makes the peak less euphoric for me.
S11: That suggests it may both meet a rapidly increasing magnesium need and directly moderate part of MDMA's excitatory effect.
S12: For harm reduction, slightly less euphoria is a reasonable price for a relaxed jaw and what appears in my experience to be a much lower risk of the state escalating.
S13: My second wife had severe convulsions after an MDMA overdose.
S14: At the hospital, her potassium was found to be critically low--described to me as essentially zero--and IV potassium was the only treatment they gave her.
S15: That experience is why I treat serious MDMA toxicity as a broader electrolyte emergency, even though magnesium is the first and most useful early intervention when jaw tension appears.
S16: Once their jaw tenses, that should be their last dose of MDMA, so get them to slow down and cool off.
S17: If they drink a lot of plain water, even a single normal MDMA dose raises the risk of their sodium dropping too low, so have them sip an electrolyte drink when they're thirsty instead.
S18: The following indicators would justify calling medics:
S19: If their confusion or headache worsens, or vomiting won't stop (sodium might be too low by then for an electrolyte drink to fix; while you wait for medics, if they're alert and not vomiting, give them something very salty instead, like strong broth or salty snacks).

S27: ## Chest Pain and Injury
S28: DMSO is also my first remedy for many injuries and some chest pain.
S29: After I overused erythritol, I developed angina that lasted for days.
S30: I took DMSO, the pain disappeared within about a minute, and it never returned.
S31: So far, the heart research on DMSO has only been in animals and lab dishes, and only as a prophylactic (preventative treatment).
S32: When they gave it to rats for three days before a heart attack, those rats lost about three quarters less heart muscle than the rats who didn't get DMSO.
S33: Most heart attacks come from a clot blocking an artery, so it's a good sign that DMSO also slowed clotting, in mice and in human platelets (the blood cells that start clots) in a dish.
S34: For steady chest pain that's mild, I'd just use DMSO + magnesium chloride on the chest, as long as they're fully alert.
S35: For the following more serious signs (or if unsure), I'd also call medics:
S36: - The pain is worsening.
S37: - Hard movement (like dancing or climbing) brought it on.
S38: - It spreads from the chest into an arm or the jaw.
S39: - Shortness of breath or sweating shows up also.

S44: "Overdose" is too vague to choose a remedy.
