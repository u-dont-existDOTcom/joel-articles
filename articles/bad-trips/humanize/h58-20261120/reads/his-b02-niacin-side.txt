- S2: 1 — "Niacin flush: itchy sunburn-like heat" is a label plus a noun phrase; his way would say what happens, e.g. your skin turns red, itchy and hot like a sunburn.
- S4: 1, 2, 4 — A bare noun-phrase fragment built the same way as S2 and S5 (a thing, then "in/after ..."). "Too much at once" doesn't say how much, and "sensitive stomachs" doesn't say whose. His way would say what the reader feels and who gets it, in a full clause.
- S5: 1, 4 — "Drowsiness, usually with terrible sleep quality" is another fragment in the same shape, and "terrible sleep quality" is vague about what happens (waking often? unrested?). His way would say you may feel sleepy and sleep badly.
- S6: 1, 4, 7 — Packs several ideas into one sentence and ends on a list of category words ("liver problems, alcohol, or medications"). "Medications" and "a real risk" don't say which or how big, and the reader gets no sign of liver trouble to watch for or what to do. His way would split it, name the kinds of medication, and say what to do or watch for.
