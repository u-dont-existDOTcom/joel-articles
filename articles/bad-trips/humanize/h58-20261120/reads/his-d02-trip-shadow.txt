MARKED:
- S1: 4 — "shadow thought" is never explained for a reader who doesn't know the term; his way would say what kind of thought it is (a dark thought about yourself) inside the sentence.
- S2: 4, 10 — "the other half of a pair" doesn't say what the pair is, and "it" (the thought? the feeling?) and "it come back stronger" could point at different things; his way would name the thought and the opposite it pairs with.
- S3: 4, 10 — "everybody" is vague about who (the people around you, the sitter?), and "confessing it" could mean the thought or the feeling; his way would name who and what, and say what to do instead.
- S4: 10 — "it" could point at the thought, the feeling, or the urge to confess; his way would name what to notice.
- S6: 2 — "A feeling is here." repeats the exact shape of S5 ("A thought is here."); he builds neighbouring sentences differently or joins them into one.
- S7: 3, 10 — "intense, but it is not the whole of me" sets a contrast the sentence could just state, and "It" could be the thought or the feeling; his way would say what the person is or does instead.
- S8: 1, 4 — "room, body, human, time, and love" is a run of category words, and "human" and "love" don't say what to do or what counts (look at the room, feel your body, find a person, remember what time it is); his way would give each a clause with an action.
