MARKED:
- S2: 4 — "whatever you were working so hard on" doesn't say what counts; he would name the kind of thing (for example the belief or the person you were working on) instead of "whatever".
- S3: 10 — it's really an instruction wearing a statement's clothes ("shoving it down only makes it come back louder"), and "it" could be the thought, the feeling, or the opposite coming into focus; he would say "don't push the thought down, because it will come back louder".
- S6: 3 — "intense, sure, but they aren't all of you" is a set-against pair; he would say the thing itself, such as "you are more than this thought and feeling".
- S7: 1 — a run of noun phrases (room, body, person, time, love) packed into one sentence; he would give each its own clause saying what to do (look around the room, feel your feet, listen to the person with you).
- S7: 6 — it ends on "love", a line that sounds wise, where he ends on what to do.
- S7: 10 — "when you can, love" could be a term of address or a thing to come back to; he would make it plain which.
