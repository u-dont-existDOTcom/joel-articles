MARKED:
- S3: 2, 10 — "If they want to ..., that can wait until tomorrow" has the same opening and shape as S4 and S6; "that can wait" is a statement that is really an instruction and doesn't say what to do (tell them it can wait, stay with them), and "that" could mean the wanting or the confrontation. His way would vary the opening and say what the helper does.
- S4: 2, 4 — "And if they want to leave, have someone go with them" repeats the "If they want to ..., [action]" shape of S3 and S6; "someone" doesn't say who (a sober, trusted person), where his way would name who counts.
- S5: 4 — "any weapons" doesn't say what counts (guns, knives, anything sharp or heavy) and "put away" doesn't say where (locked up, out of reach), where his way would spell both out.
- S6: 2 — "If they want to post ..., or message an ex, take the phone" is the third "If they want to ..., [action]" sentence in a row; his way would build it differently (for example "Another is ..." or "Lastly, ...").
