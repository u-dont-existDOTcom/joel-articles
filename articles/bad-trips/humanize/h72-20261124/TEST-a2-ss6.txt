Overstimulating also dehydrates them, but we can't give fluids unless they're fully alert, able to swallow and not vomiting, and then just small sips of an electrolyte drink. If they still show the signs of low electrolytes from [MDMA: Magnesium Alarm](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/mdma-magnesium-alarm) above, they may need **more salt, potassium and magnesium than a normal electrolyte drink has**. Salty potato chips give the salt and the potassium with nothing to cook, so I'd let them eat as many as they want. That's the only way I'd give potassium, since too much pure potassium is dangerous for anyone. Their potassium could be running high as well as low, though: MDMA can drain it, but muscles breaking down into the blood push it up. So if they start vomiting or get diarrhea, I'd stop the chips, since too much potassium brings those on and the chips have to stop either way while they're being sick. Magnesium also runs low in many people with serotonin syndrome ([a nursing review](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.NME.0000432871.59839.f3)). Magnesium chloride tastes too bad for most people to drink, but the skin absorbs it, so I'd stir a handful of magnesium chloride flakes into a little water and rub the mix on their skin. If you don't have the flakes, Epsom salt, which is magnesium sulfate rather than a kind of table salt, works the same way, just less well. If they can stand the taste, they can also take magnesium chloride by mouth, especially with MDMA-related jaw tension or twitching (the amounts are in [MDMA: Magnesium Alarm](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/mdma-magnesium-alarm), above).
