WHAT IT IS: a harm-reduction guide on bad trips and overdoses for people who take psychedelics and the friends who sit with them, by a writer who uses and supplies some of the remedies it describes

YOUR MOMENT: you are sitting with a friend who took MDMA on top of their antidepressant and now has twitching muscles, sweating and a racing heart; you have called an ambulance and are reading what to do while you wait

THE TEXT:
S1: 1.
S2: Serotonin syndrome comes from too much serotonin, so **I'd stop anything suspected of adding more** (serotonergic substances).
S3: So no more MDMA or other stimulants, of course, but also no DXM cough syrup, the painkiller tramadol or the antibiotic linezolid.
S4: It doesn't matter if they are prescribed an antidepressant (an SSRI like Prozac, or an SNRI or MAOI), it's too risky right now.
S5: If they drank ayahuasca or another harmala brew, like Syrian rue, no more of that either, since those work the way MAOIs do.
S6: And check whether they take supplements people don't think of as drugs, like 5-HTP, St John's wort or SAM-e.
S7: 2. **If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early**, since it blocks serotonin receptors.
S8: So far, the evidence for this med has only been [case reports](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9696181/), and [a recent review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39791184/) found them insufficient to show that it works.
S9: I wouldn't wait to see whether it does before doing the other steps.
S10: And most people won't have it at home anyway, since it needs a prescription in the US, though a telehealth doctor might write one quickly, and only some countries sell it without one.
S11: If you can get it, [the guidelines](https://www.mdpoison.com/media/SOP/mdpoisoncom/healthcareprofessionals/antidote-facts/Cyproheptadine%20Antidote%20Facts.pdf) say to give an adult 12mg to start, then 2mg every 2 hours if symptoms persist, up to 32mg per 24 hours.
S12: And tell medics how much you gave.
S13: 3. **When you call medics, ask for an ambulance**, since the person can get worse fast, with seizures or dangerous overheating.
S14: It may seem faster to drive them to the ER yourself, but don't: paramedics can start treating them right there, and if they have a seizure in your car, you can't help them and drive at the same time.
S15: And while you wait, call poison control if you don't know what was mixed, as they might be able to help you work that out, even if they can't treat serotonin syndrome.
S16: 4.
S17: Since bodily overheating is the most dangerous part of serotonin syndrome, you should address their twitching and tense muscles that are making a lot of heat, while you wait for the ambulance.
S18: So **I'd get them somewhere cool**, take off their extra clothes, and set a fan blowing over cool damp cloths on their skin.
S19: If they still feel hot, put ice packs wrapped in cloth where they can absorb the most heat: at the neck, armpits and groin.
S20: Although a cold bath would cool them faster, they could slip under and breathe in water if they're confused, seizing, or very weak, so it's not advisable.
S21: And while you might think fever reducers like Tylenol or ibuprofen could work by reducing body heat in general, they don't work on the heat generated by muscles.
S22: 5.
S23: Since they're already overstimulated, it helps to clear out the crowd and stop any arguing.
S24: While it's natural for people who are overstimulated to start pacing or exercising to "burn it off," the problem is that creates more body heat, and it raises the risk of their muscles breaking down into the blood, which hurts the kidneys (rhabdomyolysis).
S25: But if they feel a strong urge to keep moving (akathisia), I'd have them tap their fingertips on their head instead (the Emotional Freedom Technique), which can soothe that urge without the over-activity risk.
S26: 6.
S27: Overstimulating also dehydrates them, but we can't give fluids unless they're fully alert, able to swallow and not vomiting, and then just small sips of an electrolyte drink.
S28: If they still show the signs of low electrolytes from [MDMA:
S29: Magnesium Alarm](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/mdma-magnesium-alarm) above, they may need **a hypertonic electrolyte drink with more magnesium and potassium than a normal one**.
S30: That can be as simple as a salty potato broth with some magnesium chloride flakes stirred in, since potatoes are full of potassium.
S31: If you don't have the flakes, Epsom salt works too, although it causes diarrhea more easily.
S32: Whatever you give, give it slowly to see how it affects them, since MDMA can drain potassium, but muscles breaking down into the blood can also push it too high.
S33: So I'd get potassium from food like the potatoes, not from pure potassium, too much of which is dangerous for anyone.
S34: 7.
S35: The ER doctors will want to know exactly what they took, so before the ambulance comes, **I'd gather their pill bottles and packages**.
S36: I'd also jot down how much of each they took and when, street drugs and supplements included.
S37: If they have a seizure while you wait for an ambulance, do the first aid in [Active Seizure](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/active-seizure) above.
S38: Then I'd tell the medics whether their breathing got better or worse afterward, since [the CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/epilepsy/first-aid-for-seizures/index.html) counts trouble breathing after a seizure as a danger sign.
S39: 8.
S40: While you wait, you might think of [niacin](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/niacin-flushes-trips-out), which this guide uses for ending a trip, but it's actually unsafe with serotonin syndrome, since at the doses used to end a trip it can cause flushing, dizziness and low blood pressure ([NIH](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Niacin-Consumer/)).
S41: [DMSO](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/dmso) is different: it seems to help prevent seizures, so if there's any sign they took too much, I'd have them smell it or rub some on a clean patch of skin (add 30% water if it makes the skin itch).
S42: Then I'd raise the dose slowly as long as it seems to help, since seizures have come with large amounts of DMSO in stem cell infusions ([a case report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11880447/)).
S43: I'd also leave out this guide's [Syrian rue oil](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/syrian-rue-oil-gentler) for now: made at home it has almost none of the harmala alkaloids, but I couldn't find any research on it with MDMA, so it could help or harm here.
S44: And I'd give magnesium chloride too, since many people with serotonin syndrome run low on magnesium ([a nursing review](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.NME.0000432871.59839.f3)).
S45: If they're fully alert and able to swallow, I'd give it by mouth, especially with MDMA-related jaw tension or muscle activity (the amounts are in [MDMA:
S46: Magnesium Alarm](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/mdma-magnesium-alarm), above).
S47: Topical magnesium chloride if they're not able to swallow, or Epsom salt if that's all you have, although it gets through the skin less well.
