Chest Pain and Injury

For chest pain that's mild and familiar, I'd just use DMSO, as long as it's holding steady and they're fully alert. It's more serious if:
- It's severe, or a kind of pain they've never had before.
- It won't let up or keeps getting worse.
- Physical effort brought it on.
- It spreads out from the chest, like into an arm or the jaw.
- Shortness of breath or sweating comes with it.
- Any of the stroke signs from the brain checks above show up, like slurred speech or a drooping face.
- They feel faint, or they collapse.

Then I'd still use DMSO, but call medics at the same time. When you're unsure which kind it is, treat it as the serious kind. DMSO can also block pain signals, so if the pain fades, still let the medics check them.
