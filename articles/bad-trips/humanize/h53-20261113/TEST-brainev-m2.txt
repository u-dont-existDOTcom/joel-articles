I believe, based on my experience and those of others I've heard from, as well as the books I've read on this, that DMSO works better for most acute brain damage than anything else readily available. The clinical evidence is incomplete, but it leans this way. In animals, when given after a stroke, it shrank the area of the brain that died (which is a good thing). In one human trial, some clot stroke patients seemed to recover better on DMSO, and doctors have used it in a small series of patients to bring down dangerous pressure inside the skull. But more relevant to the drug overdose issue this article deals with, in rat brain cells in a dish, DMSO quickly calmed the overstimulation by glutamate (through its NMDA and AMPA receptors) that kills brain cells.
