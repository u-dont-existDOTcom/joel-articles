1. **Stop every suspected serotonergic substance**, meaning anything that raises serotonin. If they're on an antidepressant (an SSRI, SNRI or MAOI), give them no more of it, and the same goes for tramadol, a painkiller, and for the antibiotic linezolid. No more MDMA or stimulant redoses either, and none of the supplements 5-HTP, St John's wort or SAM-e. Any harmala or ayahuasca-type MAOI counts too, and so does DXM cough syrup.
2. **If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early**, since it blocks serotonin receptors. So far, the evidence for this med has only been [case reports](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9696181/), and [a recent review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39791184/) found them insufficient to show that it works. I wouldn't wait to see whether it does before doing the other steps.
3. **Call emergency help and arrange transport.** Don't drive yourself. If you can't tell what your friend mixed, a poison control line can help you work out the combination, so call one too once emergency help is on its way.
4. **Cool them down without holding up transport:** if they're hot, get them into a cool room and take off their extra clothing. Then cool their skin with a fan and damp cloths, and put ice packs wrapped in cloth at their neck, armpits or groin. Don't put them in a cool bath, though, if they're confused, seizing or very weak, since they could slip under the water. If you're thinking of a fever reducer, it won't help here: fever reducers do not bring down heat generated by muscles working this hard, so keep cooling them from the outside.
5. **Keep the room calm and keep them still**, since they're already overstimulated. That means no crowds or arguments around them, and no pacing or exercise to "burn it off." If they feel a strong urge to keep moving, which is called akathisia, help them stay put anyway, because every bit of muscle work adds heat. It also raises their risk of rhabdomyolysis, where damaged muscle leaks into the blood and can hurt the kidneys.
6. **Give fluids only if they're fully alert and not vomiting**, and then just small sips of an electrolyte drink, like oral rehydration salts mixed in water. Someone who is confused, sleepy or vomiting, or at risk of a seizure, could breathe in whatever you give them, so don't force fluids, food, pills or charcoal on them.
7. **Gather what the ER will need:** bring their pill bottles and packages, and write down exactly what they took. For each substance, note the dose and when they took it, which dose was the last, and any dose they recently raised, then list any supplements or recreational drugs. If you can take their temperature, pulse and blood pressure, write those down as well.
8. **While you wait, leave out niacin and DMSO.** Niacin may be this guide's trip killer, but it is unsafe with serotonin syndrome, and DMSO isn't one of these steps either. Once they're fully alert, I'd consider magnesium chloride by mouth instead, as in the MDMA section above, especially when MDMA has their jaw clenched or their muscles twitching.
