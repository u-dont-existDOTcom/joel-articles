Stop every suspected serotonergic substance, meaning anything that raises serotonin. Check whether they took an antidepressant (an SSRI, SNRI or MAOI), the painkiller tramadol, MDMA or the antibiotic linezolid, and give no more of any of it, or of supplements like 5-HTP, St John's wort and SAM-e. That goes for stimulant redoses too, for harmala or ayahuasca-type MAOIs, and even for DXM cough syrup.
If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early, since it blocks serotonin receptors. So far, the evidence for this med has only been case reports, and a recent review found them insufficient to show that it works. I wouldn't wait to see whether it does before doing the other steps.
Call emergency help and arrange transport. Don't drive yourself. If you can't tell what they mixed, poison control can help you work it out, so call them as well, once help is on its way.
Cool them down without holding up transport. If they're hot, get them into a cool room and take off their extra clothing, then cool them with a fan and damp cloths, and hold ice packs wrapped in cloth at the neck, armpits or groin. Don't put them in a bath if they're confused, seizing or very weak. Fever reducers do not bring down heat generated by their muscles working this hard, so keep cooling them from the outside.
Keep things calm and keep them still. They're already overstimulated, so keep crowds and arguments away from them, and don't let them pace or exercise to "burn it off." If they feel a strong urge to keep moving (akathisia), help them stay put anyway, since every bit of muscle work adds heat and raises their risk of rhabdomyolysis, where damaged muscle leaks into the blood and can hurt the kidneys.
Give fluids only if they're fully alert and not vomiting. Then small sips of an electrolyte drink, like oral rehydration salts in water, are fine. Don't force fluids, food, pills or charcoal on someone who is confused, vomiting or sleepy, or at risk of a seizure, since they could choke on it.
Gather what the ER will need. Bring their pill bottles and packages, and write down exactly what they took: each substance, its dose and when they took it, their last dose, any dose they recently raised, and any supplements or recreational drugs. If you can measure them, add their temperature, pulse and blood pressure.
Leave out niacin and DMSO. Niacin, the trip killer further down this guide, is unsafe with serotonin syndrome, and DMSO isn't part of treating it either. If they're fully alert, I'd consider magnesium chloride by mouth instead, especially if MDMA has their jaw clenched or their muscles twitching.
