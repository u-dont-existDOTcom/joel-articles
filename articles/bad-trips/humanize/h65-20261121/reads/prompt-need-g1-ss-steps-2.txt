You are reading one paragraph of an article for the person it is written for. Here is what the paragraph is for:

WHAT IT IS FOR: doing the right things, in order, for a friend who may have serotonin syndrome, while calling for help and waiting for it

HEADING: Do this immediately when serotonin toxicity is reasonably suspected:

PARAGRAPH:
S1: Stop every suspected serotonergic substance, meaning anything that raises serotonin.
S2: Check whether they took an antidepressant (an SSRI, SNRI or MAOI), the painkiller tramadol, MDMA or the antibiotic linezolid, and give no more of any of it, or of supplements like 5-HTP, St John's wort and SAM-e.
S3: That goes for stimulant redoses too, for harmala or ayahuasca-type MAOIs, and even for DXM cough syrup.
S4: If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early, since it blocks serotonin receptors.
S5: So far, the evidence for this med has only been case reports, and a recent review found them insufficient to show that it works.
S6: I wouldn't wait to see whether it does before doing the other steps.
S7: Call emergency help and arrange transport.
S8: Don't drive yourself.
S9: If you can't tell what they mixed, poison control can help you work it out, so call them as well, once help is on its way.
S10: Cool them down without holding up transport.
S11: If they're hot, get them into a cool room and take off their extra clothing, then cool them with a fan and damp cloths, and hold ice packs wrapped in cloth at the neck, armpits or groin.
S12: Don't put them in a bath if they're confused, seizing or very weak.
S13: Fever reducers do not bring down heat generated by their muscles working this hard, so keep cooling them from the outside.
S14: Keep things calm and keep them still.
S15: They're already overstimulated, so keep crowds and arguments away from them, and don't let them pace or exercise to "burn it off.
S16: If they feel a strong urge to keep moving (akathisia), help them stay put anyway, since every bit of muscle work adds heat and raises their risk of rhabdomyolysis, where damaged muscle leaks into the blood and can hurt the kidneys.
S17: Give fluids only if they're fully alert and not vomiting.
S18: Then small sips of an electrolyte drink, like oral rehydration salts in water, are fine.
S19: Don't force fluids, food, pills or charcoal on someone who is confused, vomiting or sleepy, or at risk of a seizure, since they could choke on it.
S20: Gather what the ER will need.
S21: Bring their pill bottles and packages, and write down exactly what they took: each substance, its dose and when they took it, their last dose, any dose they recently raised, and any supplements or recreational drugs.
S22: If you can measure them, add their temperature, pulse and blood pressure.
S23: Leave out niacin and DMSO.
S24: Niacin, the trip killer further down this guide, is unsafe with serotonin syndrome, and DMSO isn't part of treating it either.
S25: If they're fully alert, I'd consider magnesium chloride by mouth instead, especially if MDMA has their jaw clenched or their muscles twitching.

For each sentence, answer in one line: what does the reader need this sentence for, given what the paragraph is for?
Then list the sentences the reader doesn't need.

A sentence the reader doesn't need is one they could lose and still do what the paragraph is for, with nothing
missing. These are not reasons to keep a sentence: it is true; it has a source; it is interesting; it is a careful
hypothesis or a testable guess; it explains a disease or a case the reader doesn't have; it qualifies another claim the
reader didn't need either; it was already in the text. Read also for sense: a sentence whose words don't make sense
together, or that mixes pictures (a dial, a door, a floor and a leak for one thing), goes on the list too, with why.

Before you list a sentence for giving the reader nothing to do, ask what it means they should do. A caution, a risk
or a difference between people often implies an act the sentence doesn't say ("Sensitivity varies" means: start
with less): that sentence is needed, and its NEEDS line says the act it implies, so the writer can add it.
