1. **Stop all suspected serotonergic substances**, meaning anything that raises serotonin, like:
   - their antidepressant, if it's an SSRI, SNRI or MAOI, or if you're not sure what kind it is
   - tramadol for pain, or the antibiotic linezolid
   - another dose of MDMA, or of any stimulant
   - 5-HTP, St John's wort or SAM-e
   - harmala, or an ayahuasca-type MAOI
   - even DXM cough syrup
2. **If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early**, since it blocks serotonin receptors. So far, the evidence for this med has only been [case reports](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9696181/), and [a recent review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39791184/) found them insufficient to show that it works. I wouldn't wait to see whether it does before doing the other steps.
3. **Call emergency help and arrange transport.** Don't drive yourself. If you can't tell what they mixed, call a poison control line as well, once help is on the way.
4. **Cool them down without holding up transport:**
   - Get them into a cool room and take off any extra clothing.
   - Put a fan on them, with cool damp cloths on their skin.
   - If they're hot, hold ice packs wrapped in cloth at the neck, armpits or groin.
   - Don't put them in a bath if they're confused, seizing or very weak.
   - If you think of giving a fever reducer like acetaminophen or ibuprofen, it won't work: fever reducers do not help with heat generated by muscles working this hard.
5. **Keep the room calm and keep them still:**
   - They're already overstimulated, so keep crowds and arguments away.
   - Stop any pacing or exercise to "burn it off": muscle work makes more heat and raises the risk of rhabdomyolysis, where damaged muscle leaks into the blood and can hurt the kidneys.
   - If they feel a strong urge to keep moving (akathisia), stay with them and help them keep still anyway.
6. **Give fluids only if they're fully alert and not vomiting**, and then only small sips of an electrolyte drink. Don't force fluids, food, pills or charcoal on them if they're confused, vomiting or sleepy. The same goes if they're at risk of a seizure, since they could choke.
7. **Gather what the ER will need:**
   - their pill bottles and packages
   - exactly what they took, how much of each, and when
   - which dose was the last, and any dose they recently increased
   - any supplements or recreational drugs
   - their temperature, pulse and blood pressure, if you can take them
8. **While you wait, don't reach for niacin to end the trip:** it's unsafe with serotonin syndrome. Leave out DMSO too, which this guide uses for other emergencies, since it isn't part of treating serotonin syndrome. What I would consider instead, once they're fully alert, is magnesium chloride by mouth, especially for MDMA-related jaw tension or muscle twitching (the amounts are in the MDMA section above).
