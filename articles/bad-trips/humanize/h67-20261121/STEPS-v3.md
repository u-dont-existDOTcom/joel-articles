1. Too much serotonin is what causes this, so **I'd stop anything suspected of adding more** (serotonergic substances). No more MDMA or other stimulants, of course, but also no DXM cough syrup, the painkiller tramadol or the antibiotic linezolid. Even prescribed antidepressants should be held until a doctor says to restart them, whether they're SSRIs like Prozac, SNRIs like Effexor, or MAOIs. Harmala or ayahuasca brews act as MAOIs too, and don't forget the supplements people don't think of as drugs, like 5-HTP, St John's wort and SAM-e.
2. **If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early**, since it blocks serotonin receptors. So far, the evidence for this med has only been [case reports](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9696181/), and [a recent review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39791184/) found them insufficient to show that it works. I wouldn't wait to see whether it does before doing the other steps.
3. **When you call medics, ask for an ambulance**, since they can get worse fast, with seizures or dangerous overheating, and paramedics can start treating them right away. So don't drive them yourself, even if it seems faster. Poison control can help figure out which mix of drugs caused it, but they can't treat it, so I'd call them once the ambulance is on its way.
4. **While you wait for the ambulance, I'd cool them down.** Their twitching and tense muscles make a lot of heat, and overheating is the most dangerous part of serotonin syndrome. So move them to a cool room, take off extra clothes, and use a fan and cool damp cloths. If they're hot to the touch, I'd also put ice packs wrapped in cloth at the neck, armpits or groin. A cold bath would cool them faster, but if they're confused, seizing or very weak, they could slip under or breathe in water, so don't put them in one. And fever reducers like Tylenol or ibuprofen do not help here, because this heat is generated by their muscles, not by a fever.
5. Since they're already overstimulated, it helps to clear out the crowd and stop any arguing. While it's natural for people who are overstimulated to start pacing or exercising to "burn it off," the problem is that creates more body heat, and it raises the risk of their muscles breaking down into the blood, which hurts the kidneys (rhabdomyolysis). But if they feel a strong urge to keep moving (akathisia), I'd have them do some fidgeting (especially of the calf muscle, flexing the feet), which can soothe that urge without the over-activity risk.
6. Overstimulating also dehydrates them, but we can't give fluids unless they're fully alert, able to swallow and not vomiting, and then just small sips of an electrolyte drink (a hypertonic one, more concentrated than an ordinary one, may be required if they still have symptoms of lack of electrolytes).
7. **Before the ambulance comes, I'd gather their pill bottles and packages**, so the ER doctors can see exactly what they took. I'd also write down how much of each they took and when, including any recreational drugs and supplements. A dose they raised lately is worth noting too, since that's often what sets this off. If you can take their temperature, pulse or blood pressure, put those in the notes as well.
8. This guide suggests niacin for ending a trip, and DMSO for brain injuries, seizures and chest pain, but neither is for serotonin syndrome, and niacin is actually unsafe with it. I would consider oral magnesium chloride, though, if they're fully alert and able to swallow, especially with MDMA-related jaw tension or muscle activity (the amounts are in [MDMA: Magnesium Alarm](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/mdma-magnesium-alarm)).
