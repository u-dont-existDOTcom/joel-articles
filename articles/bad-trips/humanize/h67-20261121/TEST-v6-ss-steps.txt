1. Serotonin syndrome comes from too much serotonin, so I'd stop anything suspected of adding more (serotonergic substances). No more MDMA or other stimulants, of course, but also no DXM cough syrup, the painkiller tramadol or the antibiotic linezolid. I'd hold off on even their prescribed antidepressant until a doctor says to restart it, whether it's an SSRI like Prozac or Zoloft, Effexor or another SNRI, or an MAOI. Ayahuasca and other harmala brews, like Syrian rue, act as MAOIs too. And don't forget the supplements people don't think of as drugs, like 5-HTP, St John's wort and SAM-e.
2. If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early, since it blocks serotonin receptors. So far, the evidence for this med has only been case reports, and a recent review found them insufficient to show that it works. I wouldn't wait to see whether it does before doing the other steps.
3. When you call medics, ask for an ambulance, since the person can get worse fast, with seizures or dangerous overheating. Paramedics can start treating them right away, so don't drive them yourself, even if it seems faster. You can also call poison control to figure out which mix of drugs did this, but they can't treat serotonin syndrome, so I'd call them only once the ambulance is on its way.
4. Their twitching and tense muscles are making a lot of heat, and overheating is the most dangerous part of serotonin syndrome. So while you wait for the ambulance, move them somewhere cool, take off extra clothes, and use a fan and cool damp cloths. If they're hot to the touch, I'd also put ice packs wrapped in cloth at the neck, armpits or groin, where big blood vessels run close to the skin. A cold bath would cool them faster, but if they're confused, seizing or very weak, they could slip under or breathe in water, so don't put them in one. And though fever reducers like Tylenol or ibuprofen seem like the obvious thing for someone this hot, they do not help here, because this heat is generated by their muscles, not by a fever.
5. Since they're already overstimulated, it helps to clear out the crowd and stop any arguing. While it's natural for people who are overstimulated to start pacing or exercising to "burn it off," the problem is that creates more body heat, and it raises the risk of their muscles breaking down into the blood, which hurts the kidneys (rhabdomyolysis). But if they feel a strong urge to keep moving (akathisia), I'd have them do some fidgeting (especially of the calf muscle, flexing the feet), which can soothe that urge without the over-activity risk.
6. Overstimulating also dehydrates them, but we can't give fluids unless they're fully alert, able to swallow and not vomiting, and then just small sips of an electrolyte drink (a hypertonic one, more concentrated than an ordinary one, may be required if they still have symptoms of lack of electrolytes).
7. Before the ambulance comes, I'd gather their pill bottles and packages, so the ER doctors can see exactly what they took. Then I'd write down how much of each they took and when, including any recreational drugs and supplements. Mark any dose they raised lately, since that can be enough to set off serotonin syndrome. If you can take their temperature, pulse or blood pressure, write the numbers down with the time, so the doctors can see whether the person is getting better or worse.
8. While you wait, you might think of this guide's other remedies, like niacin for ending a trip or DMSO for brain injuries, seizures and chest pain, but I wouldn't use either one here, and niacin is actually unsafe with serotonin syndrome. For MDMA-related jaw tension or muscle activity, though, I would consider oral magnesium chloride if they're fully alert and able to swallow (the amounts are in MDMA: Magnesium Alarm, above).
