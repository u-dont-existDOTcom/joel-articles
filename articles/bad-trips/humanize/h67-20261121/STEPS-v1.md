1. Too much serotonin is what causes this, so **I'd stop anything suspected of adding more** (serotonergic substances): no redosing MDMA or other stimulants, and no more DXM cough syrup, tramadol or linezolid. Even their prescribed antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs or MAOIs) should stop until a doctor says it's OK to restart them. The same goes for harmala or ayahuasca brews, which act as MAOIs, and for supplements people don't think of as drugs, like 5-HTP, St John's wort and SAM-e.
2. **If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early**, since it blocks serotonin receptors. So far, the evidence for this med has only been [case reports](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9696181/), and [a recent review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39791184/) found them insufficient to show that it works. I wouldn't wait to see whether it does before doing the other steps.
3. **When you call medics, have an ambulance take them** rather than drive them yourself, since they can get worse fast, with seizures or dangerous overheating, and paramedics can start treating them on the way. Poison control can help if you're not sure which combination caused it, but they can't treat it, so I'd call them after the ambulance, not instead of it.
4. **While you wait for the ambulance, I'd cool them down**, because overheating from all that muscle activity is the most dangerous part of serotonin syndrome. Move them to a cool room, take off extra clothes, and use a fan and cool damp cloths. If they're hot to the touch, put ice packs wrapped in cloth at the neck, armpits or groin too. A cold bath would cool them faster, but if they're confused, seizing or very weak, they could slip under or breathe in water, so don't put them in one. You might also think of a fever reducer like Tylenol or ibuprofen, but fever reducers do not work on heat generated by muscles working this hard.
5. Since they're already overstimulated, it helps to clear out the crowd and stop any arguing. While it's natural for people who are overstimulated to start pacing or exercising to "burn it off," the problem is that creates more body heat, and it raises the risk of their muscles breaking down into the blood, which hurts the kidneys (rhabdomyolysis). But if they feel a strong urge to keep moving (akathisia), I'd have them do some fidgeting (especially of the calf muscle, flexing the feet), which can soothe that urge without the over-activity risk.
6. Overstimulating also dehydrates them, but we can't give fluids unless they're fully alert, able to swallow and not vomiting, and then just small sips of an electrolyte drink (a hypertonic one, more concentrated than an ordinary one, may be required if they still have symptoms of lack of electrolytes).
7. For the ER, **I'd bring their pill bottles and packages**, so the doctors can see exactly what they took. It also helps to write down what they took, recreational drugs and supplements included, with how much and when, and whether any dose was raised lately. If you can take their temperature, pulse or blood pressure, write those down too.
8. Once they're at the hospital, the staff may calm them with benzodiazepines, cool them, put them on IV fluids and monitors, and move them to intensive care if it gets severe. While you're waiting, though, don't reach for niacin or DMSO from this guide: niacin is unsafe with serotonin syndrome, and DMSO isn't for this. But if they're fully alert and able to swallow, I would consider oral magnesium chloride, especially with MDMA-related jaw tension or muscle activity.
