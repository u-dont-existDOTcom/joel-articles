- S2: 5 — "Watch for a headache that keeps getting worse" is a bare command, and its reason (low sodium) waits until the next sentence; his way gives the reason first and hangs the instruction on it with "so".
- S3: 7, 9 — it gives the trial's "about a third" before what decides whether that applies to them (how much they drank, which only comes in S4). It says what confusion or vomiting may mean, but not what to do once those signs show up (small sips is for before); his way would say first that drinking freely is what does it, then give the finding, then say what to do when those signs appear.
- S4: 4, 9 — "whose drinking was limited" doesn't say drinking what (water or alcohol) or limited to how much, and this deciding condition is tacked on after the finding; his way names it plainly and puts it ahead of the finding.
- S5: 2, 3, 4, 7 — it opens on two signs joined by "or", the same way S6 opens. "Instead" sets it against sodium rather than saying what it means, "weakness" doesn't say what kind counts, and nothing says what to do about it; his way would describe what they'd notice (muscles going weak, heart pounding or skipping), say what to do then, and build it differently from S6.
- S6: 2, 6 — it's built like S5 (two signs, then what they mean). It calls the danger "raises the stakes" and ends on a since-clause about other causes instead of on "call medics"; his way would end on calling medics, with the other causes cut or moved ahead of it.
