WHAT IT IS FOR: the reader's friend (or the reader) is on MDMA and their jaw has tensed; they are deciding what to do now and what to watch for

HEADING: MDMA: Jaw Tension Is the Magnesium Alarm (after the author's magnesium chloride method and his paragraph about his second wife, whose potassium was found essentially zero after an MDMA overdose with convulsions; the next paragraph is about people with impaired kidneys)

PARAGRAPH:
S1: Once their jaw tenses, I'd make that their last dose of MDMA and get them to slow down and cool off.
S2: I'd also watch how much plain water they drink, because if it's a lot, even a single normal dose raises the risk of their sodium dropping too low (an electrolyte drink, sipped when they're thirsty, is better).

Then list the sentences the reader doesn't need.

missing. These are not reasons to keep a sentence: it is true; it has a source; it is interesting; it is a careful
hypothesis or a testable guess; it explains a disease or a case the reader doesn't have; it qualifies another claim the
reader didn't need either; it was already in the text. Read also for sense: a sentence whose words don't make sense
together, or that mixes pictures (a dial, a door, a floor and a leak for one thing), goes on the list too, with why.
