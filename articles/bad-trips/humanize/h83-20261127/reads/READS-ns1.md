TEXT A (Beach Morning Glory, first paragraph)

S1: In my experience, beach morning glory is the ultimate sledgehammer for stopping a psychedelic trip cold and fast.
S2: By beach morning glory I mean *Ipomoea pes-caprae*, not morning glories in general, since none of the many relatives and cousins I tried worked the same way.
S3: People still find it growing in some places, under names like railroad vine.

NEEDED: none. S1 says what it is for, S2 which plant, S3 where. But S3 is needed and doesn't deliver: "where people find it" is the paragraph's job and "in some places" names no place, so the reader still can't tell whether to look for it (the kind of place it grows is the missing part; the section doesn't hold it).

- S3: "People still find it growing in some places, under names like railroad vine." — 4: "People", "some places" name nobody and nowhere. 12: "still" follows from nothing before it (nothing said it was going away). 7: missing what the reader needs to act, which is where to look. 10: "under names like railroad vine" could be the name it grows under or the name it is sold or known by.

TEXT B (Niacin, first two paragraphs)

S1: Flushing niacin has effectively aborted trips for me and friends, although I dislike it compared with beach morning glory.
S2: I include it because most people don't have access to *Ipomoea pes-caprae*.
S3: Niacin has been used for decades by orthomolecular doctors for psychotic conditions and seems to act both like a natural benzodiazepine and an antipsychotic.
S4: The clinical literature is discussed in *Niacin: The Real Story* by Abram Hoffer, M.D.

NEEDED:
- S4: "The clinical literature is discussed in *Niacin: The Real Story* by Abram Hoffer, M.D." — the reader knows why niacin is here and what it does without it; it is a source for the psychiatric use, not for ending trips. If it stays, it belongs inside S3 as where that use is written up.
- S1 is needed, and "although I dislike it" implies an act it doesn't say: use beach morning glory if you have it, niacin if you don't (S2 half says it).

Out"; "effectively" could be "successfully" or "in effect, nearly". 8: "dislike it compared with" is model wording for "I like it less than"; 3: "although" is stronger than the relation (it works, and he prefers another).
