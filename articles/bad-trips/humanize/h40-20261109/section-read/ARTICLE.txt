## When to Bring In Outside Help
## Call medics for:
- Trouble breathing, blue or gray lips, or respiratory depression
- A convulsive seizure lasting about five minutes, repeated seizures without normal recovery, or a first or substantially different seizure
- Collapse, inability to wake normally, or rapidly worsening consciousness
- Dangerous overheating, severe rigidity, or clonus with fever
- Sudden one-sided weakness, loss of speech, inability to walk normally, or loss of control of bodily functions
- A report that the brain feels damaged when the person cannot complete a basic neurological check or is rapidly worsening
- Severe, worsening, radiating, or exertional chest pain--or chest pain with breathlessness, sweating, faintness, neurological changes, or collapse
- Serious injury, uncontrolled bleeding, or a possible head or spinal injury
- Repeated vomiting, severe dehydration, blood, or severe abdominal pain
- Violence, self-harm, or a situation the sitter cannot physically contain
- Concerning symptoms after an MAOI, which slows the breakdown of serotonin (some older antidepressants, and the harmalas in ayahuasca and Syrian rue seeds), plus a serotonergic substance, one that raises serotonin, like MDMA or most antidepressants
The remedies below can begin while help is being arranged.
## Risk of Harm: When Peer Support Isn't Enough
Get urgent help when somebody may harm themselves or another person, cannot be kept physically safe, is moving toward roads, water, or heights, has command voices or fixed terrifying beliefs driving action, becomes violent, is severely confused and unreachable, has dangerous physical symptoms, has lost enough sleep that judgment is collapsing, or exceeds what the sitter can safely contain.
Use local emergency services for immediate danger or a medical emergency. In the United States, 988 can provide mental-health crisis guidance, de-escalation, and connection to local resources when no immediate physical emergency exists. Call 911 for immediate danger, a crime in progress, medical emergency, weapon, violence underway, or a situation you cannot safely contain. Outside the United States, identify the local emergency number and crisis line before you ever need them.

## Acute Medical and Neurological Rescue
An emergency means act now. It does not always mean that an ambulance is the first or only useful action. Some severe but physically stable altered states may respond faster to field rescue than to hours in an emergency room.
When the person is breathing normally, not dangerously hot, not seizing, has no neurological-injury concern, remains conscious enough to cooperate, and can be kept physically safe, calming and stabilizing the trip may be enough.
The emergency room is not magic, and for a physiologically stable psychedelic or practice-induced crisis it may be a poor environment. A scared, psychotic-feeling person often receives a benzodiazepine; those drugs don't reliably address psychosis and can cause paradoxical agitation or fear in some people. Antipsychotics may work better for psychosis, but they carry their own risks and interactions, often administered by someone who doesn't know what was taken, if anything.
## If Someone Says "My Brain Feels Damaged": DMSO + Humic Acid First
Do not dismiss that report because the person is high or cannot explain it in clinical language. It may be anxiety or excessive psychedelic intensity. It may also be acute neurological dysfunction, and the person experiencing it may be the least able to describe it neatly. DMSO is my first immediate remedy while I ask what the symptoms actually are. I usually know within about a minute whether it is helping. Smelling humic acid or tasting a tiny bit can also help a lot, in my experience, but I'm still collecting data from others.
While using DMSO, check wakefulness, breathing, temperature, speech, facial symmetry, both arms, balance, walking, bodily control, seizure activity, severe headache, repeated vomiting, chest pain, and vision. These checks include several recognized stroke warning signs. A person who cannot answer or cooperate has not passed the screen; their neurological status is still unknown. If the screen is reassuring but the brain-damage feeling remains, continue the DMSO-first route and reassess promptly.
I believe DMSO works better for stroke and most acute brain damage than anything else readily available to ordinary people. That judgment is stronger than the clinical evidence, but the evidence is not empty: a preliminary human ischemic-stroke trial reported possible neurological benefit; a small human series used DMSO for intracranial hypertension; and animal stroke research found substantial infarct reduction even when treatment began after ischemia. DMSO also suppresses NMDA- and AMPA-mediated excitotoxicity.
For objective stroke-like signs, I would use DMSO immediately **while someone calls medics**. DMSO cannot identify whether the problem is a clot, a hemorrhage, low oxygen, an electrolyte crisis, or something else. The larger adjacent risk is losing a treatment window or missing a bleed because the symptoms improved enough to feel reassuring. If someone is worried about a bleed, I'd probably skip DMSO, but it depends on how severe their issue is. It's not a powerful blood thinner like heparin, unless maybe injected; it's basically a dietary blood thinner. I've never had any issues with bleeding too much from consuming DMSO, and never heard of it either, but if bleeding is already a medical issue I'd want to think about that more.
1. **DMSO immediately. Smell humic acid first; if that doesn't help, take a 5mg lick of it. Smell the Metta NMT sniffer** (described in loveyhuasca guide).
2. **Ask what the symptoms actually are while the DMSO is being used.** Do not manufacture reassurance from missing information.
3. **If this is a psychedelic, use *Ipomoea pes-caprae* to stop the trip.** At this point safety matters more than preserving the experience.
4. **If this is MDMA, move to magnesium chloride and the broader electrolyte branch instead.**
5. **Bring in medical help in parallel** for objective neurological signs, dangerous heat, prolonged or repeated seizure, breathing trouble, severe chest pain, collapse, or when basic stability cannot be established.
## Active Seizure
Time the seizure. Protect the person from injury, clear dangerous objects, loosen tight clothing, and place them on their side when practical. Don't restrain them and don't put anything in their mouth while they are convulsing or not fully conscious.
If an appropriate rapid seizure-rescue benzodiazepine is available, use it by its intended route. Benzodiazepines are the established first-line drug class for status epilepticus. An ordinary pill is not the same thing as a nasal, buccal, rectal, intramuscular, or intravenous rescue preparation, and respiratory depressants in the mix change the risk.
Some people get a warning before a seizure takes hold, which is called an aura. That's when the things people report can still stop it, depending on what sets their seizures off. If it's flashing or flickering light, cover one eye with a hand and turn away from the light, because closing the eyes doesn't block enough of it. Some people stop a seizure at the warning stage with a strong smell like jasmine, and some train themselves to link one smell with feeling calm and then sniff it when a seizure threatens. When overstimulation is what sets it off, a cool, quiet, dark and comfortable space can help. I'd also try sniffing humic/fulvic acid powder, although that's just a hunch based on the effects I notice so far.
During the seizure, alongside any rescue medicine, I would use topical magnesium chloride and consider topical DMSO with a known-genuine CBD preparation (a lot of CBD is fake or very dilute). Seizures come in all different types, and we have no study on whether CBD could be a rescue remedy for a seizure (just anecdotes), even though we do have studies showing long-term effectiveness to prevent them: Purified oral CBD is approved for the seizures of Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome and tuberous sclerosis complex.
As for CBD/cannabis, people do smoke or vape it at the start of an aura, while they're still awake enough to do it on purpose. There are also people who find THC and/or CBD works as a nasal spray or sublingually, and there's even a medical THC nasal spray marketed for seizure interruption in Brazil by a non-profit patients' association called ABRACE. They testified in Congress that in more than 30 tests they did with children having seizures, the nasal spray ended those seizures within 30 seconds. You can see it being used on a child in this vid: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bgVi5o_2v_w
I also read on forums some swear it stops the seizure from going any further, and there was one woman whose EEG showed fewer seizure discharges on the days she smoked. But a few people on the same forums said THC vapes and concentrates brought their seizures on. And rats bred to have seizures had more of them after high-THC smoke.
As for topical CBD, it hasn't been well-studied. There was a short 12-week trial done, where they used a CBD skin gel, and although absorption was good, it didn't work any better than placebo to prevent seizures. The study authors suggested the dosing may have been too low, or that adults with focal epilepsy just don't respond as well as people with the syndromes oral CBD is approved for. The seizures in Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome are mostly generalized. Generalized are the type people usually have with MDMA & psychedelic overdoses, so topical CBD might do better for those than it did in that trial.
As for DMSO, it did reduce seizure activity in rats with temporal-lobe epilepsy, and we know it has several neuroprotective mechanisms, but it produced the opposite effect at some doses in an absence-epilepsy model. I'd treat it as a plausible experimental adjunct remedy rather than something to wait on while a seizure continues.
Call medics for a convulsive seizure lasting about five minutes, repeated seizures without normal recovery, trouble breathing, dangerous heat, serious injury, a first seizure, or a seizure substantially different from the person's known pattern.
## MDMA: Jaw Tension Is the Magnesium Alarm
With MDMA, I treat meaningful jaw tension as the earliest warning that the body needs more magnesium. This comes from repeated field experience rather than a direct clinical trial. Everyone I know who has taken enough magnesium chloride to make the jaw tension fully release has avoided the other complications I have seen when people let that tension continue.
A token dose may not be enough. On multiple occasions, I have dissolved **Dead Sea magnesium chloride hexahydrate flakes** in water and taken approximately **one gram per minute orally for around twenty minutes** before my jaw fully released. I have tolerated around twenty grams without diarrhea; for me, diarrhea has appeared only above roughly forty grams. Magnesium chloride is also generally less likely to cause diarrhea than several other replacement salts, including forms deliberately used as laxatives.
Interestingly, magnesium taken before the MDMA peak also makes the peak less euphoric for me. That suggests it may both meet a rapidly increasing magnesium need and directly moderate part of MDMA's excitatory effect. For harm reduction, slightly less euphoria is a reasonable price for a relaxed jaw and what appears in my experience to be a much lower risk of the state escalating.
My second wife had severe convulsions after an MDMA overdose. At the hospital, her potassium was found to be critically low--described to me as essentially zero--and IV potassium was the only treatment they gave her. That experience is why I treat serious MDMA toxicity as a broader electrolyte emergency, even though magnesium is the first and most useful early intervention when jaw tension appears.
Jaw tension should also mean no more MDMA, less exertion and heat, and closer monitoring. Worsening headache, confusion, vomiting, weakness, palpitations, collapse, or seizure raises concern about sodium, potassium, temperature, hydration, kidneys, and broader toxicity. MDMA is documented to cause acute kidney injury through several pathways, and controlled research confirms that hyponatremia can occur after a single dose.
**People with impaired kidneys do need to be more cautious with large, rapid magnesium doses because they may not clear magnesium normally. But they should be far more worried about the MDMA.** MDMA can cause overheating, rhabdomyolysis, dangerous electrolyte shifts, and acute kidney injury. Kidney impairment is a strong reason not to take MDMA--not a reason to sit there with worsening jaw tension after taking it. I would not tell somebody with serious kidney impairment to copy my one-gram-per-minute approach blindly; I would tell them to treat the situation as higher-risk and get help sooner.
## Chest Pain and Injury
DMSO is also my first remedy for many injuries and some chest pain. After I overused erythritol, I developed angina that lasted for days. I took DMSO, the pain disappeared within about a minute, and it never returned.
The cardiovascular science is preclinical but relevant. In rats, DMSO pretreatment reduced myocardial infarct size by about three quarters and sharply reduced cardiac injury markers. Other work found inhibition of tissue factor, platelet activation, and arterial thrombus formation. Myocardial-ischemia study; tissue-factor and thrombosis study; human platelet study.
Mild, familiar, non-progressive discomfort in a fully alert person is different from new, severe, persistent, worsening, radiating, or exertional pain--or pain with breathlessness, sweating, faintness, neurological changes, or collapse. In the second branch, I would use DMSO while arranging medical evaluation. If you're not sure which branch it is, treat it as the second. DMSO can also block pain signals, so pain relief alone does not prove that the underlying problem has ended.
## Recent Oral Toxic Ingestion
"Overdose" is too vague to choose a remedy. Ask what was swallowed, how much, when, and what the body is actually doing: respiratory depression, stimulant toxicity, serotonin toxicity, seizure, dangerous overheating, cardiovascular symptoms, severe agitation, objective neurological dysfunction, vomiting, diarrhea, or something else.
Activated charcoal may help after some recent oral ingestions when the substance binds to it and the person is fully alert enough to swallow safely. It does not bind every toxin, can adsorb necessary medication, and creates aspiration risk in somebody who is vomiting, sedated, seizing, or unable to protect the airway. Clinical review.
Poison control may be useful when the substance, combination, delayed risk, or value of charcoal is unclear. I do not treat it as a mandatory ritual; usefulness varies by country and by whoever answers. It is decision support, not first aid.
## Suspected Serotonin Syndrome (SS)
Serotonin syndrome overlaps with several symptoms above, but the rescue measures in the other branches do not necessarily treat it. It can follow serotonergic medication changes, combinations, or excessive exposure. Ayahuasca and other MAOI brews, MDMA, DXM, many antidepressants, tramadol, linezolid, other MAOIs, and some opioids can contribute, especially in combinations.
## Recognizing serotonin toxicity
Clinicians often use the Hunter criteria. One or more of the following patterns makes serotonin toxicity more likely:
- **Spontaneous clonus:** involuntary rhythmic muscle contractions and relaxations occurring without stimulation.
- **Inducible clonus plus agitation or sweating.**
- **Ocular clonus plus agitation or sweating:** rapid rhythmic involuntary eye movements.
- **Tremor plus hyperreflexia:** shaking together with abnormally brisk reflexes.
- **Hypertonia plus fever:** rigidity with temperature above 38°C (100.4°F), together with ocular or inducible clonus.
This is a recognition aid rather than a command to wait for every criterion before acting. Dangerous heat, rigidity, clonus, confusion, or rapid worsening deserves medical help.
## Do this immediately when serotonin toxicity is reasonably suspected:
1. **Stop all suspected serotonergic substances.** No more SSRI, SNRI, MAOI, tramadol, MDMA, DXM cough syrup, 5-HTP, St John's wort, SAM-e, linezolid, stimulant redoses, or harmala/ayahuasca-type MAOI exposure.
2. **If the person is fully alert and able to swallow, I would give cyproheptadine early.** It antagonizes serotonin receptors. Case reports describe rapid improvement, including complete resolution within two hours in a small series, while a recent systematic review found the evidence for major outcomes inconclusive. Case series; systematic review. I would not wait to see whether it works before cooling, stopping serotonergic substances, and arranging help for moderate or severe signs.
3. **Call emergency help and arrange transport for moderate or severe signs.** Don't drive yourself. Poison control may help when the combination is unclear, but it is not the treatment.
4. **Cool the person without delaying transport.** Move to a cool room, remove excess clothing, use a fan and cool damp cloths, and place ice packs wrapped in cloth at the neck, armpits, or groin when the person is hot. Don't put a confused, seizing, or very weak person in a bath. Fever reducers do not solve heat generated by extreme muscle activity.
5. **Keep stimulation low and prevent exertion.** No pacing to "burn it off," exercise, crowds, or arguments. Akathisia can create a strong urge to move, but muscle activity can worsen heat and rhabdomyolysis risk.
6. **Hydrate only when fully alert and not vomiting.** Small sips of an appropriate oral rehydration drink are reasonable. Don't force fluids, food, pills, or charcoal when someone is confused, vomiting, sleepy, or at seizure risk.
7. **Gather the evidence for the ER.** Bring pill bottles and packages. Write down exact substances, dose, timing, last dose, recent increases, supplements, recreational drugs, temperature, pulse, and blood pressure when available.
8. Hospitals may use benzodiazepines, external cooling, IV fluids, monitoring, and intensive supportive care. Niacin is unsafe for serotonin syndrome. DMSO is also not part of this branch. I would consider oral magnesium chloride when the person is fully alert, especially with MDMA-related jaw tension or muscle activity.

## My Acute Rescue Hierarchy of Remedies
This hierarchy is for acute situations, including emergencies--not merely uncomfortable trips.
In general it's good to try all these things in very low, increasing doses before they are needed in crisis.
Sometimes the mind isn't in a metaphysical crisis. The body feels awful and the altered mind writes cosmic horror to explain it. Nausea, heat, exhaustion, dehydration, overstimulation, and sleep loss all become spiritual content very quickly.
A horrible thought isn't automatically a revelation. The body may need quiet, water, cooling, warmth, rest, or a calmer room. The body is part of the spiritual field.
The hierarchy appears before the mystical sections because love doesn't require us to ignore the body.
A trip with diarrhea, nausea, toxic heaviness, a dirty body-feeling, loss of bodily control, jaw tension, neurological disorganization, or the sense that the mind is going crazy because the gut or brain is going crazy belongs in a different branch from a mainly emotional crisis. A gut-driven mental hell is often mistaken as shadow work.

## How I label the evidence
- **Established acute treatment:** standard seizure first aid, rapid rescue benzodiazepines, cooling dangerous hyperthermia, and treating documented electrolyte abnormalities.
- **Limited human evidence:** interventions with clinical reports or small trials but no strong modern outcome evidence.
- **Mechanism or preclinical evidence:** biologically plausible effects shown mainly in cells or animals.
- **Personal field signal:** what I have repeatedly observed in myself or other people. Repetition and a clear immediate endpoint make an anecdote more useful, but they do not turn it into a population rate.
## Some Remedies Require More Caution
Humic acid *when eaten*, can produce a hyperintelligent state for me. From the outside that may look manic, and it could destabilize somebody prone to mania or without meditation training. I wouldn't use it casually during a bad trip. Smelling it does not produce that hyperintelligent state but does feel relaxing and very beneficial for brain health, so I'd start with that.
Cinnamon can produce an intense "brain surgery" or "brain scraping" effect, especially the first time it combines with an ayahuasca-like state. That may be therapeutic in the right context, but it is not a casual rescue tool.
I would also be extremely careful with *Acacia confusa* eaten with *Mimosa hostilis*. For me, that combination produced hellishly intense psychosis-like and autism-like states before transforming into a week of extraordinary creativity and joy. The later benefit did not make the crisis recommendable.
Iboga--especially male iboga--potentiates many drugs and can be dangerous. A low amount combined well for me; doubling it became dangerously intense. I include that only as a warning.

## Gut-Toxin and Parasite-Die-Off Tools
These are the first things I would use, even preventively on the come-up, because I want to address possible toxins before assigning the distress to something more mysterious.
## Zeolite and Ferment in **ZHoly3O**
When the trip feels like sick stomach, nausea, diarrhea, toxic heaviness, a dirty body-feeling, parasite die-off, or mental hell driven by gut hell, zeolite and the ZHoly3O ferment are often my first thoughts.
Many ferments help to different degrees, including kombucha, kimchi, and apple vinegar. I prefer fermented beet juice made with apple vinegar and salt. Smelling the ferment helps a lot. Drinking a spoonful can also help, although it may cause itching in somebody who breaks down histamine poorly, which then requires activated charcoal to reduce gut histamine.
In my experiments, zeolite has to be prepared correctly. I use powdered zeolite rather than nano-liquid zeolite. Many products did nothing for me, and quality mattered enormously. Most zeolites have clogged pores because they haven't been cleaned.
For my ozonated zeolite, I mix the powder into water and use an ozone-water purifier in a glass vessel because ozone destroys plastic. I mean ozonating water, never inhaling ozone gas. With a machine producing about two grams of ozone per hour, ten minutes produced a working zeolite for me. Less time did not work as well, and more time did not noticeably improve it.
I also keep zeolite away from metal during preparation. Zeolite adsorbs metals, which is part of why it is used for heavy-metal removal, and I don't want it sitting against a metal vessel or utensil before ingestion. I use glass for ozonation and storage whenever possible.
Most sober people don't feel much from zeolite. Its effect becomes more obvious when a trip has a toxic or gut-driven component. I put a spoonful into water--some people need a teaspoon, others a tablespoon--and sip slowly. Rubbing it on the body or simply holding the jar also helps me.
## Actual Activated Charcoal
Activated charcoal can help gut toxicity, but it adsorbs drugs. Sometimes that is exactly the point. It can also flatten or reduce a trip, so I don't treat charcoal and zeolite as interchangeable.
When the goal is to reduce drug absorption or stop a toxic gut situation, charcoal may be relevant. When I want to preserve the trip while improving the gut feeling, I prefer zeolite.
Charcoal can also adsorb medications somebody depends on. I would use it cautiously around necessary medicines and tell the medics if charcoal was taken.

## **DMSO**
DMSO is another stabilizer I use when a trip becomes too intense and I don't want to abort it. It is also my primary emergency neuroprotectant when the problem feels like acute neurological damage, altered consciousness, loss of bodily control, or severe drug-related brain dysfunction.
It overlaps with aged ozonated-charcoal water, but the two don't always work equally. Sometimes DMSO works better; sometimes the water does. I don't yet know why. In my experience there is generally no harm in taking both or mixing them. Ozonated-charcoal water has what I consider a perfect safety profile; DMSO is a close second in my experience.
**Side effects:**
- **Astringency:** 100% DMSO usually irritates skin on the upper half of the body. When it itches, dilute it with water or real aloe juice without added chemicals. Leaving 100% DMSO on the skin can crenate it, and the skin may take a week to look plump again. DMSO can also burn dry mouth tissue, so the mouth should be moist first.
- **Nausea:** when drinking DMSO, dilute it in water, juice, or milk and sip gradually. Sensitivity varies.
- **Reduced sleep:** DMSO reduces how long I sleep when I take too much, take it too late, or use it over too many consecutive days. It builds up past a threshold that seems to differ between people. In rats, higher doses made sleep lighter. Some people sleep better with DMSO because it reduces whatever symptom had been keeping them awake.
- **Body odor:** above a certain dose you may smell like sulfur. I don't smell when I put it into milk.
**Interactions:**
- Don't put it on dirty skin; it can carry chemicals inward.
- Don't take it with drugs that should not absorb rapidly: DMSO can strengthen other medicines taken with it. With harmalas, don't put the harmala in the DMSO or take them at exactly the same time; taking them a bit apart is totally fine, and the point is to take DMSO if you've already absorbed the harmalas and the aya is too crazy.
- Don't combine it with cholinergic drugs because DMSO is also cholinergic, although I haven't found anecdotes of this causing a real problem.
**Contraindications:**
- DMSO has produced both anticonvulsant and proconvulsant effects depending on dose and seizure model. I consider it a plausible adjunct for excitotoxic or temporal-lobe-type seizure activity, not a proven universal seizure rescue.
- It may worsen a cluster headache, even though rubbing DMSO--especially with magnesium chloride--on the head can alleviate tension headaches.
- It may release histamine in some people. Use caution with MCAS.
## Syrian Rue Oil Makes the Trip Gentle & Sweet
Syrian rue (*Peganum harmala*) can be mixed into warm/hot oil (like coconut oil), just enough to cover it, then squeezed out thru cheese cloth. Within some minutes of the seeds soaking in oil (if it's hot, or maybe an hour if it's warm), the oil contains the oil-soluble harmalas, not harmine, harmaline, or THH in quantities that could make it a potent MAOI. In my experience there's no MAOI effect in Syrian rue extracted with oil (it won't activate DMT, rather it reduces DMT's effects). These oil-soluble harmalas have no effect when eaten, but when absorbed thru mucous membranes or vaped, they chill out psychedelic trip intensity, making them more gentle and sweet. On their own, they have some mild psychedelic properties though (like I felt like half of my head was sort of transparent), and significant mood boosting properties.
I have a friend with schizophrenia who would use about 1-2mL of this oil rectally when he was in psychosis, which helped at least as much as niacin and DMSO, equally effective as Geodon, an antipsychotic his doctors prescribed which makes him feel like a zombie.
Another option is vaping the whole Syrian rue seeds, as it seems that these oil soluble harmalas are concentrated on the outside of the seeds. Once ground up, vaping the powder is more stimulating, less relaxing (but variable since all is mixed together). However, if vaping the whole seeds, I would not assume there's no MAOI effect (even though it still seems to reduce DMT effects) since depending maybe on temperature and time, you might also vaporize the harmine and harmaline.

## Beach Morning Glory--*Ipomoea pes-caprae*
By beach morning glory I mean *Ipomoea pes-caprae*, rather than morning-glory plants in general. The distinction matters. I have tried many related plants and cousins, and they did not work the same way. As far as I know, nobody else sells it.
Its primary role in this guide is not ordinary nausea control. In my experience, *Ipomoea pes-caprae* is the ultimate sledgehammer for stopping a psychedelic trip cold and fast.
A tiny nibble can sometimes smooth psychedelic nausea and improve the trip without killing it, but the difference between that amount and a trip-aborting amount is narrow. I use it for nausea only when the person is comfortable with the possibility that the trip may end.
Even picking a leaf can affect me when the broken juicy part touches sensitive skin, especially under the fingernails. Avoiding contact with the broken portion prevents that effect.
A whole leaf can easily end the trip and, in my experience, may prevent tripping for days afterward.
This was not only personal. I once gave beach morning glory to a woman experiencing nausea and dysphoria during a psychedelic state. I was rushing to help and may have given more than I would now. Within a couple of minutes she shifted from miserable to very happy. That helped convince me the effect wasn't limited to my body.
I do not yet have data showing that pes-caprae aborts MDMA. MDMA remains a separate branch centered on magnesium, temperature, sodium, potassium, hydration, serotonin toxicity, seizure, and cardiovascular symptoms.

## A Final Trip Killer: Niacin (a Form of Vitamin B3)
Flushing niacin has effectively aborted trips for me and friends, although I dislike it compared with beach morning glory.
I include it because most people don't have access to *Ipomoea pes-caprae*, although I supply it. A vitamin is not automatically harmless. Niacin has been used for decades by orthomolecular doctors for psychotic conditions and seems to act both like a natural benzodiazepine and an antipsychotic. The clinical literature is discussed in *Niacin: The Real Story* by Abram Hoffer, M.D.
**Side effects:**
- **Niacin flush:** itchy sunburn-like heat. This is normal, although some people find it intolerable and others enjoy it.
- Heartburn in sensitive stomachs or after too much at once.
- Drowsiness, usually with terrible sleep quality.
- Higher or repeated doses can create real risk, especially with liver problems, alcohol, medications, or sensitive physiology.
Other forms of vitamin B3 seem less reliable for relaxation. Niacinamide sometimes relaxes people and sometimes creates hyperfocus, depending on their state. Avoid sustained-release niacin and the useless non-flush hexanicotinate form.

## Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes
Anything that could let someone hurt themselves in the next five minutes takes priority, so get the car keys out of reach and turn the stove off, and move them away from roads and balconies, or pools of water for that matter. Letting them move around is fine, and pacing can help, but keep them from taking more of any substance. If they want to go confront somebody, that can wait. And if they want to leave, have someone go with them. Put any weapons away and keep the person away from crowds and strangers. If they want to post their big revelation about the universe, or message an ex, take the phone.
They're feeling overwhelmed, so dim the lights, and clear out the extra people around them.
Sometimes it can be helpful to remind them where they are and what's happening, since a trip can make them lose track of the fact that they took a substance which has temporary effects.
If a practice like meditation or fasting brought this on, have them stop it now, since doing more of it only makes this stronger. There's no drug wearing off here, so it could be over soon or take a while.
As the person helping, you want to play the role of the calm, caring anchor to the safe present moment, not the witch doctor guru who knows magic spells. So don't force eye contact, touch, music, prayer, chanting on them, just offer gently if it might help. After they've slept, they may wish to talk more about their experience.
But if they want to talk about it right now, don't try to win an argument with their visions, the entities they spoke to, the memories that surfaced, or whatever cosmic conclusions they reached ten minutes ago... But don't let silence validate anything harmful either, like a plan that ends with somebody getting hurt.
Sometimes people get stuck in a panic loop, where they make interpretations to try to solve a feeling of panic, but then those create another kind of panic. It's helpful to remind them that sometimes feelings just need to be felt, so they can move and release, and then thinking can be more clear afterwards.
If they're being attacked by a demon, let your care help them connect to love, which cancels fear. Hopefully they've been practicing that prior. Don't encourage them to believe they are crazy, impure, weak or broken. While many interpretations are possible, a therapeutic one would not be that they're being punished or failing the medicine.

What actually helped me in one case when receiving this stern order from a higher power, was to ask it to clarify. I said, "I agree, that makes sense as a Buddhist, and I'd like to leave those behind at some point, especially money. But what's the best timeline to benefit all involved? ## Stillness or Movement?
Test stillness and movement rather than turning them into rival religions. Try one for two minutes and ask: *Does this make me safer and clearer, or more activated and impulsive?*
When lying down feels like imprisonment, pace slowly in a contained space. When pacing begins to resemble escape, sit against a wall or lie under a blanket. The goal is to let the nervous system discharge without creating a new danger.
**Parallel processing** can help when lying still turns into immobile introspection. Let one part of attention stay with the body, fear, grief, or memory while another part completes a simple, safe task with a visible endpoint: sort ten objects, draw, fold five towels, or wash one cup. Finishing something small helps confirm that time is moving and ordinary reality is still here. Stop if the task increases agitation, speed, or risk.
A useful order is **room → body → human → time → meaning**. Most people begin with meaning because the experience feels cosmic. When the nervous system is on fire, love becomes practical first.
## Grounding Menu: Choose One or Two Things
Grounding is love becoming practical. Choose one or two options rather than all of them. Twelve simultaneous techniques can become another form of panic.
**Body**
- Feel both feet against the floor.
- Put one hand on the heart and one on the solar plexus.
- Hold something with weight or texture.
- Slowly unclench the jaw, hands, belly, and shoulders.
**A familiar object**
- Choose it ahead of time, while calm and regulated: a childhood toy, stuffed animal, note, photograph, blanket, stone, or another object that represents your inner Nurturer or Protector--the adult part of you that offers care or safety.
- During a difficult state, let it remind you what you already decided: "I'm here. I protect this body. No big decisions tonight."
- Use it as a cue, not an oracle. It does not issue commands or interpret the trip.
- Pair the comfort with one adult action: contact the grounded person, move away from danger, drink some water when safe, or prepare for sleep.
- A calm pet can offer the same quiet company when the animal is comfortable and the situation is safe.
**Breath**
- Exhale longer than you inhale for ten breaths.
- Try inhaling for four and exhaling for six, without strain.
- Or breathe softly and slowly through the nose, taking in slightly less air than desired until there is faint air hunger.
- Never force breath-holds while intoxicated or panicked. Stop for increased fear, dizziness, chest pain, or medical concern.
- Whisper: "This is a wave. I can love the part of me that feels it."
**Music**
- Silence is a real option.
- Use familiar music when possible. I wouldn't force a predesigned emotional arc or an unfamiliar therapeutic playlist on somebody who dislikes it.
- Try a steady drum rhythm for stabilization or grounding; try airy harp-like or meditative music when the person wants something gentler and spiritually uplifting.
- Soothing meditative music, like that of Chantress Seba and others on my music page, can be very helpful.
- Keep the stop or change control immediate.
**Room**
- Name five ordinary objects.
- Look at one stable object and describe it plainly.
- Say: "This is a wall. This is a blanket. This is my hand."
**Human**
- Ask one calm person to stay as the lead anchor.
- Let them remind you where you are.
- Don't process with the most reactive person in your life.
**Time**
- Say the date, place, and approximate time.
- Remember that these states usually rise, peak, and change.
- Put every big decision into tomorrow's notebook.
**Ordinary world**
- Eat simple healthy food, such as fruit, when you can.
- Walk slowly with a clear-headed person in a safe environment.
- Go outside only away from roads, heights, crowds, and water.
- Avoid strenuous exercise with overheating, chest pain, faintness, severe confusion, stimulant use, an unknown mixture, or any medical concern.
**Movement evidence note:** the best-supported general advice is to create a safe, low-stimulation environment and choose responses that reduce danger. A 2024 study of strategies for resolving challenging psychedelic experiences identified physical movement, intentional breathing, sensory engagement, sleep-seeking, and social support among the ways people navigate difficulty.
**Buteyko evidence note:** published evidence for Buteyko-style breathing mainly concerns asthma and breathing retraining, rather than psychedelic or spiritual-practice crisis. I include it because panic-related over-breathing can lower CO<sub>2</sub> and directly cause tingling, lightheadedness, confusion, and unreality feelings (Hopkins Medicine). Use the simpler longer-exhale option first. Never strain or turn breathing into another achievement.
## Get One Human Anchor
Many bad trips get worse because somebody is alone with a frightened or over-certain mind. Other times it's the opposite: too many people crowd in and start advising, arguing, spiritualizing, or panicking.
So **one calm person should be the anchor** and take the lead. One person can only handle so much, though. When fatigue or logistics get to be too much, or keeping them safe does, or there's a risk of violence or self-harm, bring in **one more calm adult or a crisis professional** to back them up.
**Script for the person in the altered state:**
> I'm in an intense psychedelic or altered state and I need grounding. I don't need you to solve my life. Please stay with me, speak slowly, remind me where I am, and help me not make any big decisions until tomorrow.
**Script for the sitter or friend:**
> I'm here. You're not alone. This is a wave. We don't have to solve your whole life right now. Let's make your body safe, keep things simple, and stay together.
## Alone at 3 a.m.
The triage app has the stripped-down 3 a.m. plan. Once the lamp is on, the keys are away, and **"No big actions until morning"** is visible, use the phone only to reach a calm person or support service.
Send the human-anchor script.
While you wait, put one hand on your heart and the other on your solar plexus just under your chest, and say to yourself: "This feeling is allowed to move through. I can love the part of me that feels it."
When the body still needs movement, keep it contained and boring: brush your teeth, stretch gently, take a calm shower, or do a few slow wall push-ups. Then protect sleep.
## For the Person Helping: Calm Anchor, Not Guru
The Zendo Project, a harm-reduction group that offers peer support at festivals and other events to people having a hard time in an altered state, sums up its approach in four principles: creating a safe space, sitting with the person rather than guiding them, talking them through it rather than down from it, and remembering that difficult isn't necessarily bad.
Crying, grieving, remembering, or touching a genuine wound calls for loving awareness. Interrogation, analysis every thirty seconds, and "bro, you're purging ancestral karma" while somebody white-knuckles the carpet are generally less helpful.
Stay present. If it helps, you can say to yourself: *This feeling is allowed to move through. I don't have to become it. I can love the part of me that feels it.* Or simply: *Can I send love to the one who's suffering?*
## Do
- Ask simple questions: "Would less light help?" "Music or quiet?" "Closer or farther away?" "Water?" (in small sips) "Need the bathroom?"
- Reflect without arguing: "That sounds terrifying." "I'm with you." "Let's breathe together."
- Keep the room quiet, private, warm, and physically safe, and move anyone they're wary of somewhere else.
- Call for help early when safety is slipping.
- Translate metaphysics into shared safety values: "This feels sacred and huge. Sacred power protects life tonight."
- Set behavioral boundaries without humiliation: "I won't help anybody hurt themselves or another person. We are keeping every body safe."
## When What They Say Turns Toward Harm
When somebody says, "I am God, so I can kill," leading with "No you're not" or "That's delusional" can turn the encounter into a status fight. Nodding along as though harm is spiritually neutral is also unacceptable. Treat the statement as a safety signal and build a bridge back toward non-harm.
**Non-combative philosophical grounding:**
> I hear how vast and certain this feels. Many traditions say God is in everyone. That makes every body sacred, not disposable. Peace is the godly move tonight. No proof is needed. We protect life and sleep first.
Other useful lines: "Maybe you are touching something divine. Divine doesn't need a violent test." "Tonight the commandment is no harm, no proof, no mission, sleep first."
Redirect within the person's value-world toward peace and protection, increase containment, and bring in crisis support as early and as non-coercively as the situation allows.
When talk of harm continues, shift from philosophy to safety. Create distance from potential targets and keep your own exit. Don't block, crowd, threaten, mock, or physically wrestle away a weapon. Quietly remove means only when that can be done without confrontation or turning yourself into the target.
Call crisis or emergency services for a specific target, available means, movement toward action, escalating agitation, a weapon, violence underway, or a situation you can't contain.
Be brief and specific with emergency services: "Someone is in an altered-state or mental-health crisis, has threatened to harm others, may have access to [means if known], and needs a medical or mental-health crisis response with de-escalation support. ## No Rash Actions: Visions Are Not Commands
An intense altered state can make everything feel final, cosmic, and urgent, and it might feel like you have to act right now. But more likely the urgency is just part of the state, and acting on it isn't safe.
**Put yourself on a 24–72 hour action lock.**
Until you have slept and eaten, maybe don't drive, travel, break up, make up, quit your job, confess everything, confront or accuse that list of people you've been meaning to... Think twice before sending whatever ridiculous message you're planning on to Judge Amos, posting that revelation you'll forget tomorrow, giving away your BMW, buying a new BMW. After you decide that you are permanently enlightened or permanently damaged, and you explained the whole universe to your parents in 42 seconds, you can still change your mind the next day.
**Write it down, get some sleep, and go back over it with one grounded person once your head is clear.**
Treat trip content first as **experience-content**, rather than fact-content. Because you saw it, it might feel like you have to act on it, and because it felt cosmic, obeying it might seem wise. But more likely you had an experience, and it can wait until the state has passed.
**No revelation makes murder, assault, coercion, sexual boundary-crossing, reckless driving, or self-harm safe tonight.** Anything requiring violated consent or harm belongs in the notebook until you can review it with a clear head.
## When the Trip Feels Amazing: Euphoric Certainty
The action lock matters especially when the state feels blissful: *I am God. I am chosen. Everybody must hear this tonight. I finally understand everything.* Bliss is not proof of safety, and it often makes grounding less welcome.
**Two questions while glowing:**
1. **Have I slept?** Sleep comes before mission.
2. **Has this happened before, and how did it end?** A recurring pattern with lost sleep, racing thoughts, pressured speech, or everyone saying you are not yourself needs grounded support before action.
The revelation will still exist after breakfast. If it is true, it survives sleep. If it cannot survive one night of rest, it was an emergency impulse wearing angel wings.

## (Re)defining "Bad Trip"
A bad trip is one of those terms thrown around very loosely in the psychedelic space. It can refer to various things which may be intertwined.
When too much hits you at once, your nervous system can become flooded, and nothing makes sense, even as the brain still tries to derive meanings that might create unhelpful feedback loops. Underlying those interpretations are factors like the dose of what drug(s) you took and how your body handles them, or how hard you pushed a practice. Old painful memories could have been triggered, or you may have gone in with a fearful or unloving mindset, or the setting or people around you could have fed into it. Be kind to yourself or to whoever's going through it, because blaming anyone only makes it heavier.
Some say there's no such thing as a bad trip, because it's all a doorway to shadow work. But if you had no intention of doing shadow work, that explanation might have come too late. Other bad trips seem unrelated to shadow work, although any negative experience can lead into it. Samsara offers no shortage of difficult experiences to process. It still makes sense to reduce present-moment trauma so we can address what was already there.
Sometimes focusing on what we want makes the blockage visible:
A bad trip or destabilizing state may also come from drug interactions, sleep deprivation, cannabis, hunger, dehydration, sexual or social pressure, medical symptoms, psychosis-like symptoms, fasting, intense breathwork, meditation overload, or retreat pressure.
Cognitive distortions from Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) can create or prolong the crisis:
- **Catastrophizing:** "This will never end."
- **Emotional reasoning:** "It feels true, so it must be true."
- **Mind reading:** "Everyone can see I am broken."
- **Fortune-telling:** "My life is ruined now."
- **All-or-nothing thinking:** "Either I am enlightened or I am doomed."
- **Personalization:** "This cosmic horror is specifically my fault."
- **Spiritual bypassing:** "Because everything is love, I don't need boundaries, doctors, sleep, or consequences."
## Spiritual ≠ "Getting Your Ass Kicked"
The triage above is meant to make trips useful. Some people believe they deserve suffering because of karma or self-hatred. Suffering by itself fixes nobody. Suffering that yields insight requires enough clarity of mind and heart to learn from it.
This is one side of the difference between spiritual bypassing and spiritual practice:
Bypassing reaches for an ultimate view--"we are all One," "I don't exist," "this is all karma," "everything is Love"--while ignoring the person shaking in the corner.
Practice says love is here, so we make the room safer, call for help when needed, stop pretending, and hold the experience without becoming it.
That is tripsitting, shadow work, and the path becoming practical.
## The Other Bypass: Feeling Good Forever
A well-rounded trip should offer practical lessons and contact with suffering as well as freedom from suffering, lest we become "so spiritually high that we are no earthly good." "Life is full of suffering" is Buddha's First Noble Truth. Ignoring that side of life becomes dissociation or spiritual bypassing in the opposite direction, followed eventually by statements like "Everything is Love, including violence."
Many people meditate or take psychedelics to feel good. I am definitely one of them, and Buddha also said meditation should generally feel good. My friend Eshwar, however, found that shadow work made up about 99% of his path to heart-chakra awakening.
Modern people carry so much trauma--and some people did even in Buddha's time--that shadow work and inner-child reparenting may need to come first, even before authentic liberation from the identity illusion.
When shadow work is bypassed in meditation and therapy, it may appear in ordinary life without explaining itself. I encountered a lot of it in my first two marriages, to my bewilderment. I wish everybody could keep shadow work inside the safer rooms of meditation, therapy, journaling, prayer, and honest conversation.
Shadow work to me is neither BDSM nor "feeding your demons." It means bringing loving awareness to the parts of the mind built in fear, pain, humiliation, rejection, and survival. Inner-child reparenting is central. Most negative mind-states aren't some error in your philosophy, they're just young parts of you that are still waiting for love and protection and permission, and for the explanation nobody ever gave them.

## After the Storm
Don't make huge decisions or diagnose yourself from an intense night. You don't need to assume every vision was literal, nor that anything you realized is actually true (or false). Ask your body what it needs from you now. I'm betting it will tell you: Give me sleep, natural food, pure water, a nice nature walk. It helps to integrate the experience via journaling your feelings, and talking with someone grounded (not the 20 chaotic people who are still coming down). To prevent your mind from giving spirituality a negative connotation, engage in some calm prayer or gentle meditation.
## What Kind of Aftermath Is This?
When symptoms linger, people too often put everything into the "I'm broken" bucket, which solves nothing. While a professional can be called in to help at any point, self-diagnostics and basic fix strategies may obviate the need for one. Try to find the underlying psychological and/or physiological patterns driving the symptoms:
- **Anxiety and fear:** with this pattern, it helps to first figure out what is providing the stimulation for the anxiety. CBT-style reality testing, checking the frightening thought against what's actually happening, is one way to do that which could be explored with a therapist if it persists, but can be done without one to start with. In general, it can help to have grounding/relaxing routines (like morning or bedtime yin yoga, meeting a friend for lunch).
- **Sleep disturbance and nightmares:** I'd first ensure no more substances are being used, including "normal" ones like coffee, which some people become extra sensitive to after certain psychedelics (especially iboga). Pay attention to sleep hygiene, especially what you eat at what time, and what you might not be processing during the day which then grabs attention at night.
- **Derealization and depersonalization (when the world, or you yourself, feel unreal):** Is it due to isolation? Rumination (which often goes along with isolation)? Peer and family support are crucial to combat those. Some things that tend to help are related to reconnecting with the feelings in your body: taking walks in nature (with bare feet for grounding to the Earth), doing gentle yin yoga, and Somatic Experiencing, a therapy that works through body sensations.
- **Visual distortions/Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD)-like symptoms:** These can be long-lasting, and people often end up just getting used to it, but it helps to reduce stress and avoid any further use of psychedelics. I'd also try DMSO, niacin, and other neuroprotective/restorative treatments.
- **Social disconnection and shame:** The biggest thing here is if you can find one or two trusted people who won't judge you. Get whatever it is off your chest. If you can't find someone like that, talk to a therapist. You'd be surprised how many people have gone through something like this and come out the other side realizing it wasn't what they made it out to be, myself included. Check my guide on shame for more info.
- **Existential shock:** This one's pretty normal if someone has a deep journey, especially one they weren't prepared for. It helps to go back one step to focus on loving kindness and sending love to the inner child before getting lost in a philosophical ocean. Finding others who've been through a similar experience could be helpful, or harmful, depending on which rabbit hole it was you fell into. I find reading the Buddhist suttas supremely helpful.
- **Paranoia or psychosis-like beliefs:** Here we gotta be careful because this can spiral out of control and get dangerous quickly. I'd seek professional support while utilizing the relatively safe and natural antipsychotics mentioned in this guide, like DMSO, syrian rue OIL, and niacin.
- **Somatic hyperarousal:** This is not rumination, but more like akathisia, bodily restlessness. Try gentle movements (that yin yoga again!), slower breathing, grounding, and medical care if the symptoms are severe, unusual or longer-lasting than expected. I'd still go for my trusty relaxing herbs first, like beach morning glory, Syrian rue oil or the Metta NMT sniffer, and natural antipsychotics like DMSO and niacin.
**No "another ceremony will fix this" reflex.**
Don't take more psychedelics, cannabis or ketamine, do intense breathwork, fast, push kundalini, deprive yourself of sleep, meditate or stretch to extremes, or schedule "another ceremony to fix it" while destabilized. Deep practice can wait until the floor is back.
Then ask what actually happened. What was medical or environmental? What was emotional? What was old trauma? What was fantasy? What was useful? What changed behavior? What needs therapy, an apology, rest, or plain practical action?
It can feel like the more cosmic the story sounds, the bigger the insight. But the fruit of an insight is whether suffering goes down and love gets more available.
## Bring in mental-health support when:
- Psychosis-like symptoms outlast the acute state.
- Sleep won't return.
- Mania or suicidal thinking appears.
- Panic won't settle.
- The person can't function afterward.
- The trauma material is bigger than the room can hold.
## Trip After Trip? Hang up the phone, get off the Healing Treadmill
If you use psychedelics (or MDMA) to achieve a goal like healing or spiritual liberation, you need to already be on the same path so that they aren't radically changing your ordinary understanding of things. If the trip is so crazy that you can't believe it a few days later, chances are, like me, you'll end up taking that drug again and again to understand it.
With healthy drugs like Loveyhuasca, which has literally saved my life on numerous occasions from unknown infections, there's less of an argument for "Hang up the phone" as Alan Watts famously said you should do "once you get the message." But what we shouldn't be doing is feeling like we need to use these substances *because we can't integrate them.*
YouTube video 9yp0lvYKK6Q
That happened to me at first when I started with ayahuasca. The ego deaths were so far beyond my ordinary understanding of reality that I dismissed them each time within a week. Then I felt a compulsion to have another ego death soon after. Once I learned to meditate into the experience (and beyond) without ayahuasca, that compulsion was gone. If I had learned about Buddhism and if I could have accepted that my view of reality was limited prior to ayahuasca, then probably just one or two ego deaths would have been enough inspiration for me.
With toxic drugs like MDMA & ketamine, that can be a recipe for long-term harm to your physical, and/or mental health. Ketamine is super easy to abuse because it's a dissociative psychedelic. All your problems are magically **gone, **and if you take enough, you get some crazy trips that you can barely remember later. 5-MeO-DMT is the more psychedelic version of ketamine. See my guide on that, because it shares a lot of the same warnings, altho I do think it has more potential to be helpful when used correctly.
Here's a reddit poster who talks about going through tremendous pain with MDMA as she uncovered childhood sex abuse memories. It took her dozens of sessions that felt harmful before she finally had one that fully got her to accept what happened to her (and a huge somatic release which looked like a re-enactment of the abuse). So there are some people who argue that if MDMA opened up your wounds, just keep taking more MDMA until it's fixed. Although on this thread, there are some who disagree with that simplistic advice. I know a few people who quit because they simply couldn't keep going through the pain that MDMA brought up for them. And as the commenters on the first thread note, very few people would go thru that dozens of times hoping for something different to happen.
My perspective is that these people are not correctly preparing for the inner child reparenting that MDMA suddenly provides experiential access to. Because they are unprepared to make the best of the experience, and unprepared to integrate it after, it's sort of like being thrown into the deep end of a swimming pool before you learn to swim. You flail around for a bit then terrified, you go back to holding on to the wall and begging to get out. It really will take a long time to learn to swim that way, for most folks. On the other hand, without MDMA or loveyhuasca, many people are unable to really do inner child therapy, because it sounds fake to them, just as ego death sounded fake to me before I experienced it. So I think these approaches complement each other greatly.
## Planning a Trip or Ceremony? Give It a Beginning and an End
If you're planning a session, think through beforehand how it will start and end, and what happens in between. Rest and timing come first: don't begin already depleted, and ideally start earlier in the day, so there's time afterward for food, some normal company and most importantly, sleep.
Set up the space you'll be in, and decide ahead of time what would make you call for outside help. Go in curious about what might come up, along with gentle intentions, not harboring demands for revelations, buried memories, good cries or spiritual breakthroughs. And train before you need it, sending love to a frightening image, to someone you hate (without approving of what they did), and to the inner child. Sometimes people find that when they prepare to purify themselves and open up to love, they don't even end up needing the experience itself. The work is already done.
One woman I met in Kenya was going to take shrooms to fix her tobacco and marijuana addictions. Before she took the shrooms, she simply realized she was addicted to those substances because she was trying to escape anxiety. So instead, she let the anxiety come, and accepted the feeling until it left. Her addictions vanished, no shroom trip required.
If you're taking a psychoactive drug, though, know what you're taking and roughly how much, and look up what it mixes badly with and any medical concerns. If you've taken this amount before, that's a good starting place to understand how it could affect you this time, although people's sensitivities can vary from one session to the next, sometimes quite dramatically. If you've never taken this dose or done this type of meditation this intensely before, make sure you're not jumping into the deep end all at once.
During the experience, the sitter (the sober person with you, not literally someone sitting on your face!) keeps you safe and offers you choices if relevant.
As the experience winds down, give it some closure. Stop adding stimulation (I really don't like the MDMA protocols that include redosing, as it amplifies the neurotoxicity concerns). Check where you are and what day it is. Eat and drink a bit to replenish your electrolytes.
Jot down anything you want to remember before you forget, and let your body know the session is over with some gentle massage. Keep the rest of the day free, and the next morning too if possible, since your brain is still in a vulnerable, flexible, "plastic" state, and the goal is not to cement in worse new patterns than what you had before!
Use this sequence for planned sessions. Once somebody is already in trouble, return to the triage app instead of trying to improve the ceremony plan.
