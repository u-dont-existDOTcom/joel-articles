You are reading one paragraph of an article for the person it is written for. Here is what the paragraph is for:

WHAT IT IS FOR: the signs that mean the reader should call medics now, read in a hurry, one line at a time; every item is mine to reword except "No sleep for days", which the writer approved

HEADING: Call Medics For: (the first part of When to Get Outside Help; each item finishes the heading, so no item ends with a period)

PARAGRAPH:
S1: - Trouble breathing, breathing that gets slow and shallow, or lips that turn blue or gray
S2: - A [seizure](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/active-seizure) after they took too much, or an amount nobody knows, or one that goes on over 5 minutes on a normal dose
S3: - They collapse, you can't wake them the normal way, or they're getting harder to reach by the minute
S4: - They're dangerously hot (not just sweaty), their muscles go rigid, or they have a fever with muscles jerking that they can't stop (clonus)
S5: - No sleep for days
S6: - One side of their body suddenly goes weak, or they can't speak, can't walk normally, or wet or soil themselves
S7: - They say their brain feels damaged and they can't do [the brain checks](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/my-brain-feels-damaged), or they're getting worse fast
S8: - [Chest pain](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/chest-pain-and-injury) that's severe, getting worse, spreading to an arm or the jaw, or came on with hard movement, or that comes with shortness of breath, sweating, faintness, stroke signs or collapse
S9: - A serious injury, bleeding that won't stop, or a hit to the head, neck or back
S10: - Vomiting that keeps coming back, bad dehydration, blood in their vomit or stool, or bad stomach pain
S11: - [Signs of serotonin syndrome](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/how-to-check-for-ss) after mixing an MAOI, which keeps serotonin from breaking down (some older antidepressants, or the harmalas in ayahuasca and Syrian rue seeds), with something that raises serotonin, like MDMA or most antidepressants

For each sentence, answer in one line: what does the reader need this sentence for, given what the paragraph is for?
Then list the sentences the reader doesn't need.

A sentence the reader doesn't need is one they could lose and still do what the paragraph is for, with nothing
missing. These are not reasons to keep a sentence: it is true; it has a source; it is interesting; it is a careful
hypothesis or a testable guess; it explains a disease or a case the reader doesn't have; it qualifies another claim the
reader didn't need either; it was already in the text. Read also for sense: a sentence whose words don't make sense
together, or that mixes pictures (a dial, a door, a floor and a leak for one thing), goes on the list too, with why.

Before you list a sentence for giving the reader nothing to do, ask what it means they should do. A caution, a risk
or a difference between people often implies an act the sentence doesn't say ("Sensitivity varies" means: start
with less): that sentence is needed, and its NEEDS line says the act it implies, so the writer can add it.

S2: ...

UNNEEDED:
