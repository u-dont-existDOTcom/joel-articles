Overstimulating also dehydrates them, but we can't give fluids unless they're fully alert, able to swallow and not vomiting, and then just small sips of an electrolyte drink. If they still show the signs of low electrolytes from [MDMA: Magnesium Alarm](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/mdma-magnesium-alarm) above, they may need **more salt, potassium and magnesium than a normal electrolyte drink has**. Salty potato chips are the easiest way to give the salt and potassium, with nothing to cook, so I'd let them eat as many as they want. Their potassium could be running low or high, though: MDMA can drain it, muscles breaking down into the blood push it up, and high potassium brings on vomiting and diarrhea, which then drain it again. Since it could be either, I'd give potassium only in food like the chips, never as pills or powder, since too much pure potassium is dangerous for anyone. Magnesium is the one they can take in through the skin, which matters because many people with serotonin syndrome run low on it ([a nursing review](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.NME.0000432871.59839.f3)) and magnesium chloride tastes so bad that many would rather not drink it. So I'd stir a handful of the flakes into a little water and rub it on their skin, or use Epsom salt the same way if that's all you have, though Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate, not a kind of table salt) gets through the skin less well. If they can stand the taste, they can also take it by mouth, a gram at a time, especially with MDMA-related jaw tension or twitching (the amounts are in [MDMA: Magnesium Alarm](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/i/170920118/mdma-magnesium-alarm), above).
