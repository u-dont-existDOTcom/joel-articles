## Active Seizure

Time the seizure. Protect the person from injury, clear dangerous objects, loosen tight clothing, and place them on their side when practical. Don't restrain them and don't put anything in their mouth while they are convulsing or not fully conscious.

If an appropriate rapid seizure-rescue benzodiazepine is available, use it by its intended route. Benzodiazepines are the established first-line drug class for status epilepticus. An ordinary pill is not the same thing as a nasal, buccal, rectal, intramuscular, or intravenous rescue preparation, and respiratory depressants in the mix change the risk.

Some people get a warning before a seizure takes hold, which is called an aura. That's when the things people report can still stop it, depending on what sets their seizures off. If it's flashing or flickering light, cover one eye with a hand and turn away from the light, because closing the eyes doesn't block enough of it. Some people stop a seizure at the warning stage with a strong smell like jasmine, and some train themselves to link one smell with feeling calm and then sniff it when a seizure threatens. When overstimulation is what sets it off, a cool, quiet, dark and comfortable space can help. I'd also try sniffing humic/fulvic acid powder, although that's just a hunch based on the effects I notice so far.

I would also use topical magnesium chloride and consider topical DMSO with a known-genuine CBD preparation as adjuncts (a lot of CBD is fake or very dilute). Seizures come in all different types, and we have no study on whether CBD could be a rescue remedy for a seizure (just anecdotes), even though we do have studies showing long-term effectiveness to prevent them: Purified oral CBD is approved for the seizures of Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome and tuberous sclerosis complex. For focal seizures outside those syndromes, the evidence is mixed, but still long-term.

As for CBD/cannabis, people do smoke or vape it at the start of an aura, while they're still awake enough to do it on purpose. There are also people who find THC and/or CBD works as a nasal spray or sublingually, and there's even a medical THC nasal spray marketed for seizure interruption in Brazil by a company called ABRACE. They testified in Congress that in 30 tests they did with children having seizures, the nasal spray ended those seizures within 30 seconds. You can see it being used on a child in this vid: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bgVi5o_2v_w

I also read on forums some swear it stops the seizure from going any further, and there was one woman whose EEG showed fewer seizure discharges on the days she smoked. But other people say THC vapes and concentrates brought their seizures on, and in rats that get the brief staring seizures called absence seizures, high-THC smoke made them worse while injected CBD cut them about in half. So the THC may be what makes it go either way.
