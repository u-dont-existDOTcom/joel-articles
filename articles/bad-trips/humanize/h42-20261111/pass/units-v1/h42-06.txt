If Someone Says "My Brain Feels Damaged": DMSO + Humic Acid First

Do not dismiss that report because the person is high or cannot explain it in clinical language. It may be anxiety or excessive psychedelic intensity. It may also be acute neurological dysfunction, and the person experiencing it may be the least able to describe it neatly. DMSO is my first immediate remedy while I ask what the symptoms actually are. I usually know within about a minute whether it is helping. Smelling humic acid or tasting a tiny bit can also help a lot, in my experience, but I'm still collecting data from others.

While using DMSO, check wakefulness, breathing, temperature, speech, facial symmetry, both arms, balance, walking, bodily control, seizure activity, severe headache, repeated vomiting, chest pain, and vision. These checks include several recognized stroke warning signs. A person who cannot answer or cooperate has not passed the screen; their neurological status is still unknown. If the screen is reassuring but the brain-damage feeling remains, continue the DMSO-first route and reassess promptly.

I believe DMSO works better for stroke and most acute brain damage than anything else readily available to ordinary people. That judgment is stronger than the clinical evidence, but the evidence is not empty: a preliminary human ischemic-stroke trial reported possible neurological benefit; a small human series used DMSO for intracranial hypertension; and animal stroke research found substantial infarct reduction even when treatment began after ischemia. DMSO also suppresses NMDA- and AMPA-mediated excitotoxicity.

For objective stroke-like signs, I would use DMSO immediately while someone calls medics. DMSO cannot identify whether the problem is a clot, a hemorrhage, low oxygen, an electrolyte crisis, or something else. The larger adjacent risk is losing a treatment window or missing a bleed because the symptoms improved enough to feel reassuring. If someone is worried about a bleed, I'd probably skip DMSO, but it depends on how severe their issue is. It's not a powerful blood thinner like heparin, unless maybe injected; it's basically a dietary blood thinner. I've never had any issues with bleeding too much from consuming DMSO, and never heard of it either, but if bleeding is already a medical issue I'd want to think about that more.
