Lines of Joel's own from the same article, for how he sounds:
With MDMA, I treat jaw tension as the earliest warning that the body needs more magnesium. I don't have studies to back this up, but everyone I know who has taken enough magnesium chloride to make the jaw tension fully release has avoided the MDMA toxicity symptoms they get otherwise.

My second wife had severe convulsions after an MDMA overdose. At the hospital, they figured out what caused that: her potassium was found to be critically low, and IV potassium was the only treatment they gave her. That experience is what leads me to treat serious MDMA toxicity as a broader electrolyte emergency, although magnesium is still the first and most useful supplement to give once the jaw tenses, since potassium gets depleted once magnesium runs low.

Once their jaw tenses, that should be their last dose of MDMA, so get them to slow down and cool off. If they drink a lot of plain water, even a single normal MDMA dose raises the risk of their sodium dropping too low, so have them sip an electrolyte drink when they're thirsty instead. Plain water is the risk because the drug keeps them from peeing out the extra, so it thins out the salt in their blood. Women are most at risk of this, based on the results we have: at a rave in the Netherlands, sodium was mildly low in 1 in 4 of the women on ecstasy vs only 3% of the men. California's poison control found the same pattern in the MDMA cases it got, with women at four times the odds. The following indicators would justify calling medics: If their confusion or headache worsens, or vomiting won't stop (sodium might be too low by then for an electrolyte drink to fix; while you wait for medics, if they're alert and not vomiting, give them something very salty instead, like strong broth). That has to be a lot of salt, more than a snack gives: guidelines for hikers who drank too much water use three or four bouillon cubes in half a cup of water. If their muscles keep getting weaker or their heart palpitations keep getting stronger, that could be from dangerously low potassium, like my second wife had. A seizure or collapse can result from low sodium, magnesium or potassium, and can also come from overheating or MDMA toxicity.
