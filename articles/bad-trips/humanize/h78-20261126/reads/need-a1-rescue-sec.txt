NEEDS:
S1: Tells the reader the remedies are for acute trouble and emergencies, not a merely uncomfortable trip: use them when it is serious, not for every rough patch.
S2: Implies an act: try each remedy in small, rising doses beforehand, so it is known before a crisis. Useful, but it is about using the remedies, not about what they are for.
S3: Opens the idea that a crisis can be about the body, not only the mind. The reader needs it to see the split the paragraph draws.
S4: Gives the mechanism in one picture: the body feels bad and the mind explains it as cosmic horror. Lets the reader see that a dark mental state can come from the body.
S5: Names the body states (nausea, heat, exhaustion, dehydration, overstimulation, sleep loss) that get mistaken for spiritual signs. Implies: check these first.
S6: Tells the reader not to treat a horrible thought as a revelation. Implies: when the thought is horrible, look at the body before trusting it.
S7: Says what the body may need (quiet, water, cooling, warmth, rest, calmer room). Implies what to try first.
S8: Nothing the reader does differently. It is a statement of outlook; S7 already says the body needs care.
S9: Nothing for the purpose. It explains where the section sits in the article, not what the remedies are for or when to use them.
S10: Gives the signs of a body, gut or brain problem (diarrhea, nausea, toxic heaviness, jaw tension, loss of bodily control, neurological disorganization) and says the mind can feel crazy because the gut or brain is. Implies: if you see these, treat it as a physical problem.
S11: Says what loss of bodily control looks like (wetting or soiling, can't sit up, stand, walk or talk) so the reader can recognize it. Needed. The closing clause "basically everything related to controlling the body" adds nothing the examples don't, and is circular.
S12: Says to call medics for it, and that the remedies can start while help is coming. Needed, and the act is clear. "That's also" is slightly unclear: it points back to loss of bodily control, not to the whole list in S10.
S13: Gives examples of neurological disorganization (confusion, clumsiness, shaking, light and sound being too much) so the reader can recognize it. Needed. "Basically that plus a whole lot more" is vague, and it leaves open whether S12's call-medics advice covers all of these or only the severe end. Confusion, shaking and overwhelming light and sound are common in ordinary trips, so the reader is left not knowing when to call.
S14: Says these trips are what the remedies are for and the shadow work further down is for a mainly emotional crisis. This is the point of the paragraph.
S15: Implies the act: if both hells are present, don't spend time sorting which is which, treat the gut first. Needed.
S16: Nothing. It is obvious, and it is also shaky: S10 says the brain can be what is going wrong too, so a fine gut does not rule out a physical cause of mental hell.
S17: Warns that gut-driven mental hell is often mistaken for shadow work, so the reader checks the body before taking it into shadow work. Needed.

UNNEEDED:
- S8: "The body is part of the spiritual field." — It is an outlook statement. Lose it and the reader still knows the remedies are for body, gut and brain trouble, with nothing missing. (S1-S5 settled, so this is a note for the writer, not a demand.)
- S9: "The hierarchy appears before the mystical sections because love doesn't require us to ignore the body." — It explains the section's placement, not what the remedies are for or when to use them. "Hierarchy" and "love" are also not set up for a reader at this point. (Settled, so a note only.)
- S16: "If the gut is fine, obviously the mental hell is not related to the gut." — It is tautological, and it can mislead: it implies a fine gut means no physical cause, while S10 says the brain can be the cause. The reader loses nothing without it.
- Partial: the last clause of S11 ("basically everything related to controlling the body") and the first half of S13 ("basically that plus a whole lot more") are vague filler. The examples in each sentence carry the content.
