MARKED:
- S11: 4, 6 — "basically everything related to controlling the body" is vague about what counts (4) and ends the sentence on a summing-up after the concrete list (6, and it repeats what the list just said, 16). His way would stop on the last concrete act ("... walk or talk") or add one more thing a person could actually see. Also "themselves" has no named person yet, since S10 only has "a trip" and "the mind".
- S12: 10, 17 — "That's" could point to wetting or soiling, to not being able to sit up or talk, to loss of bodily control as a whole, or to the whole trip in S10. "the remedies" is used before S14 introduces "the remedies below". His way would name it ("Losing control like this is something to call medics for") and say that the remedies can begin while the ambulance is on its way, naming what is being waited for. 17 is a lighter mark: someone who cannot sit up or talk may not be able to swallow, so remedies that are swallowed may not fit, and the text doesn't say which remedies can begin.
- S13: 2, 4, 10, 7 — It has the same shape as S11 (a term defined with a "basically ..." tail), which is 2. "that plus a whole lot more" is vague about what counts (4), and "that" could mean loss of bodily control or S12's call-medics line (10), because S12 stands between them. The sentence also leaves the reader unsure whether neurological disorganization is a call-medics sign too (7). His way would name the thing and say what to do about it, for example "Neurological disorganization means confusion, clumsiness, shaking, or light and sound being too much, and those are signs to call medics too" (if that is true).
- S15: 4, 8, 14 — "address the gut" leaves the act generic (4); the comma splice "you don't need to try to tell which is from what, you address the gut first" is two or three clauses a model joins with commas where he would join with "so" (8). "you don't need to try to tell" is a flat don't that doesn't say why anyone would want to tell, or why the gut comes first (14; the reason only arrives in S17, which is the 13 and 16 problem of splitting one subject across places). His way: say why you'd want to sort it out, then "so" and the act ("use the remedies below first").
- S16: 4, 7, 16 — "If the gut is fine" doesn't say what counts as fine (4). "obviously ... is not related" is true but gives the reader nothing to act on and no next step, such as going to the shadow work (7). It is also the condition in S15 turned the other way round (16), and it pulls against S15's "you don't need to try to tell", since here the reader is asked to tell whether the gut is fine. His way would join it to S15 as the other case ("And if your gut really is fine, then the shadow work further down is where to look") and say how someone would know.

Not marked: S10, S14 and S17 (settled), and S1 to S9 (not read, as instructed).
