Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes

Anything that could let someone hurt themselves in the next five minutes takes priority, so get the car keys out of reach and turn the stove off, and move them away from roads and balconies, or pools of water for that matter. Letting them move around is fine, and pacing can help, but keep them from taking more of any substance. If they want to go confront somebody, that can wait. And if they want to leave, have someone go with them. Put any weapons away and keep the person away from crowds and strangers. If they want to post their big revelation about the universe, or message an ex, take the phone.

They're feeling overwhelmed, so dim the lights, and clear out the extra people around them.

Then remind them where they are and what's happening, since a trip can make them lose track of it:

You're in [place]. It's [date/time]. You took [substance, if known], and its effects will peak and then change.

Give the Session a Beginning and an End

If you're planning a session, think through how it will start and end, and what happens in between, before you take anything. Rest and timing come first: don't begin already depleted, and ideally start earlier in the day, so there's time afterward for food, some normal company and sleep. Know what you're taking and roughly how much, and look up what it mixes badly with and any medical concerns. Set up the room, and decide ahead of time what would make you call for outside help. Go in curious about what might come up, without a wish list of revelations, buried memories, good cries or spiritual breakthroughs. During it, the sitter (the sober person with you) keeps you safe and offers you choices. As it winds down, give it some closure. Stop adding stimulation, and check where you are and what day it is. Eat and drink when it's safe, then jot down the basic facts and let your body know the session is over. Keep the rest of the day free, and the next morning too if possible, because sleep and food come before turning any of it into a new life plan.

Use this sequence for planned sessions. Once somebody is already in trouble, return to the triage app instead of trying to improve the ceremony plan.
