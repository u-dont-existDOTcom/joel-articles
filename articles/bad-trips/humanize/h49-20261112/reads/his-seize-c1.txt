- S6: 1 — two separate warnings (don't restrain, don't put anything in the mouth) packed into one sentence; his way would give each its own clause saying what to do and what goes wrong (holding them down can hurt them or you, anything in the mouth can block their breathing).
- S10: 2, 4 — "They have trouble breathing" opens and is shaped like S11 and S12, and "trouble breathing" doesn't say what counts; he would spell out what to look for (breathing stays slow, shallow or stopped, lips or face turning blue or gray after the shaking ends).
- S11: 2, 4 — same short "They get..." shape as its neighbours, and "dangerously hot" is vague; he would say what it looks like (skin hot to the touch, flushed, drenched in sweat or not sweating at all).
- S12: 2, 4 — same short "They're..." shape again, and "seriously hurt" doesn't say what counts; he would name it (a hard knock to the head, heavy bleeding, a suspected broken bone).
- S13: 1, 4 — two ideas joined in one sentence ("first seizure" and "substantially different"), and "substantially different" doesn't say different how; he would split them and give examples (longer, a different kind of movement, slower to wake up than usual).
