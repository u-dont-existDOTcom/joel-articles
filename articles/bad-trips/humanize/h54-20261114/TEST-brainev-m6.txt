I believe, based on my experience and those of others I've heard from, as well as the books I've read on this, that DMSO works better for most acute brain damage than anything else readily available. The clinical evidence is incomplete, but it leans this way. In animals, when given after a stroke, it shrank the area of the brain that died (which is a good thing). Some patients whose stroke came from a clot seemed to recover better on DMSO in a human trial, and doctors have used it in a handful of patients to bring down dangerous pressure inside the skull. But more relevant to the drug overdose issue this article deals with, DMSO calms NMDA- and AMPA-mediated excitotoxicity that kills brain cells, at least in rat brain cells in a dish.
