THE DRAFT (what it is for: someone reading the guide's DMSO profile (in "Mental Stabilizers That Don't Abort the Trip") to decide whether and how to use DMSO to steady a bad trip, weighing what the research says it does and its risks.):
S1: One famous 1967 study by Ramirez & Luza found it more effective than the best antipsychotic drugs at the time, slashing mental patients' psych ward stays in half, and that included patients with severe anxiety, though it hasn't been tested (that I know of, yet) for PTSD.
S2: It's routinely used in humans who receive stem cell transplants, as it is the best cell preservative that we've found so far.
S3: There is evidence (Kortleve, et al, 2023) that using too much DMSO (10% vs 5%) in stem cell transfusions increases mild side effects in adult humans.

S4: Young brains may be at risk from DMSO, though: it killed brain cells in mice up to a month old, even at a low dose, and in a dish of rat neurons (Hanslick, et al, 2009).
S5: Translating mouse research to humans isn't straightforward.
