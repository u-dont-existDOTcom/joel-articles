WHAT IT IS FOR: a friend sitting with someone on a drug who says their brain feels damaged has just done the stroke checks and is deciding whether to trust DMSO as the first remedy; this paragraph gives the author's reasons and the evidence for it.

HEADING: If Someone Says "My Brain Feels Damaged": DMSO + Humic Acid + Electrolytes First

PARAGRAPH:
S1: I believe, based on my experience and those of others I've heard from, as well as the books I've read on this, that DMSO works better for most acute brain damage than anything else readily available.
S2: The clinical evidence is incomplete, but it leans this way.
S3: In animals, when given after a stroke, it shrank the area of the brain that died (which is a good thing).
S4: In one human trial, some clot stroke patients seemed to recover better on DMSO, and doctors have used it in a small series of patients to bring down dangerous pressure inside the skull.
S5: But more relevant to the drug overdose issue this article deals with, DMSO calms excitotoxicity, the glutamate overload via NMDA and AMPA receptors that kills brain cells, at least in rat brain cells in a dish.

NEEDS:
S1: the claim the reader is deciding whether to trust (DMSO beats anything else to hand for sudden brain damage) and the author's grounds for it: his own experience, other people's, and his reading.
S2: how much weight the evidence can bear: it isn't settled, but it points toward DMSO. ("Clinical" fits only S4, the one sentence about people; S3 and S5 are animal and dish work, so the word makes the evidence sound stronger than it is.)
S3: the clearest sign that DMSO protects a living brain: given after the damage had happened, which is when the reader would give it, it meant less of the brain died (in animals).
S4: nothing
S5: the evidence closest to the reader's own case, a brain hurt by a drug: DMSO calms the glutamate overload that kills brain cells, though only shown in rat cells in a dish. They need only that plain point; "via NMDA and AMPA receptors" gives them nothing they can use.

UNNEEDED:
- S4: In one human trial, some clot stroke patients seemed to recover better on DMSO, and doctors have used it in a small series of patients to bring down dangerous pressure inside the skull. — both results are about cases the reader doesn't have: a stroke, which they have just checked for, and dangerous pressure inside the skull, which they have no way to know their friend has; both are weak ("seemed to", "a small series"); what the stroke work offers the reader's case, that DMSO given after the damage saved brain in a living body, S3 says more clearly, and S5 covers the drug case.
