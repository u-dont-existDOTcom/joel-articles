## Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes

The [triage app](https://ibogaqueen.com/1625-2/) gives the order. This part is about how you carry it out, because the person in trouble doesn't need you as one more source of pressure.

![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/944bef30-ca59-415b-a2e8-809a29ac2ba6_1122x1402.png)

First comes whatever could hurt somebody in the next five minutes. That means the car keys and the stove, and getting away from roads and balconies. Water's dangerous too, and anything else they could walk into or fall off. Weapons should be out of reach, and crowds and strangers are better left behind. The phone can wait too: if they want to post what they've just figured out about the universe, or message an ex, hold on to it for them.

They can walk around, as long as it's safe. They can't drive or take more of anything, though. Going off to confront somebody is out too, and so is leaving by themselves.

Then have a look at the room. Maybe the light is too bright or the music is horrible, or five worried people are all talking at once. Check that nobody is touching them when they don't want it. They might be too hot or too cold, or badly need the bathroom. Or they could be stuck next to somebody they don't trust.

A lot of what gets called a profound psychedelic crisis turns out to be a frightened nervous system stuck in an annoying room, so fix the room. Dim the lights and turn the sound down, and ask the extra people to leave. Offer water, but let them say no, and if they do drink, small sips go down better than chugging. Ask before you touch them. Talk slowly and say less. Please don't explain the universe to somebody it's already scaring.

Ask them, "Music or quiet?" If a song starts taking the trip somewhere they don't want to go, change it, even if it's your favorite.

Then remind them of the simple facts:

> You're in *[place]*. It's *[date/time]*. You took *[substance, if known]*. It comes up and peaks, and then it changes. I know it's intense, but it won't stay like this. We don't have to figure out your life tonight.

If it came from a practice instead (meditation, breathwork, fasting, *kundalini*-style work, extreme stretching, a ritual, a retreat, anything like that), say this:

> I'm in an intense state. I'm stopping the practice now. Right now my job is just to stay safe.

After that, pick one safe thing to try with the body and one calm person to stay with; the next two sections are about those. Make sure they get some sleep, especially if they're euphoric or grandiose, and leave what it all means for later, when their nervous system has something to stand on.

A state that didn't come from a drug may not wear off on any schedule you can predict. What to do is still simple. Stop the practice, and eat something plain once it's safe to. Let your body settle and keep warm, then sleep. It can feel like you're onto something huge and should keep going. Nothing says you have to go through a portal just because it's open.

### Stillness or Movement?

Both of these can help. Try one for two minutes and ask yourself: *Am I safer and clearer now, or more wound up and impulsive?*

If lying down feels like being locked up, walk slowly around somewhere enclosed. If the pacing starts turning into trying to get away, sit with your back against a wall, or lie down under a blanket. You're letting the nervous system blow off steam, so it shouldn't happen anywhere it could get someone hurt.

**Parallel processing** can help when lying still has turned into being stuck inside your head. Keep part of your attention on what's coming up, whether it's in your body or it's fear, grief, a memory, whatever it is, and give the other part a small, safe job you can see the end of. Sort ten things or fold five towels, wash one cup, draw something, whatever has an end you can see. Finishing it helps you see that time is still moving and the everyday world is still here. If the job is making you more agitated or speedy, or more likely to do something risky, stop.

The order that tends to work is **room → body → human → time → meaning**. Most people start with meaning, because the experience feels so cosmic. But when the nervous system is on fire, the loving thing to do is the practical stuff first.
