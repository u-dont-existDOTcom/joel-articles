## Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes

The [triage app](https://ibogaqueen.com/1625-2/) gives the order. This part is about how you carry it out, because the person in trouble doesn't need you as one more source of pressure.

![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/944bef30-ca59-415b-a2e8-809a29ac2ba6_1122x1402.png)

Start with whatever could hurt somebody in the next five minutes. Put the car keys away and turn off the stove. Get away from roads, balconies and water, anything they could walk into or fall off, and keep weapons out of reach. Crowds and strangers are worth leaving behind as well. And if they want to post what they've just figured out about the universe, or message an ex, hold on to their phone for now.

They can walk around, as long as it's safe. They can't drive or take more of anything, though. Going off to confront somebody is out too, and so is leaving by themselves.

Then have a look at the room. Maybe the light is too bright or the music is horrible, or five worried people are all talking at once. Check that nobody is touching them when they don't want it. They might be too hot or too cold, or badly need the bathroom. Or they could be stuck next to somebody they don't trust.

So much of what gets called a profound psychedelic crisis is a frightened nervous system stuck in an annoying room.

Dim the lights and turn the sound down, and ask the extra people to leave. Offer water, but let them say no, and if they do drink, small sips go down better than chugging. Ask before you touch them. Talk slowly and say less. Please don't explain the universe to somebody it's already scaring.

Ask them, "Music or quiet?" If a song starts taking the trip somewhere they don't want to go, change it, even if it's your favorite.

Then remind them of the simple facts:

> You're in *[place]*. It's *[date/time]*. You took *[substance, if known]*. This comes up, peaks, and then it changes. I know it's intense, but it won't stay like this. We don't have to figure out your life tonight.

If it came from meditation, breathwork, fasting, *kundalini*-style practice, extreme stretching, a ritual or a retreat, say this:

> I'm in an intense state. I'm stopping the practice now. Right now my job is just to stay safe.

After that, pick one safe thing to try with the body and one calm person to stay with; the next two sections are about those. Make sure they get some sleep, especially if they're euphoric or grandiose, and leave what it all means for later, when their nervous system has something to stand on.

A state that didn't come from a drug may not wear off on any schedule you can predict. What to do is still simple. Stop the practice, and eat something plain once it's safe to. Let your body settle and keep warm, then sleep. Just because a portal is open doesn't mean you have to keep pushing through it.

### Stillness or Movement?

You don't have to pick a side on this one. Try one of them for two minutes and ask yourself: *Am I safer and clearer now, or more wound up and impulsive?*

If lying down feels like being locked up, walk slowly around somewhere enclosed. If the pacing starts turning into trying to get away, sit with your back against a wall, or lie down under a blanket. You're letting the nervous system blow off steam, so it shouldn't happen anywhere it could get someone hurt.

**Parallel processing** can help when lying still has turned into being stuck inside your head. Keep part of your attention on what's coming up, in your body or as fear or grief or a memory, and give the other part a small, safe job you can see the end of. Sort ten things or fold five towels, wash one cup, draw something, whatever has an end you can see. Finishing it shows you that time is still moving and the everyday world is still here. If the job is making you more agitated or speedy, or more likely to do something risky, stop.

A good order is usually **room → body → human → time → meaning**. Most people start with meaning, because the experience feels so cosmic. But when the nervous system is on fire, the loving thing to do is the practical stuff first.
