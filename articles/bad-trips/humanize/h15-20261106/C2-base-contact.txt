Psychological First Aid: The First Five Minutes

Anything that could let someone hurt themselves in the next five minutes takes priority, so get the car keys out of reach and turn the stove off, and move them away from roads and balconies, or pools of water for that matter. Letting them move around is fine, and pacing can help, but keep them from taking more of any substance. If they want to go confront somebody, that can wait. And if they want to leave, have someone go with them. Put any weapons away and keep the person away from crowds and strangers. If they want to post their big revelation about the universe, or message an ex, take the phone.

They're feeling overwhelmed, so dim the lights, and clear out the extra people around them.

Then remind them where they are and what's happening, since a trip can make them lose track of it:

You're in [place]. It's [date/time]. You took [substance, if known], and its effects will peak and then change.

Who to Contact, and When

Save the relevant links and local numbers before you need them. Hours and services change, and not every option works in every country.

During or soon after a psychedelic experience

Fireside Project: U.S.-only according to its current FAQ; phone or text psychedelic peer support at 623-473-7433, daily from 11 a.m. to 11 p.m. Pacific Time.
TripSit live chat: browser-based harm-reduction chat for people using drugs and the people supporting them.

Global crisis and emotional-support directories

Find A Helpline: a global directory of free helplines, many offering phone, text, online chat, or WhatsApp. It is not psychedelic-specific, but it can put a human on the other end quickly.

U.S. crisis chat and text

988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: U.S. call, text, and chat for suicidal or mental-health crises.
Crisis Text Line: U.S. text, chat, and WhatsApp support, with partner text lines listed for Canada, the UK, and Ireland.

Integration and post-experience support

ICEERS Support Center: up to five free online integration sessions for challenging experiences.
CPEP online peer group: a free confidential monthly group for post-psychedelic difficulties.
Cheetah House: resources, consultations, and support groups for meditation-related difficulties, including chat-based peer support.
