### A (PGQ-012, The Chicken-and-Egg Problem, after the five stages)

> Whichever stage you're at, what you practice when you sit down with your little one has to make it out into the rest of your week sooner or later. Saying the right thing in there helps, but your little one really starts believing the grown-up you when that grown-up shows up on a regular Tuesday too, even in tiny ways.

### B (PGQ-001, Catch the Hook Before the Story Takes Over, after "Here's where I want to clearly depart…")

> Feelings about yourself work the same way. "I feel attracted" or "I feel ashamed" is real as a feeling, but it doesn't settle who you are, or who you love. (If you don't want to be touched, though, say no now and work out what it means later. Same if something might be dangerous right now, or might be a medical warning sign: step away or get checked first.)
>
> And there's the opposite mistake. If it's your orientation or your gender in question, I wouldn't write it off as "just an intrusive thought" only because it's unwanted, or because your family or church wouldn't like it. You don't have to decide either way today. And when tomorrow's feeling points the other way, I wouldn't let it, or anybody else, decide which one is the "real you." What you keep finding with real people, and in how you actually live, will tell you more over time than the loudest feeling of the hour.

### C (PGQ-002 and PGQ-003, Also Look Outward, after "You can mean the apology…")

> Then there are people you're just getting to know. Meeting them can turn into a kind of test. If the real reason you're asking someone out is to prove you're lovable, or that you're sure what you want, then they've become part of the test. I'd lower the stakes. Get curious about them, and let them have their own reasons for being there. Can you just be with them without it turning into a verdict on you?
>
> And being yourself with people doesn't mean telling strangers everything. Privacy isn't fakery. People can earn more of you by how they handle the smaller things you tell them, and how they take it when you say no. But if a relationship is getting deep and you're still keeping up a fake normal, happy version of yourself to hold onto it, I'd either bring more of the truth in, carefully, or let it be less deep for now. (If telling could put you in real danger, like violence, or being outed where that isn't safe, protect yourself first.) And when someone finally accepts you, it can feel amazing. It still doesn't prove they love you, or that they've earned your trust yet.

### C2 (PGQ-002, When Healing Turns Into Checking, after the seat-belt paragraph)

> Checking doesn't have to happen in your head, either. If you're going on a date mainly to prove you're normal, or that you're really attracted to someone, it's doing the same job as another hour of worrying. It might calm you down for a day, and then the next wobble feels even bigger. I'd only go when you'd want the company anyway, and drop the proving part. Bailing just to make the doubt go away can be the same checking from the other side. (Feeling unsafe is different, and so is touch or sex you want to stop. You're always allowed out of those.) Trying things out to find who you're attracted to isn't checking, either, as long as you're not grading every moment for a verdict. Ordinary life will show you more over time.

### F (PGQ-004, Start With Whatever Showed Up, after "Or if you do get an answer…")

> Anxiety deserves the same kind of listening, but it isn't a fortune teller. It might really be warning you about something, like being overloaded, or a boundary. In everyday decisions, I'd ask it what it's trying to protect, and then check what it predicted against what actually happens. Listening to it doesn't mean letting it drive. If you're actually in danger right now, though, get safe first, and get a possible medical warning sign checked instead of testing it. And some of what it says isn't a prediction at all. If a part of you is saying no to going deeper, or to an altered state, that's reason enough to stop, not something to test. With touch or sex, even a hesitation is enough.
