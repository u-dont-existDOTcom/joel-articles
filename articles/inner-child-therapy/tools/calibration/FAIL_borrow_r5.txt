Borrow One Job at a Time

Picturing one perfect parent works for some people. The Ideal Parent Figure method is built on it, and in a small pilot with adults traumatized as children, it seemed to help. If you try it and get a greeting card, or worse, your actual parent, borrow one job at a time instead, from whoever you saw do that job well. Just skip anybody whose care needed you to disappear.

With nurturing, not judging your little one is enough to start on, even though the inherited critic will call it letting them off the hook. They also don't owe you any gratitude for finally showing up. It won't make them trust you, though, since little ones can tell when they're only being put up with. For the warm part, think of somebody who took care of you that you remember fondly, and how they'd sit beside your little one. If there was nobody like that, there's Mr. Rogers, or spiritual love if you've felt it.

Protecting can start at babysitter level. A babysitter isn't trying to raise anybody. They make sure everybody eats, the door's locked and bedtime happens, and if something's really wrong they call somebody. What would a halfway decent one do in the next ten minutes?

Guidance is the note on the fridge. Whatever you decided when you were calm, or whatever a teacher you trust would want you practicing today, can go on it.

At some point your little one will probably say some version of "You're not my mom." Which, fair. A decent babysitter doesn't argue about that, and still makes sure everybody brushes their teeth.

Plenty of babysitters are fifteen and feel about as grown-up as the little ones they're watching. You might feel like the little one the whole time, too. Afterward you get to say, "I did that for us. That was the Protector."
