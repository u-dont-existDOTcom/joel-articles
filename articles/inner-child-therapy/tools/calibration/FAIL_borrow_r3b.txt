Borrow One Job at a Time

It's hard to picture one perfect parent who does everything, especially if you never saw anybody do it. Most of us have seen somebody do at least one of the three jobs well, though, even if it was somebody else's parent, so you can borrow each job from whoever you saw doing it. Just leave out anybody whose care needed you to disappear.

If you can't find any love for your little one yet, you can start by just not judging them, even if the inherited critic calls that letting them off the hook. And they don't owe you gratitude for finally showing up. But they can probably tell when they're only being put up with, so at some point you'll want some real warmth. Think of somebody who took care of you that you remember fondly, and how they'd sit beside your little one. If there wasn't anybody like that, there's Mr. Rogers, or spiritual love if you've felt it.

They might get sarcastic, too, or just not believe you're really there this time, which is kind of fair if you haven't been. It's tempting to defend yourself, since you really are trying, but a decent adult would mostly just keep showing up.

Protecting can be babysitter level at first. What would a halfway decent babysitter do in the next ten minutes? Probably make sure everybody eats and nobody gets hurt, and keep things safe until the grown-ups get home. Guidance is even easier to borrow, because it can live on paper, like a plan you made on a calmer day or something a teacher you trust told you to practice.

You might still feel completely like your little one while you're doing all this. Children usually don't feel brave while they're doing something scary, either. Somebody tells them afterward that it was brave, and you can do that for yourself: "I did that for us. That was the Protector."
