## P1 — model

Write the furious version somewhere private. I'm serious: if twelve sentences are already screaming to get out, pretending you have only two civilized ones probably won't make you wiser. Just don't send it yet. Come back when you've cooled off a little and see what's still true. Maybe you were unfair. Maybe you also finally said no. Keep the no. Add the apology if you owe one. The useful reply might be shorter, or there might not be one. If you say you'll answer tomorrow, mean tomorrow.

## P2 — exact owner Human/medium

It doesn't mean you need to hide your upset, or apply for a permit to protest, but choose your battles and execute them wisely, so you don't end up regretting how you communicated later. As Lao Tsu might have said (if I could ever have read that whole boring Art of War book), a war with no casualties is the best possible war. When high IQ & EQ communication hits a writer's block, sometimes the best thing is to simply say, "I need some time to think about my response." And if the conflict really requires a response right now, then obviously just do your best rather than wait for the perfect timing.

## P3 — model

And then there is the person who wrote the ugly version. Don't throw them out with the draft. Read it once more after you've decided what to send and ask what they were trying so hard to get heard. Maybe they needed protection. Maybe reassurance, or actual help. Maybe they were furious and still had a point. An angry first draft can be terrible communication without being a childish protest. Cleaning up what you send is one job; staying on that person's side is another.
