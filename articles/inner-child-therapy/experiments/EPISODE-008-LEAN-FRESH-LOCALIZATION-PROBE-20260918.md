### Also Look Outward

One ugly conversation can feel like a revelation. Maybe it was. But one fight isn't a personality test. If the same person comes back later and can actually stay with what you raised—even while disagreeing with you and holding a boundary you hate—that matters too. Then there's what keeps happening: you bring up something that hurt, and somehow, by the end, you're carrying their reaction while your own experience has disappeared. That's much closer to the territory [Lindsay Gibson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) describes in her work on emotional immaturity.

You may not know for certain whether the other person is or could be capable of a healthier response, and neither do they. If they promise to change, that's probably as useful as their prior promises were. Think about how long it has taken you to become more healthy. Could you have done that from one moment to the next based on a promise? Sometimes we can be a better support for someone when we maintain a realistic boundary, so that we can hold high expectations, and still feel prepared for their failure to meet them.

If you really did hurt them, repair it. You can own what you did and still notice yourself backing away from your own complaint because their disappointment is hard to tolerate. Maybe you explain it again because the first time really wasn't clear. But at some point you may realize you've already said what mattered and you've already said no. What you're waiting for now is for them to understand. They may not. Feeling guilty doesn't tell you how much of the conflict is yours, and it doesn't prove manipulation. Sometimes the conversation ends with the misunderstanding still sitting there.
