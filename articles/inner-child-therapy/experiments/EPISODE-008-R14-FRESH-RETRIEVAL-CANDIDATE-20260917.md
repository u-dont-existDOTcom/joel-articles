### Look at What Actually Keeps Happening

Then look outward too. One ugly conversation doesn’t make somebody emotionally immature. Neither does needing time, disagreeing with you, or holding a boundary. But what keeps happening when you bring them your experience? Is there ever room for it? Can they ever own their part? What can you actually rely on? [Lindsay Gibson’s work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) on emotional immaturity can be really useful here.

You may never know whether somebody *can’t* meet you there, *won’t*, or just can’t right now. Fortunately, you don’t have to solve that mystery before protecting yourself. You can go by what’s happening, and change your mind if the behavior changes. Promises are lovely, but they aren’t the change. And you don’t need to volunteer for one more round just to make the evidence prettier.

Guilt makes this annoyingly messy. Maybe you really did hurt them and owe them some repair. You can also be giving up your own position because you can’t stand their disappointment. Feeling guilty doesn’t settle which one is happening, and it doesn’t prove they’re manipulating you either.

Sometimes the conversation isn’t going to end with two people finally understanding each other. Maybe you conveyed the thing. Maybe you said no. Maybe you stopped explaining yourself for the ninth time. That can be enough.

And if there’s still a whole speech bouncing around inside you, there’s somewhere safer to put it first.
