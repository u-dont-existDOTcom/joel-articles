# Repeats and contradictions involving the 2026-10-15 passages

Passages checked against each other and against the whole article: B102 (Borrow Love—or Borrow the Perspective of Care, P1), B115 (the paragraph beginning "This is a bit tricky to put into words", under Your Loan is PRE-APPROVED!), and B217 and B218 (Untangling the Belonging Bargains, P2 and P3). Only findings that involve at least one of these blocks are listed. Every quote is copied from the article.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

**1. B20 (My Journey) and B115 (Your Loan is PRE-APPROVED!)**
- B20: "Conditional love felt radically insufficient, so I kept looking for something deeper."
- B115: "we can fall into the trap of thinking that what we have is happiness itself. I'm ultimately reaching for something much deeper."
- Shared point: love or happiness that rests on conditions isn't enough, so the author reaches for something deeper.
- Later instance: adds the gratitude angle (thanking only for the good things you have can make you take them for happiness itself). With about 95 blocks between them, a reader is more likely to feel an echo than to feel told it again.
- Better: B20, because that's where the article explains why he went looking; in B115 it closes a run of disclaimers.

**2. B102 and B103 (both Borrow Love—or Borrow the Perspective of Care)**
- B102: "Sometimes it's not that you can't love in general."
- B103: "Knowing that love is in you somewhere counts for a lot, and you're borrowing something you already know how to do."
- Shared point: you already know how to love; the trouble is aiming it at your little one.
- Later instance: turns it into reassurance for when nothing moves. The paragraphs are back to back, so the premise reads as said twice, though gently.
- Better: B102, because the dog example makes it concrete and the exercise is built on it.

**3. B102 (Borrow Love—or Borrow the Perspective of Care) and B151 (Make a Simple Vow)**
- B102: "Just feel that love you can feel for others for a minute, in your body, before you try to redirect anything."
- B151: "Would you look at him/her with adoration and joy? Carry that into visualizing yourself with your own inner child"
- Shared point: call up the love you can feel for someone else, then carry it over to your inner child.
- Later instance: adds a newborn, real or imagined, as the source, and uses the move to make the vow feel real. The move itself is the same, and B151 introduces it with no reference back to B102, so a reader would likely feel told it again.
- Better: B102, because its section is about borrowing love and B103 follows it with what to do when it doesn't land.

**4. B115 and B118 (both Your Loan is PRE-APPROVED!)**
- B115: "we can fall into the trap of thinking that what we have is happiness itself"
- B118: "If you, like Biggie Smalls, have come to understand that having money is not real happiness, that’s a big deal!"
- Shared point: what you have, money included, isn't happiness itself.
- Later instance: adds a concrete case (money, Biggie Smalls) and makes the realization one more thing to be grateful for. Only two paragraphs sit between them in the same subsection, so a reader would feel told it again.
- Better: B118, because the money example makes it concrete and it follows directly from the line about understanding what real happiness is.

**5. B167 (The Parent You Inherited) and B218 (Untangling the Belonging Bargains)**
- B167: "hide that part of their authentic self which wasn't allowed, so they could keep belonging. They might still be doing it."
- B218: "Your little one can hang onto those bargains, specially if you haven't learned to parent him or her yourself yet."
- Shared point: your little one learned to trade part of themselves for belonging or love and may still be making that trade.
- Later instance: extends it to the earn-your-worth bargain, ties it to not yet parenting yourself, and adds a second origin (being praised for what you did). They are about 50 blocks apart, but a reader who remembers B167 would feel told it again.
- Better: B167, because it shows the rule that taught the trade; B218 states it in one line.

**6. B217 and B218 (both Untangling the Belonging Bargains)**
- B217: "Then there's another bargain, where you have to earn your worth"
- B218: "The bargain begins when your worth depends on them, like you have to keep performing them to be loved."
- Shared point: the second bargain is making your worth depend on what you perform.
- Later instance: adds where the gift ends and the bargain begins ("These can all be real gifts"). Apart from that, the bargain gets defined twice in back-to-back paragraphs.
- Better: B218, because it ties the bargain to dependence rather than to the gifts themselves, a line B217's opening doesn't draw.

**7. B218 (Untangling the Belonging Bargains) and B223 (Four Pairs That Commonly Hook the Child)**
- B218: "These can all be real gifts. The bargain begins when your worth depends on them"
- B223: "You don't have to stop enjoying pleasure, praise, gain or belonging. The question is whether losing one of them would feel like losing yourself."
- Shared point: the good things themselves are fine; the trouble starts when your worth or sense of self depends on them.
- Later instance: adds a test you can apply (would losing it feel like losing yourself) and widens the list to pleasure, praise, gain and belonging. They are five blocks apart in the same section, so a reader would feel told the same thing again.
- Better: B223, because it gives the reader a test and covers both bargains, while B218's version is tied to the earn-your-worth list.

**Callback, listed separately and not counted as a repeat**
- B111 (Ask God for a Loan): "The dog kind of love is real love." It relies on B102 (Borrow Love—or Borrow the Perspective of Care): "Your dog can chew up your good shoes and be forgiven before you've found the other one." That is two subsection headings back (A Smaller Doorway: Goodwill, then Ask God for a Loan).

## 2. Contradictions

**1. B20 (My Journey) and B115 (Your Loan is PRE-APPROVED!), mild**
- B20: "Maybe ordinary life already gives you enough love and meaning. Great. It didn't for me."
- B115: "We still need to remember that if we can only be thankful for the good things we have, we can fall into the trap"
- What pulls apart: B20 treats the search beyond ordinary, conditional love as the author's own need and says it's fine if ordinary life is enough for you. B115 says we all need to remember that resting in the good things we have is a trap. Neither passage says how the two fit. They are far apart, and the wording can be reconciled (enough love and meaning isn't quite the same as happiness itself), but a reader who remembers B20 could feel the stance shift from a personal choice to a trap for everyone.
- Closer to the rest of the article: B20's stance, since B114 opens the same subsection on the same optional footing: "I think it’s essential if we want to live in that love".

## 3. The five repeats a reader would most notice

By number, most noticeable first: 7 (the gifts are fine, dependence is the problem), 6 (the bargain defined twice in a row), 4 (what we have isn't happiness), 3 (the love-transfer move), 5 (your little one still making the old trade).
