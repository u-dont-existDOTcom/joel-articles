# The Inner Guide Comes Later

As the Guide, you choose what your little one learns and what it gets to experience, and you hold standards, the way a good parent would. You ask hard things of it so it can grow, at its own pace. That's what I was just asking of you, with that new interest. You also widen its world with art and music, and you point out beautiful things when you see them. Nature is part of that, and so is the wider world past the end of the street you grew up on.

Without that kind of direction, reparenting can turn permissive. Asking anything hard of your little one can feel like turning into the parent you inherited, the critic or the warden. Halfway through asking, you might not be sure whose voice that is. So sparing it everything feels like love, and candy and screens can pass for self-love that way. Escaping anything difficult can too.

The opposite trap is forced adulthood: demanding perfect discipline of yourself and no visible needs, with very little joy or closeness. It's the old "don't be needy" rule passing for the Guide. Your little one learned to hide their needs so they could keep belonging. No wonder tightening control again feels like the grown-up thing, and asking for anything feels needy. If you're stuck there, the next adult act may be asking for help anyway, or saying what you'd prefer.

Once a need does show, judge the strategy as well as the need. Wanting closeness is legitimate. You might want a lot of it, after all that hiding. Demanding instant replies or punishing someone for needing space isn't the same thing. The Guide helps you find some workable way to meet the need without erasing you or the other person.
