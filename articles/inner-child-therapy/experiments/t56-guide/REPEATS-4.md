# Repeats check 4: opening of The Inner Guide Comes Later

Scope: the first four paragraphs under *The Inner Guide Comes Later* (B234–B237), checked against each other and against every other block, B0–B232. Every quote is copied exactly from the article. The four paragraphs don't repeat or contradict each other; each repeat below pairs one of them with an earlier block.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

1. **B9 and B235: care without the Guide goes wrong**
   - B9, *The Chicken-and-Egg Problem*: “Our lives can become small and like a never-ending therapy session when we focus only on caring for ourselves”
   - B235, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: “Without that kind of direction, reparenting can turn permissive.”
   - Shared point: caring for your little one with no Guide behind it goes wrong.
   - Does the later one add anything? Yes. It names a different way it goes wrong (sparing the little one everything, so candy, screens and escaping hard things pass for self-love) and says why it happens (asking anything hard can feel like turning into the critic or warden you inherited). The warning itself is told again. B9 has one thing B235 doesn't: without a Guide inside, people go looking for one in a cult.
   - Better carrier: B235. It sits in the Guide's own section and explains the cause; B9 says it before the reader knows much about the Guide.

2. **B9 and B236: the opposite trap**
   - B9, *The Chicken-and-Egg Problem*: “But then some people over-rely on their mission to avoid caring for themselves.”
   - B236, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: “The opposite trap is forced adulthood: demanding perfect discipline of yourself and no visible needs, with very little joy or closeness.”
   - Shared point: you can also lean so hard on direction (a mission, discipline) that care gets squeezed out.
   - Does the later one add anything? Yes: a name for it, what it looks like, where it comes from (the old rule against being needy), and a next step. But B235–B236 follow the same two-step shape as B9 (one trap, then its opposite), so a reader who remembers B9 hears the second half again.
   - Better carrier: B236. It's the full version, in the Guide's section; B9's is one line.

3. **B123 and B236: control passing for being the grown-up**
   - B123, *When the Spiritual Relationship Hurts* (under *Borrow the Adult Before You Can Be the Adult*): “Being the grown-up doesn't mean controlling everything yourself, though.”
   - B236, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: “No wonder tightening control again feels like the grown-up thing.”
   - Shared point: grabbing control can feel like being the grown-up, and isn't; support is the way out (spiritual support in B123, asking someone safe for help in B236).
   - Does the later one add anything? It traces the urge to forced adulthood and the rule against being needy, where B123 traces it to a betrayal, and it adds saying what you'd prefer. The core claim is told again.
   - Better carrier: B123. It states the point outright; B236 only implies it on the way to explaining forced adulthood.

4. **B156 and B234–B235: holding the line is still love**
   - B156, *Make a Simple Vow* (under *Building Trust With Your Little One*): “you still go to bed, and staying kind about it shows them bedtime isn't a lack of love.”
   - B234–B235, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: “you hold standards, the way a good parent would.” and “So sparing it everything feels like love, and candy and screens can pass for self-love that way.”
   - Shared point: a loving parent still holds the line, and giving the little one everything isn't love.
   - Does the later one add anything? Yes: it widens the point from one limit (bedtime) to standards for learning and growth, names the indulgent versions, and says why holding the line is hard (it can feel like becoming the parent you inherited). The lesson of the bedtime story is told again in general terms.
   - Better carrier: B156. It makes the point through the bedtime scene the reader already knows from B136–B137.

5. **B176 and B237: hear it out, then weigh what it wants you to do**
   - B176, *Start With Whatever Showed Up* (under *When the Adult Voice Feels Fake*): “If it tells you to do something, I'd only do it if it's loving and leaves you free.”
   - B237, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: “Once a need does show, give it a fair hearing, and judge the strategy too.”
   - Shared point: listen to what comes up, then weigh the action it pushes for, and only take one that leaves people free.
   - Does the later one add anything? Yes: it separates the need, which is legitimate, from the strategy, which may not be; it gives the closeness example (instant replies, punishing someone for needing space); and it counts the other person's freedom too, “without erasing you or the other person”. The listen-then-weigh step is told again.
   - Better carrier: B237. The need-versus-strategy split and the closeness example make it concrete.

6. **B192 and B236: let someone safe see you're struggling**
   - B192, *When to Change the Strategy* (under *When the Adult Voice Feels Fake*): “like texting a friend that you're having a rough day instead of saying you're fine.”
   - B236, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: “If you're stuck there, the next adult act may be asking someone safe for help, or saying what you'd prefer.”
   - Shared point: when you're back to hiding your needs (B192: “you're skipping lunch and saying yes to everything again.”; B236: perfect discipline and no visible needs), the grown-up move is to let someone safe see it.
   - Does the later one add anything? A little: it calls the pattern forced adulthood passing for the Guide, and adds saying what you'd prefer. Otherwise the reader is told the same move again, more briefly.
   - Better carrier: B192. It has the concrete example and says why it matters: “One small thing like that can teach your little one more than ten more good meditations.”

7. **B230 and B234: bring art, nature and wonder into its life**
   - B230, *Let the Child Be Bad at Things* (under *Sometimes There Isn't a Clear Child Yet*): “Maybe a foraging group, or a painting class. Stargazing can be awe-inspiring”
   - B234, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: “You also widen its world with art and music, and you point out beautiful things when you see them. Nature is part of that”
   - Shared point: give your little one art, nature and wonder.
   - Does the later one add anything? Yes: music, pointing out beauty as you go, and the world past the street you grew up on, and it makes this an ongoing job of the Guide rather than an identity experiment. B234 points back to the new interest only for its hard-asks point, then offers art and nature as a further job, four blocks after B230's examples were art and nature, so a reader may feel told again.
   - Better carrier: B230. It gives the concrete activities; what B234 adds is the music, beauty and wider-world part.

## 2. Contradictions

None that a reasonable reader would see. Tensions I checked:

- B234–B235 (asking hard things of your little one; “Escaping anything difficult can too.”) against B178 in *Start With Whatever Showed Up* (“if it says no to going deeper in this work, or to an altered state, I'd stop there too.”) and B191 in *When to Change the Strategy* (“Not wanting to do it anymore is a reason too”). B178 and B191 cover touch and sex, how deep the work goes, altered states, and stopping the practice after you've heard the part out; B235 is about a habit of sparing the little one everything, and B236 names the opposite trap right after.
- B234 (“you hold standards, the way a good parent would.”) against B210 in *From Survival to Experimental Play* (“nobody's grading you”) and B231 in *Let the Child Be Bad at Things* (“nobody's on trial”). B234 ties the standard to the new interest itself, which means keep trying, not be good at it.
- B234 (“You ask hard things of it so it can grow, at its own pace.”) against B57 in *Your Body Might Need Some Love First* (“only when you want to”). B57 is about bringing hard therapy material back after a session that was too much; B234 is about everyday growth, and it says at its own pace.
- B234's account of the Guide's job, which leaves out a mission, against B9 in *The Chicken-and-Egg Problem*, which says giving ourselves a mission is the Guide's job (“That's the Guide's job.”). Two parts of one job, not opposites.
