# Repeats check: opening of The Inner Guide Comes Later

Scope: B234, B235 and B236 (the first three paragraphs under The Inner Guide Comes Later), checked against each other and against every block from B0 to B233. Only findings that involve at least one of those three blocks are listed. Each instance names its section (the nearest heading, plus the h1 it sits under when that's different).

B234 to B236 don't repeat or contradict each other; every finding below pairs one of them with an earlier block. I didn't count overlaps with the two chart images (B0, B28), since they summarize the whole method by design.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

**1. B9 and B235: without the Guide, caring for yourself goes wrong**
- B9, The Chicken-and-Egg Problem: "Our lives can become small and like a never-ending therapy session when we focus only on caring for ourselves"
- B235, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "Without that kind of direction, reparenting can turn permissive. Candy and screens can pass for self-love."
- Shared point: care without the Guide's direction shrinks into something less than care.
- Adds, or told again: partly told again. The warning is B9's. B235 adds a different picture of the failure (treats, screens and escaping difficulty passing for self-love) and why it tempts: it "sounds like the kindest thing".
- Carries it better: B235, because it gives concrete examples and sits in the Guide's own section, where B9 is a short preview.

**2. B9 and B236: the opposite trap, where drive crowds out care**
- B9, The Chicken-and-Egg Problem: "But then some people over-rely on their mission to avoid caring for themselves."
- B236, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "The opposite trap is forced adulthood, where you demand perfect discipline of yourself and have no visible needs"
- Shared point: the other imbalance, where purpose or discipline pushes out caring for yourself.
- Adds, or told again: told again in shape. B9 gives care-only and then mission-only; B235 and B236 give permissiveness and then forced adulthood, in the same order. B236 adds what the trap looks like (perfect discipline, no visible needs, little joy or closeness), a next step, and the check on how a need gets met.
- Carries it better: B236, because it shows the trap and what to do about it, where B9 names it in one sentence.

**3. B13 and B236: stuck in all-adult mode, and leaving it feels odd**
- B13, The Chicken-and-Egg Problem (stage 5 of the stages list): "This can actually feel really weird if you've been stuck in adult mode for a while."
- B236, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "Either of those can feel like turning into the needy one." (The two things meant are the ways out of forced adulthood: asking for help, or saying what you'd prefer.)
- Shared point: if you've been stuck in an all-adult mode, stepping out of it feels uncomfortable.
- Adds, or told again: mostly adds. B236 names the stuck state, describes it and gives a next step; B13 adds the goal to "go from weird to silly and playful". The shared warning is short, so a reader may only half-recognize it.
- Carries it better: B236, because it describes the stuck state and the way out, where B13 mentions it in passing, in a list item about a different stage.

**4. B73, B74, B192 and B236: letting someone see you're not okay is the grown-up step, and it's hard**
- B73, Also Look Outward (in Before You Try to Go Deep): "So I'd call somebody or show up somewhere, mess and all."
- B74, Also Look Outward (in Before You Try to Go Deep): "you're still keeping up a fake normal, happy version of yourself"
- B192, When to Change the Strategy (in When the Adult Voice Feels Fake): "like texting a friend that you're having a rough day instead of saying you're fine. That can honestly be harder than the meditation was."
- B236, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "the next adult act may be asking for help or saying what you'd prefer"
- Shared point: showing a need to someone, instead of hiding it, is the grown-up move, and it feels hard or needy.
- Adds, or told again: B236's remedy is told again. B192 already pairs the same no-needs pattern ("skipping lunch and saying yes to everything") with the same kind of step and the same warning that it's hard. B236 adds the forced-adulthood frame, "saying what you'd prefer", and the feeling of "turning into the needy one". B73 and B74 are looser matches: B73's reason is loneliness, and B74 is about how much of yourself to show as a relationship gets deeper.
- Carries it better: B192, because its example shows the act in one line, where B236 states it in general terms.

**5. B123 and B236: being the grown-up isn't tighter control**
- B123, When the Spiritual Relationship Hurts (in Borrow the Adult Before You Can Be the Adult): "Being the grown-up doesn't mean controlling everything yourself, though."
- B236, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "If you're stuck there, the next adult act may be asking for help or saying what you'd prefer, instead of tightening control again."
- Shared point: the adult move can be leaning on support instead of taking more control.
- Adds, or told again: mostly adds, with a faint echo. B236 applies it to forced adulthood, with everyday acts; B123 ties it to the aftermath of a teacher's betrayal and to spiritual support.
- Carries it better: B123, because it says it outright, where B236 implies it in a closing clause.

**6. B156 and B234 to B235: holding a limit is part of loving your little one**
- B156, Make a Simple Vow (in Building Trust With Your Little One): "you still go to bed, and staying kind about it shows them bedtime isn't a lack of love."
- B234, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "and you hold standards, the way a good parent would."
- B235, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "In the moment, that one sounds like the kindest thing you could do for your little one"
- Shared point: a loving parent still holds limits, and giving in only looks like the kinder choice.
- Adds, or told again: mostly adds. B234 and B235 widen it from bedtime to standards and growth, and name treats, screens and escaping difficulty as things that pass for self-love. A reader who remembers the bedtime story will recognize the lesson.
- Carries it better: B156, because it makes the point through a story the reader already knows, where B234 states it as a rule.

**7. B230 and B234: art, nature and wonder**
- B230, Let the Child Be Bad at Things (in Sometimes There Isn't a Clear Child Yet): "Maybe a foraging group, or a painting class. Stargazing can be awe-inspiring, and you might even see a UFO!"
- B234, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "You also widen its world with art and music, and you point out beautiful things when you see them. Nature is part of that"
- Shared point: bringing art, nature and wonder into your little one's life.
- Adds, or told again: partly told again. B234 gives it a different purpose (the Guide widening the child's world, rather than finding out what you like) and adds music, pointing out beauty, and the world past the street you grew up on. The overlap is easy to notice because it comes a few paragraphs after B230, right after B234 has pointed back to that passage.
- Carries it better: B230, because its examples are concrete and tied to a clear purpose ("Notice what holds your attention when nobody is impressed."), where B234 repeats the categories.

**Callbacks:** none that rely on something sections back. The one callback in the opening, B234's "That's what I was just asking of you, with that new interest." points to B230 to B232, the section just before it.

## 2. Contradictions

None that a reasonable reader would see.

Checked and not counted:
- B178, Start With Whatever Showed Up (in When the Adult Voice Feels Fake): "And if it says no to going deeper in this work, or to an altered state, I'd stop there too." and B191, When to Change the Strategy (same h1): "Not wanting to do it anymore is a reason too", against B234's "You ask hard things of it so it can grow, at its own pace." and B235's "So can escaping anything difficult." The earlier lines are about going deeper in the therapy or keeping up an exercise; B234 is about growing up in daily life, and it keeps "at its own pace".
- B7, The Chicken-and-Egg Problem: "it doesn't make sense to ask the child to heal itself", against B234's "You ask hard things of it so it can grow". B7 is about handing the child the adult's job; B234 is about the ordinary things a parent asks of a child, at the child's pace.
- B14, The Chicken-and-Egg Problem: "when they catch you lying down because you're wiped out, instead of pushing through one more chore." against B235's "So can escaping anything difficult." B14 is about resting when you're worn out, which fits B236's warning about forced adulthood.
