# Repeats and contradictions: opening of The Inner Guide Comes Later

Scope: the first three paragraphs under the h1 *The Inner Guide Comes Later* (B234, B235, B236), checked against each other and against every other block in the article. Only items that involve at least one of those three blocks are listed. Each instance names its section: the nearest heading, with its h1 in brackets when different.

The three blocks don't repeat each other. B235 and B236 are set up as opposite traps, and B234 introduces both.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

### 1. Care without direction goes nowhere (B9, B235)
Strength: strong.
- B9, *The Chicken-and-Egg Problem*: "Our lives can become small and like a never-ending therapy session when we focus only on caring for ourselves"
- B235, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: "Without that kind of direction, reparenting can turn permissive, and your little one gets lots of attention without growing."
- Shared point: without the Guide, caring for your little one turns into attention that doesn't lead anywhere.
- Does the later one add anything? It adds the forms this takes (candy, screens, escaping anything difficult) and the kind-sounding excuse for it. The core claim would still read as told again, and both passages hand the fix to the Guide.
- Which carries it better: B235. It sits in the Guide's own section and shows what the stalled care looks like; B9 gives it one sentence while introducing all three roles.
- Looser echoes, each making a different main point (not counted): B20, *My Journey*: "Spending your whole life processing your wounded inner child isn't freedom either." B87, *When Healing Turns Into Checking* [Before You Try to Go Deep]: "if that's the endgame, it's just a cope."

### 2. The opposite trap: the grown-up side overdone, care left out (B9, B236)
Strength: moderate.
- B9, *The Chicken-and-Egg Problem*: "But then some people over-rely on their mission to avoid caring for themselves."
- B236, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: "The opposite trap is forced adulthood, where your discipline is perfect and you have no visible needs"
- Shared point: the other way to fail is leaning on drive or discipline in place of care. B9 already puts the two traps side by side (care only, then mission only), and B235 and B236 put them side by side again, in the same order.
- Does the later one add anything? Yes: what the trap looks like (no visible needs, little joy or closeness), why it holds on (tightening control feels like the grown-up thing), and ways out. A reader who remembers B9 would still recognize the pair.
- Which carries it better: B236, because it describes the trap and says what to do about it; B9 gives it one sentence.
- Looser echo (not counted): B13, item 5, *The Chicken-and-Egg Problem*: "This can actually feel really weird if you've been stuck in adult mode for a while."

### 3. Their no, or their need for space, is allowed (B65, B236)
Strength: faint.
- B65, *Also Look Outward* [Before You Try to Go Deep]: "after all, they're allowed to say no to you."
- B236, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: "wanting closeness is legitimate, and demanding instant replies or punishing someone for needing space isn't the same thing."
- Shared point: someone else's no or need for space isn't an offense against you, even when it frustrates you.
- Does the later one add anything? Yes: the split between the need and the strategy, and the note that it's hard to tell them apart while you're the one waiting. The shared caution is a side remark both times, about 170 blocks apart.
- Which carries it better: B236. There the point is what the sentence is about; in B65 it's an aside at the end of a long sentence about something else.

### 4. Let a safe person see you're not fine (B74, B192, B236)
Strength: moderate for B192 and B236; B74 is looser.
- B74, *Also Look Outward* [Before You Try to Go Deep]: "keeping up a fake normal, happy version of yourself to hold onto it, I'd either bring more of the truth in, carefully"
- B192, *When to Change the Strategy* [When the Adult Voice Feels Fake]: "like texting a friend that you're having a rough day instead of saying you're fine."
- B236, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: "Or it may be letting a safe person see something imperfect."
- Shared point: the grown-up move is to let someone trustworthy see that you're not fine, instead of keeping up a front.
- Does the later one add anything? It places this as a way out of forced adulthood, next to asking for help and saying what you'd prefer. But B192 already pairs the same act with the same over-adult picture ("a few days later you're skipping lunch and saying yes to everything again."), so on this point a reader would feel told again.
- Which carries it better: B192, because it names one specific act at one specific moment.

### 5. Being the grown-up isn't more control (B123, B236)
Strength: moderate.
- B123, *When the Spiritual Relationship Hurts* [Borrow the Adult Before You Can Be the Adult]: "Being the grown-up doesn't mean controlling everything yourself, though."
- B236, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: "tightening control again probably feels like the grown-up thing, but the next adult act may be asking for help instead"
- Shared point: control feels like the adult thing to do, but it isn't what being the adult means.
- Does the later one add anything? It takes the point beyond betrayal and names alternatives (asking for help, saying what you'd prefer, letting a safe person see something imperfect). B123 has its own addition: act where it's yours to act, and let go where it isn't. The core sentence would still read as told again, with grown-up and control in both.
- Which carries it better: B236, because it states the point for everyone and says what to do instead; B123 ties it to one situation, a teacher's betrayal.

### 6. Holding a limit kindly is love; giving in isn't (B156, B234, B235)
Strength: faint to mild.
- B156, *Make a Simple Vow* [Building Trust With Your Little One]: "Now you're in my mom's place: you still go to bed, and staying kind about it shows them bedtime isn't a lack of love."
- B234, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: "You hold standards, and you ask hard things of it so it can grow, at its own pace."
- B235, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: "Candy and screens can pass for self-love."
- Shared point: loving your little one includes limits it doesn't like, and giving it whatever it wants isn't love.
- Does the later one add anything? Yes: growth as the reason for the limits, and the forms false self-love takes. A reader would more likely feel the idea extended than repeated.
- Which carries it better: B156, because it shows the point in a scene the article has already built (the bedtime story).

### 7. Get out into art, nature and new things (B230, B234)
Strength: mild to moderate.
- B230, *Let the Child Be Bad at Things* [Sometimes There Isn't a Clear Child Yet]: "go try something new just for you. Maybe a foraging group, or a painting class. Stargazing can be awe-inspiring"
- B234, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: "You also widen its world with art and music, and you point out beautiful things when you see them."
- Shared point: take your little one out into art, nature and beautiful things.
- Does the later one add anything? It makes this the Guide's job (choosing what the little one gets to experience, not only trying things to see what you like), and adds music and "the wider world past the end of the street you grew up on". With only two paragraphs and a heading between them, the art-and-nature overlap would partly read as told again.
- Which carries it better: B230, because it names specific activities and says what to watch for: "Notice what holds your attention when nobody is impressed."

## 2. Contradictions

### 1. Stopping because it has had enough: honoring a no, or permissiveness? (B178, B191, B235)
Strength: mild.
- B178, *Start With Whatever Showed Up* [When the Adult Voice Feels Fake]: "And if it says no to going deeper in this work, or to an altered state, I'd stop there too."
- B191, *When to Change the Strategy* [When the Adult Voice Feels Fake]: "Not wanting to do it anymore is a reason too" … "If it still says no, that's your answer."
- B235, *The Inner Guide Comes Later*: "So can escaping anything difficult." … "It's had enough, so why put it through this?"
- The pull: earlier, a part's no, or not wanting to go on, is enough to stop, even when the part can't say why. B235 presents the grown-up's "It's had enough, so why put it through this?" as permissiveness passing for self-love.
- Does the article say why? Partly. "anything difficult" in B235, "at its own pace" in B234, and B36 in *Before You Try to Go Deep*, where the grown-up says "Okay, that's enough for today," and tries something gentler "instead of giving up on them", together point to a line between pacing or consent on one side and avoiding all difficulty on the other. Read that way, B36 and B235 agree. But B235 doesn't say how to tell a no you should honor from an escape you shouldn't, so a reader who remembers B178 and B191 may feel pulled both ways.
- Placement: each side sits where it belongs, the consent rule where the article deals with parts that say no, and the permissiveness warning in the Guide section. Neither names the line between them.
