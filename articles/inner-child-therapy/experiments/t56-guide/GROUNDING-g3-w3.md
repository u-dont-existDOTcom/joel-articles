# Grounding, P3 draft g3-w3 (Opus, turn 56; lightly shortened in the record)

[1] FLAG CHANGED: "where your discipline is perfect" — carries "forced adulthood: perfect discipline, no visible needs, and very little joy or closeness" but states the discipline as something the reader has achieved. The guide means the demand ("Another form of forced adulthood is demanding a perfectly clean mind"). Readers caught in this trap usually hear the article's critic ("you're not good enough, and anything that does look good is fake anyway"), so they'd never call their discipline perfect and would read past themselves — the reader should get that the trap is holding yourself to perfect discipline, whether or not you manage it.
[2] OK — carries: "the next adult act may be asking for help, expressing a preference ... not tightening control again"; the NEW "probably feels like the grown-up thing" is something any reader in that spot would grant.
[3] OK — carries: "letting a safe person see something imperfect" (the "safe" condition is kept).
[4] OK — carries: "Judge the strategy as well as the need. Wanting closeness is legitimate; demanding instant replies or punishing someone for needing space is not the same thing."
[5] FLAG MISFIRES: "When you're the one waiting, it's hard to tell the difference." — a NEW claim that makes anyone waiting on a reply ask "am I the one demanding and punishing?", and it gives no way to check. It hits this paragraph's own reader hardest: they were just told wanting closeness is legitimate, their habit is "no visible needs", and the doubt gives them a reason to hide the need again — the guide's point stands without this sentence, so it can go; if it stays, the reader should get that the wanting is fine however strong, and that what they check is the action (pushing for an answer now, or making someone pay for the space they took).
[6] OK — carries: "The Guide helps find a workable way to meet the need without erasing either person"; "either of you" clearly means you and the person needing space from [4]–[5], and it covers both halves of the paragraph (your hidden needs, their space).
QUESTIONS:
Q: "How do I tell whether I'm just wanting closeness or already demanding?" — answered: nowhere. — MUST: a reader in forced adulthood who guesses "demanding" hides the need again, and a reader who guesses "just wanting" keeps pressing — dropping [5] removes the question.
Q: "My discipline is never good enough. Is this trap even about me?" — answered: nowhere — MUST: the trap is demanding perfect discipline of yourself, whether or not you meet it; a few words.
Q: "The last paragraph told me to hold standards and ask hard things. How is this different?" — answered: here ("If you're stuck there"; "you have no visible needs, and there's very little joy or closeness") and earlier ("so it can grow, at its own pace") — not open.
Q: "What if I don't have a safe person?" — answered: earlier ("So I'd call somebody or show up somewhere, mess and all."; "People can earn more of you by how they handle the smaller things you tell them, and how they take it when you say no.") — not open.
