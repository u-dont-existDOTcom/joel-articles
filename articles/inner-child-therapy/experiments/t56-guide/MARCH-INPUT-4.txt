Without that kind of direction, reparenting can turn permissive. Asking anything hard of your little one can feel like turning into the parent you inherited, if yours was a critic or a warden. Halfway through asking, you might not be sure whose voice that is. So sparing it everything feels like love, and candy and screens can pass for self-love that way. Escaping anything difficult can too.

The opposite trap is forced adulthood: demanding perfect discipline of yourself and no visible needs, with very little joy or closeness. It can be the old "don't be needy" rule passing for the Guide. No wonder tightening control again feels like the grown-up thing. If you're stuck there, the next adult act may be asking someone safe for help, or saying what you'd prefer.

Once a need does show, give it a fair hearing, and judge the strategy too. Wanting closeness is legitimate. You might want a lot of it, after all that hiding. Demanding instant replies or punishing someone for needing space isn't the same thing. The Guide helps you find some workable way to meet the need without erasing you or the other person.
