# Stance check: "The Inner Guide Comes Later", paragraphs 1–3

The rewrite is word-for-word identical to the published three paragraphs, heading included. Every finding below applies to both texts, and the rewrite still has everything listed in the second part.

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. Paragraph 1: the new-interest exercise is recast as holding standards**

- Rewrite: "You hold standards… That's what I was just asking of you, with that new interest."
- Essay (*Let the Child Be Bad at Things*): "It's okay if we're bad at this, nobody's on trial. We're just seeing if we like it." The same section adds: "Maybe after that you still genuinely dislike it, and then you may quit." *From Survival to Experimental Play* also frames these as experiments done "when nobody's grading you."
- What a reader would wrongly believe: that the new interest was a standard the little one had to meet so it could grow. The essay set it up as an ungraded experiment that you can quit once you've given it enough tries.

**2. Paragraph 2: "It's had enough" becomes the voice of the permissive trap**

- Rewrite: "So can escaping anything difficult. In the moment, that one sounds like the kindest thing… 'It's had enough, so why put it through this?'"
- Essay (*When to Change the Strategy*): "Not wanting to do it anymore is a reason too… If it still says no, that's your answer." *Start With Whatever Showed Up* agrees: "if it says no to going deeper in this work, or to an altered state, I'd stop there too."
- What a reader would wrongly believe: that deciding the little one has had enough of a specific thing is the permissive trap. The essay says a no that still holds after you've heard it out is your answer.

## Contradictions in the published text

1. **Paragraph 2 contradicts the rest of the essay.**
   - Elsewhere, the essay separates pausing from giving up. *Before You Try to Go Deep* says the grown-up can say "Okay, that's enough for today," and "try something gentler instead of giving up on them."
   - Three sections tell you to honor a part's no:
     - *Start With Whatever Showed Up*: "I'd stop there too."
     - *Hear the Protective Part First*: "If it says no, I'd listen."
     - *When to Change the Strategy*: "If it still says no, that's your answer."
   - Paragraph 2 doesn't keep that line between pausing and giving up. It puts the language of a protective limit ("had enough") on a thought about one specific "this," and presents that thought as what the trap sounds like. The only thing separating the trap from the stopping the essay endorses is the word "anything" in "escaping anything difficult."
   - A reader who remembers those sections would see the essay take them back.
   - **The rewrite still has it**, because the two texts are identical.

No sentence within the three paragraphs takes back another. I don't count conflict 1 as a contradiction. Read the way people talk, "hold standards" can mean the "enough tries" ask, and that fits "nobody's on trial." A reader would notice the change of frame, but nothing is taken back.

Conflicts in the rewrite: 2. Contradictions in the published text: 1.
