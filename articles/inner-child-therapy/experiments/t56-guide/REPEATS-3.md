# Repeats check 3: the opening of The Inner Guide Comes Later

I checked the first four paragraphs under the h1 The Inner Guide Comes Later (B234–B237) against each other and against every other block (B0–B237). Only repeats and contradictions that involve at least one of B234–B237 are listed. Repeats are numbered in article order, by their earlier instance. Each instance is named by its nearest heading, with the h1 in brackets when that heading is a subsection.

Counts: 7 repeats (2 of them partial), 0 contradictions, 1 callback that relies on an earlier section (plus 1 adjacent callback, noted).

## 1. Repeats

**1. B9 and B235: looking after yourself without the Guide's direction goes wrong**

- B9, The Chicken-and-Egg Problem: "Our lives can become small and like a never-ending therapy session when we focus only on caring for ourselves"
- B235, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "Without that kind of direction, reparenting can turn permissive."
- Shared point: care without the Guide's direction turns into a trap.
- Added or told again: B235 adds why it happens (asking anything hard can feel like turning into the parent you inherited) and what it looks like (candy, screens, escaping anything difficult). The warning itself is told again, as the first of two opposite traps, in the same order B9 uses (see #2). B234's "widen its world" also answers B9's life that can "become small".
- Better carrier: B235. It sits in the Guide's own section and gives the cause and the examples; B9 previews it in one sentence.

**2. B9 and B236: the opposite trap, where direction crowds out care**

- B9, The Chicken-and-Egg Problem: "But then some people over-rely on their mission to avoid caring for themselves."
- B236, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "The opposite trap is forced adulthood: demanding perfect discipline of yourself and no visible needs, with very little joy or closeness."
- Shared point: the other extreme, where mission or discipline stands in for caring for yourself.
- Added or told again: B236 adds a name (forced adulthood), a source (an old rule against being needy, with needs hidden to keep belonging) and a way out (asking for help, saying what you'd prefer). Read together with #1, B9's two-sided warning comes back a second time, in the same order.
- Better carrier: B236. It names the trap and gives the exit; B9 states it in one sentence while first setting out the three roles.

**3. B123 and B236: control passing for being the grown-up (partial)**

- B123, When the Spiritual Relationship Hurts [Borrow the Adult Before You Can Be the Adult]: "Being the grown-up doesn't mean controlling everything yourself, though."
- B236, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "No wonder tightening control again feels like the grown-up thing, and asking for anything feels needy."
- Shared point: after a hurt, controlling everything yourself can feel like being the grown-up, and the way out is leaning on support.
- Added or told again: partial. The hurt differs (a teacher's betrayal vs. needs hidden in childhood), and so does the support (spiritual support and the Serenity Prayer vs. asking a person for help). The core claim, that tight control isn't what makes you the grown-up, is told again.
- More natural place: B236. The Guide section is where the article sorts real adult direction from what passes for it; B123 makes the point inside a narrower situation.

**4. B156 and B234–B235: holding a limit kindly is love, and sparing the child everything isn't**

- B156, Make a Simple Vow [Building Trust With Your Little One]: "you still go to bed, and staying kind about it shows them bedtime isn't a lack of love."
- B234, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "you hold standards, the way a good parent would."
- B235, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "So sparing it everything feels like love, and candy and screens can pass for self-love that way."
- Shared point: a good parent holds the line, kindly, and a limit isn't a withdrawal of love.
- Added or told again: B234–B235 add growth as the reason for hard asks, and the fear behind giving in (sounding like the parent you inherited). B235 gives the reverse side of the same lesson (no limits passing for love), so a reader who remembers the bedtime story (B136–B137, Love Doesn't Have to Wait for Trust, which B156 already calls back to) meets it again.
- Better carrier: B156. A concrete scene tied to a story the reader already knows; B234–B235 put it in general terms.

**5. B167 and B236: the little one hid part of themselves to keep belonging**

- B167, The Parent You Inherited [When the Adult Voice Feels Fake]: "your little one learned bit by bit to hide that part of their authentic self which wasn't allowed, so they could keep belonging."
- B236, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "Your little one learned to hide their needs so they could keep belonging."
- Shared point: an inherited rule taught the little one to hide something in order to belong, and the rule seemed legitimate. B167: "The rules can even sound reasonable"; B236: "It's the old "don't be needy" rule passing for the Guide." B236's "perfect discipline" also echoes B168's "impossible ideal" in the same earlier section.
- Added or told again: B236 narrows "that part of their authentic self" to needs and ties it to forced adulthood. Otherwise it's the same claim in nearly the same sentence, right after B235's explicit callback to "the parent you inherited", which makes the echo easy to hear. The h1 Sometimes There Isn't a Clear Child Yet builds on the same mechanism (B207, B210, B216), but B167 is the close match.
- Better carrier: B167. It's the section about the inherited parent and has the concrete example (being sent from the table until the crying stops).

**6. B192 and B236: the next adult act is letting someone see a need (partial)**

- B192, When to Change the Strategy [When the Adult Voice Feels Fake]: "like texting a friend that you're having a rough day instead of saying you're fine."
- B236, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "If you're stuck there, the next adult act may be asking for help anyway, or saying what you'd prefer."
- Shared point: when you've been hiding your needs (B192: "skipping lunch and saying yes to everything again"; B236: "no visible needs"), the small adult step is to let another person know what you need.
- Added or told again: partial. B236 adds "saying what you'd prefer" and ties the step to the trap it answers. The instruction itself is told again.
- Better carrier: B192. It gives a concrete act and says it can be harder than the meditation; B236's version is general.

**7. Inside B236, second and fourth sentences: the stance passes for adulthood, and needing feels needy**

- B236, sentence 2, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "It's the old "don't be needy" rule passing for the Guide."
- B236, sentence 4, The Inner Guide Comes Later: "No wonder tightening control again feels like the grown-up thing, and asking for anything feels needy."
- Shared point: the rigid stance looks like proper adult direction, and needing is treated as needy.
- Added or told again: sentence 4 adds "No wonder", tying it to the childhood origin in sentence 3. Otherwise the reader gets the same two ideas again, two sentences later.
- More natural place: sentence 2. It follows the definition directly and names the disguise in the section's own terms (the Guide).

## 2. Contradictions

None that a reasonable reader would see. These pairs point different ways on the surface; in each case the article says why they differ.

- B234 "You ask hard things of it so it can grow, at its own pace." (The Inner Guide Comes Later) and B231 "It's okay if we're bad at this, nobody's on trial." (Let the Child Be Bad at Things): B234 ties the hard ask to "that new interest", meaning giving it enough tries rather than being good at it, and B235 names the worry that asking can sound like the parent you inherited.
- B235 "So sparing it everything feels like love" and the earlier advice to ease off: B36 "I'd go shallower" (Before You Try to Go Deep), B178 on stopping when a part says no to going deeper (Start With Whatever Showed Up), B191 "If it still says no, that's your answer." (When to Change the Strategy). B234 says "at its own pace" and B235 says "everything"; the earlier passages are about intensity and specific refusals.
- B234's hard asks and B7 "it doesn't make sense to ask the child to heal itself" (The Chicken-and-Egg Problem): B236 names forced adulthood as the opposite trap, so the article separates asks that help the child grow from handing it the adult's job.
- B236 "the next adult act may be asking for help anyway" and B125 "try it before you call next time" (Become the Adult Apprentice): each speaks to an opposite habit and says which (B125: people who call the same friend every time; B236: "If you're stuck there").
- B234's art, music, nature and wider world and B214 "through long reflection with no outside input" (From Survival to Experimental Play): B214 marks that as "early on", and the h1's title puts the Guide later.
- B235 putting candy and screens under a missing Guide, and the top image (B0) listing "Protector: limits": the roles overlap, and B8 calls them "one grown-up doing three jobs".
- B235 "Escaping anything difficult can too." and B194 "I'd thank whatever part of you came up with the escape plan, and slow down." (When the Urge to Escape Arrives): B194 thanks the part but doesn't follow its plan.
