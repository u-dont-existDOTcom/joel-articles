Céline is here for a reason beyond being a beautiful illustration of the inner child. Sometimes you understand what you feel perfectly well, but you still can’t get emotionally close enough to it for the rest of the practice to become real. Music can be a doorway. A song that already carries something for you can bring tears, tenderness, grief, longing, love, courage, bodily feeling, or memory into reach much faster than trying to manufacture the feeling deliberately.

I used to listen to The Queen and the Soldier every night because it reliably got the tears out. If you already know music that opens something in you, use it deliberately before or during inner-child work. Don’t judge the session by how dramatic the response is. Crying, chills, a rush of energy, or a vivid memory mean that something became accessible; they don’t by themselves mean that the underlying pattern changed.

Once the door opens, notice what is available now that wasn’t available a minute ago. Can the Nurturer get closer? Can the Protector stay with what is happening? Is there grief, love, a need, a memory, a bodily feeling, or a sentence from the child that you couldn’t reach before? Continue the practice from there rather than chasing intensity. If the music makes you increasingly overwhelmed, unreal, panicky, disoriented, or unable to stop, turn it down or off and come back to the room and the present before going deeper.
