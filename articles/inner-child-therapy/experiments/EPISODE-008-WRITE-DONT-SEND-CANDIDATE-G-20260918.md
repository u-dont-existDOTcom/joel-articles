### Write It. Don't Send It Yet.

Write the ugly response and don't send it yet.

Here's the part I don't want to lose: cleaning up the message is not the same as deciding the person who wrote it was being ridiculous. The draft may be unfair in places. It may be way too long. You may owe an apology. Fix those parts before another human has to read them. But somewhere in the rant there may also be the first honest “no,” or the request you kept talking yourself out of. Don't edit that away just because the rest is embarrassing.

Maybe what finally goes out is two calm sentences. Maybe it's, “I need some time. I'll give you a fuller response tomorrow.” If you say tomorrow, mean it. Maybe nothing needs to go out at all. And if somebody needs protecting now, stop worrying about the quality of the message and deal with that.

Then look back at the unsent version. The person who wrote it is still there even if the text is gone. What were they trying so hard to make somebody understand? Did they need protection, reassurance, actual help? A cleaner message may solve the communication problem. It doesn't automatically take care of its writer.
