# Before You Try to Go Deep

How much of the grown-up you is actually here right now? Try looking around the room, pausing before you act, making a choice and following through on it. If you can't do that because you're basically the frightened kid, leave the deeper conversation until later. You may need somebody steady there with you, or to do whatever helps you come back a bit before you try to talk to the child.

## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child

There's another reason to leave the child alone that has nothing to do with being too flooded. The adult can be fully present and still not be safe for vulnerability. This is not a belief test. A religious or unusual belief does not establish current harmful intent, and neither does an intrusive thought you hate. Anger, charged language, an unwanted fantasy, or something harmful you did in the past do not establish it either. The question is what the adult currently endorses and intends: whether they want or enjoy frightening, humiliating, exploiting, or harming what's vulnerable.

If that's where you are, being calm enough to pause and choose doesn't solve the problem. Don't bring the child forward even for a minute to see what happens. A frightened reaction proves that the child is frightened, not that you're safe. And if producing that fear is rewarding, then using the child as a test has already made the exercise part of the harm.

With the child out of the picture, the attention goes to the adult. Something brought you here, and the identity or stance itself can become part of the material: what you're getting from it, what it has cost, who benefits, and whether your life and happiness look anything like what you expected. An identity that calls itself sovereign gives you one obvious place to look. If an urge or an authority has to be obeyed—whether that's a doctrine, a group, a spirit, or a master—can you pause and question it? Can you refuse it or change your mind? If not, that is part of the adult work.

This is still a situation for care. If someone is helping you, their job is to listen without praising cruelty, turning it into a theology fight, or making exploitation more effective. For some people, sustained care from actual people may matter more here than another exercise done alone or with a bot.

A promise alone still doesn't clear the gate. Protective credibility comes from repeated choices not to exploit vulnerability, including when doing it would feel rewarding. Until that exists, don't bring the child in through a side door. A childhood photo can do it. So can hypnosis, or imagining a borrowed parent or spiritual figure making contact for you. Inner dialogue is only the most obvious route.

## You Don’t Need an Inner Monologue

“Voice,” “ask,” and “answer” below don't have to mean a literal conversation in your head. If you don't hear a little kid talking in your head, don't invent one. Actually, this is one place where the usual inner-child prompt can get confusing, because “ask” sounds like you're supposed to ask a question and then wait for a second voice to answer. Maybe that's what happens for some people, but you might not be some people.

You might barely have spontaneous words in your mind and yet still be able to think a sentence on purpose. Try it and see. If words are there, speak or write them. You might feel more comfortable that way than just staying in imagination land. And if that starts feeling like homework you're doing for a child who isn't answering, drop it.
