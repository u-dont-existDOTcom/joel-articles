## Strategy-A fingerprint for this pass

For each red span:

1. Preserve the current idea order.
2. Identify the **hot thought** — the one part a reader is actually feeling or wondering about.
3. Give that thought more rhetorical time than its neighboring qualification or caveat. Expansion is **unequal**, not global.
4. Let secondary functions hitchhike in dependent clauses, asides, examples, or the next paragraph rather than each receiving a clean sentence and stop.
5. Permit a thought to linger, circle once, or continue through a connective before resolving. Do not make every sentence advance the manual by exactly one unit.
6. Use play where it performs work: a recognizable cultural image, a meme-like contrast, a slightly absurd hypothetical, or an affectionate tease can make the abstract thought visible. One good image is better than several decorative references.
7. End the local passage when the live thought naturally runs out, not when all implications have been summarized.

## Playfulness admission rule

Playfulness is not a quota and not detector camouflage. Use a playful image/reference only when it does at least one of these jobs:

- makes an invisible mental dynamic instantly visible;
- lets the reader recognize themselves without clinical language;
- releases tension without weakening a safety instruction;
- creates a memorable image that could later become a legitimate meme/illustration;
- restores a genuine author-reader social act (teasing, shared recognition, absurdity).

Do not stack references. Prefer one image that survives rereading. Do not invent Joel-specific memories or claim Joel saw/liked/experienced a cultural artifact unless sourced.
