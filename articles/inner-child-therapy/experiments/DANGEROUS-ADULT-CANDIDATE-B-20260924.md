## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child

The last section was about whether enough of the adult is here. There is one more gate before putting the child in that adult's care: does the adult actually want to protect them? Most of this guide assumes yes. Usually that's a fair assumption. But being calm, organized, or perfectly capable of making choices does not make somebody safe if they still enjoy frightening or humiliating vulnerable people, want to exploit them, or hope this work will make manipulation easier.

If that's true for you right now, don't bring the child forward to see what happens. A frightened child proves only that the child is frightened. It does not tell you you're safe, and the exercise cannot become one more way to get a reaction you enjoy.

Keep the work with the present-day adult instead. Start with whatever actually brought you here and what, if anything, you want to change. The identity or stance itself can be looked at without using the child to break it: what it gives you, what it costs, whom it serves, and whether the life or happiness it produces is what you expected. The same thing goes for sovereignty. If you say an urge, doctrine, group, spirit, or master has to be obeyed, then "sovereign" is not doing much yet. Can you stop, question it, refuse it, or change your mind? That's the part that matters here.

None of this is a religion test, or a test for having ugly thoughts. An unusual belief, an intrusive thought you hate, anger, charged language, an unwanted fantasy, or something harmful you did in the past does not by itself mean you intend harm now. The relevant line is what you endorse and intend to do now. Care can stay in the room on either side of that line. Listening does not require praising cruelty, fighting about theology, or helping somebody get better at exploiting people.

Nor does saying "I promise" clear the gate. What changes it is a pattern of not exploiting vulnerability, including when exploitation would feel rewarding. Until that pattern is real, keep child contact out of the work—dialogue, childhood photos, hypnosis, borrowed-parent imagery, spiritual figures, whatever route would put the child back in front of the same harmful intent. And for some people, the thing that helps most here may be steady care from actual people over time, not another exercise done alone or with a bot.
