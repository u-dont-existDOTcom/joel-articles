I think the Guide also points beyond itself, toward a belonging that doesn't ask you to betray yourself (no membership dues, no loyalty oath). That might be nature, or existence itself, or whatever your tradition recognizes as ultimate, whether you call it God or the Tao or something else. It gets more real with time outside and in contemplative practice, more than it ever will as an idea. And that ground can make belonging with people a lot less desperate. Not overnight, but the Guide keeps reminding your little one that a group's approval, the kind you only keep by performing, isn't the deepest place it belongs.
