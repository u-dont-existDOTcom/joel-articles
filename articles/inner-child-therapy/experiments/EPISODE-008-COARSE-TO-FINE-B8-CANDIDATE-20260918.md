### Also Look Outward

You may not know for certain whether the other person is or could be capable of a healthier response, and neither do they. If they promise to change, that's probably as useful as their prior promises were. Think about how long it has taken you to become more healthy. Could you have done that from one moment to the next based on a promise? Sometimes we can be a better support for someone when we maintain a realistic boundary, so that we can hold high expectations, and still feel prepared for their failure to meet them.

Sometimes the evidence is annoyingly mixed. One conversation is a disaster: you say you were hurt, they get defensive, and somehow you're calming them down while your own point disappears. A day or two later they come back and actually talk about it. Maybe they needed the time. They can still disagree with you, including about a boundary you hate, without that first blowup suddenly becoming okay. Then another subject comes up later and you hear yourself thinking, wait, how am I comforting you again? That recurring shape is closer to what [Lindsay Gibson's work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) asks you to notice about emotional immaturity.

And sometimes you aren't only the person whose point disappeared. You really did hurt them. The apology is real, but they are still disappointed, so you keep going. Somewhere in there you start retracting the part you still believe. What exactly are you apologizing for now? You can feel guilty through the whole conversation. Some of that may belong to the harm you actually caused, but guilt still isn't a meter for how much of the entire conflict is yours. It also doesn't tell you whether the other person is manipulating you.

Maybe after all that they can repeat your point back to you and still think you're wrong. Your no may still seem unfair to them. So you explain it another way. Then another. Eventually you hear yourself explaining why the last explanation wasn't quite what you meant. At some point there may really be nothing else to add.
