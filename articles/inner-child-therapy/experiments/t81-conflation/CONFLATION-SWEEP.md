### 1. B318, "Let the Child Be Bad at Things" (under "Sometimes There Isn't a Clear Child Yet"). Type (a), firm

> The old parent in your head may demand immediate talent, though, and then the first time you're awkward at it, that's "evidence" that you're stupid or clumsy. Or you hear, "Come on, you're not really the type." I think your little one is listening to see if anybody's going to stick up for them. The grown-up you has to be the one who does: "It's okay if we're bad at this, nobody's on trial. We're just seeing if we like it."

This is the article's copy of the hypnosis-guide passage fixed on 2026-10-21, and it has the same problem.

- Two sentences earlier, the internalized voice is named as an adult living inside you ("The old parent in your head").
- Then "The grown-up you" appears with nothing to say which grown-up is meant.
- A reader can take it for that same voice. They can also take it for their present-day self, the one who "hear[s]" the voice.
- Elsewhere in the article "the grown-up you" means the built adult (B16, B43, B225, B226), which helps. But here it comes right after the inherited voice, the same setup Joel fixed in the guide.
- The intended figure is the built adult, the one who sticks up for the child.

**Smallest fix:** "The grown-up you has to be the one who does" → "The grown-up you're building has to be the one who does". This uses B205's "building" and B408's "the inner adult you build".
**Alternative:** "The new grown-up has to be the one who does". This is the name B206-B207 use.

**Optional, same block:** "The old parent in your head" → "The parent you inherited", the name used in B204-B207 and B211. Only "old" separates this phrase from "inner parent", which B8 and B11 use for the built adult. The demanding and name-calling already make the inherited reading clear, so this change is just for consistency with the fix.

**Addressee of the quoted line (e):** it can be said to the little one as reassurance, or back at the old parent as the sticking-up. Either reading fits the map, so it needs no change unless you want to commit to one.

### 4. B15, five-stage list, item 5 ("The Chicken-and-Egg Problem"). Open: either reading fits

> This can actually feel really weird if you've been stuck in adult mode for a while.

Two lines up, item 4 names the built adult "Adult mode itself": "you become a natural at sending love to little you, protecting them, and directing them." Item 5 then uses the same term for something you get "stuck in". A reader can take it as either of these:

- (i) Stuck at stage 4. The built adult does its jobs with no play, so the relationship in stage 5 feels weird.
- (ii) The over-functioning grown-up that B324 later calls "forced adulthood" ("Nope, all good!"). This is the present-day adult running the life with the child shut out.

Both readings fit the map. Under (ii), one name covers two versions of the inner adult in neighbouring list items. That's the kind of slip Joel described. Here it's built adult vs present-day adult, though, not built vs inherited, so it falls outside the letter of (a)-(e).

**Fix:** if you mean (i), no change is needed ("stuck at stage 4" would make it explicit). If you mean (ii), "stuck in adult mode" → "stuck in forced adulthood".

### 5. B259, "Altered States Can Require Reparenting". Open: either reading fits

> My inner guide saw it as a perfect opportunity to demonstrate the power of love over fear.

- When the article means the built adult's third job, it capitalizes it: B10 "an inner Guide", B320 "The Inner Guide", B411 "The inner Guide".

**Fix:** if you mean the role, write "My inner Guide". If you mean something else, keep it lowercase. Your call.
