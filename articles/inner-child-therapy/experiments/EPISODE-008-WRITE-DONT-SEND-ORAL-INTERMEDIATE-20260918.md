# Episode 008 — Write It. Don't Send It Yet — oral-friend intermediate

Date: 2026-09-18
Status: **NON-PUBLIC INTERMEDIATE / DO NOT TEST AS ARTICLE PROSE**

Friend prompt:

`I'm furious and I've written a message I want to send. What should I do with it?`

Raw response:

Honestly, keep writing it. Just don't send it while you're still in that state. Let yourself write the embarrassing version, because otherwise you can end up editing yourself before you even know what you're trying to say.

Then come back later and read it like somebody you care about handed it to you. You'd probably notice pretty quickly that some lines are just there to hurt the other person back. Okay, those don't need to go anywhere. But there may also be a sentence in there that's basically, “No. I'm not okay with this.” Don't delete that just because it arrived surrounded by a rant. And if you actually did something wrong, the apology belongs there too.

What you send might be much shorter than what you wrote. It might just be, “I need some time. I'll get back to you tomorrow.” If you say tomorrow, though, actually come back tomorrow. Or maybe you decide not to answer at all. And obviously if there's an actual safety issue, deal with that instead of sitting there polishing a text.

The part people miss is that after you've figured out what to send, the angry draft still tells you something about the person who wrote it. What were they desperate to get heard about? What did they need right then—protection, reassurance, actual help? You can throw the message away without throwing that person away.
