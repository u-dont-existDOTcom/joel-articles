## Exact candidate

### Okay, But What Are They Actually Doing?

Say you tell somebody they hurt you. They get upset, and now you feel guilty too, which is not the same thing as knowing you were wrong. Maybe you really did say something hurtful and need to apologize. Fine. But then what happens? Does the apology stay an apology, or does it somehow eat the whole conversation until the thing you brought up is gone? You can be wrong about one part without giving away the rest, and you don't need to decide they're manipulating you to notice that you're giving up the thing you came to say.

One lousy conversation doesn't tell you much about somebody. They may need time, or you may have hit a boundary of theirs. But if this keeps happening, eventually “maybe they were just having a bad day” stops explaining it. [Lindsay Gibson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) writes about emotional immaturity with more attention to repeated behavior than motive-hunting. You may never know why they couldn't hear you that day. If next time they come back and actually deal with what you said, great—change your mind. If all that's changed is the promise, the behavior hasn't changed yet. You don't need another round just to become extra certain.

The really annoying version is when you think none of this counts until they understand you. So you explain it better. Then you explain why the better explanation sounded wrong. Somehow you're now defending your right to have brought it up in the first place. They may never see it your way. You can still let your no stand and end the conversation while they think you're completely wrong.

The rest of the argument may still be screaming in your head.
