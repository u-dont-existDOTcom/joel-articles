### Also Look Outward

Sometimes an awful conversation looks different after there has been a little time. The person comes back and actually talks about what you raised. They still disagree with you. Their boundary still makes you mad. Now you have more to go on than the first fight. If the subject changes but you keep ending up taking care of their reaction while your own experience disappears, that's a different kind of repetition. [Lindsay Gibson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) writes about those recurring patterns in her work on emotional immaturity.

You may not know for certain whether the other person is or could be capable of a healthier response, and neither do they. If they promise to change, that's probably as useful as their prior promises were. Think about how long it has taken you to become more healthy. Could you have done that from one moment to the next based on a promise? Sometimes we can be a better support for someone when we maintain a realistic boundary, so that we can hold high expectations, and still feel prepared for their failure to meet them.

If you really did hurt them, repair it.

You can mean the apology. They're still upset, and somehow you start backing away from the complaint too. Maybe you really did explain it badly, so you say it again. But there isn't always a version that makes somebody understand. Guilt can be there while you still say no. It doesn't prove the whole conflict was yours, and it doesn't prove they're manipulating you.
