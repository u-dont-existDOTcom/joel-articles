`# The Inner Guide`

*(P1, mine, unchanged)*

As the Guide, you choose what your little one learns and what it gets to experience, and you hold standards, the way a good parent would. Your parent's harsh tone doesn't come with the job, though. You ask hard things of it so it can grow, at its own pace. That's what I was just asking of you, with that new interest. You also widen its world with art and music, and you point out beautiful things when you see them. Nature is part of that, and so is the wider world past the end of the street you grew up on.

`## Between Spoiling and Clamping Down`

*(new heading)*

*(P2, mine, unchanged)*

Maybe you've let a small child win at Go Fish and never found a good time to start playing for real. Who wants to be the grown-up who finally wins? Without the Guide, you can slide from looking after your little one into spoiling it the same way. Sweets and screens can pass for loving yourself, and so can getting it out of anything hard. Comfort does better at set times, where it can't get out of hand. It shouldn't get canceled as a punishment after a bad day, either (ST-CA, section 17.1.4).

*(P3, yours, unchanged)*

After all, sometimes “I want it NOW!” is just that: a rung on the desire treadmill. No hidden guard or old trauma required, because all creatures have endless desires naturally anyway, especially when immature. You can hear that want without shame about it. Hearing it doesn’t require giving into it, especially if getting your way would hurt you or somebody else.

*(P4, mine, unchanged)*

I think the opposite trap can feel a lot like finally having your act together. It's forced adulthood, and it sounds like "Nope, all good!" from somebody with a fever who's cleaning the whole kitchen anyway. Not a lot of fun in that kitchen, and nobody gets very close to the person in it. If that's you, clamping down harder is going to feel like the fix, but it's just more of the same. I'd try croaking to someone you trust, "Could you pick up some soup?" If you keep swinging between spoiling and clamping down, try staying warm while you say what happens next, like "Not now, we'll go after lunch."

*(P5, mine, unchanged)*

Once a need does show, after all that hiding, it's okay to want a lot of closeness. So then someone you love goes for a walk alone, and you've texted "hello??" before they're at the corner. When they get back, you sulk all night. Wanting them close is fine! But they get to go for a walk too. Ask your little one what it's afraid of. I think it just wants to know they're coming back. Then the Guide can come up with something like "See you in an hour?"

`## A Healthy Mind Is Not a Perfect Mind`

*(new heading)*

*(P6, yours, unchanged)*

The Guide doesn't need to know everything for sure (that would be an OCD Guide, and then you'd need a Guide for the OCD Guide). It chooses the next sane step that fits your values based on the info available, and that's how it helps your little one grow toward a whole life. Waiting to be sure first is sometimes proper & prudent, but if it's a pattern, it can keep life on hold, and then your little one's world stays small. Sometimes something really is stuck on someone else's end (you've asked, and followed up, and there's nothing left for you to do yet), and waiting on that isn't avoidance. The Guide can let it wait, keeping an eye out for when it frees up, and keep the rest of your life moving, so your little one keeps growing anyway.

*(P7, mine, unchanged)*

Forced adulthood can get into your head too, where it wants everything as spotless as that kitchen. At your grandpa's funeral, some dumb jingle starts up in your head and you nearly laugh during the eulogy. That doesn't mean you didn't love him, or that something's wrong with you. Minds come up with ugly and embarrassing stuff all the time, some of it the opposite of what you believe, and bodies do their own thing too. The Guide isn't there to police every thought. It notices the jingle and knows you didn't mean it, and that it isn't who you are. Then it goes with what you'd stand behind, which here is probably squeezing your mom's hand and listening, and lets the jingle go.

*(P8, yours, unchanged)*

I think this is closer to Buddhist practice too. The point is not an impossible performance in which nothing unskillful ever appears. In one discourse (sutta), AN 6.63, the Buddha defines karma in terms of intention (so if you didn't mean to cause harm, your mind is not poisoned). In DN 2, he explains that in the training to purify the mind, one first recognizes a fault, makes amends, and then restrains themself to prevent that kind of thing in future. So it's not like, "you're either pure, or you're not," which is where a lot of folks stay stuck. If you're ashamed about something you chose to do, that might be a signal to give some attention to that. If it's shame just because of an intrusive thought, then it's probably not adaptive and can be simply let go of.

`## A Belonging Deeper Than Any Group`

*(new heading)*

*(P9, mine, unchanged)*

The Guide also points beyond itself, toward a belonging deeper than any group, one that doesn't ask you to give up being yourself. That might be nature, or existence itself. It might be whatever your tradition sees as ultimate (that part is optional). Love can come from any of these places, and you also belong there, which is a little different. Maybe you've felt that outside somewhere, like you didn't need anybody's okay to be there. The way in is time in nature and contemplative practice, like meditation.

*(P10, mine, unchanged)*

With that under you, belonging with people can get a lot less desperate. Not all at once, but the Guide keeps watching for the moment your little one starts thinking, "If they stop liking me, I won't belong anywhere," and keeps reminding it that a group's approval isn't the deepest belonging there is. By the way, this job doesn't have to be called the Guide. The Wise One works, or your Inner Teacher, and "Guru" does for some people, though for cult survivors it can be a trigger word, which is understandable.
