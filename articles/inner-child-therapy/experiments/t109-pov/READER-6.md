## 1
SECTION: Physical Pain Makes It Harder
QUOTE: I’d ask for a chronic pain specialist and a written plan for the flare-ups you can see coming
LABEL: POV
MISSING: The reader is told what to ask for when attacks come almost daily; "I'd" is a preference with no reason behind it, and a pain specialist plus a written flare-up plan fits any chronic-pain article.
POVS:
SOURCE-SUPPORTED: "normal test results don’t prove the pain is imaginary, just as a treatment that helps doesn’t explain what’s causing it" is his reason for pushing on past relief; the specialist and the plan are the practical form of it.
ARTICLE-HINTED: "You can’t move into the ER." and "the best time to get diagnostics done could be right at the actual time of the crisis" hint that the written plan is how near-daily attacks get seen while they are happening.
NEW OWNER CHOICE: Did you or someone close to you get a specialist or a written plan, and what did it change?
PICK: SOURCE-SUPPORTED, smallest form: make it a plain "Ask for ..." (the same fix he made to "I'd look for what exactly is in the way") and keep the ER signs and the "don’t let amelioration of symptoms be the end of the investigation" clause, which is his position.
QUESTION: Is "I'd ask for" something you or a friend actually did with a pain specialist? If yes, one sentence on what happened; if no, plain "Ask for".

## 4
SECTION: When Being Happy Feels Like Abandoning Someone
QUOTE: I'd take that loss seriously, and look at what they do and what you owe them, before deciding it's envy.
LABEL: POV
MISSING: The reader is told not to write off someone who pulls away as envious; "I'd" gives a preference without a reason, and "what you owe them" is never said.
POVS:
SOURCE-SUPPORTED: "Do your part for people who are suffering, and skip the part where you have to be miserable too." That is what he says you owe: your part, not your misery.
ARTICLE-HINTED: "That's exactly why I was crying for 14 years" and "she actually agreed it was a good reason to be depressed!" show he knows how real the loyalty is, so "it's envy" is too easy a label for someone hurt by you leaving the shared misery.
NEW OWNER CHOICE: Did someone you cared about pull away as you got happier, and how did you tell whether it was envy or something you owed them?
PICK: SOURCE-SUPPORTED: tie "what you owe them" to his own line (your part, not your misery), and make the opening a plain "Take that loss seriously" unless he has the story.
QUESTION: When someone pulled away from you as you got happier, what told you whether it was envy or a real loss?

## 5
SECTION: When Being Happy Feels Like Abandoning Someone
QUOTE: I'd still try a small good thing, like a nice meal, and see what actually happens.
LABEL: POV
MISSING: It is the only step for a reader who believes happiness is abandonment, but "a small good thing, like a nice meal" is portable advice with no result behind it, and "I'd still" is a bare preference.
POVS:
SOURCE-SUPPORTED: "What I found, once I started cultivating my own happiness, was that I got a lot better at helping other people toward theirs." His reason to try is that it worked, and it made him more use to others, not less.
ARTICLE-HINTED: The family bargain "If I'm happy while someone I love is suffering, I'm abandoning them." A small good thing is a test of that bargain, and "see what actually happens" means checking whether the feared abandonment occurs.
NEW OWNER CHOICE: What was the first small good thing you let yourself have after those years, and what happened?
PICK: SOURCE-SUPPORTED joined to ARTICLE-HINTED: make the small good thing the test of the bargain, with his finding as the reason; swap in his own first small good thing if he has one.
QUESTION: What was the first small good thing you let yourself have, and what happened, to you and to the people you worried about?
