QUOTE: The way in is time in nature and contemplative practice, like meditation.
LABEL: POV
MISSING: The paragraph wants the reader to find a belonging that needs nobody's okay; this is its only how-to and it is the stock answer (nature, meditation), with no sign of what he did or why those two, right after the paragraph's one concrete felt detail.
POVS:
ARTICLE-HINTED: "Maybe you’ve felt that outside somewhere, like you didn’t need anybody’s okay to be there." hints that the belonging shows up outdoors and alone, and the only contemplative practice the part actually describes is loving-kindness ("it’s one way people discover that there is such a love and learn to expand it"), which "like meditation" may be pointing at.
NEW OWNER CHOICE: Where were you, and what were you doing, the time you felt you belonged without anybody's okay, and is there a particular practice you would point to as the way back to it?

## 3
SECTION: How Much Happiness Do You Know Is Possible?
QUOTE: Maybe your life isn’t meant to be just something to put up with, something to slog through, a hamster wheel, a rat race. Maybe you could live it with a sense of zest and discovery. What would you need for that?
LABEL: POV
MISSING: The reader is meant to feel the existential gap that remains after being fed and safe; this names it in stock images (slog, hamster wheel, rat race, zest) and ends on a coach's question, and the whole section has no "I", so nothing shows what he saw that made him say life could be more.
POVS:
ARTICLE-HINTED: The section's own thesis, "the horizon of your wishes may be partly determined by how much happiness/peace/freedom you know to be possible", and its ending, "But what if the experience is possible for me?", hint that the real obstacle is not knowing what you need but not having seen that it is possible, so "What would you need for that?" asks the reader to name something past their own horizon.
NEW OWNER CHOICE: Was there a time your own life felt like a slog, and a person or experience that showed you it didn't have to be?
PICK: NEW OWNER CHOICE if he has the moment (one concrete scene replaces the images and the question); otherwise ARTICLE-HINTED, trading the need-question for the section's own point that you can't want what you haven't seen is possible.
QUESTION: What first showed you that life could be more than a slog (a person, an experience, a practice), and would you tell it in a couple of sentences here?
