## Hear the Guard First
Whichever protective part showed up, try talking to it in the same Nurturer voice you meant for your little one. At some point you can ask that part, "What are you afraid would happen if you stepped back?" "Tell me what you remember, I'm listening." works well here. It puts you into the role of the Nurturer, which alleviates the instinct to argue back. Usually your little one only comes forward after that part feels heard. Peek-a-boo!
A harder voice can seem more fitting for something that's been in your way. I'd still keep it the same. If that voice isn't there in you yet, borrow it from someone who's been safe for you.
That part might say this will just be one more performance, and you'll fool yourself again. That one can sting, because there could be something to it. Maybe you've fooled yourself before, and that part remembers. If you push past it, you might get exactly the performance it was afraid of. Even if you didn't win an Emmy last year, that can look convincing, with the right words and maybe even tears, while nothing underneath feels any safer.
That part doesn't have to move out. It's been living there way longer than the grown-up you. It can keep watch while you take one small step toward your little one, and I think watching is what it wants anyway, to find out if there's a grown-up here now who could call it off if things got to be too much. It doesn't have to be all or nothing, either. Maybe it would settle for a lighter cover for now, like blinds tilted open a crack, and let you peek out for a minute (ST-CA, section 9.3.4). If it says no, I'd listen. When it lets you, keep an eye on that part too, for any change at all.
If it's been calling all this stupid, it might turn out to be scared of getting its hopes up. I wouldn't want to get mine up either. I'd tell it something like, "You don't have to hope yet, I'll keep coming back even when I mess up." Sometimes it doesn't say anything back for a while.
A new roommate doesn't win you over with a speech on move-in day (hopefully, right?). The rent just has to keep showing up. Your Protector earns that guard's trust about the same way, mostly out in ordinary life. The things you do can be small, like keeping a promise to yourself nobody else knew about. Or saying no to someone, and not texting an hour later to take it back. That second one can be really hard.
Blaming yourself can be doing a job like that, too. "It's all my fault" might be your parent's line, word for word. Then again, if it's your fault, maybe you can fix it before they leave. I'd stop the cruelty either way. And I'd get curious about what makes it so hard to give up.
## When to Change the Strategy
Weeks in and nothing much is changing? I’d want to quit too, or else keep doing the exact same thing forever so I never have to find out what's underneath this. Before going with either of those paths, look for what exactly is in the way. Sometimes the strategy was not actually implemented, just read about or played around with lightly. Or there wasn't the right environment to really practice it, like no alone time. Or perhaps you did sit down with your little one, but it felt scary or like some part of you was trying to protect you from going deeper. Whatever it turns out to be, it makes sense to work on that blocker instead of giving up on everything.
Maybe the words are all there, but the loving grown-up behind them isn’t, not yet. Some people at that point might try saying sweeter and sweeter things, and then upon that not working, perhaps just “throw the baby out with the bathwater,” and decide reparenting is a scam. I’d borrow that grown-up first. Then try again, and see if your little one can tell the difference.
Let's say an exercise leaves you feeling lighter for a few hours, but then the old feeling comes back later on. Some relief is nice, but we still want to know if we are fixing the problem at a deeper level and integrating the benefits into regular life (like texting a friend that you're having a rough day rather than saying you're fine). Jot down what changed and how long it lasted. Although if that feeds into your OCD patterns, drop it.
How many tries would I give it? It's not like we can just put a number on it. Some things shift in a day. Others take about as long as learning guitar. But do notice if progress is stagnating. If it’s making things worse, or you just can’t do it right now, I’d change course sooner (stopping counts).
Not wanting to do it anymore might be reason enough, but if that “nope” sounds like a part of you that’s trying to protect you, I’d hear it out first to get a better understanding. And if you keep following the reparenting steps correctly without getting the expected results, consider if you have a unique case these steps don’t match yet. Maybe you’ll find something that works better for you, or you can ask me what I’d do for this case, or ask a therapist you work with. Either way, I’d like to hear about it!
## When the Urge to Escape Arrives
Sometimes the urge to get away from what’s coming up emotionally gets a lot bigger. All of a sudden you need a drink, or that ex doesn’t seem so bad after all. Some people start a whole new business. If that’s you, it might mean you got close to something that really hurts, and that can be progress, honestly! I’d thank whatever part of you came up with the escape plan, and slow down. If it’s more than you can hold right now, I’d get someone steady with you.
## Intensive Meditation and Psychedelics
So why not just blast right past that part, with intensive meditation or psychedelics? Both can get you past the guards before there’s a grown-up big enough to hold whatever comes through, so I’d hold off until there’s enough Nurturer and Protector around. How much is enough depends partly on the medicine and what therapy is done with it.
Like MDMA, San Pedro is more protective and nurturing than many other medicines, especially at lower doses, so you don’t need as much Nurturer and Protector built up for those, though you still need some. Some people benefit more from solo therapy, while others benefit more from having a trained therapist guide the experience.
## Altered States Can Deepen the Therapy
I’d learn to do inner-child therapy sober first, in your ordinary mindstate, since that’s the one you’ll be living in. Cars don’t start at 60mph, that’s not the way the universe seems to work. First gear leads to second leads to third, etc. Second gear, in this case, may refer to gentle meditation or self-hypnosis. When you’re ready, my self-hypnosis guide and lucid-dreaming guide can take those deeper, into third gear. Low doses of entheogens can also synergize compatibly with various gears: cactus, CBD tea with sublingual mapacho, Loveyhuasca, iboga, and so on.
Remember those guards from The Urge to Escape, above? Same deal here, so get enough Nurturer and Protector in the room before you go deep. Borrowed is fine, as long as it’s from someone steady who won’t try to run your trip for you. But feeling attacked by a demon on a psychedelic is not necessarily paranoia. It can be real, and it’s also not bad unless you’re unprepared. It can be supremely empowering when you can send love and see how it fixes the situation.
## What to Do With a Peak
Now, the beautiful stuff. Say one night (on a psychedelic, in a dream, ten days into a meditation retreat, whatever) you felt more love than you knew was possible. Wonderful, and it matters! Keep it as a reference point, and chase it playfully, because it is already a part of you, and you’ll be like a cat chasing its tail, which can be cute and fun, or dizzy and absurd!
Jack Kornfield has a whole book on what comes next, After the Ecstasy, the Laundry. The title kind of says it all. That night can't be the bar for every morning after, or regular life starts to feel like failing. And gorgeous as it was, it doesn't mean you're done healing. The laundry will tell you more, and it's the same test as in Timing Is Individual, further down.
One reason the peak is so momentary is because we often don’t need to truly give anything up to get it. Say you’re still pretty angry, and you want enlightenment, but it has to come with the money and the partner in one package. Ketamine can get you to a peak anyway. Then, you might now have some kind of transcendental “understanding” glimpse that seems like it could fix everything, if only you could be more aware of it, which often leads into a cycle of taking more and more medicine over time. I’d prefer to ask if any of the discontent has been let go, or the anger? If not, that peak was less the love we’re after here than “I’ll be grateful once I’ve got it all,” the gratitude with conditions from Ask God for a Loan.
And if your little one still can’t take the love in? Hold off on going for bigger, crazier breakthroughs, however tempting it is, or at least know that they are a double-edged sword. Something’s in the way, and getting to know it is your next job. Often it’s a protective part that’s seen love come with strings before. Hear it out first, the way you did in Hear the Guard First.
## When Escape Leaves Reality Behind
All kinds of psychoactive drugs can mess with your sense of what’s real, and that’s often the point of them, so you can break free of all your old patterns completely for a bit. But in general, try to select the healthiest ones and use slow and gentle protocols. I’d say one of the most abused psychedelics (aside from ketamine, if you call that a psychedelic) is 5-MeO-DMT. I have a guide on that. People very often believe you need a huge dose of it for a “full release.” They go through all kinds of screaming and shaking, nearly killing themselves, with absolutely zero inner-child work being done. It’s all spiritual bypassing.
Your own drug history matters too. If you go manic, psychotic (by which I mean losing your sense of reality to the point of posing a risk to yourself or others), get super paranoid, or end up worse afterward than before, those are signs to reconsider trying the same thing again, and try to figure out why that happened. Don't try again until you understand what would make it safe. I’d look at the drug and the dosage, as well as mindset and setting etc.
Paranoia during a rough trip can be a warning sign for psychosis, but it doesn’t automatically lead there, since it can be valid. It tends to go that way when it isn’t balanced by practicality. “The CIA is mind-controlling me!” is paranoia. “The CIA, marketers, and other forces are conspiring to mind-control me, the way they do to everyone else! But since I can see that, I can prevent it to some extent, just by being aware of how it operates” is paranoia too, but it’s practically useful, and less likely to lead to psychosis. “...which means I can prevent it by standing on my head and counting backwards from 33, since 33 is their magic Freemason number!” is leaning psychotic. Some drugs are known for causing paranoia, like THC, so I’d avoid those. If you can’t clearly understand what happened yourself, get someone qualified to help. And if you have an idea of what happened but aren’t sure, ask someone who knows you well.
Any of those reactions, from mania to paranoia, can also point to a vulnerability in you, so I'd be careful with other drugs that mess with what's real too. And any predisposition toward mania or psychosis is a warning sign too, whether it's family history, a bad environment or a bad mindset. These are just caution signs, and when you see them, you take more precautions of every kind. You have to figure out what they mean for you, and if you can't, get help from trusted advisors.
By trusted, I mean someone you know is trustworthy from a long relationship with them, not someone people on Reddit said was super magical and healing. That's called a crapshoot. It might turn out well, it might not. It might seem like it did, and then later you realize you were duped, as happened to me with Sidney the iboga shaman. A new therapist is not as trusted as an old one. As they say, "new friends are silver, but old friends are gold."
Say you keep coming back from trips confused or spaced out. Maybe nothing worse happens, but after several trips like that I'd be concerned. If you're having episodes when you're sober, or any of them are severe, see a psychiatrist. And don't suddenly stop a medication just because you suspect it's involved. Talk to the doctor who prescribed it. Benzos can be dangerous to quit cold turkey. Alcohol withdrawal can be dangerous if you're physically dependent.
Be careful what you mix, too, and not just prescribed medicines. Even some basic things like cinnamon can be dangerous (and helpful) with ayahuasca, and doctors don't know about a lot of this. I also learned that mixing acacia confusa with mimosa hostilis is very destabilizing and challenging, but excellent if you want to know how it feels to be painfully autistic + schizo! I have a list of things to be cautious about with ayahuasca in particular.

## Altered States Can Require Reparenting
Have you ever been attacked by a literal demon? Maybe during sleep paralysis or meditation or on a psychedelic trip? A lot of modern folk laugh at this, yet it’s really quite a common experience. I was being psychically attacked by ghosts in my home as a child. Because I was literally a child, I was an easy target. And since most people never actually grew up, they are easy targets for every kind of influence, including spirit influence.
Those ghosts knew my weakness: dares. So they would dare me to jump out the window. I wasn’t suicidal, but I hated to seem like a “wussy.” Luckily, I trusted my parents with this information, and instead of taking me to a psychiatrist and pumping me full of anti-psychotic drugs, they put bars on the windows. Many kids are not so lucky in modern societies. They may get pumped full of anti-psychotic drugs for the rest of their life once diagnosed. That said, it'd make sense to get a professional to weigh in for some situations. I still played around with half-hanging out the windows, but I wasn’t quite dumb enough to jump out, and I really appreciate that my parents took it seriously without catastrophizing.
As a semi-adult, age 26, I discovered meditation. After I began meditating deeply for a while, it seemed like my astral portal opened. I started getting attacked by spirits during sleep paralysis. They would sit on me, prevent me from breathing, and instill tremendous fear into the core of my being.
Yep. Exactly like that.
Sleep paralysis is a trance-like state, and trance can be "more scary" because you're trapped in it, but it's also less actually dangerous. You can't ruin your life at all in trance (unless a bad hypnotic suggestion gets deeply implanted, perhaps), while without trance you can "freak out" in a way that might end up getting you committed to a mental hospital, thrown out of your housing, going to jail, possibly even killing yourself. In trance you can basically work with the fear. You recognize the fear has no real world significance and it's purely just fear, so it's super useful that way. The thing to fear is fear itself, since it actually opens us up to being manipulated by demons, which feed on it directly. So it's not really that they have no power. We give them the power.
After multiple episodes of this I decided to stop giving in to the victim mentality, and to start taking it seriously as a part of spiritual practice. My inner Guide saw it as a perfect opportunity to demonstrate the power of love over fear. The next time it happened, despite how scary it was, I sent unconditional love to this demon on me. It took some minutes, but once that love was seen as enduring, the demon left, and it never returned.
I’ve also trained other people to do this during spiritual attack while on psychedelics. It works a lot better if you are already trained in loving-kindness meditation to the point where it’s natural. I’d say it works way better than exorcism techniques (like Julian Palmer’s torping, which he also admits doesn’t work as well as love).
The astral plane is a bit different from the 3D. In the astral realm, unconditional love is a true, ultimately powerful physical force. Like a river, or a moat. There’s no evil that can trespass that moat. But don’t assume it will always work like that in the physical plane. It definitely has worked for me sometimes when facing some thugs, but definitely not with others. Loving someone does not mean you leave the front door unlocked with a sign saying “take whatever you want.”
If you’re not able yet (or in the moment) to summon real love for an enemy, you can first send love to your frightened little one from your adult Nurturer. Many people also find they can borrow support from God, Jesus, angels, devas, or whatever loving spiritual presence fits their tradition. If I were to do that, I would still be framing it as “Please help this situation with love” rather than “Strike down the enemy!” But if a presence seems to enjoy your fear, it's not one of your grown-ups, borrowed or not, whatever it says it is. Same goes if it demands you hurt or use anybody, yourself included.
Borrowing support like that is basically borrowed adulthood. At first you may need to feel protected or loved by something bigger than you, before that love starts to feel like yours, and then bit by bit it starts to become your own. Send some of it down to your frightened little one, and when that's possible, toward whatever you're afraid of. Continuing to relate to benevolent spiritual powers/beings is still perfectly fine, but they would surely be happy if they rubbed off on you!
If a voice or urge is directing you towards harm, and you feel at risk of acting on it, as with myself and those ghosts at home, move away from that open window and get some outside support.
## Non-altered states can feel like this too
Even social media algorithms will turn your life into hell if you don’t have the loving, protective inner parent developed. When you do, social media actually becomes useful and feels nice to interact with now and then.
Many people think reparenting sounds silly, or like the kind of ridiculous therapy people do when they have too much time or money. But you know what’s even sillier? Wandering the world aimlessly and getting manipulated by all kinds of evil forces. 💀
