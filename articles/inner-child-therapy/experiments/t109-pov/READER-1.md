## 1
SECTION: The Chicken-and-Egg Problem
QUOTE: But on the other hand, some people over-rely on their discipline or their mission to avoid caring for themselves.
LABEL: POV
MISSING: The Guide paragraph argues for having a mission and this is its counterweight (too much Guide), but "some people" is a both-sides move with nobody's case behind it, so the reader can't tell who it describes or whether he means it.
POVS:
SOURCE-SUPPORTED: He says this outright about himself in My Journey: "I used to preach Buddhist enlightenment too much" and "most people (like me) who chase spiritual freedom, as valid as that is, are, at least at first, trying to get away from something painful in themselves".
ARTICLE-HINTED: The over-reliant person is the stage-1 forced adult using competence as cover: "the adult qualities that we might pretend to have" (this section) and "forced frequently into handling situations that require more maturity" (Five Stages of Reparenting).
NEW OWNER CHOICE: Is there someone you've watched (a friend, a type you've met) whose discipline or mission was how they skipped looking after themselves, and what did you see them do?
PICK: SOURCE-SUPPORTED, because it is already his claim: swap the unattributed "some people" for his own enlightenment-preaching years and the counterweight stops being a hedge.
QUESTION: When you leaned too far toward the Guide side, was it discipline (pushing through) or the spiritual teaching itself? The answer decides whether "discipline or their mission" stays or becomes "their teachings".

## 4
SECTION: Don’t Give the Inner Adult Away
QUOTE: I'd keep whatever you feel around them, and look at their track record.
LABEL: POV
MISSING: This is the check the reader is supposed to run on a helper, and "I'd" makes an instruction sound like one person's taste (the questions after it carry the real content); "keep whatever you feel" also reads as if "track of" dropped out.
POVS:
SOURCE-SUPPORTED: If it is his own judgment, the reason is his trust habit: "my childhood was actually so nice that I kind of just assume I can trust everyone, and that gets me into trouble".
ARTICLE-HINTED: Feelings alone mislead around a manipulator, which is why they are paired with a track record: "They know me better than I know myself".
PICK: Neither is needed: make it a plain instruction (drop "I'd" and restore the verb), because the "Do they respect a no" questions already give the reasons; keep an "I'd" only where it is his own judgment.
QUESTION: Is the instruction to notice what you feel around them, or to write it down?
