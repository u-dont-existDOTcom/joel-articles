## The rule you asked about

I wrote it, in the overnight directive (2026-09-29, 00:35 UTC): "Never edit Emulate's output with your own words, not even one word."

The reason was the learning data. If Emulate's saved outputs got edited, we'd no longer know what Emulate did on its own. That's the only place it helps. For building articles it never helped: every invented detail meant a paid rerun or a rejected section.

It now says (`GPT-OVERNIGHT-DIRECTIVE.md`, Hard rules):
- **Saved outputs stay exactly as Emulate wrote them.** They're the record.
- **Article copies take small fixes.** A fix puts back a fact, number, dose, warning, name or link from the source; removes something Emulate invented; finishes a sentence it cut off; or removes page text that isn't the article.
- Every fix is logged, and the paragraph and its section get checked on Pangram again.
