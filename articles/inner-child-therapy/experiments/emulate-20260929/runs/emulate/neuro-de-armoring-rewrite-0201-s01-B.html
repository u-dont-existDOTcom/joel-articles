Part 2: Reducing Anxiety & Depression

Lower the Cortisol Floor (Phosphatidylserine)

Before ketones or D-Serine, treat cortisol. High Cortisol sensitises the brain to Glutamate which in turn armours it (see The Isolation Trap above). One of the best nutrients for doing this is Phosphatidyl Serine (PS). PS has been shown to help reduce excess cortisol from the HPA axis.

Who it helps: Anyone with high or overly reactive cortisol levels. People who feel wired but tired, those that can’t switch off, people who wake at 3am etc. It may help those with slow forms of COMT (Val158Met variant) as COMT removes catecholamines from synapses and when slow this can lead to overly high levels of these stress-related neurotransmitters. I have a friend (Scott) who has this combination of genes and he found PS to be the most helpful part of this protocol. NB: PS is shown to be helpful for lowering cortisol in those that are overly stressed. Its potential help for slow COMT is based on the mechanism of action of COMT and my experience to date (which is not a controlled finding).

Dose: 300-600mg/day in the earlier part of the day seems to be effective. Less than 300mg may not have much of an effect on cortisol and greater than 800mg may not add any benefit.

Food sources: PS is one of the few supplements that can be consumed in food. The richest food sources are found in fish such as Atlantic Mackerel (~480mg/100g), Herring (~360mg/100g) and to a lesser extent Tuna (~194mg/100g – but don’t eat this everyday due to mercury content). So if you eat a 200g piece of Mackerel you will get ~960mg of PS which is greater than that shown to be helpful in the clinical literature.
