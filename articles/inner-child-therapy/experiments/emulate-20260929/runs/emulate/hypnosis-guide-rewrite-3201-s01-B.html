Appendix K: Sources and further reading

Please be aware that some of these words are my own, and the structure of this guide is a combination of some body-oriented therapy and of the hypnotic methods described below. I am in no way implying that this particular way of doing it has been tried and proven. This text is not required for practice, merely for reading.

Learning, entering and returning:

"Healing the Divided Self: a guide to the experiential treatment of dissociative identity disorder" by Maggie Phillips, PhD and Claire Frederick, PsyD, 1995. Relevant parts are pages 24-27, 45-50 and 83-90. The book discusses in particular interactive hypnosis, learning with the senses, self-hypnosis and returning with a definite manner. It is a book for professionals in a clinical setting, not a self-help book. Be wary of accepting blindly what they say about history and of using their exact methods of interacting with an alter as if you were two different people. Also relevant is "Self-Hypnosis" by Adam Eason and Benjamin Parris, published in the International Society of Hypnosis journal. It discusses learning and the relationship between self-hypnosis and hypnosis with another person, including use of recordings.

Language and getting back:

"Healing the Divided Self", pages 53-57 and 96-100. It discusses pacing, utilization, the use of metaphors and an associational method of cueing for returning. Also useful is Nimja's tutorial on hypnotic language, especially on triggers . It is useful to see the different language techniques, and to see how to use cues, although of course you shouldn't accept all the NLP stuff as true. Please understand that I have rewritten the language a bit and designed a specific cue to use for getting back. Nimja neither wrote nor endorses this guide.
