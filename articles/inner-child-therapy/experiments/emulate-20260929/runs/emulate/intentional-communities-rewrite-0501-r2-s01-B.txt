The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn’t There

As psychedelics move from being illegal to regulated/legal, a number of places are moving to legalize regulated use faster than the literature in the community has been able to catch up. For example, in Oregon the first licensed psilocybin service centers are expected to open in 2023; in Colorado a regulated system for natural medicines will begin licensing in 2025; New Mexico passed the Medical Psilocybin Act of 2025; and in Australia since 2023 psilocybin and MDMA have been able to be prescribed by psychiatrists in a very limited context for specific conditions.

On the other hand, the psychedelic renaissance hasn’t been a straight shot upward. For example, in 2024 the US FDA issued a Complete Response Letter (CRL) for MDMA-assisted therapy, meaning that they weren’t satisfied with the evidence submitted and are asking for more. Another example is that in Portugal many people say that they have legalized everything, when in fact they’ve only decriminalized the possession of small quantities of drugs for personal use - trafficking drugs is still a crime.

There are lots of differences between countries, states, substances, religious statuses, activities, etc. If you’re interested in using psychedelics as medicine, I encourage you to seek out legal advice in your location.

Most communities that are using psychedelics in some way are keeping it quiet. Even where it is legal, there are often social pressures not to advertise this part of your community (from parents of kids you want nearby, other neighbors, insurance companies, local government officials who don’t understand the law, etc.).
