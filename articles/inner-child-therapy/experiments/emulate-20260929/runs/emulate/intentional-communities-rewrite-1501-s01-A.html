What This Looks Like From Sénégal

For the record I’m currently in Sénégal.

Sénégal is unlike anything I’ve ever experienced in my life. There are devotional prayers that go out into the streets five times a day and it’s lovely and soothing. There is also this pervasive Sufi spirituality that infuses everything from the music to pictures and murals to peoples names to casual conversation.

Also, people are outside laughing together for hours at a time. It’s probably the most joking culture I’ve ever come into contact with. It’s also the first time in my life I really feel like the difference between basic conversational skill and “graduate school” level of a language might keep me from understanding some really complicated jokes in Wolof.

To answer your question, in the villages and with my family, there is a very robust system for sharing land, kids, houses, food, and obligation within extended families and villages.

Even here in Dakar someone sprayed on a wall: “Tolerance! Openness! Union! Happiness! Love!” and in other places there are big murals of Sufi spiritual leaders. I never expected to see spiritual graffiti. I’ve always assumed that there was only product promotion or the inability to draw a penis successfully.
