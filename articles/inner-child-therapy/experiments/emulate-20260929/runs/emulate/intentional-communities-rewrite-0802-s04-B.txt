I've looked through a lot of material in the corpus and, while there have no doubt been many cases of seriously dangerous kids in these communities over the years, I haven't seen a single case well-documented in the corpus in which the community tried to deal with it internally -- detailing what they did, what they considered, what advice they took, how it went, what they reviewed, what they learned, and what they would do next time. If there is one, it's not here. So it seems essential that the child, the family, and the people the kid is dangerous to, all have access to sources of help and review that are not controlled by the home community.

The Door Must be Open Outward When They Reach Adulthood

In the Amish case, youths make a free choice as adults about whether or not to join the church. It is true that over 85% of them do join, and it's also true that the Rumspringa is widely caricatured, but the act of joining the church as an adult is very important.

Let the children go. Let them see the world. Let them go to school, travel, make friends, even hate the founding members of your community.
