All is not well in paradise

I don’t want to represent Sénégal as some kind of utopia for everyone there.

Back in March 2026, they passed a law (Loi No. 2026-08) increasing the penalty for consensual same-sex intimacy to 5 to 10 years in prison. The amended article also criminalises the “apology” (public promotion) of homosexuality in public, and some forms of financial and other support to promote homosexuality. There’s a lot of arrest, family rejection, harassment, assault and mob violence against homosexuals in Sénégal, and they say it’s actually safer being in prison than on the outside. It’s not something I want to ignore or gloss over in an attempt to romanticise communal society. It’s important to remember that people could be living their lives in this “communal paradise” while hiding who they really are, or after they’ve escaped to Sénégal from elsewhere.

It’s also important to recognise that a society can be spiritual, communal, hilarious, etc. while also being incredibly cruel to those who step outside their social boundaries. Community doesn’t always equate to liberation, it depends who you are.

There are other aspects of Sénégal’s incredibly forgiving social culture that can be problematic. For example, people don’t like confrontation. Everybody probably knows who stole that sheep, but nobody wants to cause trouble.
