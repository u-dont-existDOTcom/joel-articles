It might be useful to evaluate people's behavior in each phase without trying to use special vocabulary or pretending that it requires some special spiritual or therapeutic skill. As people have noted, six months doesn't make it any better at detecting "good people" than shorter timeframes, it just gives you more information about how they've actually been behaving. If they're going to be put in a high-risk position (with kids or other shared resources, or access to meds, cars, or private information), then evaluating how they've behaved in that specific context, even if it's only been for a few days or weeks, is more useful, especially if complemented with references (where appropriate) and phased access in a way that can be challenged by someone not involved in the original decision.

It might be worth requiring people to do at least one group decision, one work/shared resources related task, and one post-conflict processing session with the group before provisional membership, as a way of simulating at least some of the effects of moving in before someone sells their house to do so.

Even people who aren't considered "politically" a part of the commune (visitors, volunteers, work or renters, interns, etc.) should have rights in any decision that affects them (compensation, access to shared resources, schedule, safety, housing, access to private records, or discipline). Failing to do this is one way that communes end up with de facto "servant" classes that they pass from commune to commune.
