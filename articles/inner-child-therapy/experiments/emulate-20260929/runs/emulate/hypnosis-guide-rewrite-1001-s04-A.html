That is not a good way to end the complaint. We simply listen to the complaint, hear the truth in it, and respond to that truth. Our responding, our ability to hear the anger without attacking back, our ability to admit we didn't do what we should, our ability to make right internally an attack against the child, all of these are evidence, as are bringing home dinner, setting limits appropriately, going to appointments on time, and following through on what we said we would do. Non-retaliation is evidence of love, but it should not replace love with something colder.

What we are looking at here is the teaching of both sides. The side that vowed, and the side that blames for not keeping the vow. It should be noted that although I write as if they were two different positions, it may be one position that both blames and vows. It also may be a blend of both. It is not necessarily related to chronological age.

On the other hand, if we are dealing with a younger version of ourselves, it may be appropriate to begin by looking at what that version understood at the time, what that version had to work with, what the results of the choice were, etc. This is not to get the younger version "off the hook," as he or she may be responsible for making right the harm caused, but it is not helpful to punish for something that we couldn't do at the time.

Working with Two Hands

Place one hand on the center of your chest, physically connecting with your love, and the other at your solar plexus just under your chest, connecting with the child.

Begin sending love down from your heart to the child. Pay attention to whatever comes, and continue the dialogue, sending more love as needed.

Love is on top; child is on the bottom; you are in the middle.
