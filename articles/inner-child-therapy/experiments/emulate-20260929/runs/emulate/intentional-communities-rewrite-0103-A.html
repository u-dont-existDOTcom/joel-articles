The East Wind commune felt almost like the opposite. They had about 80 people at the time I was there, which is big enough to have a lot of variety, but still small enough that you can feel like you might actually be able to be friends with everyone there. I was very happy there. I also loved their communal showers, which were mixed sex. I wonder if any communes still have those. It's the sort of thing that doesn't make it into the history books.

Even at East Wind, though, there was a power issue. They had a nut butter factory, which was providing a lot of their income, and the woman who was running the factory was very hard to question. Otherwise I thought it had a great social life, a nice way of distributing labor, and no obvious guru.

Acorn, on the other hand, was tiny when I got there, maybe 8 people, and had no kids. I couldn't help but feel that, if I stayed there, I wouldn't be living in a commune, but just moving in with a bunch of people who were already friends. Which seems sort of fun, actually, if they happen to already have some beautiful land and are good at building cool shit on it. Though, yeah, it would be really hard to join a group of eight people who are already friends.

Twin Oaks was huge, maybe 200-300 people, and felt almost institutional. It was nice enough, and interesting to talk to all the people there, but I didn't feel like I was part of a tribe. More like a tiny little hippie town, with a crazy complicated labor credit system.

One thing I noticed, though, was that almost none of them had any sort of therapy practice within the commune. So all of the human pain that was coming into the commune got translated directly into politics.

This took the form of weekly meetings using Robert's Rules of Order to wade through enormous agendas of things that needed to be talked about, while everyone present thought "There's nothing I really want to talk about right now, and I'd rather be sleeping." Some people even solved that problem by going to sleep during the meetings and just openly snoring.
