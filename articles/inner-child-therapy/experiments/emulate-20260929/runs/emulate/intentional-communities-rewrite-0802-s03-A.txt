Educate your children to tell the truth. Take what they tell you seriously, but don’t turn the process into an interrogation or punish the child for being unsure. This is a critical time for intervention, and even poorly told stories need to be taken seriously.

Be willing to listen even if the accused is a charismatic and beloved member of the community. Often a community won’t take action because the alleged perpetrator is too valuable or liked. Have a process you’re willing to follow that will make sure the children are immediately safe and then handled in a way that is both competent and fair.

Make sure your children are involved with people outside your community. Whether they’re grandparents, cousins, local friends or other adults who can hear their stories, make sure there is at least one route for your children to report things to a trusted adult, advocate or reporting service that doesn’t automatically go to the community’s leadership. In 1984 in Vermont, the authorities swooped in and took 112 kids (the Island Pond raid). Though the court refused the state’s request, the kids were returned to their families, demonstrating that turning everything over to the state doesn’t work either. Total enclosure of the community doesn’t work.
