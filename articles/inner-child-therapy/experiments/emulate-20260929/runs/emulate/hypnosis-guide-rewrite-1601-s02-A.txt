Sometimes it can be helpful to be firmer, if that's what you choose. "No, I'd rather not talk about why right now, I want to see what happens if I just stay here and be direct like this. What does it feel like?" If you're happy to change the direction things are going, and you want to keep going, there's no need to make things nice to do that. Something can be sad, or difficult, but that doesn't mean you need to move away from it.

It can be helpful to distinguish between noticing something, deciding what it means, and then thinking about what that means. These three things can all be blurry, and that's fine, you don't have to prove to anyone what was what and when. But sometimes it can be useful to wonder if there was something you noticed, what it seemed to mean to you, and then if that still seems like what it means, or if something makes you think it might mean something else.

"I noticed my tummy got knotty" is a different type of statement to "I decided he was an evil person." The second one is a conclusion, even if it was nonverbal. It's still ok to explore what led to the conclusion, but it's something you can think about and try to figure out now, even using words that you didn't use at the time. Sometimes something felt nonverbally, but that doesn't mean it was deeper or better or truer than stuff we can think about later. The same goes for something that was earlier - it might have been first, but that doesn't mean it was more right than something you can figure out later. Feelings are fine to feel, but it's also ok to wonder about what they mean, and think about them.
