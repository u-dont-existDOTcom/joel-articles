Let the response change the invitation

Many people use the sentence: “When she says something like that, my shoulders tighten up.” Sometimes that’s all they say. I ask them to investigate what’s going on a little further. Sometimes, the shoulder tightening comes from a realization that the way they phrased something is not optimal. Then it simply requires them to say something else. Sometimes, however, it’s simply a “No!” to whatever is being suggested. In that case, no further investigation is necessary. The person just needs to learn to say “No!” more directly and not repeat the suggestion that was made to them.

How can you tell which is going on? You can begin by simply being aware that your shoulders are tightening and letting it be. Then you can ask yourself if what’s being suggested to you is something that fits for you or not. If it’s not, then say something that does fit.

Many times, the sentence is simply not the right one to say. On other occasions, however, it may be a perfect suggestion for you to try something that you’ve never done before. Your skepticism doesn’t necessarily do you any good, so don’t fool yourself into thinking that there’s some benefit to not speaking when you have nothing to say.

Look at the situation, too

When another person is involved in the situation, examine their behavior, too. Often, people rationalize bad behavior on the part of another by saying that their “inner child” is reacting. That may be true, but it doesn’t eliminate the possibility that the other person is being emotionally immature. You don’t have to put yourself in a dangerous situation several times to “prove” that it’s dangerous. Just recognize that it is or isn’t, make a judgment that is specific to that situation, and move forward from there.
