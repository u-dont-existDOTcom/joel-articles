This old way is changing, though. Youth want to be part of the greater world – to live in the cities, have jobs, mobile phones, import foods and, for the men especially, to risk their lives on perilous passages to Europe.

Lessons from the Black Tax

One thing I’ve noticed is that a small part of the old ways has survived in the concept of the Black Tax. In short, when a person becomes financially successful, it’s their duty to provide for the family – not only immediate family, but also siblings, cousins, parents, school fees, medical expenses, funerals, and so on. I can understand why this would be, when you consider that this comes from our ancestors, who worked in a communal system. Everyone had to work for the good of all, and if you’ve been fed, raised, provided a home for and protected by your family, then it only makes sense that when you have the opportunity to earn money you should provide for them too. In that environment, it makes perfect sense – everyone is looking out for everyone else, and the entire organism thrives.

The problem is that the Black Tax, as it operates in today’s world, is soul destroying. The entire communal system is gone – people no longer work together, don’t share land or labour or food or child care – but the Black Tax remains. This means that the person who is paying it can never gain financial independence or stability, while the people receiving the payments are stuck in a perpetual state of dependence. It’s a perfect example of an ancestral way that makes perfect sense being transported into an individualistic cash-based world, and still being allowed to operate – and while it does keep people alive, it also consumes them.
