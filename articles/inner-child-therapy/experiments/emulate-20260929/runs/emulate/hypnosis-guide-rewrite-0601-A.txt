Hypnosis Evidence

There is no magical evidence, but there is real evidence. Research is strong in some areas and not as strong in others. Overall, the quality of the studies differ depending on the outcomes measured and the studies designs. There was an overview in 2024 of 49 meta-analyses that included a total of 261 distinct primary studies. Strongest evidence was shown in areas of pain, medical, and with children/adolescents.

Studies looked at self-hypnosis as well. Systematic review of Randomized Controlled Trials of self-hypnosis training demonstrated self-hypnosis to be beneficial in studies related to pain, child birth, pediatric, stress, and anxiety. Self-hypnosis seems more effective when used as a real self-hypnosis and done multiple times, versus just pushing play on a file.

My goal is to help you develop the real self-hypnosis skill.

According to Mayo Clinic, hypnosis may not be the best option for everyone. “If you’re using hypnosis to deal with something that was stressful when it happened in your earlier life, be aware that hypnosis might trigger a strong emotional reaction. Other treatments might be a better choice for dealing with historical trauma.”

Use self-hypnosis to help regulate, dialogue, re-parent, gain clarity, change habits, feel safe in the present, integrate emotions, and do life.

Do NOT use self-hypnosis to force trauma memories, gain proof of trauma memories, suppress medical symptoms, destabilize, or replace the appropriate care you need.
