Educate your children to speak the truth. This doesn’t mean interrogate them. It does mean to make them aware of the power of their words and how seriously we will take them.
Agree to listen to them, even when the accused is someone you love. Very often in these situations the accused is a charismatic, valuable, well-liked, wealthy, spiritually influential person in the community. Work out how you will respond and what the process will be in advance of knowing who is involved. Agree to look first to ensure safety, then to ensure the investigation is done competently and fairly.
Encourage your children to have relationships outside your community and therefore outside your group. Have a reporting route that is outside of your community. This may be grandparents, cousins, local friends, teachers etc. There needs to be at least one trusted adult, advocate or reporting route that is not subject to your community leadership. Why? Because, if all else fails, you may need state intervention and the state can fail spectacularly – see the Island Pond Child Raid in Vermont, USA where authorities removed 112 children from their homes in 1984. It took a court to realise that they could not simply hand over the children to the state as the state requested, and so the children were returned. But equally, total enclosure in your community can fail. So your children need more.
