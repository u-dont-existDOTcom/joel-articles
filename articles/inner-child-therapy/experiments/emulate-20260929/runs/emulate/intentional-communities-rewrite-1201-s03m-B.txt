The hard truth is that small communities are most in need of community members and the least able to deal with any disruption in the group. While the eight people in the group may desperately need YOU, you may also desperately need them to grow food, fix stuff, be good with money, care for children, take care of the water system, etc. The well won’t stop running dry because you have a beautiful heart.

Early in a group’s life, it is most in need of people who have more resource than need, who bring skills with them and who are willing to learn new ones.

I am not sure how to avoid using this as a way of screening out people who are challenged, poor, not well-spoken, or not conversant in therapeutic language. Pretending that we can take anyone in any condition doesn’t solve the problem. We will have to come up with some concrete ways of deciding when someone is not ready, and have some outside review and a way of coming back later, because this is an area where power can be abused.

This community probably isn’t a good idea for you at this time if:

You are running from a difficult situation and need a place to be safe, get treatment, find housing or legal assistance. It is unrealistic to expect the founding group of a new community to meet these needs for you.

You want the benefits of “being in a village” without participating in governance, maintenance, conflict, and caring.

You are unwilling to look at your own behavior in times of jealousy and conflict.

You have more need than resource at this time. Look at ways of building up your resource in your current life.

Develop a membership process that allows us to say “later” or “in a different capacity” or “with more support” without rejecting people completely.
