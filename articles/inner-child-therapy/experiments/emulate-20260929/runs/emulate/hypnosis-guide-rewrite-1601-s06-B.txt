You might also want to try providing the missing support (hand on chest, imagine someone sitting next to them, say something you think they would find helpful, move further away, etc). How does that change things? In fact, any resource that sounds like it might be comforting might be worth trying.

Remember that it’s okay for the process of giving love to bring up grief about the lack of it. If the client wants to stop and is able to be present in that stopping, then there’s no need to push through to more tears. If the voice, proximity, or physical contact feels intrusive in this moment, then change it. It’s not an indication that the client is incapable of receiving love.

As always, you can work with the metaphor that emerges. Before you worry about what it means, just work with it. If there was an image or story to this problem, what would it be? Then work with that by providing whatever resource, boundary, distance, or other action seems useful. Just because you haven’t tried something doesn’t mean it would work, but it can provide you with useful symbolic information; it gives you a hypothesis to work with.

Of course, you can always spend the session developing something useful like love, play, courage, or curiosity, or rehearse taking an action that the client wants to take. There’s a lot that hypnosis can do!
