This isn’t meant as a blanket endorsement for psychedelics, there are people who should not take them. In fact there are many people for whom psychedelics should not be an option, and there should be communities where they aren’t. Even so, I think that if we are enthusiastic about them, and there will be casualties. If as a community we decide this is a path we want to walk, we must be very humble about our capacity for saying no, for saying stop, for saying go see someone else, and for admitting when things go wrong.

Some principles of Ceremony:

No Shamans (this will upset most traditional lineages) Many ayahuasca and Bwiti communities out there feel that the medicine should only be taken under the authority of someone trained in their lineage. This is understandable and there is much to be learned from these lineages.

The problem I have is that it elevates one person above everyone else. If that person is a shaman traveling the retreat circuit, they are an unvetted, unaccountable authority figure who may or may not be wise, but who is certainly sexually vulnerable to people in deep open states, and who certainly profits from people needing to return for more.

I am not sure how to prevent this in a culture where people take medicine together without shamans. There will be people who become de facto shamans simply by being asked for advice and wisdom. Rotating roles and public accountability may help prevent this, but I doubt it will solve it.

Part of the solution is for people to learn to do this on their own and for each other, while still being humble about the limits of our capacity. Some more on doing ceremonies peer held and on the best psychopath shaman I ever met.
