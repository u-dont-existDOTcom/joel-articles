All is not well in paradise

I don’t want to give the impression that Sénégal is some kind of surviving island of communal-society bliss.

In March 2026, Sénégal enacted a new law (Loi No. 2026-08) increasing the prison term for homosexuality to 5-10 years, and including in the article language criminalizing the “apology” of homosexuality in public and some forms of financing and support for promoting homosexuality. Homosexuals in Sénégal face arrest, rejection from their families, and often harassment, assault, and mob violence. As one person told me, “for homosexuals in Sénégal, prison is often a place of protection.” I do not wish to praise the communal aspects of Sénégal’s society without acknowledging that, for some, survival depends on hiding important aspects of themselves, or leaving the country.

The potential for cruelty in a spiritually-communal society to those who violate the bounds of acceptable behavior is very real. Whether this is a downside of communal living in particular, or simply a factor of what kind of people are in that particular community, is an open question.

Other downsides to the social culture that would be very forgiving to me include the lack of confrontation around certain things. “We all know who stole the sheep,” one person told me, “but no one wants to make a problem.”
