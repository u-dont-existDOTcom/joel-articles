To some degree, yes. Not many normal families in a money world give their kids money when they turn 18 and say, "good luck!" Also, if people are choosing not to use money because they're poor, then they certainly have better things to worry about, like feeding their kids, getting medical care, etc.

The most we can expect is to not artificially prevent kids from leaving. If they've grown up without money, to make sure they know as much as possible about the money world. To try to make sure that their upbringing gives them as many options as possible when they leave--that their education and skills are portable, that they have friends and family outside the community, that their records and documents are accessible, that there's at least one obvious next step that they could take after leaving (whether that's joining another community, starting a family, getting a job, etc). If the community has the resources, it may also be able to help them with temporary housing, transportation, tools, a transition fund, etc. But if they don't, that's okay too, as long as they're not making it harder for the kids to leave, and as long as they're giving them the knowledge and records they need. No kids have completely free choice of what they do after they turn 18--there's always going to be some path dependence, but we should try to minimize it.

Of course, some kids might still leave. Some might even want to start their own communities with different values, even though this is an evolutionary movement. This might hurt their parents, but...
