Books & papers (in reading order)

On child-rearing, if you’re into that:

Jean Liedloff’s The Continuum Concept (1975). This is the most important book on natural childrearing, based on her experience among the Ye’kuana. Some of the anthropology may be off, but it’s important to read anyway.

Michaeleen Doucleff’s Hunt, Gather, Parent (2021). This is a great practical and accessible book on how to get kids involved in the life of the family, give them a lot of autonomy, and distribute the work of taking care of them.

On community-building in a practical how-to sense:

Diana Leafe Christian’s Creating a Life Together. If you’re looking for a starting place, this is it. It’s got a lot of good information on sequencing, membership, money, land, agreements, and other topics.

Yana Ludwig’s Building Belonging. This is a great whole-systems guide to how to do intentional community.

Rau and Koch-Gonzalez’s Many Voices One Song. This is a good manual on how to do sociocracy in practice.

Schaub et al.’s Building United Judgment. This is on how to do consensus decision-making.

Ludwig and Gimnig’s The Cooperative Culture Handbook. This is a set of exercises on how to do things cooperatively, handle conflict, and build a good group culture.
