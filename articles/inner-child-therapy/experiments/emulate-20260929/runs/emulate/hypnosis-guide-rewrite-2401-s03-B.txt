Pattern 3: Presuppositions

Presuppositions are very powerful, so you have to use them ethically. Only use them to presume something that is ethical to presume, such as presuming that the client is choosing the change, presuming that the change will happen at a good time, presuming that the client is capable of the change, presuming that it’s safe to make the change, presuming that the client wants to make the change, presuming that parts love each other, presuming that the client has an adult self that can take over, etc. Don’t use presuppositions to presume that the client should obey you.

“You can understand it more before you need to change it.”

Pattern #4 – Use Cause-Effect Bridges Carefully

Cause-effect bridges are words such as “as,” “while,” “because,” “when,” “after,” “before,” “which means,” etc. You have to be very careful when using them, even when you say them in a gentle way.

It is better to say, “As you take a breath, you might begin to allow your adult self to come in and listen to what the wounded part needs.”

Pattern #5 – Use Embedded Invitations

Here are some embedded invitations to use:

As you take a breath, you might begin to allow your adult self to come in and listen.

As you sit here for a moment, you might allow yourself to realize something.

You might begin to allow the younger part to realize that you’re still there for it.

The key is to not over do it. If you use ten embedded invitations in a row, you will sound like a loser who just got certified at some weekend.
