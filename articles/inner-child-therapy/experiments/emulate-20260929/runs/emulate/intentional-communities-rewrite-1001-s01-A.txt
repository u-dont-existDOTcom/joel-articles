From One Community to a Movement

Experiment Before You Divorce

Of course, not all experiments require everybody to buy new land. A new idea or rule could be tested out first within the parent group, or semi-autonomously, or as a seed a few miles away sharing some of the same infrastructure (tool library, child care, business, friendships, etc). Even full-on fissioning is one point on a gradient of possibilities for trying things out.

That said, it's true that you can't test all experiments cheaply. Trying out a different land holding system, population, or set of mutually-supportive rules, for example, might not work well if they aren't "fed" the assets they need to succeed.

Federation: Without Making a State

If we want our communities to spread, we need to think about how we want them to be connected. I want to avoid one big super-commune, but I also want a way for the communities to support each other, to allow people to move between them, to share knowledge, etc. And I do think it's good to have some variety between the communities, to be able to try out different things, but only within limits.
