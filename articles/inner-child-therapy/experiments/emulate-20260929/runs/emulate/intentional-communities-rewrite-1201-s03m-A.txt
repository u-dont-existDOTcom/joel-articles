It's a tough issue, because generally the people who most need to be included in a small community are the ones who will create the most disruption for the members. Even a community of eight is still going to need each member to plow their share of the garden, fix things when they break, do the bookkeeping, care for the kids and keep the water system running. The well won't necessarily be very sympathetic if someone's beautiful heart cracks before they've pumped enough water.

In general, I think the earlier in a community's life, the more potential members need to have resource available to them rather than acute need, as well as skill or a willingness to learn.

How we avoid using this as an exclusionary tool against the disabled, the poor, those who aren't good at talking in "therapy speak," etc, is something I'm still working on. I know that pretending that we can take in any amount of dysfunction isn't the answer. It will require very specific criteria, external review, and a willingness to revisit "it's not a good idea right now" decisions. It's dangerous business.

It may not be a good idea for you to join our community right now if:

You are running from an active crisis and need to find a safe place to get treatment, housing, legal assistance, etc. It's irresponsible for a founding community to pretend that we can take that on.

You want the benefits of village life without taking on village responsibilities (governance, maintenance, dealing with conflict, caring for each other, etc).

You're not willing to look at your own behavior in the face of jealousy or conflict.

Your need is much greater than your available resource. Your worth is still enormous, but it may be a better idea to start building up your resource in your current situation.

Of course, there are ways of saying "later" or "in a different capacity" or "with more support" as well.
