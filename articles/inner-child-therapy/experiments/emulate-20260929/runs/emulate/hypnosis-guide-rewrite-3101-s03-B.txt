Use a gradual wave approach. If it works better for you, you may wish to work with a small, neutral area, ‘wave’ to the next area and so on, being careful not to claim medical effects. Or you can simply ‘wave’ to one area if that’s easier. Pause and notice if there’s a difference. What does the body need now? Contact? Space? Words? Care? ‘Wave’ back, feeling your body as a whole and all the points of contact you have with the world. Notice if there is a difference. ‘Wave’ back again.
Use a ‘preparing for workshop’ approach. What do you need to set up a safe enough space to work on caring for the patient? What tools? What boundaries? Allow the signal / feeling / younger state to come as close as it feels ok for it to come. Think about how to talk to it so that you don’t feel dissociated. Ask it questions. Wait. Make contact if it feels ok to do so. Rest and re-assess. Call in an ‘adult’ part if you have one or ‘borrow’ a function. Choose your words carefully. Remember that you don’t need to fix anything, you just need to be ‘good as you’ for this part. History is history. Close the ‘workshop’ and affirm your sense of being whole and an adult in the world. ‘Come back’.
Use a ‘resource’ approach. What resource might be useful here? Embodiment may be useful, but make sure you understand the resource well. You might like to start by imagining yourself embodying just 1% more of the resource. Or you might prefer to observe it. What might it do / say / look like? It might say or do nothing. Repeat the exercise a few times to consolidate your sense of the resource. What would this resource do in the situation you’re in now? Thank the resource and ‘come back’, keeping any qualities of the resource and one action you can take in the world. Affirm that you are yourself.
