Feel the moment when attention says, “Whoa.”

i. Use it to connect with your feet/body/breath/room

ii. Repeat to yourself something like “The noticing is a wake-up bell, not a message.

iii. Ask your Nurturer, Protector, Leader (or whatever parts you’ve got)

iv. Practice moving back into a grounded, wider perspective

v. Finish by moving into the next thing you want to do to be fully awake

You can make the cue whatever you like, as long as you don’t give it more power than you intend – just orienting.

At a psychological level, what you’re doing here is very similar to setting an ‘implementation intention’ – pre-deciding what you will do if a certain thing happens. There’s lots of evidence that this works, e.g. in this key paper from Peter Gollwitzer . I don’t know of any direct research on setting implementation intentions in response to synchronicities, though.

The flipside of all this, of course, is that if you’re not careful you could rehearse yourself into ‘seeing’ synchronicities that aren’t there (just as, in general, people who rate highly on the ability to see patterns also score higher on false-positive tasks , and on psychosis spectrum traits – see Blain et al. , Grant et al. ).

So if you find that using these cues is making you engage in compulsive synchronicity-checking, keeping you awake, making you anxious, leading you to think that random events are there for you, or in any other way destabilising your ability to work out what’s going on, turn them off again.
