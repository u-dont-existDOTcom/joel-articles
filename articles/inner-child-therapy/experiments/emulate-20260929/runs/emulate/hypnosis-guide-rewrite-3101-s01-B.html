Appendix J: Using this as an induction library

Pick a sequence to try, decide on a purpose for it, and decide whether or not to do it. The entire family of inductions can be run lightly, with eyes open, or substituted for no-trance contact methods. Notice what happens (which may be nothing). Modify the sequence on the fly -- move, add imagery, switch sensory channels, do nothing, or stop -- depending on how the induction is working. At the end of every induction, bring the subject back to full wakefulness.

Note: if imagery doesn't work well, rephrase the visualization into whatever form works better for you -- description, rhythm, spatial sense, touch, movement, or just a sense of knowing, etc.

  1. Have the subject stretch and relax.

Have the subject notice how he is being supported, and the position of one extremity (hand or foot). Have him put a little pressure on the supporting surface, stretch a bit, and relax, or just imagine doing so. Notice if anything happens -- a sense of ease, of tension, of warmth, in his breathing, or nothing at all. This can be done with up to three different areas; if so, make it a sequence, driven by the idea of releasing tension. Let the subject pick the area, or let an internal voice pick it for him. Ask what that area needs in terms of support. Have him return to normal.

  1. Have the subject feel himself being supported.

Have him imagine that he is making one percent less effort, floating a little bit, but still in contact with the supporting surface, which is exerting the same pressure as before. Notice what happens. He can also make one percent effort the same as before, but notice that he is being supported. Have him alternate consciously, to heighten the difference between the two states; for instance, normal when he breathes in, and floating when he breathes out. (It's best to tell him that this is fractionation in advance.) Ask him what it's like to hear his inner voice in each state. Have him return to normal, feeling his chosen amount of weight, firmly in contact with the supporting surface.
