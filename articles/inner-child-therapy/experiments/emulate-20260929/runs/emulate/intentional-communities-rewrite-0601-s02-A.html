Health care is one of the most difficult couplings to deal with. While many people want to “do it all naturally” there are often times where someone needs a trauma surgeon, antibiotics, dental care, emergency obstetrical care or a specialist. It is important to know ahead of time how you will access the level of health care you want and need, what level of care the community will provide from the common and what level of care you need to obtain outside resources for, and how you will deal with people who choose different levels of health care.

It is also important to have an agreement in place for what will happen if someone in your community has a catastrophic illness. This agreement should ensure that the person receives the level and kind of health care that they choose, that they can access common community resources and/or outside resources as needed, that they can access transportation as needed, and that they have as much privacy as they need and desire.

The Surgeon General’s Advisory on Social Connections notes that social connections impact numerous health factors in measurable ways. While being part of a community will not keep you from getting sick, it can help shape the circumstances in which you become ill, recover, age and die.
