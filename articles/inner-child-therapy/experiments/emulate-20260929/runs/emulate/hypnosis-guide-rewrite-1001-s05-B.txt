The Nurturer embodies the part of you that can feel warm towards your child without feeling the need to rush it. They enjoy their company just for what they are and can really embody ‘I’m really glad you are here.’ Your child needs this too, they need to be enjoyed as well as comforted.

It’s okay for you to feel like this. I’m here for you. You don’t have to do anything. You are not too much. It’s okay to be small. It’s okay to be angry. It’s okay to be sad. I will be here.

The Protector

The protector is all about embodying that warmth.

I will set that boundary for us. I will stop talking to that person. I will stop texting them. I will eat. I will sleep. I will do that thing we said we would do. I won’t let you face the consequences of being an adult alone.

This is where so much inner child work can fall short. It can be a bit fluffy. You can say all the right things to your inner child but if the adult (the protector) is still texting the person that is destabilising you, not eating, not doing the thing you said you would do, it’s really hard to feel trusted. It’s when you can do that thing, face the consequences.

If you don’t feel like you can offer warmth yet, start by being protective of your child. Make sure you do that thing. If you haven’t done it, don’t be hard on yourself. Just work out why. Were you afraid? Were you tired? Didn’t know how to do it? Something got in the way? Did you plan it properly? Also, just doing the thing doesn’t mean it was a good idea. Just because you ticked it off your to do list, doesn’t mean it was the thing to do.
