Asking or telling (including non-verbal gestures like blinking or making a noise) the subject to do something after the session (or part of the session) has ended. Usually done by repeating the word or sound to perform a later task.

Convincing the listener that, simply because something feels dreamlike, or they can’t remember, or they are seeing images or feeling something in their body, it must be true.

Ending the recording so that the subject remains in a role, has reduced ability to judge or move, becomes blank or enters any other state that may or may not be indefinite.

In order for the subject to have a choice, there must be at least one option that doesn’t further the induction. For example, allowing them to treat it as a lighter induction, use a different induction, be fully alert, stop the induction at any time, etc. It’s important to remember that ‘no’ means no, and that lack of ‘yes’ is also no – simply because someone is talking in a nice voice doesn’t mean that the subject has agreed to anything.
