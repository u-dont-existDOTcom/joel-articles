First thing to know is that FEC is currently rebuilding. The work of a federation is not small: it requires delegates, record-keeping, communities to stay on top of whatever is agreed upon in the 'constitution', coordinated utilization of resources, meetings, succession planning, and hopefully some communities agreeing to let the federation handle some conflict resolution or other function. Also budget, if the outside system requires cash money. Federation-level functions can be separated out as needed; communities can be friends bilaterally, or be local clusters, or form institutions for specific functions open to certain kinds of communities (such as FEC's current health insurance pool, PEACH, whose governing body is currently separate from the FEC delegates).

Another thing to know is that we don't all have to be the same! Currently FEC has full member communities, communities working toward membership, and Friends who are friends and may stay that way. People need to be able to move between communities if they want to. This doesn't necessarily require harmonizing internal systems between communities; it just requires enough of an interface between communities that when people move, their housing, status in the community, health insurance, records, labor 'credits' (if any), transportation, tax issues, and potential conflicts don't fall through the cracks.
