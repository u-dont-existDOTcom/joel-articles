You could also deliberately try out a specific kind of support, to see if it works. You might want to move your hand, imagine someone else or a different person, say something, change your posture, or move further away. Try something and see if it works, before deciding whether that resource is going to be of help to this person.

It is possible that whilst your client is receiving your love, they are also grieving the fact that they didn’t have it before. This is okay, and you can let them continue as long as they want to and can, but there is no need for them to fall apart in front of you. If this feels intrusive to them in any way, or if they find that they don’t like this kind of support, then change it. Don’t assume that they are unable to receive love.

You could also take the problem metaphor in a different direction, to see if it provides some useful information in a symbolic form. You might ask the question “what if we tried…” and then suggest something that you think the problem might need, to see if that would work. This isn’t proof of what did or didn’t happen, but it could provide a useful hypothesis.

You could also use the session to develop some resource such as love, play, courage or curiosity. You might also rehearse some useful action that your client has chosen to take. Remember that hypnosis is not just about listening, it can also be a powerful tool for change.
