Things To Consider When Writing Your Own Scripts Decide what it is you want to accomplish, what anchor or images you will use to begin with and how you will go back. Do you want to be warm, authoritative, directive or neutral? Do you need to use floating, stairs, gardens, go inside, change colour or talk to children?

Do you need any imagery at all? Perhaps you can use sensation, rhythm, movement, words, spatial or just knowing. Or none at all. Whatever you do, try to keep it consistent and in context. If you are counting, make sure the counting means something in your story and when you come back, tell yourself when you have finished and retrace your steps. Steal attributes but not identity.

Decide on one verb, one quality, one refrain and one transitional phrase and stick to them. Read the script out loud as you go. Long, erudite sentences make great reading but soup when you are being induced.

Things To Do Before You Start Read the script through, including Come all the way back, when you are wide awake and get a feel for what you are doing. Decide what you want to find out or what quality you want to develop. It doesn't have to be a problem, it can be love, play, confidence, creativity or communion. Decide what you want to leave alone and how long you can spend.
