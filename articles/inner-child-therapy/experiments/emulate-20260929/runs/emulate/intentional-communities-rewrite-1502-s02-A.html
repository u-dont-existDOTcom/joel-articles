This is a similar problem to the one faced by money-free communes that still need to deal with money in order to pay taxes, deal with hospitals, get visas, pay school fees, deal with family, get tools, etc. The question is not so much about abolishing money within the commune, but about how to handle the interface with the outside money economy. Do we want a pool of cash that the collective uses to deal with the outside? How do we prevent people from using their access to outside money as a way to gain status inside the commune?

A related problem is what to do about people who want to work outside the commune. As well as bringing money into the commune, this introduces an outside status system, a different work schedule, a different social system, different loyalties, different consumption expectations, etc. It’s fine for people to work outside the commune in the first stage, but they should be expected to bring in a certain amount, and not allowed to buy their way out of commune work, or to get extra political say with their money. In addition, the commune needs to make sure that people aren’t doing outside work at the expense of developing commune work. The commune shouldn’t become a dormitory for the outside economy, but should work to develop a common purse.

The Zapatistas have struggled with this problem as people have sought to migrate out of their communities. They’ve gone from trying to prevent people from leaving, to negotiating deals with them so that they leave but have certain obligations to the commune.
