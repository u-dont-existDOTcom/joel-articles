The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together

One of the main reasons communities fail, I think, is that they focus on what the founders are interested in. If they’re political, they write up by-laws. If they’re “spiritual,” they do a lot of meditation. If they’re therapists, they talk it all out. If they’re trippy, they all realize they’re God and then can’t make decisions because they’re too busy floating around in the undifferentiated.

I break it down into 4 parts, for simplicity’s sake. I’ve written fairly in-depth guides to the first three, which you can find here, here, and here (free, no weasel, no retreat), and most of this article is going to be about the 4th.

Also, a quick note: While having something that provides food and shelter and takes care of logistics is really nice, I find that having an inner practice to pursue together, which hopefully involves healing and freedom from fear, is super important, as is having a purpose outside the escuelita / federation, so I’m including those here.

All of these are based on the basic principle that

Nothing should be suppressed, everything should be processed, the authority should lie with the practitioner, the community should be a container for this, and everything should be fluid and organic.

Obviously, these aren’t really cleanly separated, and if you were to actually do all of this, you’d probably find ways to integrate the parts. I break them out, however, because it’s useful to be able to identify what your community might be missing. Also, while none of these should be rigid, you do want some stable agreements to work from, otherwise you’d need to renegotiate everything all the time. In religious communities, these frozen agreements are called doctrine; in secular communities, they’re usually called process.
