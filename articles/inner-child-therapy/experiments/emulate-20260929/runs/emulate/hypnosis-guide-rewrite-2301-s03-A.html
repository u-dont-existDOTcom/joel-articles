One prompt I use when I want to separate intuition from conditioning is:

"Wise parts of my subconscious mind, if the impulse I'm getting is based on conditioning -- family, religious, marketing, erotic, political, trauma, or any other way I made a deal as a child to be accepted -- then please show me that this is conditioning and turn that part down a bit (but not so much that it's dangerous to do so). But if the message I'm receiving is coming from the universal heart and is actually true guidance that will help me have a better life, then leave that alone. And let my body know the difference."

When I catch myself daydreaming, I say, "Wise parts of my subconscious mind, if this daydream is useful to me, please show me how it's useful. But if I'm doing this to avoid dealing with something, then please help me come back to the present moment and deal with whatever it is I need to deal with."

When I'm anxious, I ask, "Wise parts of my subconscious mind, what's the positive intent of this anxiety, and how can I help this part feel like it doesn't need to do its job anymore?"

When I sense there's a truth I'm not seeing but that would be too much for me if I did see it, I ask, "Wise parts of my subconscious mind, please don't show me the truth yet, but show me how to increase my ability to handle the truth."

And after asking any of these questions, I always follow up with, "Wise parts of my subconscious mind, if you've given me the answer to this request, what can I do in my ordinary life to know that I've integrated the answer?"

The prompt sets up the search parameters, and the NLP language helps make sure the question is asked in a way that will elicit the best answer.
