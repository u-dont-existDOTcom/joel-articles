Borrow: Bring in a trustworthy adult, mentor, older brother or sister, future self, spiritual figure, personal value, plan, etc. Borrow what you need from them.

Be: Become this trustworthy adult yourself. What would you say or do in this situation? Be sure to “be” at least a little bit of this yourself.

Return: Send this trustworthy figure back from where it came, noticing what they modeled for you and how you can now be at least 5% of what they are.

Note: You should not let this trustworthy figure “take over” for you. Rather, allow them to stand beside you as you make decisions and take action. For a more complete description of this process, I recommend my Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide.

What if you don’t have any trustworthy people in your history? It’s okay to imagine this person. Try to imagine what a good person would say or do, given your particular history and limitations. Start with the least cruel option if you can’t think of a warm, loving one. Once you have a good idea of what to say or do, try it out in real life instead of repeating this exercise over and over again.

Sometimes, even the “adult” part needs to borrow from a trustworthy adult figure. What would a decent adult do if he or she didn’t get trusted by someone? They wouldn’t take it personally and retaliate, they wouldn’t go out of their way to prove how good they are, they wouldn’t collapse, they wouldn’t abandon the other person, etc. Borrow these responses for yourself. You may not “feel” like an adult yet, but if you act like one, you will soon “become” one.
