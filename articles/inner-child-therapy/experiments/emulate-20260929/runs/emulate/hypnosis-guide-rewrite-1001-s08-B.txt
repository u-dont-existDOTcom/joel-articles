Unless they have evidence. Usually, the Protector doesn’t need a spiritual insight, they need you to hold a boundary, do a task, eat a meal, not send a message, keep a promise, get some sleep, contact your therapist, acknowledge a debt, or write down the truth.

Knowing When to Quit Scanning for Danger

If you wanted to practice this, here’s a sequence you might try:

Orient yourself where you are, including how you’re being supported.

Assess the situation, including what you know, what you’re not sure about, and what you need to find out.

Distinguish what might be an actual danger vs. an uncertainty, a past danger, or an imagined danger.

Do one thing to set a boundary that is proportionate to the dangers.

Do enough to convince yourself that you’re safe enough based on something that’s actually going on in the world.

Stand down and allow yourself to rest.

As your Protector matures, it should become more comfortable saying, “I don’t know,” asking other humans for help, putting off interpreting what’s going on for a while, or doing a practice that doesn’t involve trance. My guess is that you’ll be happy when your Protector can quit scanning for danger after a while and become discerning enough to take action when needed and quit scanning when not needed.

Reparenting Your Inner Child

Reparenting therapy and self-hypnosis are a perfect match. There are many variations for working with your inner child. Sometimes, you work with a specific child. Sometimes, you don’t. Sometimes, you get a strong sense of the child’s age. Sometimes, you don’t. Sometimes, you see the child. Sometimes, you don’t. Sometimes, you have an image of a child’s posture, voice, memory, emotion, or other kind of vulnerability. Sometimes, you don’t. Sometimes, you just stay with a Protector you know. Sometimes, you just stay with a sensation, feeling, or preference.
