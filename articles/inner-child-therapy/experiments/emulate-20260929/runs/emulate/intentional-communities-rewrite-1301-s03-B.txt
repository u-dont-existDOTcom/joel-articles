Resources, Land, and Exit Terms

Land ownership is a big source of power.

If it’s in the hands of the founder, you’ve got a de facto landlord with a philosophy. If it’s in the hands of 3 out of 12 members, you’ve got two classes of members, regardless of talk of “all being equal”.

Prepare for it beforehand. Figure out what kind of legal entity you want to be (co-op, trust, association, company…) and figure out issues of voting rights, access to and use of resources, whether or not you’re keeping account of contributions, inheritance, outside debts, improvements, and above all, leaving.

Even if you’re not using money, there will be bottlenecks and scarce resources. All members should know what resources are held in common, what resources are held in common for specific purposes, what outside obligations the community has, how major resources may be apportioned, and what conflicts of interest exist. Transferring major resources (land, equipment, stores, outside money) should not be at the discretion of a single member. All members should be able to leave with a reasonable share of community resources, denominated in whatever unit makes sense (might be cash), like tools, transportation, temporary housing, stores, use rights, etc.

Even good people leave for good reasons. It’s coercive to not let them leave with what they brought.
