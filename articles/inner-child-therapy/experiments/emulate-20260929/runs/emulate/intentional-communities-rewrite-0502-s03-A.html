I actually don’t feel like peer led should be that casual and free form at all. Medical and psychiatric contraindications need to be screened for. Medications need to be carefully considered, both in and of themselves, and what might be a bad combo with other medications. A sober sitter who is appropriate needs to be present. A plan should be put in place to know what would constitute the need for professional and/or emergency medical care. When authority is distributed among a group, it means that each person involved needs to be highly competent.

I have additional cautionary notes about certain meds and protocols that can be needlessly destabilizing and/or medically burdensome, which I have addressed in the above linked articles (eg: certain ketamine and mDMA protocols and bufo).

  1. The medicine needs to be held by the other pl/or k. This means that reparenting and somatic capacity needs to be developed, and peer counseling needs to be developed, prior to medicine work. The medicine cannot create these capacities out of thin air.

This also helps to screen out experience collectors.

  1. There needs to be integration, which happens in the context of everyday life. A ceremony isn’t enough. How are you relating and functioning in the weeks that follow? Did you sleep enough? Is your behavior different, and if so, how? Are you frustrated and declaring spiritual emergency?
  1. Appropriate supports need to be put into place. This includes preps, nutritional, screening of who should/shouldn’t interact with you, being careful not to take more than the prescribed dose, and as mentioned above, being aware of what would constitute the need for emergency care. I am compiling this safety information in a new resource I’m putting together called Altered States Triage.
