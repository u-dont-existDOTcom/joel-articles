Part 2: Reducing Anxiety & Depression

Lower the Cortisol Floor (Phosphatidylserine)

I’d use ketones and D-Serine before this unless you have a glutamate problem. Why? Cortisol is what drives Glutamate in the brain and is key in armoring up (See isolation trap above). The most useful nutrient for me personally has been PhosphatidylSerine (PS for short). It is a Phospholipid that helps lower Cortisol output from the HPA axis.

Who it is for: Those with high or reactive cortisol issues (you feel wired and tired all the time and find it hard to ‘downshift’). This also includes people with slow versions of the COMT gene which breaks down catecholamines (so you’re constantly on high alert). I think it was most useful for my friend Scott who has these issues. Note, it seems to lower cortisol most in people who are chronically stressed, the evidence for helping slow COMT’s is weaker (purely theoretical and my personal experience, not scientifically rigorous).

How much? 300-600mg per day in the earlier part of the day. It won’t have much effect on cortisol if <300mg but >800mg doesn’t seem to help any more.

Food sources: One of the few supplements that you can get enough of through your diet. Atlantic Mackerel has one of the highest levels (~480mg per 100g) as does Herring (~360mg per 100g). Tuna is much lower (~194mg per 100g) and you shouldn’t eat it every day (mercury), but a 200g piece of Mackerel would give you 960mg of PS which is more than most clinical studies have used.
