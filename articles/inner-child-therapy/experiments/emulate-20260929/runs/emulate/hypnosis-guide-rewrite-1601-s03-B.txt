Let the response change the invitation

This is not the same as just "oh, my shoulders are tightening" which could be about anything, and to be curious about why they are tightening is a good move. It's instead like "uh, this is worded wrong." If it's a clear "no", no further explanation is needed. Just say it differently, or don't say it at all, or approach it differently, etc. The point is that there is no reason to keep trying to argue for the thing that didn't work.

"Part of me is like, 'yeah, my shoulders are tightening. I'm just going to let them be tight for now. But also, this doesn't seem like it will be a good fit. Maybe I should say it differently or not say it at all?"

(okay sometimes it's just like "this is worded wrong" but sometimes it's also like "hey, what if we tried this weird thing that i'm not familiar with?") My immediate skepticism does not always have my best interests at heart. Just because something isn't immediately attractive doesn't mean that it's not already doing some important work under the hood.

Look at the situation, too

When someone else is involved, it can be helpful to look at what they're doing, too. Unless I have reason to believe the other person is emotionally immature (which shouldn't take a lot of exposure to danger to conclude, and shouldn't be based on anything other than specific, updateable criteria for what is and isn't emotionally mature), then it's not necessarily just an internal child reacting.
