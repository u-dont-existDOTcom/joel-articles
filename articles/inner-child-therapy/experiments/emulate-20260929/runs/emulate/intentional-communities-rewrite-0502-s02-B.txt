I don’t want to idealize psychedelics. There are people and communities who should not be using them. There will be casualties from people who are too eager to go down this path. Communities who do choose to go down this path need to have a deep humility about what it means to say no, to stop, to refer, to admit harm.

Some Principles of Ceremony For Me

There are no shamans. I know many traditional lineages disagree with this. In particular, many ayahuasca and Bwiti communities do not think the medicine should be taken outside of the authority of trained lineage bearers. I have learned a lot from these communities, and feel so grateful for their wisdom.

But I don’t trust anyone to be above others, to invalidate people’s direct experience. The “shaman” in many retreat circuits is someone unvetted and unaccountable, who is presumed to be wiser than the participants, and who may be sexually and financially predatory. Or they may just be wrong about what is best for you, and you are in a deeply open state to receive this.

I don’t yet know how to avoid “shamans” arising in peer medicine taking communities. Maybe rotating and holding each other accountable will be sufficient. I don’t think so.

Each of us needs to learn how to facilitate our own process, as well as each others, while acknowledging the limits of our competence. See also: Ceremonies held by our peers and The Best Psychopath Shaman I Ever Met.
