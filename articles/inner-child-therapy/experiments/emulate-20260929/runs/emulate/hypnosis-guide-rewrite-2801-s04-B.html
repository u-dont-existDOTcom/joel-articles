In my practice, I am often times asked to help people work through the process of forgiveness. I will help you forgive without gaslighting you with spirituality and without suggesting that this be the focus of your life forever!

What questions do I ask to support people in this journey?

What actually happened? How has it impacted you? What boundaries are needed now? (sometimes, not ever). What do you believe contributed to someone being the person who could do this to you? What parts are theirs? What parts are yours? How is accountability needed here? What does it look like to release yourself from this without denying what actually happened?

How do I support people to forgive themselves? The same way. Remorse is important. Sitting with the feeling that you did something wrong or violated a personal value does not mean that you are a criminal. Once you have taken responsibility, sat with remorse, done your best to rectify the situation and/or make it not happen again, there is no reason to hold yourself hostage to your action any longer. Remember the 4 R’s: Responsibility, Remorse, Rectify and Release.
