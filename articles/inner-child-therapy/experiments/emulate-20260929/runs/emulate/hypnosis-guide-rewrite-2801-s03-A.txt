There is another possibility to be wary of, though, and that is giving your power away. Sometimes the admonition to “seek counsel” can devolve into a search for an authority figure to tell us what to do. Someone other than ourselves is not to be blindly trusted, nor is that person to be automatically ignored.

Even among peers, however, there can be assistance. If we can offer each other equal time, a warm embrace, confidentiality, and an unshakable respect for each other’s right to say “no,” then each of us can bring forth our Nurturer and Protector to serve the other’s Guide, and then switch places. We may even be able to ask a carefully formed question that helps our peer to hear his or her own Guide more clearly, but we should not interpret what the other one is experiencing.

Avoid forgiveness bypass

We cannot force ourselves to forgive anyone. Forgiveness comes when we are able to understand why someone did something, and trying to force it can lead to yet another kind of bypass. On the other hand, we may decide to remain angry forever, and that may be a way of allowing the injurious event to continue to run our lives.
