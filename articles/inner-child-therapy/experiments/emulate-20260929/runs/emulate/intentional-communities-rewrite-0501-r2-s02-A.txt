The main reason I’m discussing this at all is because if we don’t include medicine in the model, the model will be false. Many of you here are aware of my work writing about psychedelic medicines, both here and on my loveyhuasca / Haoma work. I don’t think this article could be honest if I didn’t cover the two sides of this issue.

On the one hand, there are many arguments to be made against incorporating medicine into a community. The Global Ayahuasca Survey has data showing that while severe adverse effects are relatively uncommon and heavily contextual, both physical and mental adverse effects are quite common. The medicines bring people into territories they may not have the capacity to support, whether individually or in a group.

They also tend to attract people who want the revelatory experience but don’t want to do the work. It’s very hard to organize a community around the peak experiences from medicine.

On the other hand, in my experience there are very compelling arguments in favor of using these medicines communally. They can help people drop the shitty behaviors they use to control conversations. They can help form bonds between people. They can also be confusing, grandiose, lead to dependency, be messy in a community, etc, etc. It all comes down to the maturity of the community I think.
