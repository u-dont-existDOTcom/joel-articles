What This Looks Like From Sénégal

I live now in Sénégal, where the idea of ‘intentional community’ is almost alien.

It’s one of the many things that make Sénégal so special and unlike anywhere else I’ve ever lived. To begin with, the prayers five times a day are amazing. Not just amazing, but soothing, in a way I never expected devotional prayers to be. Sufi spirituality runs through the music, the images, the murals, the names, the conversations.

I love to hear the laughter of people out and about, talking for hours. It’s easily the most joking culture I’ve ever lived in, and it makes me sad that I won’t ever be able to understand some of the more complicated Wolof jokes. (Wolof conversation is easy, but Wolof graduate school is another story.) A lot of people here are laughing and joking all the time, to the point that you’ll sometimes see large groups of people walking down the street crying with laughter.

In villages and among extended families, a lot of the things we do with institutions – land ownership, childcare, housing, food security, etc. – are handled through extended family and local memory. It’s a great way to remind people of their connection to multiple generations of community.

One graffiti (graffiti? graffitis?) I saw in Dakar reads, in French, ‘Tolerance! Openness! Union! Happiness! Love!’ And above, I mention murals of Sufi leaders. I still get excited to see murals about spirituality – growing up, it seemed like everything was either advertising a product or advertising the artist’s inability to draw a penis.
