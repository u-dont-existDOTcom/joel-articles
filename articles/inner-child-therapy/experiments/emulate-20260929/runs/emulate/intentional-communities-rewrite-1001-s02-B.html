Some context from someone involved with an existing federation of intentional communities , the Federation of Egalitarian Communities : We are currently in a rebuilding phase. It does take work to run a federation. It needs delegates, and people to take records, make sure everyone is abiding by whatever rules/standards are needed for the federation to do whatever things it does, coordinate resources, meet, do succession planning, and handle disputes if you choose to let it do that. The work it does will require material and labor resources, and in most cases, given the legal and economic systems we currently exist within, it will need to acquire some cash from "outside" to pay for whatever things it does. There can, of course, be many kinds of federation, bilateral "friendships", local clusters, and/or institutions to serve specific functions (currently our health insurance pool, PEACH, is governed separately from the FEC delegates) in addition to or in place of a general federation.

One thing that is important to remember, though currently well remembered within the FEC, is that cooperation doesn't have to mean "everyone has to be the same". Currently we have full members, communities "in process" to becoming members, and "Friends" who are never expected to become members, but with whom we may want some kind of relationship.

People need to be able to move between communities. This may or may not require harmonizing a lot of internal procedures and such; often, it may be enough for the communities to have some kind of interface that defines who is responsible for making sure that various things (housing, status, health care, other records, labor, travel, taxes, etc) don't fall between the cracks when people move. Handling disputes around the transition would be a good thing to think about, too.
