Resources, Land, and Exit Terms

The problem of land ownership is crucially important, since the land owner has immense power.

If the land is owned by the “founder”, be careful, you are dealing with a landlord with a philosophy. If the land is owned by 3 of the 12 members, then you have 2 classes of membership, equal or no.

If the decision is made to buy land, one must decide what legal entity is going to own it (co-op, trust, association, company, etc…) and what rights that entity grants its members (voting rights, access and use rights to the resources attached to the land, accounting of contributions to the entity, etc…), and what happens when a member dies or wants to leave.

In a community without money, one still needs to be aware of bottlenecks and to have some kind of transparency with regard to what is “community” property, what is in common use, what is reserved, what obligations the community has to the outside, who has the right to allocate the major community resources, what conflicts of interest might arise, what prevents major community resources (land, equipment, stores, “money” from outside work) from being transferred to outside use, and what rights members have to take their share of community resources with them when they leave. That last part is important, since even good people leave for good reasons, and it is coercive to force people to stay by holding their contributions hostage.
