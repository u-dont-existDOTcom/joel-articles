Do one of these (typically I’d borrow a function from a safe adult – it could be a mentor, older sibling, your future self, a spiritual figure, an embodiment of a value, a written plan… whatever works):

a) Be the adult and figure out what you need to say or do and do at least part of it.

b) Notice what the adult you borrowed the function from would do and do 5% of that.

The important thing is that you have someone to help you, but you’re not abdicating all responsibility to them. (In my Inner Child Self-Love Reparenting Guide I go through this sequence more completely. )

You don’t have to have had safe adults in your life in order to do this work. You can borrow from someone you remember OR you can make someone up who you think would be good at taking care of you. Then ask yourself what they would say or do if they knew your history, limitations, etc. If you have trouble thinking of something nice to say, start by figuring out the least cruel thing you can think of. Once you’ve done it once, you don’t have to keep repeating the exercise. Just do the thing you came up with.

You can also use this technique on the part of you who is supposed to be the adult… what would a decent adult do if they didn’t trust someone? They wouldn’t do what? (retaliate, protect themselves by proving how good they are, fall apart, run away, etc.) Just borrow that function… you can still FEEL like a kid while you’re doing it. You’ll develop the adult part later.
