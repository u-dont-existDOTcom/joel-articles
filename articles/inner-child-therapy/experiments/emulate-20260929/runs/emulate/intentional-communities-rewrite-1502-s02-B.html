It's a similar problem to what a commune without money has to deal with: you might want no money inside the commune, but the commune still has relations with the outside world, so it has to deal in money to pay taxes, or send kids to the hospital, or get visas to travel to other countries, or pay school fees, or help out family members, or buy tools that can't be manufactured inside the commune, etc. So how do you want to deal with the interface between the moneyless commune and the money-using outside world? How much money should the commune hold collectively in order to deal with the outside world? How do you make sure that dealing with the outside world doesn't create a hierarchy among the members of the commune?

Dealing with people working outside is another issue, since those people aren't just bringing in cash, but also a different culture with a different schedule, a different hierarchy of status, a different social world, different loyalties, different expectations about consumption, etc. etc. I think that in the first stage, people who work outside should have to contribute part of their earnings, but should not be able to buy their way out of doing work inside the commune, nor should they get any extra say in political decisions. On the other hand, it shouldn't be allowed to undermine the attempts to create communal means of subsistence, since eventually everyone needs to be moving toward a common purse.

I understand that the Zapatistas have had this problem with people migrating outside, and that they've gone from trying to prevent it to negotiating arrangements with those who leave.
