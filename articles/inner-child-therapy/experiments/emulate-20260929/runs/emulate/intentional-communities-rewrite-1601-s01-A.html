Practice Comes Before Property

Too often people start at the wrong end of the scale -- getting the land, then talking about how they want it to be, establishing ownership patterns, and skipping the inner work needed to be prepared for the closeness, intimacy, and challenge of life in community.

The other order makes a lot more sense -- working on practice, process, value clarification, membership issues, ownership patterns and exit policies before even thinking about land.

The same is true on the economic side -- start with a cluster family arrangement where some basic internal functions are removed from purchase, but where people who earn outside bring in a certain amount. Only later, when the group is able to provide for all its members and has sorted out the issues of fair allocation of common resources, does it make sense to move to a common purse and decrease or eliminate outside money earning. Otherwise you're just building on fantasies about money being bad/impure/etc.

If You Can't Move

Many (most?) people have responsibilities that make it hard to move right now -- jobs, kids, pets, financial obligations, mortgages, sick parents, etc. The most important kind of pl'ork, however, can be done wherever you are.

All too often, people make the mistake of buying land and then transporting all their unprocessed sh_it out to their forty acres and adding goats.

You can start doing inner pl'ork right now -- form peer counseling relationships, work on reparenting, somatic work, jealousy issues, conflict resolution, etc.
