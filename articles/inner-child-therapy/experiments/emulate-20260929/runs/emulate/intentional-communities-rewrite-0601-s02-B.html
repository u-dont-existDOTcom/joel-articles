Medical care is one of the hardest couplings to get right. Most people in community prefer a more natural approach to health care, but all communities need a plan for trauma surgery, antibiotics, dentistry, emergency obstetrics, and access to specialists. How will the members of your community access these services? What will the community provide from common money? What will members need to pay for themselves, perhaps with insurance? How will you deal with people who want different levels of medical care than you do?

How will you make sure that people aren’t held to some kind of ideological litmus test in a time of need? What will your community agree to for people with catastrophic illnesses? How will you make sure that people can both get the care they want and have access to common resources (or outside sources of money if needed)? Transportation? Privacy? What if people try something that your community supports and it isn’t enough?

One of the best things you can do for your health is to live in community. The Surgeon General issued an advisory on how isolaton increases your risk for a variety of things. It is not just about how we deal with illness. Community has an impact on the whole cycle of how we become ill, how we deal with illness, how we age, and how we die.
