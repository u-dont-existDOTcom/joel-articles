These are often presented in conversation as questions.

Example: Can you allow the system to show you...

Pattern #8: Tag Questions – use lightly, very oily

Example: The body knows how to rest, doesn’t it?

Pattern #9: Nominalizations ( vague terms that the sub-conscious can interpret as something meaningful and concrete ) healing, safety, trust, integration, communion, clarity, rest, etc.

Example: You can allow yourself to move into more safety, in whatever way that means to your body.

Pattern #10: Meta Model Questions (always good to know how to “de-hypno” someone)

The Milton Model is great for opening up possibilities for change, but it is equally important, ethically speaking, to know how to use the Meta Model to find out what someone means when they say something.

How specifically? According to whom? Compared to what? Always? Never? What exactly would happen? How do you know? What would you have to see/hear/etc. to change your mind?

Remember to use the Milton Model to get people “soft” and then use the Meta Model to get them back to the truth!

Other useful sensory/state/strategy change tools:

Submodalities (this doesn’t change the “truth” but can change how it is experienced by the person). Many of the “bad” states that people experience (fear, craving, fantasy, belief, memory, etc.) have sensory components. Notice if your client describes it as near/far, bright/dim, loud/quiet, moving, still, heavy, light, inside/outside of their body, etc. If you don’t notice any of these things, just don’t use this tool.
