In answer to the question "Why do Communities Fail?"

From my experience, I can say that most secular communities fail for a certain reason.

Reason For Failure Of Most Secular Communities

They distribute authority and labor but have no structure for dealing with deep inner issues.

The Federation of Egalitarian Communities and many other income-sharing communities have developed sophisticated ways of dealing with labor, money, and decision-making. But they have no way of dealing with two people who really just hate eachother.

This ultimately leads to emotional issues entering the political scene.

Reason For Failure Of Most Spiritual Communities

They recognize the need to deal with inner transformation but put all of the weight on a single authority, usually a founder, teacher, channel, etc.

The Farm in Tennessee, for example, grew to 1500 people and had a lot of great things about it, but in the early years the spiritual and social life of the community was heavily influenced by Stephen Gaskin. From talking to many former members, it sounds like the community doctrine influenced even the most intimate life decisions of many members of the community.

These two issues are really two sides of the same coin. They both fail to

separate out the various types of authority that are needed in a community (therapist, teacher, farmer, business management, etc.), and allow someone who has authority in one area to accumulate authority in other areas.

What I'm searching for is a way of dealing with depth issues without a guru, and a way of distributing authority without assuming that everyone is fully formed emotionally. Oh, and let's not forget play.
