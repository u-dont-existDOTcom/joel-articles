Mother is Primary. Village is Real. But they do not replace each other.

I do think that Mothers should raise their own children, but that they should be supported by a Village that shares the load. Not a village that takes the kids away from the Mother to "liberate" her, but a village that shares the burden of carrying, supervising, feeding, teaching, protecting, playing with, and to a large extent, "fathering" the kids.

One of the biggest benefits for kids that I can imagine from community living is highlighted in Jean Liedloff's "Continuum Concept." Even taking into consideration that the book is one woman's interpretation of the Ye'kuana (and she was no anthropologist, and may very well have been highly idealistic in her perceptions) the simple act of carrying babies while they participate in normal adult life, as well as having multiple age groups together for socialization, and not placing the entire social burden on one exhausted parent, are things that can be mimicked to a certain extent in a nuclear household setting, but could be done so much better in a real village.

As for experiments where kids are allowed to sleep and live on their own, away from parents - some of these experiments (such as in the Israeli kibbutz's) have proven unsuccessful (they stopped sleeping apart eventually, due to a combination of developmental findings, parental liking, and social changes), but I'm sure that even high quality communal sleeping arrangements could be beneficial to kids, simply due to the quality of the care and the multiplicity of people for kids to form attachments with.

I would suggest that you look into Tamera's Children's Place. But as I said, I'm biased :)
