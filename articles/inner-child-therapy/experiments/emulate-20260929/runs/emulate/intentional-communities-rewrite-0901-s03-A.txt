In a way, the Zapatistas have said, “Don’t import Zapatismo. Don’t export Zapatismo. Learn from the process by which we constructed it. If you see something there that is useful for you, build it. If you discover that it doesn’t work, change the structure.”

However, it may not be a good example of the full path I envision for moving away from money to a system of unpriced internal necessities and a common purse, because they don’t have totally eliminated money from outside and instead use it to sell their products, trade, and receive aid. They do, however, use collective work to provide for most major needs, such as building and running their schools, clinics, and autonomous government, as well as their resistance. They also show the danger of relying on outside support, because it can lead to unequal dependence and gatekeepers.

To celebrate the 10th anniversary of their autonomous governments, they held an Escuelita (little school) in which outsiders were welcomed to live with families and learn how their autonomy works by participating in it. The first escuelitas had 1500 people, and later ones have had several thousand.

One possible failure mode for the escuelita is that it becomes a business in which the people living there are essentially acting for paying customers, and the measure of success becomes how many guests you can get rather than how many daughter communities you create.
