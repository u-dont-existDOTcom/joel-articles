When “your voice” is everybody else’s voice

One thing to consider: for some people, it may be hard to even find their inner child. If they grew up in a very controlling family, a religion or community that did not allow for much individuality, or a family that pushed achievement starting at a very early age, they may not have a very developed inner child. They might not even know how they feel, or what they want, what they believe, what they like or dislike, until someone tells them how they’re supposed to feel first. In that case, the advice to “love yourself” might feel confusing–who is yourself? In these cases, it might help to work on reparenting and building a self in tandem. For some people, it’s easy to feel connected to their child self. For others, it takes building in safety and space to experiment and find out what they prefer.

You might be in this group if:

You don’t know what you want unless someone tells you what they want first

You find yourself having wildly different opinions about something depending on who you’re talking to

You’ve found yourself agreeing with someone in the moment, but when you thought about it later you realize you didn’t actually agree

You feel responsible for other people’s emotions to the point of erasing yourself

Trying to love yourself feels performative

When asked how your inner child might be feeling, you mostly feel like you’re guessing
