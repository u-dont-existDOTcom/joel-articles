Founderism: The Four Questions

In my experience communities don't happen without somebody taking initiative and making it happen: finding land, writing documents, teaching methods, attracting people, or hanging in there when people lose interest.

This gives that person influence, and pretending it doesn't exist doesn't make it go away.

In the case of this particular article, the methods described herein are in large part my methods and I wrote the materials. It'll be tempting for me to turn into one of those authors who gets to decide whether you understood the material correctly. I'm susceptible to that temptation just as much as anybody else despite (or because of?) the anti-authoritarian flavoring of the material.

A few things may help. First, judge the methods based on whether the outcome seems good: are people flourishing and safe, able to deal with conflicts, able to disagree and leave, able to tell a different story after they leave, are the kids turning out okay, is the community viable in material terms, did we correct our predictions when they were wrong, has the community succeeded in replacing its founder, and if it's supposed to be part of a larger movement, are new projects being born and lasting for a while? Don't judge the methods based on whether the community lasts a long time, whether the occupancy is high, whether consensus is achieved, or whether people stay in the community: all of these might be good for the wrong reasons. And of course don't judge the methods based on how well the community follows my preferred interpretation of the methods.
