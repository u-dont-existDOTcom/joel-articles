Induction: gathering attention without disappearing

Inductions serve to gather a persons attention, to bring up stuff they might not be able to bring up while comparatively alert. They do not need to be surrendering, sleep-inducing, or obedience-inducing. When a person is sober, distracted, analytical, or not very surrendering, it may be useful to have an actual induction, but when a person is already trancey (whether from meditation, loveyhuasca, lucid dreamwork, grief work, music, prayer, exhaustion, etc), it may be better to move straight into orientation and containment stuff.

It is useful to have some trance, but it needn’t be too deep.

What’s the induction actually doing?

Sometimes it’s nice to have something to prepare for the work you want to do, to gather the persons attention, or to deepen the trance. There are lots of ways to deepen trance, ranging from counting to just talking to telling stories to using movement or sounds, etc. I prefer to just say when I am doing a trance deepener rather than trying to do it sneakily in a beautiful narrative or something.

When you have a pretty simple target you might also want to try it out before deepening trance, in order to work with what works. The induction serves to get you in a receptive place to listen to the suggestions.
