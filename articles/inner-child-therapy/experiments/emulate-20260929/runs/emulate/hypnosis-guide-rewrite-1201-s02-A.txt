What exactly is the claim? What are you being asked to frame as a choice you have? What is being presupposed in this? Who benefits from this framing? What emotions are being ratcheted up? What is the information that isn't being given to you? Are you able to say "No" without being punished, shamed, mocked, cast aside, or declared to be spiritually fallen?

These will help you be more present to yourself as an adult when reading a guide that might make use of powerful states that require more boundaries to navigate.

Ways that language can be soft but not leave you any choice:

Permissive language that is actually coercive. Some signs:

Utilizing indirectness (which can slip past the critical factor in the conscious mind) to make very specific suggestions in vague, conversational, or suggestive language.

Utilizing the mechanics of induction (focused narrowing of attention) while seemingly just "explaining" things (even, say, about why hypnosis isn't a good idea).

Setting it up so that any response (yes, no, noticing, not noticing, resisting, etc.) is framed as compliance with a suggestion.

Using "whether you notice it or not or not" binds.

Using identity or ownership language to frame being safe or good as obedience, a specific role, approval by the "guide", or other form of belonging to someone else.

Utilizing fractionation (moving in and out of absorption) without declaring that they're going to move in and out repeatedly.
