I cannot point you to an example of how an intentional community has handled a child who was seriously dangerous to others, from beginning to end, well-documented in my research corpus. This is one reason why a child, and a family, must have access to competent help and review which is not under the control of the home community.

The Door Must Be Open When They Become Adults

There’s a reason that Amish youth don’t join the church until they’re adults. Even though 85% or more of them do join eventually, and the Rumspringa is not quite as wild as it’s made out to be, it’s important that they choose the community as adults. If you’re baptizing people as infants, you’re not giving them that choice.

Let the kids get out. Let them explore, let them question, let them learn, let them speak against the founders without being considered traitors.
