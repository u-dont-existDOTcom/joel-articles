APPENDIX E: Is Your Daydream Helping or Taking Away from Your Life?

Daydreaming can be useful. It can be a form of intuition, simulation, a way of digesting grief, creative preparation, a way for our subconscious to teach us in pictures or a way of showing us a possible future or something to be afraid of or to desire. Daydreaming can also be a form of avoidance, addiction to fantasy, dissociation, romanticism, or a way of avoiding growing up and life in general.

How do you know if your daydreaming is taking you back to life or taking you away from life?

If it’s taking you back to life ask yourself:

“What is the guidance within this daydream?”

If it’s taking you away from life sit up, open your eyes a bit and say:

“Hey subconscious, if this daydream is useful to me then show me what it is you are trying to show me. If this daydream is a form of avoidance help me to come out of it and deal with life in a gentle but structured way. I know you have been showing me images in my head. If this is useful to me show me what it is you are trying to show me. If it’s not useful help me to deal with life in the present moment in a gentle but structured way.”

Then ask yourself, “What do I need to do in real life that I’m avoiding by daydreaming?”

You may need to send or not send a message, clean up, eat, sleep, deal with grief, create, apologize, leave a situation, tell the truth, make a plan, ask for help, get back to work or stop chasing people who aren’t available for love.
