I’m mostly bringing this up here because not including it would falsify the model. Most of you are familiar with my writing on psychedelics , including some on loveyhuasca aka Haoma . I want to be honest about both the good and bad side in this article.

The downside of including medicine in a community is obvious and has been brought up in many places , including the Global Ayahuasca Survey . To summarize, physical and mental adverse effects are actually quite common, although severe effects are rare and context dependent. The medicines give you more “opened” material to deal with than you may be able to handle.

The medicines also attract people who are more interested in the high point of experience than integration, and often collect ceremonies, lore, and fluff instead of doing actual inner work. Communities that base their activities around the high point of the experience will have a hard time doing practical tasks, or raising kids.

On the other hand, in my own experience, taking psychedelics in a community setting can be very good for bonding, and allows you to communicate more honestly by getting past defensive behaviors that might dominate sober conversations. It can also be very confusing, and can lead to a lot of “baggage” like grandiosity, dependency, and general messiness. Whether or not it’s a good idea depends on the maturity of the community.
