Appendix K: Sources and further reading

All of the personal content and structural arrangement in this guide is original. The actual content of the practice has been adapted from several body-based and hypnotic techniques, described below. The technique as presented here has not been validated. You don't need to read these notes to use this guide.

Learning, Entering, and Returning

Much of the content can be found in Healing the Divided Self (Maggie Phillips and Claire Frederick, 1995), especially pages 24-27, 45-50, and 83-90. The relevant techniques are interactive hypnosis, learning through the senses, self-hypnosis, and returning to the room in a definite way. Note that this is not a self-help book, but a professional reference for clinicians. The information here is not a slavish copy of the procedures described in that book for use with clients, nor do I endorse all of the historical claims made there. Some of the ideas about learning in hypnosis and using it for yourself come from an article on self-hypnosis published by the International Society of Hypnosis (Adam Eason and Benjamin Parris).

Language and Re-entering

For more on the kinds of language used here, see Phillips and Frederick again (53-57 and 96-100), especially pacing, utilization, working with metaphors, and using associations as cues. You might also be interested in this tutorial on hypnotic language and this tutorial on triggers , both by Nimja. Note that I do not endorse all of the claims made by NLP, and that the content here is not written by Nimja or an endorsement of her work; rather, I've adapted her discussion of language and cueing to work in this context, using my own words.
