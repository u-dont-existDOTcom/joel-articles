I think you’re overlooking that for westerners the process has been primarily about isolation and moving towards tribe, whereas for most Africans it’s been the opposite. And those Africans that are interested in these ideas and who are in your situation in a city are probably alienated from both.

Also, I don’t see this as primarily a western thing, but a global one. And if you want to experience this you need to go to where the villages are still alive, be respectful, be an asset, learn the language, and not expect anyone to serve your spirituality.

The process for getting residency here in Senegal was pretty cheap and easy. It’s a French speaking country, but you’ll also need to learn some Wolof, and forget that anyone will speak English. It’s true that they really value Teranga, or hospitality, and I’m currently living for free in a friends family’s house (they don’t want my rent), but it doesn’t come without some watchfulness, social conservativeness, bureaucracy, and other issues.
