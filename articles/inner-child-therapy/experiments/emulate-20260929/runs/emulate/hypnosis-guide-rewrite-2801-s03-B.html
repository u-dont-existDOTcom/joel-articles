On the other side, there is a danger of giving away your own power, and turning “seek counsel” into “seek an authority who will tell you what to do.” Other people are neither to be automatically trusted nor ignored.

Peers can be helpful. Hearthwork creates a container where people can support each other from the Nurturer and Protector aspects without interfering in each others’ Guide aspects, by giving each other equal time, warmth, confidentiality, and an honor of “no.” Then switch roles. When someone asks you the right question at the right time, it can help you hear your own Guide more clearly. But it is up to you to determine the meaning of your experiences.

Forgiveness without bypass

You can’t force yourself to forgive someone, forgiveness has to grow from understanding. Forcing it can become a form of bypass. But holding onto anger forever allows the bad event to continue to control your life. It can be true that: a) what they did was bad, b) you still need to enforce your boundary, c) they are still responsible for their behavior, and d) you can forgive them later.

In order to forgive someone, it helps to understand that they were acting according to their nature. That doesn’t excuse what they did, but when you can see how they were conditioned, what their history and pain is, and what they were and were not capable of, you can see that this is the only move this total person could have made. It doesn’t negate the badness of their action. It doesn’t let them off the hook, they still need to be held accountable, experience the consequences of their actions, and to develop the ability to make a different move. But it helps you not to expect more from them than they are capable of.
