Important Note: PS is only useful for people who have high cortisol. There are two ways to know if you've made a mistake and you didn't have high cortisol. Remember that Phosphatidylserine reduces cortisol. The symptoms of low cortisol are very similar to high cortisol. These are the two ways you might discover you're using PS when you shouldn't be:

You find that you feel flat, numb, without drive etc. This is a classic symptom of too little cortisol.

This one is a bit counter-intuitive but when your cortisol gets too low, the body starts to use too much adrenaline to keep your blood pressure up and to keep you alert. As a result you feel more wired than normal, you have racing thoughts, you wake up at 3am, you get more irritable, you get more stressful vivid dreams etc. It's a weird one but in effect you feel more stressed when your cortisol is too low.

If you notice either of the above two issues then PS is not for you. Rather than increase the dose, which some might be tempted to do, just stop taking it. The only way to know if you have high cortisol before starting PS is to test it. A 4 point salivary cortisol test is ideal. Other less common symptoms of low cortisol include low blood pressure, gaining water weight, restless legs syndrome etc.

As a rough rule of thumb, if you feel wired, tired, unable to switch off mentally, waking up at 3am etc. then its likely you have high cortisol and PS might be worth trying. If you feel numb, with low drive etc. its unlikely you have high cortisol and PS is likely to make you feel worse. Its best to avoid it or get some testing done to find out for sure.
