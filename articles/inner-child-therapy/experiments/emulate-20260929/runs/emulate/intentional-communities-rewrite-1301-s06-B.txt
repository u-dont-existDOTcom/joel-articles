When Somebody Has to Go

I think in the case of a generally or seriously dangerous individual, or someone who repeatedly violates basic boundaries, or is otherwise making communal living impossible, ejecting them from the community should be a possibility, even if restorative processes could theoretically fix the problem on a longer timeline.

On the other hand, this is a power that could be very easily misused, so it’s very important to have good processes for when and how this power is enacted. It is also important to have pre-established processes for when this power needs to be enacted immediately in order to prevent harm, including processes for evidence gathering, recusal, temporary permission denial, response, review/appeal, temporary permission granting (housing, common resources, outside money if applicable, records, family, etc.), and calling outside entities for assistance.

Note that, while my research here is unfortunately skewed towards involving nation-state entities, that doesn’t need to be the case. You just need some competent outside entity. It may be another community, or a federation, or professionals, or autonomous regional entities, or courts, or regulators, or emergency services, or whatever. (Thanks to the Zapatistas for helping me correct my biased thinking here.) Of course, no entity should be able to violate non-waivable individual rights, even if they are autonomous.
