NB I’ve developed this map more practically in the rest of the method, which is why I’m comfortable publishing this provisional synthesis (parts of which are directly research based, the whole of which is my own):

i) child on her own – orientation and external safety

ii) child with borrowed adult – experience of at least one good adult function, personified by a real/remembered/imagined/symbolic figure

iii) adult as apprentice – with help, adult tries to do at least one part of looking after the child

iv) adult in her own right – adult repeats as many times as necessary, to build up the capacity to summon it at will: offering the child warmth, protection and direction

v) adult in relation to child – adult in charge but not banishing child, child no longer solely responsible for managing life

You don’t have to stop being the child. You become larger than the child-state. A useful sentence is: “I am still this child, and I am also becoming the adult who stays.”
