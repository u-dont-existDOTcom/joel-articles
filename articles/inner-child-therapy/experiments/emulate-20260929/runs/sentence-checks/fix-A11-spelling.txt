Sometimes we may notice when we’ve been hooked, before we know what part of us has been hooked. Sometimes it may be a bit later, perhaps after we’ve noticed the tightening in our jaw or the getting involved of our stomach and the writing of half of the text message we really, really shouldn’t be sending. In which case it may be a good idea to forget about finding out who’s hooked for the moment – Child, Protector, past hurt. Our hand may already be reaching for a glass of wine or turning to our phones or forming an opinion about our relationship. Pema Chodron has a great word for this tightening/urge – shenpa.

So, let’s just notice that we’ve been hooked. If we can take a breath or two (or three) and finish what we were doing (assuming no one is in danger) all the better. If we don’t manage to take the breaths to settle down, we may at least have time to summon enough Adult to be nice to the Child, or Protector to stop us doing something stupid, or simply say to ourselves ‘not yet’.
