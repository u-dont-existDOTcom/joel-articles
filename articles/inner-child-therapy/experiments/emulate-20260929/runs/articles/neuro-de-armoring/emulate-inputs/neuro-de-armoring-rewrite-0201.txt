Part 2: Reducing Anxiety & Depression

Lower the Cortisol Floor (Phosphatidylserine)

Before ketones, before D-Serine, address cortisol directly — because cortisol is what sensitizes the brain to Glutamate and locks the armor in place (see the Isolation Trap above). The single most useful nutrient I’ve found for this is Phosphatidylserine (PS), a phospholipid that blunts the HPA-axis cortisol response to stress.

Who it’s for: People running high or reactive cortisol — the “wired and tired,” can’t-downshift, waking-at-3-a.m. type. This includes many people with slow COMT (the Val158Met variant that clears catecholamines slowly, so stress hormones linger and you stay on high alert). One friend, Scott, with exactly this profile got more out of PS than anything else in this protocol. Be honest about the evidence, though: the cortisol-lowering effect is best documented in chronically stressed people; the slow-COMT angle is mechanism plus my own observation, not a controlled finding.

Dose: 300–600 mg/day, taken earlier in the day. Below ~300 mg the cortisol effect is unreliable; above ~800 mg adds nothing.

Food instead of capsules: One of the few supplements you can actually eat your way to. Atlantic mackerel (~480 mg/100g) and herring (~360 mg) are richest; tuna is lower (~194 mg). A 200g mackerel fillet ≈ 960 mg PS — above clinical doses. (Don’t lean on tuna daily — mercury.)

⚠️ The catch, again: PS is only for genuinely high cortisol — and there are two ways to find out you guessed wrong. PS works by blunting cortisol. If yours isn’t actually elevated, lowering it pushes you down the other side of the curve, where the symptoms look almost identical to high cortisol. Watch for either signal:

Flat / numb / no drive — you’ve over-suppressed. Classic too-little-cortisol.

More wired, not less — racing thoughts, 3 a.m. waking, irritability, vivid or stressful dreams. Counterintuitive but real: when cortisol drops too far, the body leans on adrenaline to hold up blood pressure and alertness, and you feel more stressed, not less.

Either one means PS is the wrong tool for you right now. Don’t raise the dose to push through — stop. If you want to know before you start, a 4-point salivary cortisol test tells you whether you’re actually high. (A minority also report low blood pressure, water-weight gain, or restless legs — all consistent with cortisol going too low.)

The rule of thumb if you can’t test: Wired, tired, can’t switch off, 3 a.m. waking → probably high, PS is worth a trial. Numb, low-drive, flat → probably not high; PS will likely make it worse. Skip it or test first.

Agmatine vs PS vs Phosphatidylcholine

Do not confuse these three tools:

Phosphatidylserine (PS) is the cortisol-floor tool. Use it only for the high-cortisol “wired and tired” profile.

Phosphatidylcholine (PC) is a membrane/choline nutrient. It may support general cell-membrane and acetylcholine biology, but it is not a targeted cortisol-lowering or glutamate-braking intervention.

Agmatine is different from both. It is an optional glutamate/NO/imidazoline “voltage governor.” It belongs in the Shield section, not as a replacement for PS or PC. Like PS, it can overcorrect the system: PS can push low-cortisol people into flatness; agmatine can push already-numb people into more emotional blunting.

Dopamine Receptor Burnout / DAWS Pattern: Uridine Monophosphate

Who this is for: This is not for everyone with depression. This is for the specific “burned-out dopamine receptor” pattern: gray anhedonia, weak dreams, weak psychedelic visuals, needing unusually high doses to feel anything, compulsive reaching for ketamine/LSD/novelty just to get a hit, and especially a history of dopamine agonist withdrawal from drugs like pramipexole or ropinirole. The medical literature calls this dopamine agonist withdrawal syndrome (DAWS), and describes anxiety, panic, dysphoria, depression, agitation, fatigue, autonomic symptoms, pain, and drug cravings after dopamine-agonist tapering. In Scott’s case, the pattern looked like DAWS with D2/D3 reward-system downregulation, then made worse by frequent LSD microdosing and ketamine attempts to force dopamine hits. That is a specific phenotype, not a generic “everyone needs dopamine” claim.

The tool:Uridine monophosphate (UMP). The best evidence is not “UMP cures DAWS”; that study does not exist. The evidence is more basic: uridine, choline, and DHA are circulating precursors for brain phosphatide / synaptic membrane synthesis. Wurtman’s group has shown that DHA + uridine + choline can increase synaptic membrane components and synaptic proteins, that DHA-induced dendritic spine formation is enhanced by UMP in animals, and that dietary UMP increased potassium-evoked dopamine release and neurite outgrowth in aged rats. So the mechanism is plausible, but the DAWS use is still a cautious human extrapolation plus anecdote.

Scott’s signal: Scott already had choline from eggs and DHA/EPA from fish oil. After adding standard-dose UMP, he got a striking response within a day: vivid dreams returned, real childhood memories came back in dreams, psychedelic tolerance seemed to drop, and ketamine/LSD craving fell hard. That does not prove UMP rebuilt D3 receptors overnight. It does suggest that, in the right person, UMP can be a very clean signal that the synaptic-membrane / dopamine-receptor-recovery axis was starved.

Trial: UMP 150–300 mg in the morning with food for 3–7 days. Keep omega-3s in place, especially DHA/EPA. Make sure there is enough choline from eggs, phosphatidylcholine, CDP-choline, or another tolerated choline source. Do not start UMP on the same day you start 5-HTP, agmatine, D-serine, keto, iboga, shrooms, LSD, ketamine, or any other major variable. You are trying to see whether UMP itself changes the signal.

Side effects / stop signs: The most common problem is the “choline headache” or spaced-out brain fog: if UMP accelerates membrane synthesis while choline availability is low, the system can feel under-supplied. Other stop signs include insomnia, jitteriness, racing thoughts, irritability, emotional flatness, nausea, stomach cramps, or loose stools. If it makes you wired, numb, or weirdly irritable, stop. Do not push through.

Psychedelic caution: If UMP makes dreams vivid or suddenly lowers psychedelic tolerance, treat that as a safety signal. Do not take UMP on psychedelic days. If you use psychedelics later, assume your previous dose may be too strong and that chasing the old visual threshold can overshoot.

Serotonin Bottleneck Pattern: 5-HTP + Low-Dose P5P

Who this is for: This is for the low-serotonin-looking pattern, not for every depressed person. Think poor sleep initiation, low mood, low warmth, night anxiety, repetitive rumination, and a genetic or clinical reason to suspect a bottleneck in serotonin synthesis. Scott’s genetic data suggested a TPH2 bottleneck, and 5-HTP helped his sleep and mood. If you are already agitated, manic, on serotonergic meds, or using serotonergic psychedelics/entactogens, this is not a casual experiment.

The tool:5-HTP bypasses the tryptophan-to-5-HTP step and gives the body a more direct serotonin precursor. A broad review of 5-HTP discusses its role in serotonin biology and its use in mood, anxiety, sleep, obesity, myoclonus, and serotonin-toxicity contexts. The evidence for depression and sleep is mixed and not as strong as people online imply, but the mechanism is real enough that responders can feel it quickly.

Scott’s version: 100 mg 5-HTP sublingually at night, with 10–20 mg P5P sublingually in the morning. The P5P is there as the active B6 cofactor for aromatic amino-acid decarboxylase, not as a stimulant. Scott is slow-COMT and sensitive to too much P5P, so the low dose matters. More is not better.

Trial: Start lower if you are sensitive: 50 mg 5-HTP at night for 2–3 nights, then 100 mg only if the lower amount is clearly helpful and not activating. Optional P5P: 10–20 mg in the morning, especially if 5-HTP feels like it “almost works” but is not converting cleanly. Do not combine the trial with UMP, agmatine, D-serine, psychedelics, or medication changes. If it is the right tool, the first signal is usually sleep quality, dream tone, mood warmth, or reduced nighttime rumination.

Side effects / stop signs: Nausea, stomach discomfort, loose stools, headache, vivid or intense dreams, insomnia, sweating, tremor, anxiety, irritability, emotional agitation, or a too-bright / hypomanic feeling. Stop immediately if you get agitation + sweating + diarrhea + tremor/clonus + fever, confusion, fast heart rate, or unstable blood pressure. That pattern can indicate serotonin toxicity, which can become dangerous.

Hard interaction rule: 5-HTP should not be mixed casually with serotonin-raising drugs or supplements. Case literature and clinical reviews warn about serotonin syndrome risk when 5-HTP is combined with antidepressants or MAOIs, and serotonin syndrome is most often a multi-drug / stack problem. See the dietary supplement interaction case report and the general serotonin-syndrome overview from MedlinePlus. For this readership: avoid 5-HTP on psychedelic days, and be especially strict around ayahuasca / harmala MAOI chemistry and iboga / ibogaine.
