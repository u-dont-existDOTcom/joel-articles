Serotonin Bottleneck Pattern: 5-HTP + Low-Dose P5P

Who this is for: This is for the low-serotonin-looking pattern, not for every depressed person. Think poor sleep initiation, low mood, low warmth, night anxiety, repetitive rumination, and a genetic or clinical reason to suspect a bottleneck in serotonin synthesis. Scott’s genetic data suggested a TPH2 bottleneck, and 5-HTP helped his sleep and mood. If you are already agitated, manic, on serotonergic meds, or using serotonergic psychedelics/entactogens, this is not a casual experiment.

The tool:5-HTP bypasses the tryptophan-to-5-HTP step and gives the body a more direct serotonin precursor. A broad review of 5-HTP discusses its role in serotonin biology and its use in mood, anxiety, sleep, obesity, myoclonus, and serotonin-toxicity contexts. The evidence for depression and sleep is mixed and not as strong as people online imply, but the mechanism is real enough that responders can feel it quickly.

Scott’s version: 100 mg 5-HTP sublingually at night, with 10–20 mg P5P sublingually in the morning. The P5P is there as the active B6 cofactor for aromatic amino-acid decarboxylase, not as a stimulant. Scott is slow-COMT and sensitive to too much P5P, so the low dose matters. More is not better.
