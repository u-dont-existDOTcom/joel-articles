# The global healing goal 🌍🫶🏿

The actual project—the one that matches the scale of what we lost—is forming communities capable of holding a whole life: ongoing, geographically rooted as far as the digital age permits, intergenerational, intentional, and able to accompany birth, death, conflict, grief, celebration, and the long ordinary stretches between them.

Intentional communities have attempted versions of this with mixed results. ZEGG and Tamera in Europe, Kufunda in Zimbabwe, some kibbutzim, Bruderhof communities, Catholic Worker houses, and residential Buddhist sanghas have produced real knowledge. Other communities have produced cult dynamics, sexual exploitation, financial abuse, and charismatic capture. The safeguards are part of the practice: consent, transparent money, real exit rights, accountability for facilitators, anonymous feedback, and a refusal to make the group anybody’s entire life. These are practical requirements.

Hearthwork has limits. Capitalism will continue. Climate collapse will accelerate. Platforms will keep profiting from loneliness. State surveillance will keep substituting itself for village care. A peer-counseling pair in a living room will not repair the whole civilization.

We still don’t have to wait. Mutual-aid societies have always existed inside hostile economies. Religious communities have survived regimes that disliked them. Intentional communities have built small alternatives in whatever cracks were available. The work is available now. It requires no permission and spreads through replication rather than funding.

## How does this spread?

I don’t have a great marketing answer, and I’m not convinced a marketing strategy would help. One pair becomes two. Two pairs become a small group. A group may become a circle. This article and the manuals are seeds rather than a campaign. If one hundred people read this and one person starts a group that lasts ten years, the writing has done its job.

There’s also a reason not to optimize for virality. Viral spread brings people looking for quick relief, while sustained practice asks for slow, repetitive work. The methodology and the audience filter each other. People who would burn out on the discipline often never begin. People who might build something durable tend to recognize the pace.

Share this with one person who might actually do it. Don’t post it widely. A carefully chosen invitation matters more than broadcasting it to everybody. Think of the person whose life might change if they had one protected hour each week to be heard.

## What the manuals cannot promise

Hearthwork is a synthesis. Its component methods have been refined for decades, and I assembled them while paying attention to where each tradition broke down. No manual can guarantee that a particular pair or group will succeed. Peer counseling depends on the people, the culture, the coordinator, and the moment in ways no protocol can fully control. Anyone starting with this material is testing it in their own context and owns what unfolds. The manuals at [hearthwork.u-dont-exist.com](https://hearthwork.u-dont-exist.com/) discuss this more fully.

## Start with one person

Find one person you can be honest with. Tell them you want to try a disciplined practice of mutual support. Read one of the manuals together. Choose a time. Meet for an hour, divide it equally, and follow the basic form.

If it helps, meet again the next week. After a month, if you both want more, invite a third or fourth person. Don’t begin by announcing a community. Begin by becoming useful to one person, then let community grow from repeated reciprocal care.

Keep the peer relationship free. There will be a temptation to charge for it, turn it into a business, or seek credentials. The methodology loses something essential when it is monetized between peers. Teaching may be paid. The counseling relationship between peers should never carry a price tag.

Don’t romanticize the group either. Ruptures will happen. People will project. Charismatic dynamics will appear unless the group actively resists them. Healing communities have sexual misconduct, financial exploitation, and cult pressure. Their appearance doesn’t prove the whole group is wrong; these are normal pressures that require explicit safeguards, exit rights, anonymous accountability, and a refusal to make any facilitator indispensable.

And mostly: just begin. The human container we lost was built across generations through small acts of mutual care held in honest discipline. Whatever we rebuild will be made the same way. There is no shortcut. There is also no waiting required. Start with one other person, in a living room, for one hour.

We will not get the village back from any government or market. The only people who can build it are the people who need it.

***That’s us.***
