Peer-led never means casual. Screen for medical and psychiatric contraindications. Review medications and combinations. Keep an appropriate sober sitter. Decide what requires professional or emergency care before anybody takes anything. Distributed authority raises the competence requirement; it doesn’t lower it.

I’m especially cautious about medicines and protocols I consider more destabilizing or medically burdensome, including some uses of ketamine and MDMA and bufo. Those judgments are explained in the linked articles rather than smuggled into one sentence here.

The medicine stays behind the other pl/ork. Reparenting, somatic capacity, and peer counseling come first. Medicine can deepen a practice that exists. It cannot create months of honest relationship by pharmacological decree.

This prerequisite also filters out experience collectors. Somebody unwilling to spend six months learning to listen without fixing is unlikely to become more relationally mature because the visions were especially geometric.

Integration is ordinary life. Ceremony opens something. The following weeks reveal whether anything changed—in relationships, daily behavior, sleep, decisions, and the person’s ability to tolerate frustration without declaring a new spiritual emergency.

Supports matter. Preparation, nutrition, interaction screening, dose discipline, and emergency planning matter more than aesthetic ceremony details. I keep my current safety material in Altered States Triage.
