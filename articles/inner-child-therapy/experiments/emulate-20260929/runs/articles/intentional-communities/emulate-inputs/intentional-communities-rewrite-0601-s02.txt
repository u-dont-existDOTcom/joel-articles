Medicine is one of the hardest couplings. A community may prefer natural care and still need trauma surgery, antibiotics, dentistry, emergency obstetrics, or a specialist. It should decide in advance how members access care, what the community guarantees from common resources, what may require outside money or insurance, and how to treat somebody who chooses a different level of medical involvement.

No member should have to prove ideological loyalty while frightened or sick. A catastrophic-illness agreement should protect choice, access to common resources or outside funds when needed, transportation, privacy, and the possibility that the community’s preferred approach won’t be enough.

At the same time, social connection itself has measurable health value. The Surgeon General’s advisory links isolation with higher risks across several outcomes. Community won’t cure every illness. It can change the daily conditions under which people become ill, recover, age, and die.
