# Stance check v8: section 4 rewrite against the whole published essay

**1.** Rewrite: "Those who have positions with more control of shared resources will automatically be affected by that psychologically (power corrupts, as they say)."
Essay (section 4 published, "One principle runs through all of them"): "control of shared resources can become psychology"; ("Why Communities Keep Dying in the Same Two Ways"): "The danger begins when the same person can define the problem, control the evidence, choose the remedy, and make disagreement or exit costly."
Reader would wrongly believe: Joel thinks control of shared resources always corrupts whoever holds it, when the essay says it can, under specific conditions.

**2.** Rewrite: "Those who know medicine tend to have another type of god complex since they may make life or death decisions for people routinely."
Essay (section 4 published): "medicine can create status"; ("The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn't There" → My Ceremony Principles): "an informal shaman from emerging anyway—the person everybody consults, trusts, and gradually stops questioning".
Reader would wrongly believe: Joel thinks people with medical knowledge tend to develop god complexes from making life-or-death decisions routinely.

**3.** Rewrite: "learning to identify as the best inner adult, which is comprised of the three inner adult roles: Nurturer, Protector, and Guide."
Essay (section 4 published, Inner pl/ork): "identifying as the inner adult—or the three inner adults: Nurturer, Protector, and Guru/Leader"
Reader would wrongly believe: Joel's reparenting method calls its third inner adult "Guide" and treats the three as roles of one "best" adult.

**4.** Rewrite: "The child receives all that love, protection, and guidance that you give it, and in this process actually gains more freedom…"
Essay (section 4 published, Inner pl/ork): "The child receives love, protection, guidance, and enough freedom to remain alive inside you."
Reader would wrongly believe: freedom follows automatically from love, protection and guidance, instead of being a separate, measured ("enough") thing the inner adult gives.

**5.** Rewrite: "And it's built on somatic regulation first. Listening to the body's feelings before making a decision with the mind."
Essay (section 4 published, Inner pl/ork): "Then there's somatic regulation, because the mind can make a magnificent decision while the nervous system quietly files an appeal."; ("Practice Comes Before Property" → What to Do This Month): "Read the free reparenting guide and Hearthwork manual, or begin with another serious practice you already trust."
Reader would wrongly believe: reparenting rests on somatic regulation as its first foundation, and somatic regulation means consulting bodily feelings before deciding.

**6.** Rewrite: "And that actually symbolizes the adult/child relationship to me in some way as well."
Essay: nothing supports this; section 4 presents somatic regulation only as a separate item ("Then there's somatic regulation, because the mind can make a magnificent decision…") and never links it to the adult/child relationship.
Reader would wrongly believe: Joel personally sees the body–mind relationship as a symbol of the inner adult–child relationship.

**7.** Rewrite: "Most are treasure, while some are obviously late additions (sometimes to the point of real absurdity)."
Essay (section 4 published, Inner pl/ork): "Most are treasure. Some seem like late additions."
Reader would wrongly believe: Joel is certain some suttas are late additions and considers some of them absurd.

**8.** Rewrite: "While there may be some pure learning that could come from having several altered members figure out what rules and boundaries are needed…"
Essay ("The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn't There" → My Ceremony Principles): "Decide what requires professional or emergency care before anybody takes anything. Distributed authority raises the competence requirement; it doesn't lower it."
Reader would wrongly believe: Joel sees some real learning value in improvising ceremony rules while altered (section 4's "very pure form of collective learning" is irony).

**9.** Rewrite: "it may be impractical in some locations because of government requirements like taxes on land."
Essay (section 4 published, The container): "Governments may make it literally impossible through land taxes and other required payments."; ("What This Looks Like From Sénégal" → Lessons from the Black Tax): "…taxes, hospitals, visas, school fees, family obligations, imported tools, and other outside relationships still arrive denominated in money."

**10.** Rewrite: "It's rare to see someone come back from an ecstatic vision with news of how to revamp the community's policies on provisional members…"
Essay (section 4 published, The container): "the vision can't determine a departing member's share … or decide what happens when a child reports something serious"; ("The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn't There"): "insight does not wash dishes, revise agreements, or calm a frightened child."
