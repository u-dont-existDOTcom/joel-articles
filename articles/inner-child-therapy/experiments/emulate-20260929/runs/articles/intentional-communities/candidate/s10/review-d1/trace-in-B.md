## P7 → P7a

CONTEXT BEFORE: Cooperation also shouldn't quietly turn into assimilation. The FEC currently has full members and communities working toward membership, and it also has Friends who may just stay friends. People need the same kind of practical freedom between communities. If someone can move in theory, but their housing, status, health coverage, records, labor accounting, travel costs, taxes and transition disputes all fall through the cracks, that freedom is just decoration. Often the answer is a thin interface that says who's responsible for what while somebody's crossing between them, without making every internal system match.

PUBLISHED P7:
Exchange and obligations need the same honesty even if nobody uses money internally. A gift of food, pooled medicine, a loan of a vehicle or tool, reciprocal labor, emergency housing, a shared building project, and a visit meant mainly for training or relationship-building are different events. Some create a future obligation; some do not. A federation can keep purpose-specific records without pricing everything or forcing every transfer into parity. If cash is used at the outside interface, grants, loans, guarantees, and shared-enterprise commitments need the same separation. Solidarity is not unlimited joint liability—or an unlimited claim on another community’s labor, stores, land, or tools.

REWRITE P7a:
Exchanges and obligations need the same honesty, even if nobody uses money internally. A gift of food, pooled medicine, lending a vehicle or a tool, trading labor, emergency housing, building something together, a visit that's mostly for training or building relationships: these are different events. Some of them create a future obligation and some don't. A federation can keep records for each purpose without putting a price on everything or forcing every transfer to come out even. If cash gets used where the federation meets the outside world, grants, loans, guarantees and shared-enterprise commitments need to be kept separate the same way. Solidarity doesn't mean unlimited joint liability, or an unlimited claim on another community's labor, stores, land or tools.
