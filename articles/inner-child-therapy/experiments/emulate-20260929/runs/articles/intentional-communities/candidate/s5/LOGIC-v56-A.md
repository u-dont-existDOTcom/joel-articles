# Logic audit v56-A: P11 and P29

Scope: P11 and P29 only, on the caller's instruction; the other 31 paragraphs were audited separately. Numbering counts every block that isn't a heading or an image (33 in each file). P11 opens "Trusted support is different…" / "Do you trust the people…". P29 is the first block after the image under "After the Psychedelic Calling": "Many people come out…" / "A lot of people feel the urge…".

Neither version of either paragraph contains a modal verb, so there is no modality shift to report. Tables list only relations whose wording differs.

## P11

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Added question; who relies on whom | (opens with the assertion) | "Do you trust the people you're relying on for support?" | SAME. It presupposes the reader relies on supporters; this "you" is the same generic ceremony-goer as the published "has known you". The assertion follows intact. |
| Contrast between the two kinds | "Trusted support is different from purchased support." | "Support from people you trust is different from paid support" | SAME. Trust is still the recipient's; payment still marks the other kind. |
| Substitution | "The commercial alternative is often…" | "paid support, which is often…" | SAME. "Alternative" (instead of community support) drops, but the contrast with trusted support and the section heading keep paid support as the substitute. |
| Frequency and its scope; negation | "often paying strangers for one night of care" | "often paying people you don't know to look after you for a night" | SAME. "Often" still covers both the strangers and the single night; "people you don't know" = "strangers"; "a night" = "one night". |
| Who acts on whom | strangers give care to an implied recipient | "people you don't know to look after you" | SAME. The beneficiary is now explicit. |
| Pronoun: who is excellent | "Sometimes those strangers are excellent." | "Sometimes they're great" | AMBIGUOUS (weak). Three plural antecedents precede "they". Reading 1 (nearest, and the one the feather clause supports): the paid people you don't know, as published. Reading 2: "the people you're relying on for support", the topic the new opening question set up ("people you trust" leads to the same place). Under reading 2 the "sometimes" covers all supporters: a reader would take the author to say even trusted supporters are only sometimes great, and the concession would no longer be specifically about paid strangers. |
| Degree | "excellent" | "great" | SAME. |
| Contrast between the two cases | two parallel "Sometimes…" sentences | "…, but sometimes…" | SAME. The contrast was implicit, and the negative case already came last. |
| Focus "only"; time "already"; "and" | "has known you for four hours and already has a cosmology" | "has only known you for 4 hours and already has a cosmology" | SAME. "Already" already marks four hours as too soon; "only" restates it. Both facts still hold together. |
| Quantifier "everything"; purpose | "a cosmology for everything you say" | "a cosmology to explain everything you say" | SAME. |

## P29

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quantifier: who has the urge | "Many people" | "A lot of people" | SAME. Same scope in both. |
| Disjunction of triggers | "deep psychedelic or mystical experience" | "some serious psychedelic and/or mystical experiences" | SAME. Here the published "or" is an inclusive type disjunction: either kind prompts the urge, and one experience can be both. "And/or" states that same inclusive "or"; neither version requires both. Unlike the facilitator sentence, there is no "one of these, and you don't know which" reading for anything downstream to depend on. |
| Number of experiences | generic singular "experience" | "some … experiences" | SAME. "Some serious" reads as an intensifier, a generic plural doesn't require several experiences per person, and "whatever just opened up" keeps a single recent opening. Reading "some" as "several" (the urge only after a run of experiences) is a stretch. |
| Degree | "deep" | "serious" | SAME. |
| Time and implied cause | "come out of … with the same urge" | "feel the urge … after going through …" | SAME. In both the experience comes first and prompts the urge; "just opened up" keeps the recency. "After…" attaches naturally to "feel", not to "form a village". |
| "Same" as shared across people | "with the same urge" (many people share one urge) | "A lot of people feel the urge…"; "same" moves to the next sentence as a back-reference | SAME. "A lot of people feel the urge" still carries the commonality. |
| Structure of the urge: coordinate list vs. part and whole | colon plus "and": "find the tribe, build the village, and help other people reach whatever just opened" (three coordinate aims; none heads the others) | "the urge to form a village" … "Finding their people and helping others get to whatever just opened up is part of the same urge" | AMBIGUOUS. Reading 1 (literal: "the same urge" = "the urge to form a village"): finding one's people and helping others reach the opening are components of the urge to form a village, so village-building becomes the defining aim and helping others falls under it. The published neither ranks the aims nor puts helping others inside village-building; helping other people reach what opened is its own aim beside building the village. Reading 2 ("it's all one urge"): one urge with three expressions, named first by its village aim, as published. |
| Verbs and articles | "build the village" (generic "the") | "form a village" | SAME. |
| Order of aims | tribe, village, help (list order; finding people naturally precedes building) | village named first; finding people and helping come in as "part of" it | SAME. Neither version asserts a sequence, and "part of" doesn't say the village comes before finding one's people. |
| Pronoun "their" | "find the tribe" (agent = the many people; no pronoun) | "Finding their people" | SAME. "Their" = "a lot of people", the only plural human antecedent; "their people" = "the tribe". |
| Who is helped | "help other people" | "helping others" | SAME. Both leave open whether "other(s)" means anyone besides the experiencers or outsiders beyond the tribe they found. Putting "others" right after "their people" makes the outsider reading more salient, but the published list already allows it. |
| "And" inside the urge | "…, and help…" | "Finding … and helping … is part of" | SAME. The singular verb packs the pair into one unit; both stay inside the urge. |
| What opened, for whom | "reach whatever just opened" | "get to whatever just opened up" | SAME. In both it opened in the experiencer, recently. |

## Pronoun reference in the rewrite

- P11 "they" ("Sometimes they're great"): most natural = "people you don't know" (the paid supporters); also available = "the people you're relying on for support" or "people you trust" (see AMBIGUOUS above). The published names them: "those strangers".
- P11 "you" (every instance): the same generic person in ceremony as the published "you".
- P29 "their" ("Finding their people"): = "a lot of people", those who feel the urge. Unambiguous.
- P29 "others": indefinite; same open scope as the published "other people".

## Findings

CHANGED: none in P11 or P29.

AMBIGUOUS:
1. P11, "Sometimes those strangers are excellent" → "Sometimes they're great" (weak): if "they" is read as the people you rely on (or trust), a reader would think the author says even trusted supporters are only sometimes great, and the concession would no longer be specifically about paid strangers.
2. P29, "the same urge: find the tribe, build the village, and help other people reach whatever just opened" → "the urge to form a village … Finding their people and helping others get to whatever just opened up is part of the same urge": read literally, a reader would think village-building is the defining aim, with finding one's people and helping others reach the opening as parts of it; the published lists three coordinate aims.
