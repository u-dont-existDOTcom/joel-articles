# Stance check: section 5 rewrite, "The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn’t There"

Items 3–7 all come from first-person material added to the opening paragraph of "My Ceremony Principles." I can't tell from this file whether Joel supplied it. The published essay doesn't carry it, so each sentence is reported as an unsupported attribution.

**1.**
- Rewrite: "After my original ayahuasca ego deaths at 25, I had trouble finding anyone…"
- Essay (section 5, "Community Is What Makes Psychedelics Pl/ork Best!"): "After my original ayahuasca ego deaths at 26, I had trouble finding anyone…"
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel's first ayahuasca ego deaths came at 25, not 26.

**2.**
- Rewrite: "A few friends who know your history can tell the difference between new understanding and… mom."
- Essay (section 5, same subsection): "Friends who know your history may notice which parts resemble a new understanding and which parts resemble your mother." The section's opening keeps this conditional: "The question is whether the surrounding community has enough maturity to tell those apart."
- Reader would wrongly believe: friends who know you can reliably sort insight from old family patterns, where the essay says only that they may notice.

**3.**
- Rewrite: "I respect what those traditions have preserved, and I've learned what to do, and what not to do, from watching them."
- Essay (section 5, "My Ceremony Principles"): "I respect what those traditions have preserved and have learned from them."
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel learned from the ayahuasca and Bwiti lineages as an onlooker and partly by counterexample, a mixed verdict on them that the essay doesn't give.

**4.**
- Rewrite: "I see a lot of people need sacred rituals, and the look and vibe to match… even if I don't do them myself."
- Essay (section 5, "My Ceremony Principles"): "Preparation, nutrition, interaction screening, dose discipline, and emergency planning matter more than aesthetic ceremony details."
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel holds that many people need ritual trappings before medicine work and that he himself doesn't practice sacred ritual; the essay says neither, and treats ceremony aesthetics only as less important than safety supports.

**5.**
- Rewrite: "I've also seen that the spirit realms are quite a mixed bag" … a shaman who heals well "may take money to send evil spirits to kill someone."
- Essay (section 5, "My Ceremony Principles"): "A retreat-circuit facilitator may be wise, or may be unvetted, unaccountable, sexually predatory, financially dependent on repeat customers, or simply wrong."
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel vouches from his own observation for spirit realms and for healers who sell killing sorcery, a metaphysical claim and a charge against shamans that the essay never makes; its objection rests on vetting and accountability.

**6.**
- Rewrite: "And people do very often decide they're ready to do ceremonies long before they're really trained and prepared."
- Essay (section 5, "My Ceremony Principles"): "I want people to learn self-guidance and reciprocal care while respecting the limits of their competence."
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel has found premature readiness very common, a frequency claim the essay never makes; set among the lineage remarks, it can read as conceding their case for trained authority over peer-led ceremony.

**7.**
- Rewrite: "I'm sure I could still learn a lot from those traditions, and they could still learn a lot from me."
- Essay (section 5, "My Ceremony Principles"): "I respect what those traditions have preserved and have learned from them."
- Reader would wrongly believe: Joel claims the ayahuasca and Bwiti lineages have much to learn from him; the essay presents him only as a respectful dissenter who has learned from them.
