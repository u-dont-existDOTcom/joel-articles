In my research, I also tried to trace what happened after the Escuelita. I found alumni networks and people carrying practices home. I found organizing that came later, too. But I was looking for a clear chain from an Escuelita student to a newly founded communal institution, and then evidence about how that institution's members and children actually did over time. I didn't find one. Zapatista influence was already spreading internationally before the Escuelita, so if something looks similar later, that alone doesn't prove descent. I'm borrowing a pedagogy and a replication ambition, but I'm not claiming the Zapatistas have already proved my whole model.

Sociocracy has a very different history, but it ended up with some similar forms: circles and consent. It also has distributed authority, and links between levels. So builders have another serious system to study, instead of inventing governance during their first dispute. The resemblance doesn't prove a universal formula, though.
