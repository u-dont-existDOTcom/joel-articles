# Logic audit v49-A: P4, P11, P12, published vs rewrite

Scope set by the caller: P4, P11 and P12 only. The other 30 paragraphs were audited separately. Counting every block that isn't a heading or an image, both files run P1–P33; P4 opens "Most intentional communities…", P11 "Trusted support…" / "Do you trust…", P12 "In community, a sober sitter…" / "In a community, your sober sitter…". Rows cover only relations whose wording differs.

## P4

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Negation, agency | "still avoid saying so publicly" | "are still not public about it" | SAME. "Avoid" names a choice and "not public" a state, but "due to possible trouble" supplies the motive. |
| Cause | Two sentences side by side; the second explains the first | "due to possible trouble … from" | SAME. Same direction (risks → silence), now stated. |
| Concession, scope | "Even where the law permits some use," opening the four-item sentence | "(even where the law allows some use)" inside "possible trouble … from" | SAME. It still governs all four sources. |
| Modality: parents, neighbors | "parents may worry about children, neighbors may imagine chaos" | "possible trouble … from parents worried about children, from neighbors imagining chaos" | AMBIGUOUS. (a) The worry, the imagined chaos and the trouble are all possibilities (= published). (b) Parents are worried and neighbors are imagining chaos now; only the trouble is uncertain. The last two items keep their modals ("might", "may"), and that contrast pushes toward (b). |
| Modality, event: insurers | "insurers may disappear" | "insurance providers who might drop them" | SAME. "Might" ≈ "may". "Them" = the communities (the parents, children and neighbors just listed don't fit), so it is the same loss of cover with the object named. |
| Negation, quantifier, modality: officials | "a local official may understand none of the distinctions" | "local officials who may not understand any of the distinctions" | SAME. "None" = "not … any" (nothing understood), under the same "may". One official becomes officials generally, still under "may". |

## P11

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Claim: "Trusted support is different from purchased support." | Question: "Do you trust the people you're relying on for support?" | SAME for the contrast, which the next sentence still asserts. The question adds a plural noun phrase that later competes for "they". |
| Degree | "is different from" | "is very different from" | CHANGED. The published asserts a difference of unstated size; the rewrite asserts a large one. |
| Second term of the contrast; frequency | "purchased support" … "The commercial alternative is often paying strangers for one night of care." | "paying someone you don't know to look after you for a night" (no "often", no "alternative") | CHANGED. The published contrasts trusted with purchased support in general and calls the one-night stranger only its frequent form. The rewrite sets trusted support against that one case, unqualified, so it reads as what paid support is. |
| Subject of the "sometimes" verdict | "Sometimes those strangers are excellent." | "Sometimes they're great" | AMBIGUOUS; see the pronoun table. |
| Contrast between the cases | Two "Sometimes" sentences side by side | "…, but sometimes…" | SAME. |
| Degree | "excellent" | "great" | SAME. |
| Focus | "has known you for four hours" | "has only known you for 4 hours" | SAME. "Only" states the shortness that "already" implies. |
| Purpose | "a cosmology for everything you say" | "a cosmology to explain everything you say" | SAME. "Everything" kept. |

Who "they" is, sentence by sentence:

| Sentence | Published | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | No "they". | No "they". "You" = the reader. Introduces "the people you're relying on for support" (plural). |
| 2 | No "they". The payer is the person seeking care. | No "they". Introduces "people you trust" (plural) and "someone you don't know" (singular: the paid carer). |
| 3 | No "they": "those strangers" = the strangers paid for one night. | "They're" has three possible antecedents. (a) "Someone you don't know", as singular they: the paid stranger (= published). (b) "People you trust", the nearest plural: trusted supporters are only sometimes great, and "but" sets them against the feather person. (c) "The people you're relying on" (sentence 1): any supporter, paid or not, the feather person among them. |
| 4 | No "they". "The person with the feather" = one of those paid strangers; "you" = the person in ceremony. | Merged into sentence 3. Under (a) the feather person is the paid stranger; under (c), one of the people you rely on. |

Under (b) and (c), the published concession that some paid strangers are excellent is lost: the praise moves to trusted people, or to supporters in general. Swapping plural "strangers" for singular "someone" is what lets the plural-looking "they" attach to (b) or (c).

## P12

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Determiner | "a sober sitter may be somebody…" | "your sober sitter may be someone…" | SAME. "May" kept. |
| Link, frame and modality of the watched/held line | "In community, a sober sitter may be somebody who has known you for years. Being watched and being held feel different." | "In a community, your sober sitter may be someone who's known you for years, and you feel the difference between being watched and being held." | AMBIGUOUS. (a) The two states feel different: a general fact (= published). (b) In a community you do feel held rather than merely watched: an outcome stated as certain, inside "In a community" and joined by "and" to a clause that only says the sitter "may" know you. The published line sits outside both "In community" and "may", and never says you will feel held. |
| Degree | "This matters especially" | "This is especially important" | SAME. |
| Category and example | "long, medically risky experiences such as iboga" | "iboga or other long and medically risky experiences" | SAME. Iboga belongs to the category in both; "or" lists cases (any of them), and both properties are required in both. |
| Condition | "with" | "when doing" | SAME. |
| Modality and scope of the requirement | "where screening and continuous observation are essential" | "For those you need to be properly screened beforehand and monitored continuously." | SAME. Necessity in both; "those" = iboga and the other long, risky experiences, the same scope as "where". |
| Who | Nouns with no agent or patient | "you need to be … screened … monitored" | SAME. The person taking the medicine is the patient, as implied; the agent is still unnamed. |
| Degree, time | "screening" | "properly screened beforehand" | SAME. Adequacy and prior timing are implied by "essential" screening. |
| Wording only | "continuous observation" | "monitored continuously" | SAME. |

## CHANGED and AMBIGUOUS

- P4, AMBIGUOUS: "parents may worry about children, neighbors may imagine chaos" → "possible trouble … from parents worried about children, from neighbors imagining chaos". A reader may take the worry and the imagined chaos as already happening, with only the trouble uncertain.
- P11, CHANGED: "is different from" → "is very different from". A reader believes the gap is large; the published says only that there is one.
- P11, CHANGED: "purchased support … The commercial alternative is often paying strangers for one night of care" → "paying someone you don't know to look after you for a night". A reader takes the one-night stranger as what paid support is, not its frequent form.
- P11, AMBIGUOUS: "Sometimes those strangers are excellent" → "Sometimes they're great". "They" can be the paid stranger (= published) or the people you trust or rely on; on the second reading the praise leaves the paid strangers, and trusted support becomes only sometimes good.
- P12, AMBIGUOUS: "Being watched and being held feel different." → "…, and you feel the difference between being watched and being held". Either a general fact (= published) or a certain claim that in a community you feel held, though the sitter only "may" know you.
