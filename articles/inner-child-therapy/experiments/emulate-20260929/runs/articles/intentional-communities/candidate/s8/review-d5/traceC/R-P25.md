The realistic standard isn't unlimited freedom. It's not making it artificially impossible for people to leave. Kids who grow up without using money especially need to learn enough about how the money world outside operates that they aren't helpless in it: jobs, rent, contracts, banks, scams, ID, getting around, and how to ask for help.

Kids also need transferable skills and education, relationships with people outside the community, access to their own records and documents, and at least one plausible next step outside the community (school, an apprenticeship, work, family, another community). It would be great if the community could also provide resources to smooth the transition, like temporary housing, help with travel, tools or a small stash, but even if it can't, it can at least not make things worse by punishing people for leaving, or by holding back knowledge or records a young adult would need to function outside the community. No upbringing can provide perfect freedom, and the point isn't to erase path dependence. It's to not turn belonging into a trap.
