# Stance check: section 6 rewrite ("Which Connections to the Technosphere Do You Keep?"), review d11

I checked every sentence in the rewrite that states a position, rule, assessment or claim about how things work against the whole published essay. Three of them change a stance. The rest mean the same as the published text.

## 1. Not knowing how much of the fear is accurate becomes not knowing whether it is legitimate at all

- Rewrite: "Do I think the fears about the future are legitimate? I don't know. Maybe."
- Essay: "AI stopped being a tech-news curiosity and began reorganizing people’s jobs, feeds, relationships, and expectations of the future." (Communes Are Hot Again). The published section 6 sentence it replaces: "I don’t know exactly how much of the future fear is accurate."
- What a reader would wrongly believe: Joel isn't sure the fears behind the polycrisis urgency are justified at all, but the essay treats AI disruption as already happening and leaves only its extent open.

## 2. A member's medical choice becomes a question of what the community wants to provide

- Rewrite: "And what if someone wants more (or less) medical care than what the community wants to provide?"
- Essay: "There is also a floor the majority cannot vote away: bodily safety, enough food and sleep, independent medical care, …" (Founderism: The Four Questions, subsection How My Own Model Could Become a Cult). The published section 6 wording: "how to treat somebody who chooses a different level of medical involvement."
- What a reader would wrongly believe: the community's preference caps how much medical care a member gets and the amount is open to negotiation. In the essay it's the member's own choice, backed by a right to independent care that no majority can vote away.

## 3. Social connection's measured health value becomes community membership's

- Rewrite: "At the same time, being part of a community, having good social connections, is measurably good for a person's health."
- Essay: "I was also lonely, bored, irritated by the cult programming, and missing my friends in Philadelphia." (The New Age May Dawn Suddenly, on Love Israel). Also: "Community itself isn’t automatically liberating. Everything depends on who is permitted to belong without pretending." (What This Looks Like From Sénégal, subsection All is not well in paradise). The published section 6 sentence: "social connection itself has measurable health value."
- What a reader would wrongly believe: research shows that belonging to a community is good for health in itself and is the same as good connection. The essay ties the measured benefit to social connection only, and it shows people being lonely or harmed inside communities.
