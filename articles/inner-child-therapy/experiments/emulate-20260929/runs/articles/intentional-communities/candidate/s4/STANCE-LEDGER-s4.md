## 1. Themes

**T1 Founders solve their favorite part.** Communities lean toward what interests their founders; the psychedelic people forget ownership. *Where:* S4 P1; S1 "The secular communities often buried hurt under procedure." *Strength:* hedged ("tend to"); examples are jokes.

**T2 The four parts.** Inner pl/ork (reparenting, somatic, contemplative), relational pl/ork (Hearthwork, jealousy, romance), medicine pl/ork, the container. *Where:* S4 P2, H3a–d, P22; S5 "leaving medicine out would falsify my model". *Strength:* flat. *Pulls:* S5 "The medicine stays behind the other pl/ork."

**T3 The parts leak; the boxes are a diagnostic.** Categories overlap at once; Joel uses them to spot neglect. *Where:* S4 P5; S7 "jealousy cannot become the hidden governor". *Strength:* flat; crossovers "can".

**T4 Why bind lives together.** Logistics aren't enough: shared inner practice gives meaning, an outward purpose another reason. Property upkeep and protecting insiders still count. *Where:* S4 P3; S1 "a lovely refuge for whoever got there first". *Strength:* flat. *Pulls:* S9 "An escuelita can fail by becoming a visitor business."

**T5 The one principle.** Nothing suppressed, everything processed; authority stays with the practitioner, the person doing the practice; the community provides the container; answers stay fluid. *Where:* S4 H2, P4; S11 "commitments I choose for my own practice, never powers the community gains over me". *Strength:* flat. *Pulls:* S11 "“Nothing suppressed, everything processed” can become compulsory disclosure."; S3 faults inner life left "largely private or ignored".

**T6 Stable agreements versus doctrine.** Agreements stay stable yet open to lived experience; frozen answers become doctrine, religious or procedural. *Where:* S4 P4, P6, P7, P13; S9 "build the practice first and develop the theory afterward". *Strength:* emphatic both ways; P7 hedged.

**T7 Inner pl/ork: play, reparenting, the body.** The inner child also wants play and teaches too; adult selves reparent with protection and freedom; the body must agree. *Where:* S4 P8–P12; S3 "enough play that it doesn’t become a permanent repair shop". *Strength:* flat; P10, P12 jokes.

**T8 Inner practice and the Buddhist texts.** Joel's own practice rests on the early suttas read critically; he calls the Abhidhamma a "wrong turn". Communities should hold their own teachings the same way. *Where:* S4 P13; S5 "I couldn’t even find them at Buddhist monasteries" (the only other Buddhist mention). *Strength:* flat; "seem"; "I consider"; "should". *Pulls:* Buddhism is never made the community's practice.

**T9 Peer counseling and its limits.** Hearthwork spreads help across the group instead of one professional, guru or founder, within limits. *Where:* S4 P14, P15, P17, P29. *Strength:* flat. *Pulls:* S12 "That does not make the community infinitely absorbent."; S11 "Peer counseling can become surveillance delivered in a warm tone."

**T10 Care versus jurisdiction.** Listening must not automatically give power over the speaker's membership, work, housing, children, medicine or disputes. *Where:* S4 P16; S3 "someone carries authority earned in one domain into the others". *Strength:* flat; "automatically" bounds it.

**T11 Referral outward.** Some needs exceed peers; outside help need not mean the state. *Where:* S4 P17; S8 "competent help and review that the home community does not control"; S13 "“Outside” does not always mean the nation-state." *Strength:* S4 mild ("is important"); S8 firmer.

**T12 Jealousy handled early.** Address it while it belongs to those involved, before it turns procedural. *Where:* S4 P18; S3 "The feelings just became agenda items." *Strength:* should. *Pulls:* S7 "The group can offer support and enforce basic boundaries"; S3's agenda (Part 4).

**T13 Romance.** Pairs will form; acknowledge romance without choosing everyone's form. *Where:* S4 P19; S7 "I don’t think a community should choose the form for everyone." *Strength:* flat ("will"); S7 hedged.

**T14 Plant medicine and the shaman.** Careful peer-guided plant medicine with no shaman above everyone; peer-led raises the safety bar. *Where:* S4 P20–P21; S5 "No special shamans." *Strength:* emphatic ("all have to"); ironic "I don’t recommend it". *Pulls:* S5 "Traditional lineages usually disagree with me here."; "I respect what those traditions have preserved"; "I don’t yet know how a peer-led medicine culture prevents an informal shaman"; S11 "Medicine is optional."

**T15 The container: land, ownership, exit.** Membership, land, shared resources, governance, children and exit give the inner work a stable place; beautiful land can't offset one person's final say; exit terms first. *Where:* S4 P1, P22, P28–P30; S13 "Whoever owns the land holds enormous power over the community."; "Write the exit terms while departure is still hypothetical." *Strength:* flat. *Pulls:* S11 "Ask them about me if people end up living on land I buy."

**T16 Less money, in three stages.** Joel's own direction: unpriced internal basics with outside earners sharing; then a common purse; furthest, no outside money; each step after shown capacity. *Where:* S4 P23–P26; S1 "when I talk later about moving beyond internal money" (points here); S16 "only after it can provision members reliably"; S15 "money cannot buy exemption from ordinary communal work". *Strength:* "I think", "probably", "may". *Pulls:* S13 "If leaving means forfeiting everything contributed, staying becomes coercive."

**T17 The Bruderhof and his father.** The Bruderhof's generations-long common purse, where Joel's father lived 17 years, shows the idea is tested. *Where:* S4 P25; S1 "He had already spent 17 years in the Bruderhof". *Strength:* flat. *Pulls:* S17 "including the autonomy it requires members to surrender".

**T18 Does a fully moneyless community exist?** Joel found no well-documented modern case; governments may block it; the end may be nearly moneyless with one small collective cash boundary. *Where:* S4 P26; S9 "not proof of my complete economic path". *Strength:* first-person finding; "may" twice. *Pulls:* absence of evidence only; the Bruderhof is moneyless internally (S1).

**T19 Scarcity and power after money.** Dropping money removes neither scarcity nor economic power. *Where:* S4 P27; S13 "A money-free community still has scarce things and bottlenecks." *Strength:* flat; "can".

**T20 Vision can't settle practical questions.** No vision yields membership policy, exit shares, land control or child-report procedures. *Where:* S4 P6, P28; S5 "The call may be real. Omnipotence is a terrible founding document." *Strength:* flat; "nobody has ever" a joke. *Pulls:* S5 "The case for communal use is also real in my experience."

**T21 Healed first, admitted at capacity.** People should arrive largely healed, without pretending they're finished, and be admitted no faster than the group can support; healing continues inside. *Where:* S4 P3, P29; S3 (Joel) "people who’ve done the healing first can join without pretending they’re fully finished"; S12 "early members usually need more available resource than acute need". *Strength:* S4 flat; S12 "usually". *Pulls:* S12 fears capacity talk becomes "a respectable way to exclude"; S1 (Joel) "the healing communities I’m proposing, to heal".

**T22 Skipping a part.** Skip one of the four and the group meets it later, under pressure. *Where:* S4 P31; S16 "Most community projects begin in the wrong order". *Strength:* hedged ("eventually", "usually"). *Pulls:* medicine is optional (Part 4).
