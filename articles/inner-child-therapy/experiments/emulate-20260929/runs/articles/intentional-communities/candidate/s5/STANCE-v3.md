# Stance check v3: section 5 rewrite ("The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn’t There")

Every other sentence in the rewrite that states a position, rule, wish, assessment or claim agrees with the whole published essay, and the hedges are kept. The two findings below make one safety rule weaker. Neither one reverses it.

## 1. A decision made in advance about escalation becomes "a clear idea"

- **Rewrite** (My Ceremony Principles): "a clear idea, before anybody takes anything, of when to bring in professional or emergency care"
- **Essay**, section 4 ("The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together", under "3. Medicine pl/ork"): "Screening, sober sitters, emergency plans, integration, and clear boundaries all have to exist before the first ceremony." The published section 5 sentence it replaces: "Decide what requires professional or emergency care before anybody takes anything."
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** a general understanding of when to escalate is enough, when the essay asks for an explicit decision and a plan made beforehand.

## 2. Emergency planning becomes "knowing what to do"

- **Rewrite** (My Ceremony Principles): "That means preparation, nutrition, screening for interactions, dose discipline, and knowing what to do in an emergency."
- **Essay**, the same section 4 passage ("emergency plans … all have to exist before the first ceremony"). The published section 5 list: "Preparation, nutrition, interaction screening, dose discipline, and emergency planning matter more than aesthetic ceremony details."
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** one person's know-how can stand in for an emergency plan the group made before the first ceremony.

These count because the essay keeps asking for hard calls to be settled in advance instead of left to understanding in the moment. For example: "Decide the response process before anyone’s face is attached to it" (section 8, "The Grown Children Get the Final Review").
