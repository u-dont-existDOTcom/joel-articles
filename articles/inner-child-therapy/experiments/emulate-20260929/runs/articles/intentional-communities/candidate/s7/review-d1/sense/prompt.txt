# Which Connections to the Technosphere Do You Keep?

Loneliness is only one reason building community feels urgent. I wrote separately about the wider polycrisis:

[An embedded post card: "Facing the Polycrisis: Is There Hope For Humanity?", by the author, 15 November 2025. Its excerpt:]

I just read this post from Jamie Wheal about the “polycrisis,” which I suppose needs no explanation, but it’s basically a word for summarizing that every kind of shit possible is hitting the fan which was already half broken:



How accurate are the fears about the future? I don't know exactly. Maybe AI will improve the lot of humanity enormously. Maybe millions of people will lose their jobs to AI. Maybe AI will become the administrative layer between us and nearly everything. Maybe all of these things will happen before Chantress Seba finishes going through all the applications to her commune. 😂

Ok, so on a personal level, it makes more sense to me to focus on cultivating more human independence than to wait for certainty. It's better to have community and find out I didn't need an exit than to need an exit after spending ten years optimizing my social media feed, right?

What connections to the technosphere do you keep, and which do you limit, when you're designing a community? This is also one area where many modern folk will balk. Let the community decide if I can use my social media, or ChatGPT? Are you kidding? I get it. I really do. You're not a child anymore. But on the other hand, these technologies don't just affect you, they affect how you relate to the community as well.

[an image]
[caption] New friends are silver, but old friends are gold

Medical care, banking, phones, vehicles, schools, supply chains, government ID, internet and energy are each a separate decision. If you opt out of them completely, it can cause needless suffering. If you depend on them completely, the outside system can reach directly into every essential function.

The Amish, over generations, have made community decisions about where and if they want to use a piece of technology based on how it affects their community. Different Amish communities make different decisions. Sometimes they use it at work but restrict it at home. Sometimes they share it instead of everyone owning one. Sometimes they hold off on using it until they know more about its social effects. Whatever else you think of the Amish, they at least understand that adopting a technology is a decision for the group to make, rather than something that gets downloaded onto your device while you sleep.

Medical care is one of the toughest dependencies to work out. A community may prefer natural care, but what happens when someone in the community needs trauma surgery, antibiotics, dentistry, emergency obstetric care, or any number of specialist services? The community should think through how members are going to have access to care. What kind of care is going to be guaranteed to community members by the community using common resources? What things might require outside dollars or some form of insurance? And how will the community deal with people who choose to accept (or not accept) different levels of medical care? These are all questions that a community should consider in advance.

No member should have to prove their ideological loyalty when they're scared or sick. There should be pre-existing agreements in place so that if a member gets catastrophically ill, their choices will be protected, they will have access to common resources or external funds if needed, they will be transported if needed, their privacy will be maintained, and there's room for the possibility that the community's preferred methods are not sufficient.

At the same time, having social connections is measurably good for a person's health. The Surgeon General's advisory notes an increased risk for a range of health outcomes among people who are socially isolated. Community's not a panacea, but it can make a difference, day to day, in the conditions people get sick, heal, age, and die in.

(heading) Jealousy Can Capture Monogamy or Free Love

[1] Any community that lasts will eventually reorganize itself due to attraction and pair-bonding. [2] When two exes live in the same city, they can move to different sides of the city. [3] When they're part of a thirty-person commune, they may still be in the same kitchen the next morning, working in the same team, attending the same meetings, sharing childcare and the same friends. [4] A couple's jealousy or breakup becomes a problem for the entire community, because it will change the loyalties, the sleeping arrangements, the work load, the kids, the alliances, and sometimes who's leaving.

[5] The discussion generally goes straight to "should people be monogamous or free-love?" but as I discuss in my Romance Guide, this is one level too late in the conversation. [6] The community problem is that, in close quarters, jealousy gets magnified, and it can capture either system.

[7] If people are doing rigid monogamy, they can turn fear and possession into a moral imperative. [8] If people are doing free love, but aren't doing the inner pl/ork, they can just end up with a larger spreadsheet to distribute their fear over. [9] Calling relationships by this or that name doesn't release childhood panic, or stop people from comparing themselves, feeling terror at the prospect of abandonment, or wanting to control other people.

[10] I don't think it should be up to the community to decide for everyone what form relationships will take. [11] People can make different agreements with each other, in pairs, threes, fours, whatever. [12] They can change those agreements as they wish. [13] And they can ask for support when those agreements bring up old material.

[14] Whatever forms people choose, I do think that housing, security for kids, membership and access to common necessities should not be contingent on a person staying in some agreement with a person they're romantically involved with.

[15] ZEGG and Tamera have spent decades experimenting with relationships inside community. [16] Their answers are not a set of commandments that everyone must follow. [17] But they are an example of how a community can acknowledge the erotic aspects of life. [18] It is not necessary for 20 adults to pretend that they've become neutral colleagues just because the by-laws say so.

[19] The non-negotiable for me is that jealousy not be allowed to become the hidden governor. [20] This doesn't mean it can't come up. [21] It needs to be able to be talked about and felt in the body. [22] It needs to be explored without humiliation. [23] And it needs to be let go of gradually, and not used to punish, or make rules.

[24] There are some ways the group can help, and some basic boundaries the group can enforce, without determining what type of relationships people have. [25] Even so, there will still be disagreements. [26] That's more realistic than saying it's all been resolved.
