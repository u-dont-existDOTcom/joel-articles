# Stance check: section 5 rewrite (v38)

Scope: I checked every sentence in the rewrite that states a position, rule, assessment or claim against the whole published essay. Sentences that aren't listed matched the essay.

## 1. Age at the first ego deaths
- **Rewrite:** "After my original ayahuasca ego deaths at 25"
- **Essay** (section 5, "Community Is What Makes Psychedelics Pl/ork Best!"): "After my original ayahuasca ego deaths at 26"
- **A reader would wrongly believe:** Joel's first ayahuasca ego deaths happened at 25.

## 2. Respect for the lineages becomes a mixed verdict
- **Rewrite:** "I've learned what to do, and what not to do, from watching those traditions, though."
- **Essay** (section 5, "My Ceremony Principles"): "I respect what those traditions have preserved and have learned from them."
- **A reader would wrongly believe:** Joel treats the lineages partly as cautionary examples he watched from outside, and not as traditions whose preserved knowledge he respects.

## 3. Rituals: a new claim about people and about Joel's own practice
- **Rewrite:** "A lot of people need the rituals, and the look and vibe to match … even if I don't do them myself."
- **Essay** (section 5, "My Ceremony Principles"): "I’ve written more about peer-held ceremonies" and "Preparation, nutrition, interaction screening, dose discipline, and emergency planning matter more than aesthetic ceremony details."
- **A reader would wrongly believe:** Joel doesn't take part in ritual himself and holds that many people need ritual trappings to feel comfortable. The essay says neither of these things; it only ranks aesthetic details below safety supports.

## 4. Spirit realms and paid sorcery
- **Rewrite:** "The spirit realms are quite a mixed bag: … a shaman who heals … may take money to send evil spirits to kill someone."
- **Essay** (section 5, "My Ceremony Principles"): "My objection is to placing one charismatic person above everyone else’s direct experience."
- **A reader would wrongly believe:** Joel treats spirit realms and killing-sorcery for hire as real, and bases "no special shamans" on the idea that shamans may be malevolent. The essay's actual objection is structural.

## 5. An unhedged claim about how often people jump ahead
- **Rewrite:** "And people do very often decide they're ready to do ceremonies long before they're really trained and prepared."
- **Essay** (section 5): "enthusiasm will create casualties wherever competence lags behind access" ("Community Is What Makes Psychedelics Pl/ork Best!") and "Distributed authority raises the competence requirement; it doesn’t lower it." ("My Ceremony Principles")
- **A reader would wrongly believe:** Joel states as a common fact that people take up ceremonies long before they're trained. Inside a paragraph about the lineages' case for trained authority, it can read as giving in to that case. The essay answers that case by raising what peers must be competent to do.

## 6. The lineages could learn a lot from Joel
- **Rewrite:** "I'm sure I could still learn a lot from them, and they could still learn a lot from me."
- **Essay** (section 5, "My Ceremony Principles"): "I respect what those traditions have preserved and have learned from them." See also "Founderism: The Four Questions": "Anti-authoritarian language does not immunize an author against enjoying authorship."
- **A reader would wrongly believe:** Joel claims the lineages have much to learn from him. The essay pairs his disagreement with respect and with what he learned from them, and never makes that claim.

Note: items 2 to 6 all come from one paragraph. There the rewrite drops the essay's single sentence of respect for the lineages and puts five new sentences in its place. None of that new material appears anywhere in the published essay.
