# Stance check: §5 rewrite, "The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn’t There" (v43)

Scope: every rewrite sentence that states a position, wish, rule, assessment, or claim about how things work was checked against the whole published essay. No source beyond the essay was available, so added first-person material is judged only by whether the essay supports it. Four of the seven conflicts come from material added to the first paragraph of "My Ceremony Principles."

## 1. Joel's age changed

- **Rewrite:** "After my original ayahuasca ego deaths at 25, I had trouble finding anyone…"
- **Essay (§5, "Community Is What Makes Psychedelics Pl/ork Best!"):** "After my original ayahuasca ego deaths at 26, I had trouble finding anyone…"
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** Joel's first ayahuasca ego deaths came at 25, not 26.

## 2. Fellow ceremony-goers offered as an early check on grandiosity

- **Rewrite:** "In a community of people who’ve sat through the same nights … someone can catch the God complex early."
- **Essay (§5, "My Ceremony Principles"):** "I don’t yet know how a peer-led medicine culture prevents an informal shaman from emerging anyway…" Also §4, "The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together": "medicine can create status."
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** Joel says people who share ceremonies can catch one another's God complexes early; the essay never claims this (its peer reality check is for downloads) and calls the related informal-shaman problem unsolved.

## 3. Respectful learning recast as watching and negative example

- **Rewrite:** "I respect what those traditions have preserved, and I've learned what to do, and what not to do, from watching them."
- **Essay (§5, "My Ceremony Principles"):** "I respect what those traditions have preserved and have learned from them."
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** Joel learned from the ayahuasca and Bwiti lineages as an onlooker and partly from their mistakes; the essay says only that he respects them and learned from them.

## 4. New claims about ritual and about Joel's own practice

- **Rewrite:** "I see a lot of people need sacred rituals, and the look and vibe to match … even if I don't do them myself."
- **Essay (§5, "My Ceremony Principles"):** "Preparation, nutrition, interaction screening, dose discipline, and emergency planning matter more than aesthetic ceremony details."
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** Joel works without ritual himself and thinks many people need ritual trappings before taking medicine; the essay says neither and ranks aesthetics below safety supports.

## 5. Sorcery reported as something Joel has seen

- **Rewrite:** "I've also seen that the spirit realms are quite a mixed bag … he also may take money to send evil spirits to kill someone."
- **Essay (§5, "My Ceremony Principles"):** "A retreat-circuit facilitator may be wise, or may be unvetted, unaccountable, sexually predatory, financially dependent on repeat customers, or simply wrong." Compare §9, "The Best Model I’ve Seen: Zapatistas": "I present it only as my impression—not as fact."
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** Joel vouches from his own observation that shamans can be paid to kill with evil spirits; the essay lists only human risks and marks its unverified impressions as impressions.

## 6. The lineages cast as having much to learn from Joel

- **Rewrite:** "I'm sure I could still learn a lot from those traditions, and they could still learn a lot from me."
- **Essay (§11, "Founderism: The Four Questions"):** "Anti-authoritarian language does not immunize an author against enjoying authorship."
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** Joel claims the ayahuasca and Bwiti lineages have much to learn from him; the essay's only statement about them runs one way ("have learned from them," §5), and it flags his own pull toward authorship.

## 7. Residents' purpose narrowed to training others

- **Rewrite:** "if people are living in a community in order to train up so they can help others build … a real alternative."
- **Essay (§1, "The New Age May Dawn Suddenly"):** "people live there, heal, learn the relational and practical methods, and some eventually leave to start the next one." Also §4: "An outward *escuelita* and federation purpose adds another reason to keep going…"
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** members live there chiefly to train as builders of other communities; the essay has them there to heal and learn, with only some leaving, and treats outward building as an added reason (the rewrite also cut "care for one another" from the sentence before).

Conflicts found: 7
