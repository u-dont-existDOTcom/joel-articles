# Stance check r2: section 12, "The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn't For"

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1.** Rewrite: "Many people, intelligent and well adjusted, would rather talk for six hours about solar panels."
Essay (section 12, published): "Modern life trains us to perform competence while hiding whatever might complicate the performance." The published sentence calls these people only "perfectly intelligent".
A reader would wrongly believe that Joel considers people who would rather avoid these subjects to be emotionally healthy. The essay treats the avoidance as trained hiding behind a show of competence.

**2.** (Minor: an added hedge.) Rewrite: "some very practical questions to ask might be, 'How much attention can this person bring to other people right now?'"
Essay (section 12, published): "So the question isn't whether a person is 'good enough' for community. The practical question is how much attention they currently have…"
A reader would wrongly believe that capacity is a line of questioning Joel only floats, not the test he uses in place of worth. The section's "right now" list then applies it as that test.

**3.** Rewrite: "that's going to require actually making concrete criteria, allowing for outside review, allowing for re-visiting a 'not now' decision"
Joel's stated position (2026-10-11 18:35 UTC): "before critical mass, my rules apply, because it's my land ... i decide who stays, i decide who leaves, i decide everything."
Essay ("Communes Are Hot Again"): "Every warning in this article applies to me too."
A reader would wrongly believe that capacity-based "not now" decisions need set criteria and outside review even at the founding stage this section covers ("Early on, members…", "a founding group"). Joel says that before critical mass he alone decides who stays. The sentence comes from the published text (see the next part). It is a conflict only if "process" covers the stage before critical mass, and the section's framing suggests that it does.

**4.** Rewrite: "(It's good that some groups can provide that, but a founding group shouldn't pretend it can.)"
Essay ("The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together", 2. Relational pl/ork): "Some things also remain outside peer competence. Knowing when to refer to outside or expert assistance is important." On The Farm ("From Visitor to Member—Slowly"): "many people arriving in need and few effective filters. Openness felt spiritual. The practical burden accumulated anyway."
A reader would wrongly believe that Joel approves of some groups acting as crisis landing places that provide treatment, housing and legal aid. Since the sentence contrasts them with "a founding group", the natural reading is established communities. The essay says only that a founding group should not pretend it can "replace" those services, and it treats them as outside help.

**5.** Rewrite: "finding support where you are so you can build up your resources a bit."
Joel's stated position (2026-10-02 00:52 UTC): "I DO want people to come in largely pre-healed, which is why I talk about that in the article."
Essay (section 12, published): "So early members usually need more available resource than acute need, plus practical skills and a willingness to learn."
A reader would wrongly believe that someone whose need "far outstrips" their resources needs only a small top-up before joining, rather than arriving largely healed.

## Contradictions in the published text

Section 12 never takes back one of its own claims, and I found no clash between it and the rest of the essay. One passage conflicts with a position Joel has stated since the essay was published:

**1.** Published (section 12): "Any real process would need concrete criteria, outside review, and a way to revisit a 'not now' decision."
It conflicts with Joel's statement of 2026-10-11 18:35 UTC: "before critical mass, my rules apply, because it's my land ... i decide who stays, i decide who leaves, i decide everything."
Why a reader would see it: the section applies its capacity test to "early members" and "a founding group", which is the stage before critical mass. The essay also says "Every warning in this article applies to me too." So "any real process" includes Joel's own land, where he says he alone decides who stays. Read as a fixed requirement, "outside review" also clashes with his 2026-10-07 objection to forcing communities to accept outside intervention, unless the community picks its own reviewer. If the sentence is meant only for the membership process of an established community, there is no conflict.
Rewrite: still has it. "allowing for outside review" is slightly softer, but the rewrite still presents it as required.
Not counted (outside this section): "From Visitor to Member—Slowly" asks for "an independent way to challenge the decision" for high-consequence roles, which raises the same question.

Conflicts in the rewrite: 5. Contradictions in the published text: 1.
