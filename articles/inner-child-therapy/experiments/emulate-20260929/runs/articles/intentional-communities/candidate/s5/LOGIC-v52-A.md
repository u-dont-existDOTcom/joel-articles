# Logic audit v52-A: P7, P11, P26, P29, P32

Scope: these five paragraphs only; the other 28 were audited separately. Paragraphs are numbered by every block that is not a heading or an image (33 in each file, aligned). Rows list only relations whose wording differs. Pronoun lines resolve every they/them/it (and their) sentence by sentence (S1, S2…).

## P7

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Addition | "They also attract" | "They do also attract" | SAME: still one more item in the case against; emphatic "do" changes no relation |
| Who; modality | separate sentence: "A person can collect … while remaining unable to apologize to a housemate" | apposition: "people who can collect … and still can't say sorry to their housemate" | SAME: the example is now explicitly the attracted type, as the juxtaposition implied; inability persists despite collecting in both |
| Quantifier; modality; time | "Any community … will eventually discover" | "Any community … is going to find out sooner or later" | SAME |
| Negation scope; frequency | "insight does not wash dishes, revise agreements, or calm a frightened child" | "the insight doesn't do the practical work for it. Sometimes the dishes need doing and a scared kid needs comforting." | AMBIGUOUS: (1) the last sentence gives examples of work insight won't do, as published; (2) "Sometimes" limits the needs: such demands come up only now and then, so insight's shortfall is occasional. The published claim is categorical, its examples sit under the negation, and it states no frequency |

Pronouns: published S1 "They" = these medicines (end of P6); S2–S3 none. Rewrite S1 "They" = these medicines, "their" = those people; S2 "it" = the community (less likely the peak experience; the claim is the same either way); S3 none.

## P11

| Relation | Published | Rewrite | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Added question | — | "Do you trust the people you're relying on for support?" | SAME: alters no published relation, but adds an antecedent for "they" (below) |
| Axis and scope of the contrast | "Trusted support is different from purchased support. The commercial alternative is often paying strangers for one night of care." | "Support from people you trust is different from paying people you don't know to look after you for a night, which is often the alternative." | CHANGED: published sets trust against purchase, covering bought support as such, with strangers-for-one-night only its frequent form. Rewrite sets people you trust against people you don't know, paid for a night: a reader takes the problem to be unfamiliarity, and paid support from someone you know, or for longer, falls outside the claim |
| "often" | "The commercial alternative is often paying strangers…" | "…, which is often the alternative" | SAME: given the published binary (trusted vs. purchased), both say the alternative is often paid strangers for a night; "which" = that option |
| Pronoun; concession | "Sometimes those strangers are excellent." | "Sometimes they're great," | AMBIGUOUS: (1) "they" = the people you don't know: the published concession that paid strangers can be excellent; (2) "they" = the people you trust, or the people you're relying on: trusted supporters are only sometimes great, and the concession to strangers disappears |
| Contrast; degree | two juxtaposed "Sometimes" sentences; "has known you for four hours" | "…, but sometimes…"; "has only known you for 4 hours" | SAME: contrast made explicit; "only" states the shortness that "already" implies |

Pronouns: published none ("those strangers" is a full noun phrase). Rewrite S1–S2 none; S3 "they" ambiguous, as above.

Pronouns: published none. Rewrite S1 "It's" = dummy subject; S2 "their" = the people who feel the urge.
