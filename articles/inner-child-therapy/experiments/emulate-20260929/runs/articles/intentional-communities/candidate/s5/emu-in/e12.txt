A group formed during collective spiritual euphoria can recruit too quickly, promise too much, and discover arithmetic only after several people are already dependent on it. The capacity and membership sections later in this article exist partly to protect the original generosity from that first rush.

Once the call sobers up, it can become useful. A community can serve as a training ground where people learn the practices, care for one another, and sometimes leave to begin another experiment. That answers the reasonable objection that communes are merely privileged escape pods.
