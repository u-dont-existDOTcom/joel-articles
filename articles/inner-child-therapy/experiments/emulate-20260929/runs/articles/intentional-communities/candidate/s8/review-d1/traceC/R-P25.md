The realistic standard isn't unlimited freedom. It's that leaving shouldn't be made artificially impossible. Kids who grow up without using money especially need some education about how the money world works, so they aren't helpless in it: how to get a job, pay rent, sign contracts, deal with a bank, avoid scams, get ID, get around, and ask for help when they need it. They need skills and an education they can take with them, relationships outside the community, access to their records and documents, and at least one plausible next step, whether that's school, an apprenticeship, work, family or another community. If the community can help out with temporary housing, travel, tools, or a small reserve for the transition, great. But even if it can't, it can at least not punish people for leaving, or withhold the knowledge and records they need to function somewhere else. No upbringing gives perfect freedom. The point isn't to erase path dependence. It's to keep belonging from turning into a trap.
