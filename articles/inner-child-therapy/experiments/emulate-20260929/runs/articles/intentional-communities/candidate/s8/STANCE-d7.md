# Stance check: section 8 rewrite vs. the whole published essay (d7)

Scope: every rewrite sentence stating a position, wish, rule, assessment or how-things-work claim, including Joel's own 10-07 paragraphs (marked [J]). Joel's 10-07 statement on outside help ("position (b)") is treated as his current view. Sentences that passed are not listed.

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. Communal fathering becomes conditional**
- Rewrite: "If the father is unknown, the whole community can do the fathering"
- Essay (§8 published, "The Mother Is Primary…"): "The village can carry meals, supervision, teaching, protection, play, and much of fathering."
- Reader comes away believing: the village fathers only children whose father is unknown (and then does all of it), rather than carrying much of fathering for every child.

**2. The mother's primacy becomes "honored"**
- Rewrite: "but the biological mom's role should always be honored."
- Essay (§8 published, same subsection): heading "The Mother Is Primary." and "My position is that mothers raise their own children inside a community where care is genuinely shared."
- Reader comes away believing: the essay asks only that the mother's role be respected, not that mothers raise their own children.

**3. [J] Being taken seriously depends on a reputation for honesty**
- Rewrite: "Make sure kids understand what they say will be taken seriously if they are known to be honest."
- Essay (§8, kept in the rewrite's next paragraph): "Hear the child even when the accused person is beloved… Immediate safety comes first; investigation must be competent and fair"
- Reader comes away believing: a child without a record of honesty may not be taken seriously, though the next paragraph's response applies to every report. The Boy Who Cried Wolf framing invites that reading. (Separate from the published contradiction Joel fixed; see A1.)

**4. [J] Outside help in the hardest cases becomes a hope, and gentle**
- Rewrite: "need access to competent help and review, hopefully including non-intrusive help from outside the home community."
- Essay (When Somebody Has to Go): "The important thing is a competent layer that is not reviewing its own conduct: sometimes … a court, regulator, or emergency service."
- Reader comes away believing: a seriously dangerous child's case can be handled and reviewed in-house with outside help optional, though for removals the essay calls an independent reviewer "the important thing" (and The Math of Absorption says a capacity process "would need … outside review").
- Note: this fits position (b), which as stated objects to forced intervention, not to an independent reviewer the community chooses; the gap is independence. "Non-intrusive" also reads as excluding the courts and emergency services listed there, which goes beyond (b).

**5. [J] Amish belonging presented as a free choice**
- Rewrite: "they normally choose to stay in the community" … "they're baptized as adults, which means belonging is a commitment they choose on their own"
- Essay (Founderism: The Four Questions): "A group can have high retention because people love it—or because leaving costs too much." And §8: "A community whose children never leave cannot know whether they would freely return."
- Reader comes away believing: the essay treats Amish belonging as freely chosen, though most youth stay and the essay elsewhere says neither staying nor high retention shows that. Published §8 claimed less: adult baptism "still matters" because belonging becomes "a commitment rather than a fact imposed at birth."

**6. [J] Outside relationships go from a need to a help**
- Rewrite: "It helps if they have relationships with people outside the community."
- Essay (§8, Children Need Freedom Inside Real Protection): "Keep relationships and a reporting route outside the community… Children need more than one world."
- Reader comes away believing: outside relationships are a useful extra for young people leaving, though the essay, including the rewrite's own "Make sure kids have relationships…", treats them as a need; the floor in How My Own Model Could Become a Cult also includes "contact with people outside the community".

## Contradictions in the published section (requested separately)

**A1. Teaching truthfulness. Fixed by Joel's rewrite.** "Children should understand that their words carry power…" then "This education must never become … a reason to dismiss a report." Confirmed: the paragraph motivates truthfulness by the weight of a child's words, then rules out the teaching ever bearing on whether a report is dismissed. Finding 3 is a different clash, which the new wording creates with the next paragraph.

**A2. No-secrets rule vs. surprises. Fixed by the rewrite.** "No adult asks a child to hide a gift … from the child’s mother" then "Surprises have an ending date." A surprise gift for the mother is a hidden gift, so the rule forbids what the next sentence allows. The rewrite's "A surprise isn't a secret" resolves it.

**A3. Transition help vs. Exit Terms. Tension; still in the rewrite.** §8: "If the community can offer temporary housing, travel help, tools, or a small transition reserve, great." Resources, Land, and Exit Terms defines exit rights in tools, transport and temporary housing "so exit is not a paper right that works only when the community has spare money." The community's own grown children get as an option what departing members get as a right. Exit Terms addresses members, so the difference may be deliberate. The rewrite keeps "It would be great if…".

**A4. Help for a dangerous child. Not an internal contradiction.** The published "competent help and review that the home community does not control" matched When Somebody Has to Go ("a competent layer that is not reviewing its own conduct"). The essay's cautions about outside control (Island Pond; outside help that can "create gatekeepers") lean toward position (b) without contradicting independent review. The published sentence conflicted with Joel's 10-07 position (b); the rewrite adopts (b), and what remains is finding 4.

Conflicts found: 6 in the rewrite; separately, 2 contradictions and 1 tension in the published section.
