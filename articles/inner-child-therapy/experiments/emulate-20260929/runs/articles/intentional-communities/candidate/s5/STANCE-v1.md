# Stance check: section 5 rewrite, "The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn't There"

Each sentence below changes a position the published essay states. They are ordered by how far each one moves a reader's picture of Joel's view.

## 1. The medicine gate becomes a caution

- **Rewrite:** "If a person hasn't done the reparenting, somatic capacity, peer counseling, etc. before the ceremony, the medicine can't do that work for them."
- **Essay:** "MEDICINE PL/ORK (peer-guided, no shamans, gated behind the other work)" (diagram, "The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together"); "Medicine prerequisites can create gatekeepers with power over people at their most open." ("How My Own Model Could Become a Cult")
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** the earlier practice is advisable preparation that someone can skip and still sit in ceremony, rather than a prerequisite the medicine stays behind.

## 2. A shared calendar becomes withheld permission

- **Rewrite:** "Many medicine traditions use community timing for their ceremonies, rather than allowing people to take medicine when they want. This can be helpful…"
- **Essay:** "authority stays with the practitioner; the community provides the container" ("The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together", "One principle runs through all of them"). The published sentence here credits "A shared calendar" with reducing compulsive repetition ("Community Is What Makes Psychedelics Pl/ork Best!").
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** Joel finds it helpful for a tradition or community to decide when individuals may take medicine, rather than structuring ceremonies around a shared calendar.

## 3. The limits behind the founding rush are softened

- **Rewrite:** "there are people who don't want to be fixed … and those that need more help than you imagined."
- **Essay:** "A group can absorb only as much need as its members have free resource to meet." ("The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn't For"). The published sentence names "people who don't want to change" and "people whose crisis exceeds anything your enthusiasm can hold" ("After the Psychedelic Calling").
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** the inflated founder's mistake is underestimating how much help people need, and those who resist are refusing to be fixed, rather than unwilling to change or in crises beyond what enthusiasm can hold.

## 4. The legal record is downgraded to a basic understanding

- **Rewrite:** "some basic understanding of what is or is not allowed in a given community … what might be insurable … should be put in writing."
- **Essay:** "Legality needs a written answer." and "A community should document what activities are allowed, prohibited, private, insured, or legally reviewed." ("My Ceremony Principles")
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** a rough written sense of what's allowed, with guesses about insurability, is enough, rather than a definite written record of legal status and actual insurance.

## 5. Humility narrowed to owning the group's mistakes

- **Rewrite:** "sometimes the answer will be no, or not now, or go work with so-and-so, or we messed that one up and someone got hurt."
- **Essay:** "needs enough humility to say no, pause, refer out, and admit when an experience caused harm." ("Community Is What Makes Psychedelics Pl/ork Best!"); "Knowing when to refer to outside or expert assistance is important." ("The Four Parts That Have to Pl/ork Together", "2. Relational pl/ork")
- **Reader would wrongly believe:** humility means passing someone to another person and owning the group's mistakes, rather than referring out to outside or expert help and admitting harm an experience caused even when nobody erred.
