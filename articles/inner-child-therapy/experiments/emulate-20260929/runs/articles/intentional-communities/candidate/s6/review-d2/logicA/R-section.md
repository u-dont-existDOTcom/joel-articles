I don’t know exactly how much of the future fear is accurate. AI may make human life enormously better, suck millions of people out of their jobs, put itself between us and nearly everything as a sort of digital gatekeeper, or do all three before Chantress Seba gets through her commune applications.

Personally, I think it's a better idea to invest in being more independent as a human than to wait for certainty. It's better to have community and find out I didn't need an exit than to need an exit after spending ten years optimizing my feed, right?

What connections to the technosphere would be useful to limit or keep if you were to design a community?

Things like medical care, banking, phones, vehicles, schools, supply chains, government ID, internet and energy are each a separate decision. If you opt out of all of them completely, it can cause needless suffering. If you opt into all of them, the outside system can reach directly into every essential function.

The Amish, over generations, have made community decisions about where and if they want to use certain pieces of technology based on how it affects their community. Different Amish communities make different decisions. Maybe they use it at work but not at home. Maybe they all share one but don't each have one. Maybe they don't feel it's appropriate to use until they know more about what it does to people. Whatever else you think of the Amish, they at least understand that adopting a technology is a decision for the group. It shouldn't get downloaded onto your device while you sleep.

Medicine is one of the toughest couplings to work out. A community may be interested in having as "natural" medicine as possible, but what happens when someone in the community needs trauma surgery, antibiotics, dentistry, emergency obstetric care, or any number of other specialist services? It is really important to think through in advance how people in the community are going to have access to these services; what will be guaranteed with common money; what may require people to get money from outside sources (including insurance); and how the community will treat people who want different levels of care.

No one should have to jump ideological hoops when they're scared or sick. If a community member becomes catastrophically ill, all community members should already have agreed to support that person in whatever they decide to do, including making common and/or outside money available when it's needed, making sure they can get wherever they need to go, respecting their privacy, and acknowledging that the community's preferred approach may not be enough.

At the same time, the Surgeon General's advisory documents the impact social connection has on several health outcomes. It's not a panacea, but community can make a difference, day to day, in how people get sick, heal, age, and die.
