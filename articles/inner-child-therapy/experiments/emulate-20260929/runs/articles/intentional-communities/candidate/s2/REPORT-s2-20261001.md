## What it shows (the lessons, for `docs/EMULATE-FALLBACK.md` once Joel has decided the questions below)

1. **Putting the source's sentence back brings its flag back.** Section 4 of the Emulate doc says to fix Emulate's sentences "with small logged fixes" and to put our sentences back one at a time. v2.1 fixed every trace finding by restoring the published sentence, often whole, and 7 of 10 texts flipped to 100% AI.
   - The fix for a meaning shift is the smallest change to the words that restores the meaning, not the source's sentence.
   - This is the second case, after the escuelita proposal sentence in section 1.
2. **On argument paragraphs, faithful rewording fails, whoever does it.** Fresh writers failed 34 of 35 drafts:
   - 19 of 20 with the published paragraph as the brief;
   - 15 of 15 with bare points, the article so far, and one noticing allowed.

   The one pass reordered its sentences so each reacted to the one before. The Inner Child system passes most paragraphs (5 of 6 on first check, 2026-09-30), but that's new writing from a guide. Here every claim had to stay at its strength, so the paragraph's skeleton stayed.
3. **Emulate passes by changing exactly what carries the argument.** Its versions drop the four needs and the 1972 line, change the quote, add hype ("so beautifully and powerfully") and invent reasons. On these paragraphs, keeping the meaning and passing Pangram pull against each other. Which parts of the meaning can flex is Joel's call.
4. **For a published article, the section check is the better first diagnosis.** Checks of 50 to 100 words came back 100% one way or the other, flagged the whole text, and were marked "confidence limited". The section check gave a percentage and three localized spans, and it showed context carrying sentences that failed in shorter checks. C9's first sentence failed in every check of C9 alone, and C3 and C4's first sentence failed in the C3-C4 pair; inside the section, none of them was flagged.
5. **My predictions:**

   - The shared gate requires both, from the Inner Child rule that "a section can pass while one of its paragraphs is AI".
   - *My opinion:* yes for published articles, and keep both checks for new writing. The paragraph checks here are binary and unlocalized. A published article is read and checked as a whole. And the rule's purpose, stopping padding around an AI paragraph, is met when no span in the section is flagged.
