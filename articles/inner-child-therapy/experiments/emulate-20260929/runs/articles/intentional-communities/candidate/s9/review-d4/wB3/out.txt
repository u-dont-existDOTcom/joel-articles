In my research, I also tried to trace what happened after the Escuelita. I found alumni networks and people carrying practices home. There was organizing later, too. But I didn't find a clear chain from an Escuelita student to a newly founded communal institution and then evidence about how that institution's members and kids actually did over time. I'm borrowing a pedagogy from the Zapatistas, and an ambition to replicate. I'm not claiming they already proved my whole model. And if something looks like them later, that by itself isn't proof of descent, because their influence was already spreading internationally before the Escuelita.

Sociocracy comes from a very different history, but it has some similar forms. It uses circles and consent. Its authority is distributed, too, and there are links between levels. The resemblance doesn't prove there's a universal formula. Still, it gives builders another serious system to study, instead of inventing governance during their first dispute.
