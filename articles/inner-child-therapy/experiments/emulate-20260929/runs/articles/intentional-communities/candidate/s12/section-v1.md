# The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn’t For

This article asks people to talk about jealousy, God, death, childhood, money, sex, psychedelics, and what happens when somebody wants to leave. A lot of perfectly intelligent adults would much rather spend six hours talking about solar panels.

That makes sense to me. Modern life trains us to look competent and hide whatever might complicate the act. Screens let us disappear the second something gets uncomfortable. And institutions reward narrow skills, then act surprised when nobody can sit through a conflict without appointing a subcommittee or blocking somebody.

So whether someone is “good enough” for community isn’t really the question. What matters in practice is how much attention they’ve got for other people right now, how willing they are to look at their own reactions, and whether the group can support them without getting swallowed up.

Some people have years of contemplative practice or therapy behind them. Some got really lucky in childhood. Some are in the middle of an emergency, just surviving, and need care more than they need responsibility. Most of us move around between those over a lifetime.

In the model I’m imagining, some of the jobs modern society hands to therapists get shared out across the community. [Peer counseling](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling) spreads the capacity to listen, to be with someone through something, and to help people work through ordinary emotional stuff. That doesn’t mean the community can absorb everything, though.

The phrase *[free resource](https://www.rc.org/)* comes from Re-evaluation Counseling. It means how much attention, steadiness, skill and practical capacity you’ve got left once you’ve dealt with enough of your own immediate stuff. *Need* is whatever needs support right now.

Everyone carries both, and the ratio changes.

My own need has exceeded my free resource at many points. Good Lord. During one stretch, my first wife tried to kill me while psychotic because she believed I was her sociopathic father. Then [she took my son](https://youtu.be/0eOInpVYnsk?si=dbxjP76Rbk5FmjDs). Later she tried to kill my mother, while her pimp stalked my sister’s family until they moved to Canada.

I could go on and on, but the math is pretty clear by now. There wasn’t much of me left over for anybody else. So when I say someone might need to stabilize and do inner pl/ork before helping found a community, I’m not saying it from a mountaintop.

A group can only take on as much need as its members have free resource to meet. Eight people with two in a long crisis may end up spending most of their attention on emergency care. Eighty people can often spread that same need across lots of relationships and practical roles.

So whether a group can welcome somebody is partly a question of resources, not a measure of how compassionate it is. Saying yes beyond what the group can carry can hurt the people already there, and the person who was promised support.

It’s a tough paradox. Small communities need new members the most, and they can handle disruption the least. Eight people still have to grow food, fix equipment, keep the books, look after the kids, and keep the water system working. The well doesn’t care how beautiful the new member’s heart is.

That’s why early members usually need more free resource than acute need, along with practical skills and a willingness to learn.

I still don’t know how to keep all this talk about capacity from becoming a respectable way to shut out people who are disabled, poor, less articulate, unfamiliar with the language of therapy, or just inconvenient. Pretending capacity is unlimited won’t fix that. Any real process would need concrete criteria and outside review. It would also need a way to revisit a “not now” decision. Even then, that power is dangerous.

This community may not be right for you *right now* if you’re fleeing an active crisis and mostly need safety, treatment, housing or legal help. A founding group shouldn’t pretend it can replace those.

Or if you want the feeling of village life without the responsibility for running it, the upkeep, the conflicts and the care.

The same goes if you won’t look at your own behavior when jealousy or conflict comes up.

And if your need right now is way bigger than what you have to give, that says nothing final about what you’re worth. It’s about timing, and the first step might be building up support and capacity where you already are.

A humane way of taking in members should be able to say “later,” “in another role,” or “with more support,” instead of turning every limit into a flat no.
