# Stance check: section 10 rewrite ("From One Community to a Movement")

All four conflicts below are with the published version of this same section. No rewrite sentence conflicts with another part of the essay.

## Conflicts in the rewrite

**1. "Assimilation" narrowed to "merging"**
- Rewrite: "One key to this whole thing is that cooperation shouldn't quietly turn into merging."
- Essay (From One Community to a Movement › Federation Without Building Another State): "Cooperation also shouldn't quietly become assimilation. The FEC currently distinguishes full members, communities moving toward membership, and Friends that may simply remain friends."
- A reader would wrongly believe the worry is communities fusing into one body, while the essay's worry is cooperation pulling a community into membership or sameness it didn't choose; the FEC example now reads as if full membership meant merging.

**2. Honesty turned into a duty to keep records**
- Rewrite: "Even if nobody uses money internally, communities still need an honest way to keep track of exchanges and obligations."
- Essay (same subsection): "Exchange and obligations need the same honesty even if nobody uses money internally… A federation can keep purpose-specific records without pricing everything"
- A reader would wrongly believe every community is required to track its exchanges, while the essay requires honesty about which transfers create obligations and offers purpose-specific records as something a federation can keep.

**3. The cash-interface requirement softened and split in two**
- Rewrite: "If cash is used for transactions with the outside world, the same goes for grants and loans."
- Rewrite, next sentence: "Guarantees and shared enterprise commitments should be tracked separately too."
- Essay (same subsection): "If cash is used at the outside interface, grants, loans, guarantees, and shared-enterprise commitments need the same separation."
- A reader would wrongly believe cash grants and loans get the optional "can keep track … without having to put a value on them or require every exchange to be 'even'" treatment given to in-kind gifts, and that only guarantees and commitments must be kept apart; the essay requires all four kept distinct.

**4. "Which principles actually conflicted" blurred into "the principles involved"**
- Rewrite: "Store the hard case too: what happened, the principles involved, your first judgments, your final decision and rationale…"
- Essay (same subsection, last paragraph): "Keep the hard case: what happened, which principles actually conflicted, the first judgments, final reasoning, dissent, consequences, and the exact rule version."
- A reader would wrongly believe listing every principle that came up is enough, while the essay asks the record to identify which principles genuinely clashed.

## Contradictions in the published text

None found, either inside the published section 10 or between it and the rest of the essay, so the rewrite has none to carry over. The closest tensions I tested hold up:

- The gradient ("Try the different rule inside the parent group…") against "Some experiments cannot be tested cheaply… not always a fair test": the second paragraph qualifies the first, and the gradient already escalates "if it needs more independence."
- "Full fission should be one step on a gradient, not the admission price for experimentation" against "others may start communities with different values rather than waiting decades" (The Door Must Open Outward at Adulthood): section 10 only says splitting shouldn't be required in order to experiment; it never asks anyone to wait.
- "People need a similarly practical freedom between communities" against the slow, capacity-limited membership process (From Visitor to Member—Slowly; The Math of Absorption): section 10 asks that a mover's housing, status and records not fall into cracks; it promises nobody admission.
- "Solidarity is not unlimited joint liability" against the ancestral "real claim on whatever prosperity later came through you" (Lessons from the Black Tax): that passage goes on to show the modern, unlimited version consuming both sides, which supports the limit.
- The federation's "records, compliance… any dispute function" against the essay's mockery of Robert's Rules (The New Age May Dawn Suddenly) and of "the process" (One principle runs through all of them): the essay attacks procedure standing in for emotional work, and elsewhere demands processes decided in advance ("Decide the process before the name").
- "eventually somebody has to solve the boring problem of how they remain connected" against the closing "If you teach other people to build, hearing about it matters less" (What to Do This Month): the closing concerns Joel hearing about other people's projects; it says nothing against communities staying connected.
- "the federation also needs an external cash budget" against the moneyless goal (4. The container): it matches "one small collective cash boundary."

Conflicts in the rewrite: 4. Contradictions in the published text: 0.
