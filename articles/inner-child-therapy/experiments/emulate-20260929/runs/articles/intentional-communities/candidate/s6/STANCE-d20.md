# Stance check: section 6 rewrite ("Which Connections to the Technosphere Do You Keep?")

I checked every sentence in the rewrite that states a position, wish, rule, assessment or claim about how things work against the whole published essay. I found two conflicts.

## 1. The community gets a say over a member's own social media and AI use

**Rewrite:** "Let the community decide if I can use my social media, or ChatGPT? Are you kidding? I get it. I really do."

**Rewrite:** "But on the other hand, these technologies don't just affect you, they affect how you relate to the community as well."

**Essay:** "a floor the majority cannot vote away: … private thought, contact with people outside the community" (section "Founderism: The Four Questions", subsection "How My Own Model Could Become a Cult").

**What changed:** This added passage starts with "This is also one area where many modern folk will balk." It moves the design question away from the published section's frame, which is the community's connections to outside systems. Instead it becomes a question of group authority over an adult member's personal apps, and it answers the autonomy objection against the member. The Amish passage does approve of communal adoption decisions, including tools "restricted at home." But the essay never applies that to an adult's own communication tools, and it puts contact with outsiders beyond majority vote. Category: a view the essay doesn't support, and a personal choice turned into a group decision.

**A reader would wrongly believe:** Joel's model lets the community decide whether an adult member may use social media or AI tools, and he leans toward the community's side.

## 2. "Decide in advance" softened to "think through" and "consider in advance"

**Rewrite:** "These are all questions that a community should consider in advance." (Also: "The community should think through how members are going to have access to care.")

**Essay:** "It should decide in advance how members access care, what the community guarantees from common resources, what may require outside money or insurance" (section "Which Connections to the Technosphere Do You Keep?").

**Essay, elsewhere:** "Decide what requires professional or emergency care before anybody takes anything." (section "The Medicine Part, Without Pretending It Isn't There", subsection "My Ceremony Principles").

**What changed:** Here and throughout (the response to a child's report, the removal process, exit terms), the essay requires decisions to be settled before a crisis. The rewrite keeps a binding agreement only for catastrophic illness. Four questions become topics to reflect on rather than decisions to make: ordinary access to care, what the common resources guarantee, what needs outside money or insurance, and how to treat members who make different medical choices. Category: a rule softened.

**A reader would wrongly believe:** the essay only asks a community to discuss medical access ahead of time, leaving the answers open until someone is sick.
