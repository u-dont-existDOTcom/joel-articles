## Preservation units (step 2)

- **H1** "Founderism: The Four Questions" (published heading).
- **P1** every community starts from someone's initiative; examples of that initiative (land, first documents, teaching the method, first members, keeping it alive when others lose interest; "or": any of these).
- **P2** that contribution creates real influence; pretending it doesn't exist makes the influence harder to examine.
- **P3** he knows the tension personally: many methods here are his, he wrote the materials; so he can easily become the one who decides whether others "understood" them; anti-authoritarian language doesn't stop an author from enjoying authorship.
- **P4** some things help (the list follows); judge the method by observable outcomes: members flourishing and safe; conflicts resolvable; people can disagree, leave, and later tell a different story; children doing well when grown; the community materially viable; it can correct a bad prediction; it can replace the founder; if meant to spread, later projects emerge and last. Longevity, occupancy, consensus and retention aren't enough alone: high retention can mean people love it or leaving costs too much ("or"). Don't judge by fidelity to the founder's preferred interpretation.
- **P5** allow parallel implementations / fork the method; a community that changes Hearthwork and gets better results has taught him something; if every adaptation needs the founder's approval, the versions aren't really parallel.
- **P6 and P7** the four questions: can the community respectfully override the founder; replace the founder's role; revise the founder's vision; survive the founder's absence.
- **P8** then look at the practical, material power: whose name is on the deed; who controls housing, food stores, vehicles, tools and any outside bank account; who keeps the records, sets admissions, chooses reviewers, decides access to children or medicine, and can make a complaint disappear; the org chart can say horizontal while keys, passwords, titles and reputation all point to one person.
- **P9** an override is real only if it can work against the person being challenged; if the founder chooses the reviewer, holds the records, owns the land and can remove the complainant, the community can't meaningfully override the founder however many circles it holds.
- **P10** ask these about any community, teacher or method; ask them about him if people end up living on land he buys.
- **P11** also watch the daily signs: the founder grows more central over time; disagreement treated as failure; every bad result blamed on poor implementation; insider knowledge grows while public accountability shrinks; outcomes never measured; former members become morally contaminated once they leave.
- **P12** none of these proves abuse alone; several together deserve attention, before the founder explains why this situation is spiritually unique (the quip carries a point: the special-case explanation is itself a sign).
- **H2** "How My Own Model Could Become a Cult". **Caption** "I could totally start a cult. Trust me." (his, kept exactly).
- **P13** every practice in this article can be turned against the people using it.
- **P14** "Nothing suppressed, everything processed" can become compulsory disclosure; he has seen the risk in communities using ZEGG Forum (link); a group begins by inviting honesty and ends by treating privacy as resistance.
- **P15** peer counseling can become surveillance in a warm tone; medicine prerequisites can create gatekeepers with power over people at their most open; "willing to be seen" can become a demand that everybody reveal whatever the group wants to know.
- **P16** the boundary: the principles are commitments he chooses for his own practice, never powers the community gains over him.
- **P17** "Nothing suppressed" describes his relationship with his own material; it doesn't authorize anyone to extract it; members can decline to share without giving a therapeutic explanation for declining.
- **P18** medicine is optional; abstaining can't reduce membership rights; no individual or permanent clique controls admission; disagreement has to be answered as disagreement.
- **P19** "Your resistance is your wound talking" is a very efficient sentence for avoiding an argument; when a community starts diagnosing dissent instead of answering it, leave before they schedule a group process about your leaving (the quip carries the point: the diagnosing goes on even about leaving).
- **P20** a floor the majority can't vote away: bodily safety, enough food and sleep, independent medical care, private thought, contact with people outside, a way to report harm without asking the accused person's friends for permission, a usable way to leave; a majority vote doesn't make deprivation consensual.
