Appendix K: Sources and further reading

The personal accounts and the way I arrange this practice are mine. The revised teaching brings them together with body-oriented and hypnotic approaches described below. That combination is an adaptation, not a treatment validated as a whole. These notes are for readers who want to follow an idea further; you don’t need them open while practicing.

Learning, entering, and returning

Maggie Phillips and Claire Frederick, Healing the Divided Self (1995), especially pp.24–27, 45–50, and 83–90, discusses interactive hypnosis, sensory learning, self-hypnosis, and a definite return. It is a professional clinical book, not a self-help manual; its case procedures and historical claims shouldn’t be imported wholesale into solo practice. Adam Eason and Benjamin Parris’s ISH article on self-hypnosis discusses self-directed learning and its relationship to another hypnotist and recordings.

Indirect language and re-entry cues

Phillips and Frederick, pp.53–57 and 96–100, explain pacing, utilization, metaphor, and associational cues. Nimja’s hypnotic-language tutorial and trigger tutorial describe language and cueing techniques. They are technique sources, not evidence that every NLP claim is established or every example suits trauma work. The original wording and deliberately chosen re-entry sequence here are adaptations; Nimja did not write or endorse this guide.
