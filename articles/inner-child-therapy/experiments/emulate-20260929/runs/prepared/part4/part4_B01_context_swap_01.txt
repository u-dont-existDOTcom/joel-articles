Building Trust With Your Little One

Keep Your Word

So set one boundary. "What's a 'boundary'?", I might be asking. That looks like saying no to something that's costing you too much (money, time, whatever it is). Throw the moldy bread in the trash. Handle that task you've been putting off, or the tax man, or your DUIs in Maryland, even if all you do today is open the envelope. Keep one small promise to your little one. Clean your room! And if a relationship keeps making you less safe, leave it, which is not a small thing at all, I know. Your little one has had enough grown-ups who said sweet things and then did nothing, so this is how they start to believe you.

You can protect your little one from yourself too, like by repairing after an internal attack. "What's an 'internal attack'?" Calling yourself an idiot for losing your keys again, for one. Your little one was listening, so you go back and apologize to them. If they're sarcastic about it, you let it go by instead of snapping back. They don't trust you yet, and you don't ask them to. They complain about something, they're right, and you say so. All of that shows as clearly to them as the clean room did. It's proof you love them, but they can still tell the difference between being put up with and being loved.


Don’t worry if you can’t find anything that makes you feel warm and fuzzy for your little one. Locking the door, eating a meal, cancelling something on your calendar, putting your phone down when you wake up in the middle of the night — the warm feelings can come later.

Pick one thing from the above that you can do. When will you do it? What’s the first step you can take? What do you need to help you do it? If there’s a hard part, say it out loud now to practice. Afterwards, debrief. If you weren’t able to do it, what got in the way? Were you scared? Did you not know what you were going for? Did you not know how to do it? Were you too tired? Was something else in the way? If you did it, did it help? Just because you did it doesn’t mean it will help. Keep only those things that help your little one to feel safe, to feel close to you, to be more functional, or to be happy.
