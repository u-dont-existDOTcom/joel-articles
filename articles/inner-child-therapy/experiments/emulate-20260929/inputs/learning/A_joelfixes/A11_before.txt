You might notice the hook before you have any clue what part of you is doing the hooking. Or you might notice it embarrassingly late. The jaw is already tight, the stomach is already involved, half the text is written—and then you remember you're supposed to be observing yourself. Maybe forget the detective work for a minute. Child, protector, old wound... you can figure that out later. Sometimes the hand is already going toward a drink, or you've vanished into scrolling, or you're halfway through deciding what the entire relationship means. Pema Chödrön calls this tightening and urge before the story has finished forming *shenpa*.

When you catch it, look around and say you're hooked. If nobody is in danger, take a breath or three before you finish the thing you were doing. You do not need those breaths to settle the case. Maybe there's enough adult around to be nice to the kid for a second. Or enough Protector to stop something stupid. Or maybe all you've got is “not yet,” which is still more useful than sending the message from inside the reaction.
