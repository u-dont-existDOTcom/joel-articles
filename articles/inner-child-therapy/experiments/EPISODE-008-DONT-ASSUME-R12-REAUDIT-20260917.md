## Strongest findings

### 1. Heading repeats the upstream owner-final thought

`Don't Assume It's All You` substantially re-announces the owner-final sentence that all problems are not self-created and somebody may really have crossed a boundary. R12 removed the old repetitive opening sentence but left the repetition in the heading.

Disposition: **repair**. Preserve the outward-looking heading function but use a forward-moving heading realization rather than restating the just-completed thesis.

### 2. Paragraph 1 still has a mini responsible-advice arc

The lived scene is strong:

`You tell somebody they hurt you. They get upset. Somehow the conversation ends with you comforting them, and the thing you originally brought up has disappeared.`

But it is immediately followed by diagnosis-defense packaging:

`That one scene by itself doesn't tell you they're emotionally immature. Maybe they needed time, or maybe they were holding a boundary of their own. But if you keep ending up there...`

The qualification functions are source-required, but their placement turns the scene into `example -> fairness caveat -> repeated-pattern threshold` before the scene has had room to do the work.

Disposition: **repair placement/realization, not meaning**.

### 3. Paragraph 2 is comprehensively diagnostic

The Lindsay Gibson paragraph tries to explicitly discharge nearly every preservation unit at once: repeated behavior over motives, disagreement/room for experience, reflection/responsibility, cannot/will-not/cannot-now uncertainty, present reliance, revisability, promises versus behavior, and protection without collecting another injury.

The two consecutive questions are a soft checklist, and the following sequence keeps closing one responsible caveat after another. `If their behavior changes, you can change your mind about them` is especially explanatory because the time-bounded `what you can rely on now` already makes the judgment provisional.

Disposition: **repair**. Keep Gibson/link and demonstrated-capacity-over-motive function. Let multiple preservation functions ride inside fewer live sentences instead of giving each a visible sentence-sized discharge.

### 4. Paragraph 3 uses formulaic therapeutic balance

`Maybe you really did hurt them... Maybe you're also... Those can both be true.` is a classic symmetrical `two things can be true` counseling realization. The substantive thought is necessary; the packaging is not.

Disposition: **repair**. Keep coexistence of repair owed + self-abandonment and keep guilt as proof of neither wrongdoing nor manipulation, but realize it as one messy conflict rather than two matched possibilities plus synthesis.

### 5. Paragraph 4 recreates the role taxonomy without the labels

`You can notice you're upset, see what's happening between you, and decide what to do next` is the source `Witness / Protector / Guide` triad rewritten as three ordinary verbs. It removes premature terminology but keeps the listicle/taxonomic architecture.

`Then there's...`, `Sometimes they still don't.`, `Say what actually needs saying.`, and the final sentence also create a generic advice reset followed by a neat ending.

Disposition: **repair**. Preserve inside-awareness, between-us reality, and next-action functions through one continuous interaction rather than three parallel jobs. End on action-without-understanding in a way that hands naturally into the following `Write It. Don't Send It Yet.` practice.
