### Target 3 — unsent-draft exercise

Current R8 span:

> Read it again as the adult who's on the writer's side. Fix what is unfair or confusing without erasing what is true—including what you did wrong, what hurt, what you're asking for, or what needs to stop. Better doesn't necessarily mean softer. If somebody needs protection now, don't postpone that for the exercise.

The angry draft may be the first place you actually said what was wrong or what needed to stop.

### Target 4 — healing/checking tail

Current R8 span:

> Take care of whatever safety issue or practical thing is actually there. Before another round, ask whether the last one moved anything. Maybe a feeling finally became real, or you saw something that actually changed a decision. If you're asking again only because the same answer still doesn't feel certain enough, that's checking. Grief can come back to familiar ground, but repetition by itself doesn't mean the child has a new request.
>
> If it's checking, leave the thought unfinished and go back to the person, activity, or life you chose. The thought can stay there while you do that; it doesn't have to be settled first. Caring for the child doesn't require a meeting every time the mind says something.
>
> Sometimes you keep leaving these sessions calmer and full of insight while the original problem doesn't move, or you start monitoring yourself more and everyday life gets worse. If that keeps happening, stop repeating the same exercise. Keep the part that genuinely helps and meet the remaining need another way. And if the exercise itself has become something you have to finish before you can get on with the day, stop there too.

Must preserve:
- real wound and unhelpful response/checking can coexist;
- handle actual safety/practical issue;
- distinguish genuinely new feeling/understanding/decision/response from reassurance checking;
- grief may revisit familiar ground without repetition proving a new child request;
- checking-loop thought can remain unresolved while life resumes;
- no need to argue with/chase/erase the thought or wait for complete resolution;
- caring for child does not require processing every thought;
- evaluate practice by observable useful change, not calm/insight alone;
- no useful contact / more self-monitoring / worse daily life should lead to reconsideration rather than intensification;
- keep what helps and meet the need another way;
- exercise should not become a ritual required before living.

Why this span is suspicious:
It still resolves as a full evaluator rubric: movement test -> checking definition -> grief caveat -> unresolved-thought rule -> outcome evaluation -> stop/keep/replace conclusion -> ritual warning.
