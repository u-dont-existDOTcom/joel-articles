## 2. Literal output audit

### Paragraph 1

Text:

> And don't let “maybe I was triggered” turn into “therefore this was all my fault.” If you were mean, apologize. Good. Now what were you trying to say before the apology became the whole conversation? Guilt can make you drop your own point without anybody manipulating you.

Real gain: the opening genuinely inherits the upstream owner-final thought. It catches a plausible overcorrection from `they're evil` to `it was all my fault` rather than manufacturing a detached vignette.

Remaining model shape:

`correct overcorrection -> directive -> approval beat -> recovery question -> generalized diagnosis`

The final sentence is explanatory aftercare: the preceding question already places the reader back inside the swallowed grievance, then `Guilt can make...` steps above the moment and explains the mechanism.

Preservation problem: `If you were mean, apologize` is narrower than the protected function `harm requiring repair`. Harm can require repair without meanness. The candidate does not preserve the broader function elsewhere.

Verdict: **editorially improved versus R14, but not clean; preservation concern is substantive**.

### Paragraph 2

Text:

> Maybe the answer is that there wasn't much to say after all—it was one awful conversation, they needed time, or you ran into one of their boundaries. Fine. But if the thing you came to say keeps disappearing, that is no longer just one awful conversation. [Lindsay Gibson's work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) on emotional immaturity keeps coming back to what somebody repeatedly does in a relationship, not whether you've figured out why. You may never know whether they couldn't meet you, wouldn't, or just couldn't that day. Getting hurt again probably won't explain them any better.

This is the strongest failure.

Its actual skeleton is still:

`one-event caveat -> time/boundary qualification -> recurrence threshold -> Gibson/repeated behavior -> motive uncertainty -> do not require another injury`

That is substantially the rejected R13/R14 middle architecture. The first audit passed it because `revisability` and `promise vs behavior` had been moved into P3. That was the wrong unit of comparison. **The framework survived across the P2/P3 paragraph boundary.** Moving two rungs into the next paragraph is repartitioning, not architectural removal.

There is also a semantic problem in the opening. The source protects `one disagreement/time/boundary alone does not prove emotional immaturity`. R15 says `Maybe ... there wasn't much to say after all`. Grammatically, that can minimize the grievance itself rather than merely limiting what one event proves about the other person's relational capacity. Those are not the same claim.

The source also protects concrete demonstrated-capacity functions: disagreement tolerance, reflection, responsibility, and room for the reader's experience versus repeated self-abandonment. R15 compresses these mainly into `the thing you came to say keeps disappearing` and generic `what somebody repeatedly does`. That is not enough to claim complete forward preservation of the richer capacity function.

Verdict: **FAIL**.

### Paragraph 3

Text:

> If next time they actually show up differently, great—change your mind. A promise that next time will be different isn't the same thing yet. Sometimes you've already said what needed saying, or said no, and they still think you're wrong. That's the ending. Your head may hate it and keep writing another one.

This avoids R14 P3's synthetic three-step explanation spiral, but it still forms a compact guidance chain:

The protected source says that conveying information, saying no, or ending without prolonged self-defense **can count**, and that action need not wait for mutual understanding. R15 makes the ending categorical in a way the source does not.

The final sentence is a clean handoff into the private-draft exercise, but it also gives the paragraph a polished module close. The handoff function is valid; this exact realization is not protected.

Verdict: **better than R14 P3, but still over-organized and partly over-strengthened**.
