## Recovery of Your Inner Child

The safety material sits in four places, and the exercises themselves carry almost none of it.

1. **One note at the front.** An "Author's Note" headed WAIVER: the book isn't medical or psychiatric advice, and readers who use it are "prescribing for yourself". It's legal boilerplate, said once.
2. **One note on pace,** in how to use the book: read a chapter at a time, do its exercises, then go on, and don't expect to read and do everything in one sitting.
3. **One section framed as safety.** It opens "Safety is essential", whether you're working with a professional, in a group or alone, and what it means by safety is privacy: keep the work confidential, share it only with people who support you, and if you're not sure who, ask your Vulnerable Child in a dialogue.
4. **One section on choosing helpers,** near the end: support groups and therapy; ask your Vulnerable Child whether it feels safe with a therapist or group; check credentials, since unethical helpers exist and initials after a name guarantee nothing; get referrals from people you trust.

Inside the exercises, "safe" is a setting instruction ("Find a quiet, safe place"), not a caution. When an exercise could go wrong, the book doesn't add a disclaimer; it says what to do in the exercise itself, once.

## Emotional Alchemy

GPT's reading: Bennett-Goleman puts her general guidance on self-directed work, support and stopping in one separate reader's note, so the chapters don't repeat it; an exercise keeps only what applies to it (her childhood-feelings meditation says to start with a simpler issue you feel ready for). Most chapters end with an optional exercise headed "If You Want to".

## What that means for this guide

- **The general part already has a home.** Before You Try to Go Deep (Joel's own section since 2026-10-22) is the guide's note on pace and stopping: check how much of the grown-up you is here, go shallower, know where the exits are, take a break and jot down where you got to, call somebody if being alone was part of what hurt. Finding a Therapist to Borrow Adulthood and Don't Give the Inner Adult Away are the guide's section on choosing helpers, like Capacchione's last one. And Opening up honestly to the right people at the right pace is the guide's version of her confidentiality section.
- **So a later caution that says the same thing again can go,** or become a pointer back, the way Altered States Can Deepen the Therapy already does ("Remember those guards from The Urge to Escape, above? Same deal here").
- **What stays where it is:** a caution that changes what you do in that exercise or situation (don't use a photo to confirm a memory; move away from the open window and get outside support), and the sections whose subject is safety (When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child, If You Want to Kill Yourself).
- **What I wouldn't copy:** a waiver. Capacchione's is a publisher's legal note, and a blanket "this isn't medical advice" is the kind of line Joel's 95.6 answer is about.
