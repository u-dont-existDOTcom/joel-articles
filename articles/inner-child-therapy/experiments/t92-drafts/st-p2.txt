Where it really differs is spirituality. Schema therapy doesn't build it in, though the preface to its main textbook calls blending it with meditation or religious practice an important development. That's not so surprising, since it grew out of cognitive therapy ([Jeffrey Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Young_(psychologist)), who developed it, did his postdoctoral work with Aaron Beck). Some people do blend them, though. Tara Bennett-Goleman's book *Emotional Alchemy*, for one, fits a Buddhist loving-kindness wish to each schema. But a loan of love from God, like back in Ask God for a Loan, isn't in her book or in the standard method. IFS goes overboard the other way, in my opinion (more on that in the next section). Past that, a lot depends on who you're seeing.
