### Also Look Outward

You tell somebody they hurt you. They get upset, you start explaining, and somewhere in there you're comforting them while the thing you brought up quietly disappears. The first time, there are too many possible explanations for that. Maybe they needed time. Maybe they still disagree after they calm down. Maybe the thing you're furious about is actually a boundary of theirs. Then another argument happens and somehow you recognize the ending before you understand how you got there. Your experience has disappeared again. [Lindsay Gibson's work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) on emotional immaturity is useful here because it pays attention to repeated relational behavior, not just one ugly exchange.

You may not know for certain whether the other person is or could be capable of a healthier response, and neither do they. If they promise to change, that's probably as useful as their prior promises were. Think about how long it has taken you to become more healthy. Could you have done that from one moment to the next based on a promise? Sometimes we can be a better support for someone when we maintain a realistic boundary, so that we can hold high expectations, and still feel prepared for their failure to meet them.

Now make it messier: you really did hurt them. There is something real to repair, and having your own complaint doesn't cancel that. But apologies can keep growing after the thing you actually did has already been admitted. Their disappointment is still there. You still feel guilty. At some point it may be worth asking what you're apologizing for now. Is it the harm, or are you starting to give away the rest of your position because they are still unhappy with you? Some of the guilt may belong to something real without the guilt itself being a good measure of how much of the conflict is yours. It also doesn't tell you that the other person is manipulating you.

And you still may not get the conversation where everything finally lines up. Maybe they understand what you're saying and disagree anyway. Maybe they think your no is unfair. You explain it again because there is a part of you still waiting for the right explanation to make the disagreement disappear. It may not. You can stop talking while the disagreement is still there.
