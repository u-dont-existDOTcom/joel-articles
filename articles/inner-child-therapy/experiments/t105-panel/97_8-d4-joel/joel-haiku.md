The strongest signals are the combination of structure and safety boilerplate:

- **Even, tidy rhythm.** Most sentences are short, self-contained, and similar in length. Human advice usually runs more unevenly.
- **Stock escalation phrasing.** "If it gets bad... I wouldn't wait to see a psychiatrist" is the kind of generic line that shows up in many health-advice templates.
- **Hedge-then-directive voice.** "I'd count..." followed by firm instructions is a common pattern in safety-tuned writing.
- **Neatly enumerated cases.** "If... And if... Some, like benzos... Alcohol too..." lays out the situations in a clean, parallel way.
- **Boilerplate caution.** "Ask your doctor before you stop" and the warning about quitting benzos or alcohol abruptly are standard safety lines that AI models are often trained to include.
- **A quotable closer.** "Alcohol too, once your body depends on it" wraps the point in a compact fragment, which is a common AI habit.

None of this is conclusive. A pharmacist, doctor, or counselor could easily write something like this, and it's a clear, careful message. Stronger evidence would be specifics about the situation, personal phrasing, or an unpolished tone.
