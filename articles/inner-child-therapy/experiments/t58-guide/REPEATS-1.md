## 1. Repeats, in article order

**1. The Guide takes life beyond self-care** (B9, B234)

- B9, "The Chicken-and-Egg Problem": "Our lives can become small and like a never-ending therapy session"
- B234, "The Inner Guide Comes Later" (P1): "You also widen its world with art and music, and you point out beautiful things when you see them."
- Later instance: it adds what the Guide actually does (chooses what the little one learns and meets, holds standards, asks hard things at its own pace, art, music, nature, the world past your street). The point that the Guide's job is to stop life shrinking to self-care was already made in B9, so a reader may feel told that part again.
- Carries it better: B234, because it has the substance. B9 works as a preview, and its sentence is the one the section makes partly redundant.

**2. Stop pushing through when you are depleted; let the need show, or tell someone** (B14, B192, B236; B9 briefly)

- B14, "The Chicken-and-Egg Problem": "when they catch you lying down because you're wiped out, instead of pushing through one more chore"
- B192, "When to Change the Strategy": "like texting a friend that you're having a rough day instead of saying you're fine"
- B236, "The Inner Guide Comes Later" (P3): "It's forced adulthood, and it sounds like "Nope, all good!" from somebody with a fever who's cleaning the whole kitchen anyway."
- Also B9, same section as B14, in different terms (mission, not chores): "But then some people over-rely on their mission to avoid caring for themselves."
- Later instance (B236): it adds the name "forced adulthood", the cost ("nobody gets very close to the person in it"), and specific asks ("Could you pick up some soup?"). But the image of resting instead of pushing through is in B14, and the remedy of telling a trusted person you are not fine is already in B192. A reader would feel told the same thing again on those two parts.
- Carries it better: B236, the fullest version, in the section about balancing care and duty. B192's line is one example of carrying the exercise into daily life, and B14's is a one-line trust-building image.

**3. Act where it is yours to act; release what depends on others** (B123, B238, B239). Weak echo; involves P5 and P6.

- B123, "When the Spiritual Relationship Hurts": "act where it's yours to act, and let go of outcomes and other people's choices where it isn't"
- B238, "The Inner Guide Comes Later" (P5): "some things just can't move yet" and "there's no point forging ahead as if you had it"
- B239, "The Inner Guide Comes Later" (P6): "I'd spend today on the cat, since she matters more to you and you can do something about her."
- Later instances: the situation is new (everyday logistics, not a betrayal by a teacher) and they add working steps (write down what you are waiting on; choose what matters and can move today). The principle is the same one B123 gives through the Serenity Prayer, so a reader who remembers that passage may notice a faint echo.
- Carries it better: B238 and B239 for use, since they are concrete. B123 only names the principle in passing, inside a different situation.

**4. Do not just give the little one what it wants; hold limits kindly** (B156, B235)

- B156, "Make a Simple Vow": "you still go to bed, and staying kind about it shows them bedtime isn't a lack of love"
- B235, "The Inner Guide Comes Later" (P2): "Without the Guide, you can slide from looking after your little one into spoiling it the same way."
- Later instance: it adds the failure itself (indulgence passing as self-love: "Sweets and screens can pass for loving yourself, and so can getting it out of anything hard.") and names the Guide as the fix. The core, that the adult sometimes says no and that this is not unloving, was already shown in B156 (and the top image lists "limits" under the Protector). A reader may feel a partial repeat.
- Carries it better: B235 for the principle, since it sits in the Guide's section and names the failure. B156's bedtime scene is the clearer picture of holding a limit kindly.

**5. A stuck thing can sit; spend today on what matters and can move** (B238, B239). Both inside the section; involves P5 and P6.

- B238, "The Inner Guide Comes Later" (P5): "go do the next thing that matters to you and can move today"
- B239, "The Inner Guide Comes Later" (P6): "One stuck thing doesn't mean everything has to wait, either." and "The signature can sit."
- Later instance: it adds a second case (two things you can act on, so pick the one that matters more) and a last sentence with a method for a head full of mess ("put it in one sentence"). The first two sentences of B239 restate P5's conclusion about the same signature, and the cat line uses P5's own test ("matters to you" plus "can move today"). A reader would feel told the same thing again for that part.
- Carries it better: B238 states the rule where it first appears. B239's new value is its last sentence.
