## Semantic brief

The section turns outward from inner-child work and asks what the other person's actual relational behavior tells you.

One ugly exchange can be emotionally enormous without being enough to diagnose emotional immaturity. Time can change what another person is able to do in a later conversation; they may hear the reader out and still disagree or keep a boundary the reader dislikes. Lindsay Gibson's useful emphasis here is on repeated relational behavior rather than overinterpreting one dramatic interaction.

The section then has to make room for the harder case where the reader really did cause harm and owes repair. Repair should not quietly expand into abandoning everything else they believe merely because the other person remains disappointed. Guilt by itself does not settle how much of the conflict belongs to the reader, and it does not prove manipulation either.

The conversation may also end without mutual understanding. The other person can understand what the reader means and still reject it. The reader may say no, convey what matters, and stop prolonged self-defense without obtaining agreement.
