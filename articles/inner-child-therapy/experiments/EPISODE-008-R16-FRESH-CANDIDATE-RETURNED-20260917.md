### Don't Decide the Whole Relationship Mid-Argument

One awful conversation can make somebody look like a case study. But people get defensive, need time, disagree, or hold a boundary. None of that by itself tells you you're dealing with an emotionally immature person. [Lindsay Gibson's work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) can be useful here, but mostly when you stop asking what this one conversation proves and start noticing what keeps happening between you.

You may not know for certain whether the other person is or could be capable of a healthier response, and neither do they. If they promise to change, that's probably as useful as their prior promises were. Think about how long it has taken you to become more healthy. Could you have done that from one moment to the next based on a promise? Sometimes we can be a better support for someone when we maintain a realistic boundary, so that we can hold high expectations, and still feel prepared for their failure to meet them.

Then there's the much messier version where you really did hurt them. You may owe them an apology or some kind of repair, while also noticing that you're about to abandon your own position because you can't stand their disappointment. Guilt can tell you that you hurt somebody. It can't settle every other question in the relationship, and it doesn't prove you're being manipulated either.

Sometimes you've said what mattered, apologized for what was actually yours, and the other person still doesn't understand you. You don't necessarily have to keep defending yourself until they do. You can say no. You can stop repeating the same explanation.
