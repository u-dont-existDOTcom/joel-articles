## Overall judgment

**There are useful edits here, but I would not approve the batch wholesale.** I favor ten of the thirteen proposed cuts. The more serious problems are in some of the additions and in the new test for whether a passage has enough of your point of view.

A judgment, a distinction, an explanation or a practical suggestion can express your point of view without a personal anecdote. Requiring a story behind every instruction would make the article longer and more self-referential. Conversely, changing every “I’d” into an imperative would make it sound more like the course manual you are trying to get away from.

The most important repairs are **S1/P8** (listening and unclear references), **S3** (a qualification separated from the advice it limits), **C2** (the difference between a feeling and an intention), **P2** (an unclear instruction), and **P9** (a proposed decision rule that goes beyond the supplied source). Keep **C1** and **C13**: their usefulness is not exhausted by whether they contain a new fact.

### How to use the recommendations

“Approve” below means **I recommend that Joel approve the specified proposal**. It does not record that he has done so. “Reject” refers to the proposed change, not the underlying passage. “Revise” means the concern is worth addressing, but Claude’s proposed solution needs changing. “Pending” identifies an actual missing fact or permission.

The pasted export sometimes joins deleted and inserted words: for example, “I’d keep Keep” and “child.child.” I have not treated those as proof that the article itself contains the errors. Where the intended replacement is apparent, I assess that replacement. **P4 is different: its two versions are identical in the supplied copy, so the proposed change is not visible.**

## Q1 — “What people most often say helped…”

**Recommendation: Keep a narrowly framed provenance question pending.**

> What people most often say helped is letting their defenses down.
