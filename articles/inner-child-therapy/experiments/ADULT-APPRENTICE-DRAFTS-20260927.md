## Source

- **S1.** The borrowed adult should stand beside you rather than replace you. The movement is: **receive care → observe care → participate in care → initiate care → internalize care**.
- **S2.** After a therapist, peer, friend, or imagined figure helps, ask:
  - What did they do that felt warm, protective, or wise?
  - Which five percent of that can I do myself?
  - What action will prove that some of this capacity now belongs to me?
- **S3.** The helper gradually hands the role back. Otherwise borrowed adulthood becomes another authority dependency, and the person remains the child waiting for the good parent to arrive from outside.
- **S4.** Keep the borrowing narrow. Somebody can model warmth without becoming an authority over your memories, relationships, medicine, spiritual life, or future. Feeling safe with a person in one moment does not make everything they believe wise.
- **S5.** There is no universal timeline. Look for ordinary signs that the capacity is becoming yours:
  - you can do one protective act without the helper present;
  - disagree with them without losing your ground;
  - recover after missing a practice;
  - ask for help without handing over judgment;
  - and leave a relationship or method that starts demanding obedience.

  Don't wait until you feel completely adult. Once you can carry five percent, test that five percent in daily life.
- **S6.** If the word *parent* summons the wrong archetype, use safe adult, guardian, advocate, older sibling, wise aunt, good host, captain, or future self. The label matters less than the functions: warmth, protection, and direction.
