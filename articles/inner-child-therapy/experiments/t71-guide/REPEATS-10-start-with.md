# Repeats and contradictions: "Start With Whatever Showed Up" (B170 to B207)

Scope: the section's own paragraphs (B171 to B180) plus its four ### subsections (B181 to B207), under the same ## heading. [sub] marks items touching only the subsections. All quotes are exact.

Counts: 11 repeats (8 touch B171 to B180, 3 are [sub] only), 2 contradictions (both mild), 5 callback groups.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

**1. B13 (The Chicken-and-Egg Problem), B156 (Make a Simple Vow), B172 (Start With Whatever Showed Up).** B13: "The child isn't pushed away". B156: "If they show up to have some innocent fun". B172: "Being the grown-up doesn't mean your little one has to leave the room." Point: the adult leads without sending the child away. B172 adds the child's gifts and the give-and-take; its opening line is told again. Better: B13 for the permission, B172 for the gifts.

**2. [sub] B14 (The Chicken-and-Egg Problem), B194 (When to Change the Strategy).** B14: "you also have to start actually doing some of it in the rest of your life". B194: "I'd try a tiny bit of it out here". Point: a session's work must carry into daily life. B194 adds a misdiagnosis and an example; the core is told again. Better: B194, since it applies the point to the stuck case.

**3. [sub] B35 (Before You Try to Go Deep), B183 (Two Common Protective Patterns), B196 (When the Urge to Escape Arrives).** B35: "You may need somebody steady there with you". B183: "a sudden pull to do anything but feel it". B196: "the urge to get away from what's coming up gets a lot bigger" and "I'd get someone steady with you". Point: the pull to escape means you are near pain; slow down, bring someone steady. B196 adds examples and "thank whatever part"; description and advice are told again. Better: B183 describes it best, B35 holds the advice.

**4. B48 (You Don't Need an Inner Monologue), B176 and B177 (Start With Whatever Showed Up).** B48: "nothing “answers” and you just know what would be nice to do for yourself". B176: "If nothing comes, that's fine for now." B177: "it could come in different forms, like as a voice or just a hunch". Point: an inner answer may not come, or may not be a voice, and that is fine. B176 adds noticing the trigger, B177 rules for acting; the reassurance is told again. Better: B48, earlier and cleaner.

**5. B63 (Catch the Hook Before the Story Takes Over), B179 and B180 (Start With Whatever Showed Up).** B63: "if you don't want to be touched, say no now and work out what it means later" and "might be a medical warning sign: step away or get checked first". B179: "If it might be real danger, though, I'd get some distance first" and "Same with something a doctor should look at." B180: "If a part hesitates about being touched or about sex, that's enough to stop." Point: with touch, danger or a medical sign, act first and interpret later. B179 adds "go see" for safe cases, B180 adds sex; the same three cases are told again. Better: B63, where signals are weighed.

**6. Better: B120 carries the lesson.

**7. B161 (When the Adult Voice Feels Fake), B173 (Start With Whatever Showed Up, same # section).** B161: "some voice says this is silly, and it's hard to tell which part of you is saying it". B173: "go looking for your little one and get mad instead, or realize you've been staring at the rug". Point: reaching for the little one brings up something else, and you can't tell what. B173 adds the next move and a list of sources; the opening scenario is told again. Better: B173, which gives the way forward.

B176: "I'm not going to force my way past you. What's going on?" B185: "What are you afraid would happen if you stepped back?" B186: "you might get exactly the performance it was afraid of". **10. B178 and B179 (Start With Whatever Showed Up, adjacent).** B178: "Have hunches like this one been right before?" B179: "It's probably been wrong before too, and you know some of those times." ## 2. Contradictions

**1. B174 and B179 (Start With Whatever Showed Up), mild.** B174: "A worry that just keeps asking to be reassured can go the same way, unless it's your little one asking." (let it go as "just thinking"). B179: "Anxiety deserves the same kind of listening." One says let a recurring worry go, the other hear it out and test it. B174 separates a fear that "keeps turning up to tell you something", but B179 never says which kind anxiety is. B63's "throwing everything transient away" pulls toward B179.

**2. [sub] B200 (When the Urge to Escape Arrives) and B314 (Altered States Can Deepen the Therapy), mild.** B200: "Paranoia during a rough trip is a warning sign for psychosis." B314: "But feeling attacked by a demon on a psychedelic is not necessarily paranoia." B314 adds "those caution signs still count" but not how to tell the two apart.

Nothing contradicts B172 or B178.
