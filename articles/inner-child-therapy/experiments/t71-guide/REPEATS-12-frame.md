# Repeats check: B169

Scope: the target is B169, the last paragraph of the opening of When the Adult Voice Feels Fake ("What if the inner-child idea just isn't yours?"). It is a single block. Its sentences don't repeat or contradict each other: the permission, the instruction to drop the language, and the reassurance are three separate steps. Everything below pairs B169 with blocks elsewhere. No deliberate callbacks involve B169.

## 1. Repeats

**1. B10, B169, B248: the vocabulary is optional; the care is what counts**

- B10 (The Chicken-and-Egg Problem): "pick another one. Your future self might work, or the captain of a ship, so long as they'd do those three jobs."
- B169 (When the Adult Voice Feels Fake, opening): "I'd let the language go then, and stop trying to sneak it back in. Looking after yourself works in any words"
- B248 (The Inner Guide Comes Later): "By the way, this job doesn't have to be called the Guide. The Wise One works, or your Inner Teacher"
- Adds or same: B169 adds something. B10 swaps the word "parent" and keeps the three jobs; B169 drops the inner-child framing altogether. B248 adds names for the Guide and the caution that "Guru" can be a trigger word for cult survivors, but the permission itself is the same one the reader has already had.
- Better carrier: B10, because it sits right where the three jobs are introduced, which is where a reader first meets the words.

**2. B169, B197: deciding this approach isn't for you is a legitimate answer**

- B169 (When the Adult Voice Feels Fake, opening): "What if the inner-child idea just isn't yours? Some people find that out right here, and it's a real answer."
- B197 (When to Change the Strategy, in the same top-level section): "If it still says no, that's your answer."
- Adds or same: B197 adds something. It has a condition B169 lacks (if the "nope" sounds like a part trying to protect you, hear it out first). It also says to stop sooner if things are getting worse, and it allows for "a unique case these steps don't match yet," with next steps (ask the author or a therapist). B197 treats the mismatch as possibly temporary ("yet"); B169 treats it as settled ("stop trying to sneak it back in"). Even so, the reader gets the same permission twice in one section, in nearly the same words ("a real answer" / "your answer").
- Better carrier: B197, because it comes after the protective parts have been introduced, so it can include the check that a protective "nope" gets heard first. B169 gives the permission before that check exists.

## 2. Contradictions

**1. B169, B337: opting out of the inner-child frame is respected in one place and mocked in the other**

- B169 (When the Adult Voice Feels Fake, opening): "Some people find that out right here, and it's a real answer."
- B337 (Non-altered states can feel like this too, under Altered States Can Require Reparenting): "But you know what’s even sillier? Wandering the world aimlessly and getting manipulated by all kinds of evil forces."
- The pull: B169 accepts deciding the idea isn't yours and says looking after yourself works in any words. B337, right after B336's line that algorithms will "turn your life into hell" without the inner parent, treats turning away from reparenting as the sillier choice, with manipulation as the cost. Neither passage explains how someone who tried it and found it isn't theirs differs from someone who dismisses it as silly. B169's "any words" could be read as bridging the two, but B337 is aimed at reparenting itself, not at what it's called.
- More natural place: B169, because it's where the reader is actually weighing whether the idea fits. B337 is a closing remark to the demon material.
