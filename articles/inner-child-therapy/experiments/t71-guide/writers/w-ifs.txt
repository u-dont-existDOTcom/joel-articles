Internal Family Systems (IFS) overlaps with this method. It recognizes protective parts and teaches people to approach them without shame or fighting, and "meet the protector first" fits with that. But my reparenting method diverges from IFS at an important point. In standard IFS, the healer is the Self, a calm and compassionate inner source assumed to be available once your parts "unblend" from it. I emphasize the inner adult you build and practice occupying: the Nurturer, the Protector and the Guide (also called the Leader or Guru). In case that sounds like three more parts moving in, it's one grown-up doing three jobs, as we discussed in The Chicken-and-Egg Problem.

Borrowed adulthood is where that difference gets sharper. When you can't reach that calm inner source, you can borrow one adult job and take it in gradually. It might come from a relationship or an image. Or from a plan, or something you do. I have no use for another neo-Advaita, [spiritual-bypassing](LINK) claim that a perfect Higher Self is already waiting underneath everything. Borrow Hindsight touched on this too: no perfect Higher Self is handing down answers. A grown-up your little one can trust gets built through care, boundaries, action and direction.

The IFS map keeps expanding, too: managers, firefighters, exiles, polarized parts, legacy burdens, Self-energy, guides, new subparts. It can become a maze. After a while you might not know if you're talking to a manager or a subpart of one. And when a framework becomes an institution, it doesn't automatically get clearer or safer, and it doesn't automatically get any truer. Some practitioners use the model gently, and others seem to deepen the fragmentation while trying to resolve it.

Before choosing a therapist, get to know the strengths and pitfalls of IFS and reparenting. Could an IFS therapist help? They may, if they're skilled and grounded. You'd want one who's trauma-informed and doesn't put ideas in your head, especially one who understands dissociation. For your little one's pain, or for shame, a good reparenting therapist may fit better. The same goes for attachment wounds or abandoning yourself, or not having an inner adult you can count on.
