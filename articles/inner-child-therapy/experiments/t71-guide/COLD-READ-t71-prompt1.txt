### When the Urge to Escape Arrives

Sometimes the urge to get away from what's coming up gets a lot bigger. All of a sudden you need a drink, or that ex doesn't seem so bad after all. Some people start a whole new business. If that's you, it might mean you got close to something that really hurts, and that can be progress, honestly! I'd thank whatever part of you came up with the escape plan, and slow down. If it's more than you can hold right now, I'd get someone steady with you.

So why not just blast right past that part, with intensive meditation or psychedelics? Both can get you past the guards before there's a grown-up big enough to hold whatever comes through, so I'd hold off until there's enough Nurturer and Protector around. How much is enough depends on the drug. Like MDMA, San Pedro is more protective and nurturing than many other drugs, especially at lower doses, so you don't need as much Nurturer and Protector built up for it, though you still need some. I already had some from my good childhood to draw on. The first time I took San Pedro cactus, at a medium-low dose, it made me super emotional, and I cried a lot. It was very therapeutic, actually. But I still squandered that opportunity: with a better understanding of reparenting, it could have been much more therapeutic.

Ayahuasca was often much more challenging, because at first I was taking huge doses for ego death. The ayahuasca by itself wasn't exactly spiritual bypassing for me, though. It gave me a connection to divinity, and inner-child reparenting does work best with a little of that. But then I squandered the opportunity to bring that love down from the heavens to my inner child, which led me to be more like a preacher than a fully well-rounded individual who could lead by example.

All kinds of psychoactive drugs can mess with your sense of what's real, and that's often the point of them. But in general, try to select the healthiest ones and use slow and gentle protocols. I'd say one of the most abused psychedelics (aside from ketamine, if you call that a psychedelic) is 5-MeO-DMT. I have a [guide on that](http://bufo.u-dont-exist.com/). People very often believe you need a huge dose of it for a "full release." They go through all kinds of screaming and shaking, nearly killing themselves, with absolutely zero inner-child work being done. It's all spiritual bypassing.

Your own drug history matters too. If you go manic, psychotic (by which I mean losing your sense of reality to the point of posing a risk to yourself or others), get super paranoid, or end up worse afterward than before, those are signs to reconsider trying the same thing again, and try to figure out why that happened. I'd look at the drug and the dosage, as well as mindset and setting etc. Paranoia during a rough trip is a warning sign for psychosis. Some drugs are known for causing paranoia, like THC, so I'd avoid those. If you can't clearly understand what happened yourself, get someone qualified to help. And if you have an idea of what happened but aren't sure, ask someone who knows you well.

I went psychotic once on ayahuasca made with pure THH (an alkaloid from the ayahuasca vine) and mimosa hostilis bark, which has DMT in it. THH alone isn't hypnotic (trance-like) enough to keep people safe with higher doses of DMT. When you're in a trance, you can't freak out: no matter how scary something is, it's just like, "Oh, this is scary, OK, oh well." That's not a good excuse to take crazy doses tho. I didn't need anyone to explain that to me, because it was obvious afterward, although it was very helpful that my landlord, a Quaker family friend, was super understanding and talked me down gently. Otherwise the cops might have been called, and I could still be in jail, perhaps, since ayahuasca was illegal there at the time, not to mention I had a bunch of mimosa bark in the house that they might have claimed was for distributing. In general, don't call the cops and expect it to go well. That's a real last resort.

Out of hundreds of times I've taken iboga, there was one time just a small amount of male iboga root bark got me a bit hypomanic, to the point where a thought came: "Everyone should listen to me!" Luckily I was trained in Buddhist meditation, so I immediately challenged it with "Why should anyone listen if they have no interest?" I stopped using iboga for a while after that, and I warn people about that potential issue with [male iboga](http://iboga.u-dont-exist.com/) specifically.

Any of those reactions, from mania to paranoia, can also point to a vulnerability in you, so I'd be careful with other drugs that mess with what's real too, though I don't mean every drug's dangerous for you. And any predisposition toward mania or psychosis is a warning sign too, whether it's family history, a bad environment or a bad mindset. I can't tell you exactly what to do about it, and nobody can tell you for sure what's best for you. There are just caution signs, and when you see them, you take more precautions of every kind. You have to figure out what they mean for you, and if you can't, get help from trusted advisors.

By trusted, I mean someone you know is trustworthy from a long relationship with them, not someone people on Reddit said was super magical and healing. That's called a crapshoot. It might turn out well, it might not. It might seem like it did, and then later you realize you were duped, as happened to me with [Sidney the iboga shaman](http://sidney.u-dont-exist.com/). A new therapist is not as trusted as an old one. As they say, "new friends are silver, but old friends are gold."

Milder reactions afterward count as caution signs too, like getting confused or spaced out (dissociating) again and again. Get professional help right away if any of this is severe or risky, and especially if it keeps going or comes back while you're sober. If you can't tell whether you're safe, I'd treat that as risky. Don't suddenly quit a prescribed medicine, or anything your body is dependent on, just because it might be part of the problem; have a doctor help you stop safely.

Be careful what you mix, too, and not just prescribed medicines. Even some basic things like cinnamon can be dangerous (and helpful) with ayahuasca, and doctors don't know about a lot of this. I also learned that mixing acacia confusa with mimosa hostilis is very dangerous. I'd never recommend anyone risk it, since it's highly destabilizing and challenging. I have a [list of things to be cautious about with ayahuasca](http://ayasafety.u-dont-exist.com/) in particular.

## “And So That’s the Way It Happened!”

My ex, Xandria, taught me to append “And so that’s the way it happened!” to a difficult memory. Say it the way a grandpa would finish a story on the porch. ("Once upon a time…" helps if the start is where you get stuck.) Somehow it moves the whole thing out of the courtroom, where the case was still being argued, and into your life story. Pick a memory you can handle for the first few tries.

Does saying it make the memory fiction? Well, no, it's still what happened, as far as you remember it, even if some details turn out fuzzy. And nobody's off the hook (you included, if you were the one who did it): whatever repair is owed is still owed. If the line helps, keep it to the one line.

Do keep an ear on who's narrating, though. If the voice starts sneering, like it's making fun of the younger you, stop right there (that's the courtroom creeping back in). Same if you feel more ashamed, or kind of floaty and unreal, since more distance won't help with that. And when it goes well, it's a good time to check in with the younger you in that story. What do they need from you now?

## Altered States Can Require Reparenting

Have you ever been attacked by a literal demon? Maybe during sleep paralysis or meditation or on a psychedelic trip? A lot of modern folk laugh at this, yet it’s really quite a common experience. I was being psychically attacked by ghosts in my home as a child. Because I was literally a child, I was an easy target. And since most people never actually grew up, they are easy targets for every kind of influence, including spirit influence.

Those ghosts knew my weakness: dares. So they would dare me to jump out the window. I wasn’t suicidal, but I hated to seem like a “wussy.” Luckily, I trusted my parents with this information, and instead of taking me to a psychiatrist and pumping me full of anti-psychotic drugs, they put bars on the windows. Many kids are not so lucky in modern societies. They may get pumped full of anti-psychotic drugs for the rest of their life once diagnosed. That said, professional assessment may be warranted in some situations. I still played around with half-hanging out the windows, but I wasn’t quite dumb enough to jump out, and it meant something that my parents took it seriously.

As a semi-adult, age 26, I discovered meditation. After I began meditating deeply for a while, it seemed like my astral portal opened. I started getting attacked by spirits during sleep paralysis. They would sit on me, prevent me from breathing, and instill tremendous fear into the core of my being.

[an image: a dark black-and-white drawing of a gaunt, long-limbed creature with long stringy hair crouched over a person asleep in bed, its face right over theirs, by a curtained window; the guide's alt text is "Jinn Al Kaboos – that attacks you while sleeping"]

Yep. Exactly like that.

A trance like that can be "more scary" because you're trapped in it, but it's also less actually dangerous. You can't ruin your life at all in trance (unless a bad hypnotic suggestion gets deeply implanted, perhaps), while without trance you can "freak out" in a way that might end up getting you committed to a mental hospital, thrown out of your housing, going to jail, possibly even killing yourself. In trance you can basically work with the fear. You recognize it has no real world significance and it's purely just fear, so it's super useful that way.

After multiple episodes of this I decided to stop giving in to the victim mentality, and to start taking it seriously as a part of spiritual practice. My inner guide saw it as a perfect opportunity to demonstrate the power of love over fear. The next time it happened, despite how scary it was, I sent unconditional love to this demon on me. It took some minutes, but once that love was seen as enduring, the demon left, and it never returned.

I’ve also trained other people to do this during spiritual attack while on psychedelics. It works a lot better if you are already trained in [loving-kindness meditation](https://love.u-dont-exist.com/) to the point where it’s natural. I’d say it works way better than exorcism techniques (like [Julian Palmer’s torping](https://julianpalmerism.com/primer-malefic-entities/), which he also admits doesn’t work as well as love).

The astral plane is a bit different from the 3D. In the astral realm, unconditional love is a true, ultimately powerful physical force. Like a river, or a moat. There’s no evil that can trespass that moat. But don’t assume it will always work like that in the physical plane. It definitely has worked for me sometimes when facing some thugs, but [definitely not with others](https://defense.u-dont-exist.com/). Loving someone does not mean you leave the front door unlocked with a sign saying “take whatever you want.”

If you’re not able yet (or in the moment) to summon real love for an enemy, you can first send love to your frightened little one from your adult Nurturer. Many people also find they can borrow support from God, Jesus, angels, devas, or whatever loving spiritual presence fits their tradition. If I were to do that, I would still be framing it as “Please help this situation with love” rather than “Strike down the enemy!” But if a presence seems to enjoy your fear, it's not one of your grown-ups, borrowed or not, whatever it says it is. Same goes if it dares you to hurt or take advantage of anybody, yourself included.

It's basically borrowed adulthood. At first you may need to feel protected or loved by something bigger than you, before that love starts to feel like yours, and then bit by bit it becomes your own. Send some of it down to your frightened little one, and when that's possible, toward whatever you're afraid of. Continuing to relate to benevolent spiritual powers/beings is still perfectly fine, but they would surely be happy if they rubbed off on you!

If a voice or urge is directing you towards harm, and you feel at risk of acting on it, as with myself and those ghosts at home, move away from that open window and get some outside support. Know the limit of what you can handle, but don't turn it into a prison for life.

#### Non-altered states can feel like this too

Even if you’ve never literally been attacked by a demon that looked like the one above, the truth is that in this world, we’re always being manipulated and attacked by whatever evil force sees us as an easy target. Even social media algorithms will turn your life into hell if you don’t have the loving, protective inner parent developed. When you do, social media actually becomes useful and feels nice to interact with now and then.

Many people think reparenting sounds silly, or like the kind of ridiculous therapy people do when they have too much time or money. But you know what’s even sillier? Wandering the world aimlessly and getting manipulated by all kinds of evil forces. 💀
