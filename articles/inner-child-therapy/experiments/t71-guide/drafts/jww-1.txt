Get a notebook and let your two hands take turns. The one you don't usually write with is for your little one, so give it the vulnerable stuff, whatever you're thinking or feeling that's hard to show. It's slow going, and the letters already look like a child's. You might look down at the page and think, huh, that's my writing from when I was little. Then switch to your usual hand and write back as the grown-up you: the Nurturer, the Protector and the Guide. Some people get only silence when they talk to their little one or try to picture them, and writing it out like this can get past that silence. If a protective part shows up instead of your little one, it gets the clumsy hand first.

If thinking things through on paper starts to leave you more confused about what's going on, you can switch to a memory aid instead. Write the date and a line or two about what happened, or a note about what helped. A reminder works too, or the next step you're planning. Or skip the pen and leave yourself a voice note, which hardly looks like journaling at all. Each of these keeps the thread of what's been happening, and you don't have to work out what it means at the same time. A memory aid doesn't need to explain your life. Looking for patterns can come later, if that turns out to be useful.
