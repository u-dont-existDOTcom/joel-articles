Why would kindness feel fake, or even scary? For people who are hard on themselves, a first try at compassion imagery "can be frightening," according to [one study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25347984/). In [another](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31301164/), people with anxious or avoidant attachment reacted to it like a threat, and a ten-minute exercise in feeling secure helped with the next try. So feeling fake could mean you need a bridge, more than a bigger performance of love. That doesn't mean picturing a loving parent can't work. A [small pilot](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5700488/) of the Ideal Parent Figure method found adults with complex PTSD from childhood trauma doing better after five weeks, and still better eight months later, though with no comparison group it doesn't prove the whole method.
