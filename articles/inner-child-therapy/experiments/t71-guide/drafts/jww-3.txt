For this one, your usual hand belongs to the grown-up you, and the hand you don't write with belongs to your little one. It's a way some people get past the silence they run into when they talk to their little one or try to picture them. Let the clumsy hand put down the vulnerable thoughts and feelings, slowly, in letters that already look like a child's. If a protective part shows up, though, it gets that hand before your little one does. Then you might sit a moment with the pen back in your good hand, wondering what the grown-up you would say to that. Write back as that grown-up.

Some entries can be as short as the date and a few words about what happened. Those are for when journaling to make sense of things starts making them more confusing. A note of what helped works too. So does a reminder. Or write down the next step. That way you don't lose track of what's been happening. And what does it all mean? That part can wait, and you can look for patterns later if it turns out to be useful. A memory aid doesn't need to explain your life. You could also leave yourself a voice note, if you'd rather say it out loud.
