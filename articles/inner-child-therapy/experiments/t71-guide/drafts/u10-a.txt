Some of the research points that way too. When people who are hard on themselves try compassion imagery for the first time, it "can be frightening," as [one study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25347984/) puts it, and in [another](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31301164/), people with anxious or avoidant attachment reacted to it like a threat until a ten-minute exercise in feeling secure helped. So feeling fake could mean you need a bridge, more than a bigger performance of love. Picturing ideal parents can help, too. In a [small pilot](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5700488/) of the Ideal Parent Figure method, adults with complex PTSD from childhood trauma were doing better after five weeks, and still were eight months later. Nobody was compared against them, though, so none of this proves the whole method.
