### Also Look Outward

One awful conversation can leave you pretty certain you've finally figured somebody out. Then they come back later and actually deal with what you raised. They may still disagree with you, and you may still hate the boundary they're holding. One fight can be awful without being the whole pattern. [Lindsay Gibson's work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) on emotional immaturity looks at what keeps happening across different conversations.

You may not know for certain whether the other person is or could be capable of a healthier response, and neither do they. If they promise to change, that's probably as useful as their prior promises were. Think about how long it has taken you to become more healthy. Could you have done that from one moment to the next based on a promise? Sometimes we can be a better support for someone when we maintain a realistic boundary, so that we can hold high expectations, and still feel prepared for their failure to meet them.

If you really did hurt them, there is something to repair. You meant to own what you actually did, and before long you're wondering whether you were allowed to have the complaint at all because they're still upset. But if you're trying to work out how much of the argument was actually yours or whether they're manipulating you, feeling guilty is pretty poor evidence.

Sometimes they still don't understand what you're saying, even after you've said what you meant and said no. You may have to leave the misunderstanding there instead of defending yourself until it disappears.
