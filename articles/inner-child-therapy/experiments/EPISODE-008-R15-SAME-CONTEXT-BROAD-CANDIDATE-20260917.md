### Look at What Actually Keeps Happening

Sometimes you realize halfway through an argument that you're explaining the same hurt again, except now you're comforting them and your original point has disappeared. One time doesn't tell you much. Someone can need time, disagree, or have a boundary of their own without being emotionally immature. But if this is what keeps happening—your experience disappears when things get uncomfortable and nobody ever comes back to it—that's not the same thing. [Lindsay Gibson's work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) on emotional immaturity is useful here because motives are the part you may never get to know.

You can spend ages wondering whether they couldn't meet you there, wouldn't, or just couldn't that day. I don't know how you're supposed to know. Meanwhile, you still have to live with what they actually do. If that changes later, fine, change your mind then. Promises are lovely, but they aren't the change, and you don't need one more injury before taking the pattern seriously. And then, annoyingly, maybe you did hurt them too. There may really be an apology in here. Give it. Just notice if apologizing for your part somehow eats the entire original issue because you can't stand their disappointment. The guilty feeling doesn't tell you how much was yours, and it certainly can't tell you whether they were manipulating you.

Sometimes that's as far as the conversation gets. They still think you're wrong. You may still have said what needed saying, or simply said no. You don't have to keep defending it until they sign off. And if there's still a whole speech bouncing around inside you, there's somewhere safer to put it first.
