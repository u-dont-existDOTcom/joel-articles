## Tell taxonomy used here

These are owner-calibrated editorial signals, NOT authorship probabilities.

**A — strong AI-shaped signal** when the sentence dominantly instantiates a repeatedly observed Joel-specific failure:
- polished thesis/summary before or after the lived thought;
- predictable staircase sequencing;
- one-sentence/one-preservation-function efficiency;
- balanced concessive packaging used to reconcile obligations;
- checklist/question inventory;
- clipped validation or explanatory aftercare;
- tidy closing diagnosis that merely names what preceding prose demonstrated.

**H — strong Human-facing signal** when the sentence dominantly contains a source-grounded pattern repeatedly useful in Joel's natural/owner-final writing:
- local self-correction or change of attention;
- unresolved tension allowed to remain;
- genuine authorial judgment rather than neutral synthesis;
- uneven thought duration;
- a source-grounded metaphor/social act doing multiple jobs without explanatory aftercare;
- a short stop whose brevity is earned by the larger section.

**E — either/weak signal**:
ordinary question, imperative, short sentence, long sentence, colloquialism, conjunction, list, etc. when it does not dominate the passage enough to classify confidently.

One sentence receives one dominant class for the headline count; secondary defects/signals are recorded in notes. This is an editorial ledger, not a claim that a Human writer could never write an A sentence or a model could never write an H sentence.

## Baseline

### When Healing Turns Into Checking

You can get hooked on healing too. The wound can be completely real while you're checking it for the hundredth time to see whether you've finally grieved it correctly.

First handle whatever safety issue or practical problem is actually there. After that, if you want to go back in, I'd ask what changed the last time. Sometimes you finally feel something you'd only been thinking about, or you notice enough to make a different choice or respond differently. That's real. But grief can revisit the same ground, and the question showing up again doesn't necessarily mean the child has a new request. Sometimes you're asking again because the last answer didn't make you feel certain enough.

If it's checking, you don't have to answer it perfectly before you go back to your life. Let the thought sit there and return to the person, activity, or life you already chose. It can still feel unresolved. Caring for the child doesn't mean calling another meeting every time your mind produces a sentence.

## Realization R3 — short question beats + uncertainty

If there's a practical problem, take care of it. Then look backward before you go deeper. What changed last time? Maybe you finally felt what you had only been explaining to yourself, or you came out of it making a different choice. If neither happened and what you mostly have is the same question again, certainty may be what you're chasing. Grief is repetitive, though. The question coming back is not enough by itself to tell you that the child has something new for you.

If you think it's checking, you can put it down without proving that it is. Go back to the person you were with or the life you were in. The thought can stay messy. Caring for the child doesn't require another meeting every time it appears.

## Realization R4 — self-correction + authorial judgment + unresolved carry

After the hundredth check—have I finally grieved this correctly?—I start wondering whether the healing itself has become another hook. None of that means the wound wasn't real.

If there's something real-world to deal with, do that before turning it into more inner-child work. Then—actually, before doing another round at all—I'd look back at the last one. Did anything change? Sometimes the return is useful—you finally feel what you'd only been thinking about, or enough clicks that you make a different choice. I've got no problem with that. What worries me is when the question is back mostly because the last answer didn't feel certain enough. Grief revisits old ground anyway, which is why I don't think the question coming back, by itself, tells you the child has a new request.

If you think you're checking, maybe don't turn that into another question you have to solve. Let it sit there. Go back to the person you were with or whatever you were doing. If the thought follows you, it follows you. The child can still matter without getting called into another meeting every time your mind says something.

If these exercises start feeling like a ritual you have to complete before you're allowed to live, stop there.
