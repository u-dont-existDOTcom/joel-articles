## P1 — owner-cognition-assisted

This happens to me all the time with texts. I'll be writing a reply and, if I'm doing well, catch myself halfway through and think, wait, that's not actually the best way to put this. I'm not trying to hide that I'm upset. I'm trying to say the same thing in a way the other person has a chance of hearing. Text makes this much easier than talking, obviously, because you get another pass before you send it, and I think that's good practice for speaking too. Sometimes the rewrite is much shorter; sometimes I realize there's nothing useful to send. Owning what I did wrong is trickier when what I did wrong was mostly just not communicating with very high IQ and EQ. If I actually did something I know was wrong, that's easier for me. Being too direct is harder to apologize for, because at some level I still associate directness with honesty.

## P2 — exact owner Human/medium anchor

It doesn't mean you need to hide your upset, or apply for a permit to protest, but choose your battles and execute them wisely, so you don't end up regretting how you communicated later. As Lao Tsu might have said (if I could ever have read that whole boring Art of War book), a war with no casualties is the best possible war. When high IQ & EQ communication hits a writer's block, sometimes the best thing is to simply say, "I need some time to think about my response." And if the conflict really requires a response right now, then obviously just do your best rather than wait for the perfect timing.

## P3 — owner-cognition-assisted

And yeah, going back to the person who wrote the first version is part of it too. When my emotions really pop up, that's often basically my inner child. I don't want the adult to arrive, grab the pencil out of his hand because he wrote something embarrassing, and replace him with a more acceptable version of himself. It's more like holding his hand and showing him a better way to say what he was trying to say. He still gets to be angry, hurt, or say no. Sometimes he needs protection, reassurance, or actual help more than another edit. The adult can help with the communication without making the kid feel like he was the problem.
