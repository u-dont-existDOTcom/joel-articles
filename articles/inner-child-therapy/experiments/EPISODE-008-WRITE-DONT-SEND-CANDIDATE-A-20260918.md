## Exact candidate

### Write It. Don't Send It Yet.

Write the message you actually want to send first. Don't make it nice yet. Don't send it either.

Then read it as if the person who wrote it is somebody you're responsible for taking care of. Maybe there's a real accusation in there that needs to come out. Maybe there's also something unfair, or a paragraph that's only there because you want the other person to finally admit you were right. Fix that part. Keep the protest if the protest is valid. Keep the apology if you owe one. Keep the boundary if it's a boundary.

The edited version might be softer, but it also might just be shorter. You can send it, say less, wait, or decide there's nothing useful to send. If you tell them you'll answer tomorrow, make sure you actually mean tomorrow. And if somebody needs protecting now, don't make the exercise more important than the protection.

After that, go back and read the first version again. Not to improve it. What was happening to the person who wrote it? What were they trying so hard to get across? Angry isn't the same as childish. Sometimes the first draft is where the clearest protest showed up. You can decide not to send a word of it and still take care of whoever had to write it.
