## 1. Repeats, in article order

### 1. Practice makes the three jobs automatic (moderate)
- B13 (The Chicken-and-Egg Problem, stage 4): "after sufficient practice with reparenting, you become a natural at sending love to little you, protecting them, and directing them."

## 2. Contradictions

### C1. Warmth first, or warmth after? (minor)
- B97 [changed] (Borrow One Competency at a Time): "Most of us wouldn't open up to somebody who'd only just stopped being mean, or let them take care of us. That takes warmth"
- B130 (Not Every Hero Wears A Cape): "the relationship can start with the Protector's plain acts and the warmth can come after". It then calls the opposite expectation the mistake: "which feels backwards, because you'd think you have to feel it first".
- The pull: B97 says warmth has to come first (borrow some if you have none) before your little one will let you care for them. B130 says you can start with no warmth and let it come later, and it treats "feel it first" as the wrong assumption.
- Does the article say why? No. A reader can square the two: B97 is about the little one letting you nurture them, and B130 is about Protector acts out in life that don't need their permission. But neither passage draws that line, and B130 doesn't mention borrowing warmth as the other route.
- Which side the article backs: both. Borrowing warmth runs through Borrow Love (B101 to B120), and acting first runs through Not Every Hero Wears A Cape (B128 to B132). The article never says when to use which.
