### `Catch the Hook Before You Interpret It`

Current shape still reads as a complete protocol card.

Make the hook itself the center of gravity. Start with the recognizable moment before interpretation takes over. Pema/shenpa should arrive because it names that exact moment, not as a teaching citation inserted after a symptom list. Preserve the examples but do not arrange them as an exhaustive body-signs list followed by an exhaustive action list.

The acute sequence must survive, but realize it as one thought under pressure: notice/orient -> brief pause unless immediate safety action is needed -> do not decide truth while maximally activated -> one small adult move / prevent harm. Do not separately conclude that pausing does not invalidate the story; work that meaning into the movement.

The later meaning-making paragraph should not reopen another mini-essay. Once activation is lower, inspect what happened: present grievance vs old material can coexist; observation differs from conclusion; literal words at the time differ from words found later. Use the stomach-tightening / evil example because it carries the distinction concretely. Stop when the distinction is clear.

The endurance/safety sentence should be attached to the practical flow rather than a polished subsection close.

### `Also Look Outward`

Open with the concrete danger of using inner-child work to explain away another person's behavior. `Being triggered doesn't automatically mean you're wrong` carries this function but currently introduces a counselor checklist.

Use Gibson to shift attention from motives to repeated behavior. Keep disagreement tolerance, responsibility, room for the reader's experience, and repeated self-abandonment, but do not present them as four equal diagnostic criteria.

Keep the qualification that one bad conversation / disagreement / time / the other person's boundary do not prove immaturity, but make it brief and subordinate to the pattern question. Preserve uncertainty about can't / won't / can't-now without making it a tidy three-item taxonomy.

Guilt, repair, self-abandonment, and realistic interaction goals should flow from the same live situation rather than each becoming a mini-lesson. Do not close with a polished Witness/Protector/Guide synthesis sentence unless those roles are doing immediate practical work.

### `Keep the Draft. Edit the Response. Care for the Writer.`

This can be a direct exercise without explanatory aftercare. Write the real response; do not send it yet. Edit without betraying the valid protest/responsibility/request/boundary. Better does not necessarily mean nicer. Then return attention to the person who wrote the first draft. Avoid a menu of all possible send/wait/no-response outcomes unless the options are needed to preserve source function. Do not end by explaining that communication and inward care are `two different jobs`; let the return-to-writer step demonstrate it.

### `When More Processing Becomes the Hook`

Current problem: another full teaching card with real wound -> checking distinction -> reassurance criterion -> return to life -> outcome metric -> final ritual warning.

Preserve the distinction between real revisiting and certainty-seeking, but do not build a taxonomy around it.
