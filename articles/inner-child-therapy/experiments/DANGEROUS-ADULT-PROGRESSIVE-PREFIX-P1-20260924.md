## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child

Readiness is not only a question of how much of the grown-up is present, though. An adult can be steady, present, and fully able to choose and still be the wrong person to sit beside a frightened child, because what matters here is less the amount of capacity than what that capacity is currently for. The real question is whether, right now, some part of you wants to frighten, humiliate, use, or hurt what is vulnerable, or finds that it enjoys doing so, and that is a narrower question than it may first seem. Firm religious conviction or unusual beliefs don't answer it. Neither does an intrusive thought you hate, anger, charged language, an unwanted fantasy, or something harmful you did in the past. None of that by itself establishes current harmful intent. Where the wish to harm, or the pleasure in another's fear, is actually alive now, it can be tempting to bring the child forward for just a moment to see what happens, but that moment settles nothing. If the child shrinks back, you have learned only that the child is afraid. If something in you is gratified by the shrinking, the experiment was never a test of safety at all, and it has already become part of the harm it claimed to be checking for.
