## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child

The check above tells you whether enough of the grown-up is here to pause and make a choice. It still doesn't tell you whether that grown-up is willing to protect someone vulnerable. A person can pass the first test and still want the fear, humiliation, or leverage they get from frightening or exploiting vulnerability. If that's true right now, being calm doesn't make child work safer. It just means the adult who wants the reaction is more capable of getting it.

The tempting thing is to bring the child forward for a minute and see. But what would that prove? A frightened child proves that the child is frightened. And if part of the payoff is getting that reaction, then the test has already turned into the thing it was supposed to test for. Leave the child out and stay with the adult who's here. Maybe something in your life isn't working, or maybe you actually do want this part to change. You can look at whether this way of treating vulnerability is serving the life you want and what it's doing to the people around you. If sovereignty is the word you use for yourself, here's a concrete question: when the pull—or something you treat as an authority—says you have to follow, can you disobey it?

Also, don't confuse this with every ugly thing that can happen in a mind. A thought or feeling you hate, an unusual or religious belief, or something harmful you did before is not the same as wanting to do harm now. A promise isn't the same as safety either. The useful evidence is boring: what you keep doing over time when vulnerability is there and exploiting it would still feel rewarding. A childhood photo can still be a side door to the child. So can hypnosis or an imagined helper. Care doesn't have to stop while you work on this. Nobody has to settle the theology first or pretend the cruelty is okay; caring about you isn't the same as helping you get better at it. For some people, actual human company over time may matter more here than another exercise done alone or with a bot. Whatever brought you here is still there.
