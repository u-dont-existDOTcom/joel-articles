## Exact internal V21

> ## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child
>
> Being able to calm down enough to think clearly is one condition for inner-child work, not a guarantee that the child is safe with the adult. If a person still wants to frighten, humiliate, or exploit vulnerability—or hopes therapy will make that easier—bringing the child forward has already gone wrong. There is nothing useful to learn from watching the child's reaction; fear is not evidence the adult is entitled to collect, and it should never become gratifying. The harder question is deciding when that danger is actually there. A religious identity, an unusual belief, an intrusive thought, anger, or a harmful act in the past may all be relevant, but none settles present intent. The issue is what the person now endorses and whether they can choose against it. Someone who calls themselves sovereign while also saying they cannot disobey an urge, doctrine, group, spirit, or master has a practical contradiction to work on before any child contact: sovereignty has to include the possibility of refusing and changing course.
>
> None of this means there is nothing to work on. Often the useful work is with the present-day adult's life: what the harmful identity or allegiance is doing for them, what it is costing, whether they want something to change. A therapist or helper can stay in relationship without endorsing cruelty or becoming a consultant on how to manipulate people better. If the person later says the harmful intent is gone, that claim has to become believable in the choices that follow, especially when exploitation would still be rewarding. Until then, the child stays out of the work, directly and indirectly. Sometimes the work is better held in a sustained relationship with another person than in another internal exercise.
