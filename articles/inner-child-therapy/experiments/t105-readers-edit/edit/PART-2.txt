## Before You Try to Go Deep
How much of the grown-up you is actually here right now? Try looking around the room, pausing before you act, making a choice and following through on it. If you can't do that because you're basically the frightened kid, leave the deeper conversation until later. You may need somebody steady there with you, or to do whatever helps you come back a bit before you try to talk to the child.
And if the grown-up you is there, and it's just how deep you're going that's too much, I'd go shallower. Like the flight attendants say, it's good to know where the exits are. 😅 So can you stop in the middle and come back to the room? If you can't, or going in at all keeps making it worse, I'd come all the way back out first. Some people try one technique after another instead, to prove to themselves they can take it. Your little one may have had to just take it back then, though. Now there's a grown-up around who can say, "Okay, that's enough for today," and try something gentler, like saying hi to them with your eyes open.
If you need a break from the communion, simply jot down the reason and the progress made so far. Then come back later, once that reason has changed and you feel comfortable.
If being alone was a big part of what hurt during your childhood, I'd call somebody now, mess and all, since reaching toward people is part of taking care of your little one too. Even if you don't have someone you know who wants to listen, there are warmlines for this.
## Your Body Might Need Some Love First
If you notice tension in your solar plexus or abdomen, or anywhere else that seems to be keeping you from relaxing into that heart chakra love, try some non-forced, slower exhales and a little gentle self-massage. I love using one foot to massage the other foot like a yin-yang symbol, but you can start with some nice circular or spiral massage right on the tense spot.
If that's not enough, there's deeper somatic stuff like Shaking, tapping (EFT), EMDR, Brainspotting, Somatic Experiencing, Water Therapy, Massage, Energy Healing, or some other bottom-up approach.
When your nervous system is flooded, your protective parts are busy throwing buckets of water out of the boat, so they’re not going to let your little one come out and talk much anyway. Some people go straight into EMDR to work on desensitizing their worst memory before they can even stay in the room. Some somatic work can feel pleasant, and some can feel like a raging storm got unleashed. It can bring up grief and fear, and sometimes nightmares later on as the memories begin to unwind. It’s a bit like going to the gym. You want to make gains, and sore muscles the next day can be good, but proper range of (e)motion here is also key to avoid injury.
Keep track of how you feel over the next few days, like how refreshing your sleep is, and how easy it feels to get through your day. Do you want to go back toward what came up, or is some part of you saying no? If you can’t quite tell yet, I’d just make the next one a bit easier, one change at a time, like a science nerd, so you learn what made the difference. You can play around with variables like shorter session length, or shallower diving, time of day, keeping eyes open, having a friend around, or holding a teddy bear as a stand-in for your little one.
And what about those hard dreams, or images that pop up during a session? They can show what feels important or frightening right now. Sometimes they show what feels missing, or what feels needed. They aren't evidence that what you saw in them happened, though, and shouldn't be used to confirm a memory or rebuild one. Or to suggest one.
## You Don’t Need an Inner Monologue
If you don't hear a little kid talking in your head, don't invent one. Actually, this is one place where the usual inner-child prompt to “ask your little one what they need” can get confusing, because “ask” sounds like you're supposed to ask a question and then wait for a second voice to answer. Maybe that's what happens for some people, but you might not be some people (hehe). If talking into an empty room feels silly, small figures on a table can help, like one for your little one and one for whatever else shows up. Put whichever one's running the show on a throne. Both ideas come from sections 12.2 and 13.1 of a book called Schema Therapy for Children and Adolescents, which I'll call ST-CA.
You might barely have spontaneous words in your mind and yet still be able to think a sentence on purpose. Try it and see. If words are there, speak or write them. You might feel more comfortable that way than just staying in imagination land.  And if that starts feeling like homework you're doing for a child who isn't answering, drop it. Maybe what's actually requiring your attention now is a knot in your stomach. Or perhaps you're in a place you want to leave. Sometimes nothing “answers” and you just know what would be nice to do for yourself. And if you don't know, it's fine to guess. Maybe it's closeness, or maybe it's more say in your own life. A lot of us got plenty of one thing growing up and almost none of another.
And it might not be the same order all the time. You could have a feeling before thoughts, or thoughts might come first and provoke a feeling. They say hindsight is 20/20, but you actually might not even know which happened first.
## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child
I've been writing this whole guide as if everybody who tries it wants to take care of the inner child. Some people get a little rush out of making somebody scared or small, though. For such folks, inner child therapy is clearly not the place to start. They need to first deconstruct the adult identity they've got.
When I talk with folks like that, I mostly want to know what the identity gets them and what it costs, and especially what it's really serving. Some of them even call themselves sovereign, and then somehow there's an urge they have to obey, which sounds more like slavery to me.
This part tends to scare the wrong people, like somebody who's horrified by their own thoughts. Everybody gets scary thoughts they'd rather not have sometimes, and they don't make you a bad person, but why would you bring your inner child into the middle of them? The nice thing about inner child therapy is that you can wait until you're calm and actually ready. A real parent doesn't get to do that, which is one reason nobody manages to be a perfect one.
And if somebody actually wonders whether they're malicious, they'd do better to meditate on it and find out why they're asking before they bring their inner child anywhere near it. Are they really feeling malicious, or is it some voice in their head saying so, like the inherited critic? If some of it turns out to be malice they actually want to act on, the inner child can wait.
In one of the Buddha's own accounts of how he woke up, ill will shows up in his mind and he sees it's hurting him. If he had to figure that out by watching, it's not obvious. Ill will can make you feel powerful. Being on fire would too, and people would definitely get out of your way, but unless you're the Human Torch from the Fantastic Four, you'd be the one burning. So it's worth noticing what it's doing to you. Has it ever left anybody at ease, with nothing to regret afterward?
The Buddha also told his son that loving-kindness is what gets rid of ill will, and compassion does the same for cruelty. I'm biased on loving-kindness, since it got me out of my depression. For these folks it starts with the grown-up they are now, though, and their little one stays out of it.
As for when their little one gets a turn, I'd say once they've had some bad days where scaring somebody would've felt good, and they didn't do it. Until then it helps a lot to have a real person in their life who likes them and won't laugh along, since loving-kindness is easier to pick up from someone who already has some.
## Borrow the Adult Before You Can Be the Adult
## Noticing Counts
"Huh, somebody in here is really scared." That might be the most adult thing you've got right now, and it's plenty to start with. Whoever's noticing isn't all the way inside the scared one.
It can help to use your own name. For me, instead of "I'm terrified," that'd be "Little Joel is terrified. What does Little Joel need right now?" You'll sound a bit like Elmo :) In lab studies, though, people who talked to themselves this way reacted less to upsetting pictures and memories, and their brains didn't seem to be working any harder to do it.
If you can tell your parts apart pretty easily, you've probably got plenty of noticing already. Don't keep drilling it just because the love isn't coming yet. A lot of meditators are great at watching. I was, and baby me was still starving.
## Borrow One Competency at a Time
A complete ideal parent may be impossible to imagine (unless you saw The Brady Bunch). And imagination is pretty hard for adults anyway since it kinda gets stamped out of kids at an early age, but it's not just a foolish waste of time. It's actually a genius mode unlock. So since the adult is actually a baby when it comes to power to imagine, start small: Borrow one function at a time from a figure who embodies it. Perhaps a therapist or your grandmother really cared for you. Perhaps your teacher at some age was a great guide. Maybe an older sibling protected you well. And there's always your future healed self if you can imagine that.
For warmth, think of someone you remember fondly. How would they sit beside your little one? Just make sure it's someone who liked you the way you were.
For protecting, ask what a minimally competent adult would do here. Maybe something simple, like actually smelling that old food you're about to eat and then trashing it if it smells a little weird. If you're about to send an angry text, hit pause. If the room itself triggers your spidey sense, leave, or lock the door and get help. Even if this still feels like the child acting like the adult, for now, that's ok. "Fake it til you make it" isn't exactly it, but yeah. Afterward, you can tell your little one, "I did that for us. That was the Protector." If it sounds funny, laugh!
The Guide can be you too, just a calmer you. That's a lot easier to imagine than your future healed self. Think back to what (and how) you decided about something like this the last time you were calm. Or ask what the wise person you respect would have you practice today. A wise tradition (like "What would Jesus do?" or "What would Buddha do?") can give some direction, too, until you develop your own internal compass.
## If the Love Isn't Coming Yet
If you weren't loved well as a child, "love your inner child" can sound like nonsense. Love them how? Maybe you really want the best for your little one. Maybe you hug them in your imagination, or miss them. And you can still feel none of the warmth people mean when they say love. That doesn't close the door, though, it just means you start smaller.
Maybe just imagining a caring grown-up makes you feel more threatened, or lonelier. Lonelier is a strange one, with somebody right there. Then go smaller still, to the least cruel response a decent adult could offer your little one. It's scaffolding, and it isn't a test of whether you were loved enough.
Taking the hostility out makes room for warmth. What people most often say helped is letting their defenses down. I mean by choice, after the part guarding you has been heard. If you've never been safe being loved, your ego can act like a rigid security guard that won't let the feeling in, and being vulnerable is the opening the feeling needs. (This guard is only about letting the feeling in. People still have to earn your trust.) Sometimes the warmth is there, with a cold part standing in front of it, guarding something. That part tends to step aside once it's been heard (see the section Hear the Guard First).
If the love still isn't coming, you can at least stop judging your little one. Obviously no insults. Helpful advice is great, we all need advice. Let them absorb it at their pace. And don't expect a thank-you for the session, like they booked it. That's enough to start being the adult, even if it isn't enough yet for them to trust you. That takes warmth, and if there isn't any yet, borrow some.
Your little one might not take it well at first. 😜 More like "Oh, so NOW you care?" Your first instinct might be "But I'm trying!" Some days, just hearing it out like a decent adult is the only thing you'll manage to borrow.
## Borrow Hindsight
I sometimes ask: "How would I respond to this once I understood it more clearly, in balance and with compassion?" It's a question borrowed from your future self, and I wouldn't take their answer as gospel. I don't think a perfect Higher Self is waiting in there with the answers, either (more on that in How This Relates to IFS).
That future you also knows why you can't pull this off perfectly today. What would they do for you, if they were here? Maybe they'd just make you some food. If you can't picture them, make the food yourself, and don't bother faking a feeling first. A "no" or a pause can count as the food, too.
You can borrow a wiser future version of whoever else is involved, too, as long as you don't assume they'll end up on your side, or sorry. What the current one keeps doing is still what you go by, however good the picture looks (the future one has never called you back).
