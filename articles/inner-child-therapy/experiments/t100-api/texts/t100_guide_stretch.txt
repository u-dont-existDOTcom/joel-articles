After all, sometimes “I want it NOW!” is just that: a rung on the desire treadmill. No hidden guard or old trauma required, because all creatures have endless desires naturally anyway, especially when immature. You can hear that want without shame about it. Hearing it doesn’t require giving into it, especially if getting your way would hurt you or somebody else.
I think the opposite trap can feel a lot like finally having your act together. It’s forced adulthood, and it sounds like “Nope, all good!” from somebody with a fever who’s cleaning the whole kitchen anyway. Not a lot of fun in that kitchen, and nobody gets very close to the person in it. If that’s you, clamping down harder is going to feel like the fix, but it’s just more of the same. I’d try croaking to someone you trust, “Could you pick up some soup?” If you keep swinging between spoiling and clamping down, try staying warm while you say what happens next, like “Not now, we’ll go after lunch.”
Once a need does show, after all that hiding, it’s okay to want a lot of closeness. So then someone you love goes for a walk alone, and you’ve texted “hello??” before they’re at the corner. When they get back, you sulk all night. Wanting them close is fine! But they get to go for a walk too. Ask your little one what it’s afraid of. I think it just wants to know they’re coming back. Then the Guide can come up with something like “See you in an hour?”
The Guide doesn’t need to know everything for sure (that would be an OCD Guide, and then you’d need a Guide for the OCD Guide). It chooses the next sane step that fits your values based on the info available, and that’s how it helps your little one grow toward a whole life. Waiting to be sure first is sometimes proper & prudent, but if it’s a pattern, it can keep life on hold, and then your little one’s world stays small. Sometimes something really is stuck on someone else’s end (you’ve asked, and followed up, and there’s nothing left for you to do yet), and waiting on that isn’t avoidance. The Guide can let it wait, keeping an eye out for when it frees up, and keep the rest of your life moving, so your little one keeps growing anyway.
Forced adulthood can get into your head too, where it wants everything as spotless as that kitchen. At your grandpa’s funeral, some dumb jingle starts up in your head and you nearly laugh during the eulogy. That doesn’t mean you didn’t love him, or that something’s wrong with you. Minds come up with ugly and embarrassing stuff all the time, some of it the opposite of what you believe, and bodies do their own thing too. The Guide isn’t there to police every thought. It notices the jingle and knows you didn’t mean it, and that it isn’t who you are. Then it goes with what you’d stand behind, which here is probably squeezing your mom’s hand and listening, and lets the jingle go.
