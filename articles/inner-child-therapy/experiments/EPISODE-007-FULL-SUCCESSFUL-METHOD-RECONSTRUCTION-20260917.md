## Correct method identity

The successful method is not `always repair a tiny local span`.

It is:

`full natural boundary -> broad editorial/architecture repair while needed -> cold audit -> owner/detector localization -> freeze reliable good islands -> repair only the remaining bad regions -> re-audit/retest -> freeze newly good islands -> progressively narrow until no residual remains`

Or, more compactly:

**coarse-to-fine iterative convergence with progressive locking.**

### Phase A — broad realization

Use the complete natural section when the section's architecture/register is broadly model-shaped and no reliable internal green islands yet justify freezing smaller spans.

- Preserve the complete source/function ledger.
- Repair architecture, reader relationship, thought order, register, and unnecessary explanatory packaging across the full boundary.
- Allow paragraph movement, compression, merging, and substantial realization change inside the authorized semantic delta.
- Deliver the complete integrated boundary to Joel.
- Cold-audit the complete boundary, not merely changed snippets.

### Phase B — evidence-guided localization

Once owner/detector/cold-audit evidence identifies reliable good spans and bad spans:

- freeze the reliable good spans exactly;
- repair only the bad regions;
- preserve the full integrated boundary for every check;
- compare predicted weak regions against actual detector segmentation when available rather than blindly chasing red windows.

### Phase C — residual convergence

As the remaining failure shrinks:

- reduce the edit dose with it;
- use literal span -> explicit operation -> concrete positive target for the residuals;
- preserve known-green neighbors byte-for-byte;
- repeat until the complete natural boundary passes the owner/editorial target.

The late Episode 007 `span -> operation -> positive target` fingerprint is therefore a **late-stage residual-repair submethod**, not the complete campaign method.
