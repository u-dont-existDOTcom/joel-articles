# Repeats check: The Inner Guide Comes Later

The section is B233 to B237: the heading and four paragraphs. I checked each of those blocks against the others in the section and against every block from B0 to B232. The four paragraphs don't repeat or contradict each other, so every finding below pairs one of them with an earlier section.

Counts: 5 repeats, 0 contradictions, 1 callback.

## 1. Repeats, in article order

### 1. B9 and B235: caring for yourself without the Guide goes wrong

- **B9** (The Chicken-and-Egg Problem): "when we focus only on caring for ourselves rather than giving ourselves a mission as well. That's the Guide's job."
- **B235** (The Inner Guide Comes Later): "Without the Guide, you can slide from looking after your little one into spoiling it the same way."
- **Does the later one add anything?** Yes, a different way it goes wrong. In B9, life turns small, "like a never-ending therapy session". In B235, care turns into spoiling: Go Fish, sweets and screens, letting yourself off anything hard. The core claim is told a second time, with a new picture.
- **Better carrier:** B235, because it shows the slide with concrete examples, in the section given over to the Guide.

### 2. B9 and B236: the opposite trap, where the Guide's side crowds out caring for yourself

- **B9** (The Chicken-and-Egg Problem): "But then some people over-rely on their mission to avoid caring for themselves."
- **B236** (The Inner Guide Comes Later): "The opposite trap is forced adulthood: demanding perfect discipline of yourself and no visible needs, with very little joy or closeness."
- **Does the later one add anything?** Yes: a name, what it looks like, where it comes from (the old rule against being needy, passing for the Guide), and a way out. B9 gives it one sentence. Read together with entry 1, B235 and B236 replay the two-sided warning B9 already gave, in the same order.
- **Better carrier:** B236, because it's the fuller version and sits in the Guide's own section; in B9 it's one passing sentence.

### 3. B123 and B236: control passes for being the grown-up

- **B123** (When the Spiritual Relationship Hurts): "Being the grown-up doesn't mean controlling everything yourself, though. After a betrayal like that, part of you might want to."
- **B236** (The Inner Guide Comes Later): "No wonder tightening control again feels like the grown-up thing."
- **Does the later one add anything?** A little. In B236 the control is aimed at your own needs rather than at outcomes and other people's choices, and it's traced to the old rule against being needy. Both passages answer it with outside support (spiritual support in B123, someone safe in B236). A reader who remembers B123 would recognize the same point.
- **Better carrier:** B123, because it says it outright and gives a usable test ("act where it's yours to act"); in B236 it's a passing clause.

### 4. B192 and B236: the grown-up move is letting someone safe see you're struggling

- **B192** (When to Change the Strategy): "texting a friend that you're having a rough day instead of saying you're fine"
- **B236** (The Inner Guide Comes Later): "If you're stuck there, the next adult act may be asking someone safe for help, or saying what you'd prefer."
- **Does the later one add anything?** Very little. B192's setting ("skipping lunch and saying yes to everything again") is close to the no-needs pattern B236 calls forced adulthood, and the act is the same. B236 adds the label and the option of saying what you'd prefer. A reader would feel told the same thing again.
- **Better carrier:** B192, because it names one specific act and says what it does ("can teach your little one more than ten more good meditations").

### 5. B230 and B234: take your little one into art and nature

- **B230** (Let the Child Be Bad at Things): "go try something new just for you. Maybe a foraging group, or a painting class. Stargazing can be awe-inspiring"
- **B234** (The Inner Guide Comes Later): "You also widen its world with art and music, and you point out beautiful things when you see them. Nature is part of that"
- **Does the later one add anything?** Partly. The reason is new (the Guide widening a child's world, rather than you testing what you like), and so are music, pointing out beautiful things, and "the wider world past the end of the street you grew up on". The art-and-nature suggestions themselves come back three blocks later, and that part would feel repeated.
- **Better carrier:** B230, because it gives concrete activities and a way to read the result ("Notice what holds your attention when nobody is impressed").
