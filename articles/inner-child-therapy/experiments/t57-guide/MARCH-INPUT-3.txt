The opposite trap, forced adulthood, looks more like hauling all the grocery bags up the stairs in one trip while a friend holds the door with nothing to carry. You hold yourself to perfect discipline and never let a need show. There isn't much joy in that, or closeness either. Halfway up, with your fingers going purple, all you're thinking about is getting a better grip. The grown-up you might say what you'd like for once, or ask someone safe, "Could you take the eggs?"

I think the opposite trap is sneakier, since perfect discipline looks so grown-up. You're on your towel twisting one arm up behind you to get sunscreen on the middle of your back, because asking someone would mean letting a need show. Meanwhile everybody you came with is in the water having fun. In forced adulthood, the urge is to try harder alone, maybe with one of those sponges on a long handle. I'd call someone safe over and say, "Could you do my back?" or "I want to swim too!"

Or you go the other way, into forced adulthood. "Keep driving, I can hold it!" You expect perfect discipline from yourself and never let a need show. Meanwhile your little one has been yelling about it since the last rest stop. There isn't much fun in living like that, or much closeness. You'll want to hold on harder, but the grown-up thing might be to ask someone safe for help, or just say what you'd like: "Can we stop somewhere soon?"
