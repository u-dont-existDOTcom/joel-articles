# Repeats check 2: The Inner Guide Comes Later

Scope: the section is B233 to B237 (the heading plus B234 to B237), and it ends the article. I checked each of its blocks against the others there and against B0 to B232. Everything below involves at least one block in that section.

Result: 5 repeats, 0 contradictions, 1 callback (to the block just before the section).

## 1. Repeats, in article order

### 1. B9 and B235–B236: the two traps around the Guide

- **B9** (The Chicken-and-Egg Problem): “Our lives can become small and like a never-ending therapy session when we focus only on caring for ourselves” and “some people over-rely on their mission to avoid caring for themselves”
- **B235** (The Inner Guide Comes Later): “Without the Guide, you can slide from looking after your little one into spoiling it the same way.”
- **B236** (The Inner Guide Comes Later): “I think the opposite trap is the sneakier one, since from the outside it just looks like having your act together.”
- **Shared point:** caring for yourself without the Guide goes wrong, and the opposite, letting the adult side crowd out self-care, is a trap too.
- **Does the later one add something?** Yes. It gives each trap a different, concrete form. Where B9 had a small, therapy-like life, B235 has spoiling (Go Fish, sweets and screens, getting out of anything hard). Where B9 had leaning on a mission, B236 has forced adulthood (sick and still saying all is well). It also says why the second trap is sneakier. A reader who remembers B9 would still recognize the same two-sided warning.
- **Better instance:** B235–B236. The point is concrete there and sits in the Guide's own section; B9 fits both traps into two lines of the overview.

### 2. B14 and B236: doing chores on empty

- **B14** (The Chicken-and-Egg Problem): “they catch you lying down because you're wiped out, instead of pushing through one more chore”
- **B236** (The Inner Guide Comes Later): “somebody with a fever who's cleaning the whole kitchen anyway”
- **Shared point:** pushing through housework when you're depleted, when the caring move is to stop.
- **Does the later one add something?** Yes. It names the pattern (forced adulthood), says what it costs (no fun, nobody gets close), and instead of just resting, you ask someone for help or company. The picture is the same, but more than 200 blocks separate them, so a reader may not connect them.
- **Better instance:** B236. Here over-functioning is the subject; in B14 the picture only shows doing the care instead of just saying it.

### 3. B73–B74, B192 and B236: drop the "I'm fine" front and reach for someone

- **B73–B74** (Also Look Outward): “So I'd call somebody or show up somewhere, mess and all.” and “you're still keeping up a fake normal, happy version of yourself to hold onto it, I'd either bring more of the truth in”
- **B192** (When to Change the Strategy): “like texting a friend that you're having a rough day instead of saying you're fine”
- **B236** (The Inner Guide Comes Later): “"Nope, all good!"” and “I'd try croaking, "Could you pick up some soup?"”
- **Shared point:** stop presenting as fine; let people see you're struggling and ask them for something.
- **Does the later one add something?** B192 makes it the small real-life step after a session that seemed to go well. B236 adds the forced-adulthood frame, the cost (no fun, nobody gets close), and asking for company as well as help. Even so, a reader reaching B236 would feel told the same thing again, mostly because of B192: “instead of saying you're fine” and “"Nope, all good!"” are the same move. B73–B74 is the loosest instance, since what sets it off there is loneliness and a deepening relationship.
- **Better instance:** B236. It is the only place where hiding behind "fine" is the topic itself; elsewhere it is an example for another point.

### 4. B156 and B235: a held limit is love; giving in only passes for it

- **B156** (Make a Simple Vow): “you still go to bed, and staying kind about it shows them bedtime isn't a lack of love”
- **B235** (The Inner Guide Comes Later): “Sweets and screens can pass for loving yourself, and so can getting it out of anything hard.”
- **Shared point:** holding a limit with your little one is loving; giving in to what they want only looks like love.
- **Does the later one add something?** Yes. It comes at the point from the giving-in side, names the slide (spoiling), widens it from treats to dodging anything hard, and ties it to the Guide. A reader may still feel partly told again, since B156, building on the bedtime story in B136–B137 (Love Doesn't Have to Wait for Trust), already showed a held limit as love.
- **Better instance:** B156. It shows the limit held kindly, in a scene the reader already knows; B235 names only the slide.

### 5. B230 and B234: taking your little one out to art and nature

- **B230** (Let the Child Be Bad at Things): “go try something new just for you. Maybe a foraging group, or a painting class. Stargazing can be awe-inspiring”
- **B234** (The Inner Guide Comes Later): “You also widen its world with art and music, and you point out beautiful things when you see them. Nature is part of that”
- **Shared point:** take your little one out to art and nature.
- **Does the later one add something?** Some: music, pointing out beauty, and the world past the street you grew up on, all framed as the Guide's job; B230 used these outings to find out what you like. The two are only four blocks apart, and B234's own callback (“That's what I was just asking of you, with that new interest.”) sends the reader straight back to B230–B232, so the overlap is easy to notice, though mild.
- **Better instance:** B230. It gives concrete outings and a reason for them (noticing what holds your attention); B234 gives a general list.

**Within the section:** no block repeats another. B235's “getting it out of anything hard” is the flip side of B234's “You ask hard things of it so it can grow, at its own pace.”, but it reads as the next step: what happens without the Guide.

## 2. Contradictions

None that a reasonable reader would see. The closest calls, and why they don't clash:

- B234 “you hold standards, the way a good parent would” (The Inner Guide Comes Later) against B231 “nobody's on trial” (Let the Child Be Bad at Things): B234 says the standard it means is sticking with that new interest, at the child's own pace.
- B235 “getting it out of anything hard” (The Inner Guide Comes Later) against B36 “Okay, that's enough for today” (Before You Try to Go Deep) and B178, which says to stop when a part says no to going deeper (Start With Whatever Showed Up): B235 is about dodging everything hard in daily life; the others are about pacing deep practice.
- B236's forced adulthood (The Inner Guide Comes Later) against B98 “"Fake it til you make it" isn't exactly it, but yeah.” (Borrow One Competency at a Time): B98 is about doing protective acts before they feel natural; B236 is about hiding your needs.
- B237 “it's okay to want a lot of closeness” (The Inner Guide Comes Later) against B103 “Getting close can be too much, too” (Borrow Love—or Borrow the Perspective of Care): B103 is about the adult pressing closeness on the little one; B237 is about the little one's own need for people.
- The heading “The Inner Guide Comes Later” (B233) against B9's warning about lacking an inner Guide (The Chicken-and-Egg Problem) and B99's borrowed Guide (Borrow One Competency at a Time): B99 already says you borrow direction “until you develop your own internal compass”, and that compass is what comes later.

## 3. The five a reader would most notice

1. **Repeat 3:** “instead of saying you're fine” (B192) and “"Nope, all good!"” (B236) are the same move, 44 blocks apart.
2. **Repeat 1:** a section about the Guide sends readers back to the Guide's introduction in B9, and both give the same two traps.
3. **Repeat 5:** only four blocks apart, and B234's callback points the reader right at B230.
4. **Repeat 4:** bedtime held kindly (B156) and sweets and screens (B235) are two sides of the same claim about love and limits.
5. **Repeat 2:** the same picture of chores done on empty, but more than 200 blocks apart.
