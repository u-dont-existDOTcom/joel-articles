## Opening

- H1 opening: start from the reader who is already too much the child to make a decision they can carry through. Do not state a generalized clinical eligibility rule. Make `go deeper` the immediate bad demand, tell them to get steadier/outside support, and stop.
- Inner-monologue opening: answer the actual friction `I don't hear a child talking in my head.` Do not define spontaneous versus deliberate inner speech as a taxonomy.
- Alternate channels: preserve speech aloud, writing, drawing, picture/song, sensation/urge, and caring action, but realize them as uneven examples of what contact might actually look like rather than a closed menu.
- Quiet mind / imagery: say directly that little inner speech does not create an obligation to be vividly visual; quiet can simply be quiet. Do not turn this into a resistance theory.
- Feeling-versus-words: preserve that either can come first and later words can help. Do not finish with a balanced `thinking isn't X / feeling isn't Y` maxim; make only the distinction needed for the reader to continue.
- Regulation transition: if the body is too charged, make that the next live problem rather than a new explanatory subsection thesis.

## Tail

- Order/roadmap: do not explain document architecture. If talking is not working, say not to talk harder; point to the roadmap only as the ordering aid.
- Imagery safety: one concrete event. If the reader is losing the actual room in imagery, eyes open and come back to room/time/body; if they are worse afterward, make the session smaller/stop/get support. Avoid warning -> instruction -> warning -> instruction symmetry.
- Hook entry: use chronology. The body/urge starts before the label is known; by the time the reader notices, they may already be texting/drinking/scrolling/explaining/attacking/disappearing/promising/deciding. Pema/shenpa arrives because it names this moment.
- Acute hook response: the next few breaths are not for solving the relationship. Orient, name the hook, pause one-to-three breaths unless immediate safety action is needed, avoid deciding truth at maximum activation, and do one small adult move. Keep the source functions but do not restate them afterward as a protocol or moral.
- Ten-minute harm reduction: preserve say/send/consume/promise/decide, but make them concrete things that can simply wait. Do not explain why postponement is wise after the examples already show it.
- Later meaning-making: once activation is down, allow present grievance and old material to coexist. Use `My stomach tightened` versus `That person is evil` to carry observation-versus-verdict. Preserve literal words-at-the-time versus later translation without another conceptual wrap-up.
- Endurance/safety: `one breath can be enough`; if inward attention worsens orientation/function/compulsion, stop and get outside support. Attach this directly to the practical flow.
- Also Look Outward: do not let inner-child work become a machine for explaining away the other person's behavior. The other person remains part of the event. Use Gibson to shift from motive-guessing to repeated behavior, not to introduce a diagnostic checklist.
- Pattern qualification: one bad day, disagreement, time, or their own boundary do not establish immaturity. Keep uncertainty about can't/won't/can't-now subordinate to the practical question of what can actually be relied on today. Do not make the three possibilities a taxonomy.
- Behavior/promises/protection: behavior may revise the assessment; promises alone are not the change; protection need not wait for repeated harm. Say this directly without a responsible-counselor conclusion.
- Guilt/repair/self-abandonment: guilt does not decide the case. Harm requiring repair and self-abandonment to stop another person's disappointment can coexist. Do not turn that into a `both sides` lesson.
- Realistic outcome: make the next interaction practical. Mutual understanding may not be available; getting one point across, saying no, or ending without prolonged defense can be enough. Do not wrap this in Joel-personal-preference language.
- Witness/Protector/Guide: keep only as immediate roles doing work in that interaction, not as a polished synthesis sentence.
- Caring-editor exercise: write the response, do not send it yet, then edit while remaining on the writer's side. Preserve valid protest/responsibility/request/boundary. Better need not mean nicer. Keep send/less/wait/no-response possibilities only as needed; do not build a menu.
- Return to writer: read the first draft for what the person was trying to get across and what protection/reassurance/help was needed. The draft is not automatically childish and may contain mature protest. Let this demonstrate inward care; do not explain afterward that communication and care are separate jobs.
- More processing: begin from the awkward fact that healing itself can become checking. Preserve real revisiting versus certainty-seeking, but do not classify every repetition. The live test is whether another round changes feeling/understanding/decision/response or merely produces more monitoring.
- Checking loop: allow the thought to remain unresolved and return to chosen life/activity/relationship; no requirement to make it disappear first.
- Outcome criterion: before another round, know what the exercise is supposed to change. Calm/insight alone can coexist with no change in the original problem. If practice gives no useful contact, increases self-monitoring, or worsens daily life, do not intensify it; keep what helps and meet the need another way.
- Final ritual warning: if the exercise becomes something that must be completed before living, stop there. Do not add another moral or recap.

No decorative listicles, balanced caveat shells, matched `maybe X / maybe Y` taxonomies, or explanatory aftercare. Do not manufacture biography, friends, jokes, errors, slang, or fake specificity. Preserve every source claim/function, safety instruction, link/attribution, native-object position, and the exact Human/high island.
