New H2:

> ## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child
>
> I was using “adult” a little too loosely above. Being able to look around the room, pause, and make choices tells me there is some adult capacity online. It does not tell me what that adult wants to do with vulnerability. If frightening or exploiting it is something you currently want—or you want therapy to make you better at doing that—the child stays out of this. Don't bring them forward for a minute to see what happens. Their fear does not get to be the test, or part of the reward.
>
> Keep the work with the grown-up problem. What is hurting, if anything, or what are you actually hoping help will change? If the wish to harm is bound up with an identity, work on the identity, not the child. What do you get from being that person, who does it serve, and what is it costing you? If you expected it to make your life better or happier, has it?
>
> I don't want that to turn into a purity test. A religious identity, an unusual belief, an intrusive thought or unwanted fantasy, anger, charged language, or something harmful you did before does not by itself tell me what you intend now. The same goes for a word like “sovereign.” If somebody says, “I'm sovereign,” and then says, “I have to obey this urge, doctrine, group, spirit, or master,” I don't have to settle the metaphysics. Can they pause? Question it? Refuse? Change their mind? That's a question about adult agency. For this gate I care about something else: do they currently endorse frightening or exploiting vulnerability?
>
> If you're helping someone here, you can keep caring about them without flattering cruelty, arguing theology, or teaching them to exploit people more effectively. A promise that the intent changed is not enough to put the child back into the exercise. Let the change show up in repeated choices, including when exploiting vulnerability would feel rewarding. Until then: no child dialogue, childhood photos, hypnosis, borrowed-parent imagery, spiritual figures, or another side door. Some people at this point may need sustained care from a real human being more than another internal exercise or a bot.
