Your own drug history matters too. If you go manic, psychotic (by which I mean losing your sense of reality to the point of posing a risk to yourself or others), get super paranoid, or end up worse afterward than before, those are signs to reconsider trying the same thing again, and try to figure out why that happened. I'd look at the drug and the dosage, as well as mindset and setting etc. Paranoia during a rough trip can be a warning sign for psychosis, but it doesn't automatically lead there, since it can be valid. It tends to go that way when it isn't balanced by practicality. "The CIA is mind-controlling me!" is paranoia. "The CIA, marketers, and other forces are conspiring to mind-control me, the way they do to everyone else! But since I can see that, I can prevent it to some extent, just by being aware of how it operates" is paranoia too, but it's practically useful, and less likely to lead to psychosis. "...which means I can prevent it by standing on my head and counting backwards from 33, since 33 is their magic Freemason number!" is leaning psychotic. Some drugs are known for causing paranoia, like THC, so I'd avoid those. If you can't clearly understand what happened yourself, get someone qualified to help. And if you have an idea of what happened but aren't sure, ask someone who knows you well.
