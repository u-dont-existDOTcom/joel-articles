# Junk sweep: Inner Child Therapy article

I read all of ARTICLE-NUMBERED.md (every section after the working note). The original sentence ("Those don't even agree with each other on everything!") is already gone from Related Clinical Work, P1.

Kinds: 1 = truism or obvious point, 2 = doesn't follow / answers nobody's question, 3 = quip or exclamation where the paragraph isn't joking, 4 = filler that only sounds like a point.

I left these alone on purpose: jokes, emojis and asides that are the author's voice, and caveats that tell the reader something real (the 17-person pilot, the false-memory warnings, "Feeling calmer doesn't mean their complaint is wrong").

1. **The Chicken-and-Egg Problem, P6**: “This is much easier when you can already reach one of those adult roles.”
   - Kind 1. The paragraph has just asked "what if you can't become the adult?" This says it's easier if you can, which repeats the problem instead of answering it.
   - Fix: cut, and start the next sentence "When you can't, it's like…"

2. **Write It. Don't Send It Yet., P6**: “Tyler's an evangelical Christian who preaches on YouTube, so it meant a lot to me that he liked this take on what Jesus was saying.”
   - Kind 2. P6 has moved on to tone of voice. This jumps back to Tyler liking the Jesus reading from P5, in the vague "it meant a lot to me that" shape.
   - Fix: move it to the end of P5, after the drafted line, so P6 stays on tone.

3. **When Healing Turns Into Checking, P2**: “Going back can still matter, since realizations are a real part of life.”
   - Kind 1. "Realizations are a real part of life" is a truism, and it doesn't say why going back matters.
   - Fix: "Going back can still matter, because sometimes you do see something new."

4. **When Healing Turns Into Checking, P4**: “Inner parenting can be like that too.”
   - Kind 1. It repeats the analogy the sentence before just made ("It's like real parenting: …"), and the next sentence applies it anyway.
   - Fix: cut.

5. **When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child, P5**: “Of course not.”
   - Kind 3. It's a punchline that answers the reader's question for them, and it contradicts "it's not obvious" a few sentences earlier.
   - Fix: cut, and leave the question for the reader to check.

6. **Borrow One Competency at a Time, P4**: “"Fake it til you make it" isn't exactly it, but yeah.”
   - Kind 4. It brings up the idiom only to half take it back, so the reader learns neither what this is nor what it isn't.
   - Fix: cut, or say what it is: "It isn't faking; it's practice."

7. **Borrow Love—or Borrow the Perspective of Care, P3**: “The quest for self-knowledge is a life's journey, but you don't have to wait for your life to finish to figure out your best next move.”
   - Kind 1. A platitude that answers a worry nobody has. It's wedged between the trick and "the gap it gets around", which refers back past it.
   - Fix: cut.

8. **Not Every Hero Wears A Cape, P3**: “But yeah, bonding starts somewhere.”
   - Kind 1. A truism that waves at the "this feels backwards" worry instead of answering it.
   - Fix: cut, or give one real case of care coming before the feeling.

9. **Not Every Hero Wears A Cape, P3**: “And this is not to say you've never done anything to care for yourself. If not, you'd be dead right now.”
   - Kind 2. It argues against a claim nobody made, then ends on a dark punchline in a paragraph that isn't joking.
   - Fix: cut both, and make the next sentence "We're making acts of care intentional now, as a base for love to grow."

10. **Not Every Hero Wears A Cape, P5**: “It doesn't mean failure, because the process is dynamic.”
    - Kind 4. "Because the process is dynamic" sounds like a reason but gives none.
    - Fix: "It doesn't mean it failed; the feeling often comes after the acts." (That's P3's point.)

11. **Start With Whatever Showed Up, P8**: “Yours too, probably.”
    - Kind 1. A quip telling the reader that their own intuition is sometimes right and sometimes wrong, which nobody doubts.
    - Fix: cut.

12. **When to Change the Strategy, P5**: “It doesn't have to happen after every session, and once isn't proof you've got it all down.”
    - Kind 4. Two hedges stacked at the end of the paragraph. Nobody thinks one honest text proves they've got it all down.
    - Fix: cut, or keep only "It doesn't have to happen after every session."

13. **Four Pairs That Commonly Hook the Child, P4**: “You'd be belonging selectively, which still leaves room to belong.”
    - Kind 1. It's circular: belonging selectively is belonging.
    - Fix: "You'd still belong, just selectively."

14. **Remember When You Already Did It, P1**: “Your own challenge doesn't need to be as dramatic as that, as long as it really happened.”
    - Kind 1. "As long as it really happened" goes without saying for a challenge you got through. Also, the sister's quote now sits in between, so "that" no longer clearly means the car crash.
    - Fix: "Your own challenge doesn't need to be as dramatic as a car crash."

15. **Just Like Me, P2**: “With that many people feeling the same thing, the theory that you're the defective one gets kind of hard to keep up.”
    - Kind 2. It follows a sentence about getting in touch with whoever hurt you, but it belongs to P1's "how many people feel this" step.
    - Fix: move it into P1, right after "and wanting it to stop, just like you."

16. **Use Childhood Photographs Gently, P3**: “I'm sure you can relate.”
    - Kind 3. A quip at the end of a serious warning about planted memories. Few readers can "relate" to a balloon ride that never happened.
    - Fix: cut, or make it real: "Most of us 'remember' something we only know from a photo or a family story."

17. **Somatic Heart Opening, P1**: “That describes what they feel, and it doesn't prove how the body produces it.”
    - Kind 4. A "not proof" hedge against a claim nobody made. The paragraph never says how the body produces anything.
    - Fix: cut.

18. **Altered States Can Require Reparenting, P2**: “That said, it'd make sense to get a professional to weigh in for some situations.”
    - Kind 4. A stacked hedge ("would make sense… for some situations") that names no situation, dropped into the middle of the childhood story.
    - Fix: name the case and move it after the story ("If a voice is urging a child toward harm, it's still worth having a professional weigh in"), or cut it, since P11 already says to get outside support.

19. **How to Forgive Without Forgetting, P3**: “Seeing how they got there doesn't make it okay, and in theory they could have done something else.”
    - Kind 2. "In theory they could have done something else" contradicts P2's "From in there, it was the only move they had." The paragraph already holds them accountable without it.
    - Fix: "Seeing how they got there doesn't make it okay."

20. **Borrowed Adulthood in Relationship, P2**: “You'll find the balance is dynamic between inner & outer reliance, and going too far in either extreme isn't generally the healthiest.”
    - Kind 4. A vague "dynamic balance" plus a hedged "extremes aren't generally healthiest". Together they only repeat the paragraph's "care from more than one place".
    - Fix: cut.

21. **Speak Toward What You Are Building, P1**: “It's not so hard to understand that words have power.”
    - Kind 1. A truism used as an opener; the next sentence makes the actual point.
    - Fix: cut, and open with "Words point your attention somewhere, so aim them at what you want more of…"

22. **Speak Toward What You Are Building, P1**: “Being totally independent also isn't realistic nor ideal, and can even seem like just a reaction to the original question.”
    - Kind 2. There is no "original question". The line before is a statement ("I'm not dependent").
    - Fix: "…and can be just a reaction against the old dependence."

23. **Speak Toward What You Are Building, P7**: “Professional therapy's still useful for what it does, and peers and community do something else, so a serious healing path can include both.”
    - Kind 4. "Useful for what it does" and "do something else" say nothing; only "can include both" has content.
    - Fix: "Professional therapy still has its place, and a serious healing path can include both."

24. **How This Relates to IFS, P6**: “Could an IFS therapist help? They may, if they're skilled and grounded.”
    - Kind 1. That's true of any therapist; the next sentence gives the real test.
    - Fix: merge it into the next sentence: "An IFS therapist may help if they're trauma-informed, don't put ideas in your head, and understand dissociation."
