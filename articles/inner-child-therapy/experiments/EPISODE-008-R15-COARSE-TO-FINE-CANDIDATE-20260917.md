## Exact candidate

### Also Look Outward

And don't let “maybe I was triggered” turn into “therefore this was all my fault.” If you were mean, apologize. Good. Now what were you trying to say before the apology became the whole conversation? Guilt can make you drop your own point without anybody manipulating you.

Maybe the answer is that there wasn't much to say after all—it was one awful conversation, they needed time, or you ran into one of their boundaries. Fine. But if the thing you came to say keeps disappearing, that is no longer just one awful conversation. [Lindsay Gibson's work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) on emotional immaturity keeps coming back to what somebody repeatedly does in a relationship, not whether you've figured out why. You may never know whether they couldn't meet you, wouldn't, or just couldn't that day. Getting hurt again probably won't explain them any better.

If next time they actually show up differently, great—change your mind. A promise that next time will be different isn't the same thing yet. Sometimes you've already said what needed saying, or said no, and they still think you're wrong. That's the ending. Your head may hate it and keep writing another one.
