### Write It. Don't Send It Yet.

Write the angry response somewhere private first. Don't send it yet.

It doesn't mean you need to hide your upset, or apply for a permit to protest, but choose your battles and execute them wisely, so you don't end up regretting how you communicated later. As Sun Tzu might have said (if I could ever have read that whole boring Art of War book), a war with no casualties is the best possible war. When high IQ & EQ communication hits a writer's block, sometimes the best thing is to simply say, "I need some time to think about my response." And if the conflict really requires a response right now, then obviously just do your best rather than wait for the perfect timing.

Later, read the mess again and keep whatever is still true—the no or request, the apology you actually owe, whatever survived the heat. The rest can stay private. Maybe nothing needs sending.

If you say you'll come back later, come back.

Then read the version you didn't send. Not because it needs another edit. What was that person trying to stop? What did they need somebody to understand? Maybe they needed protection, reassurance, actual help. If the polished reply takes care of the conversation but you still turn against the person who wrote the first draft, you only finished one of the jobs.
