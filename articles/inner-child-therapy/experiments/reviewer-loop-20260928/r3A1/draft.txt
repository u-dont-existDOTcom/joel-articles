Once you're in that happy place, look at the other person too, because some people really are hard to deal with. Can they hear you disagree, look at their own part, and make room for how it was for you, or does it keep ending the same way, where you bring up something that hurt and end up looking after their feelings, reassuring them they're not a bad person? The psychologist Lindsay Gibson calls people like that emotionally immature, and one of her books is Disentangling from Emotionally Immature People. But calling someone that after one fight, or one “no” or one boundary of theirs that made you mad, is a judgement made on too little evidence, and that “no” or boundary might even have been a fair one.
