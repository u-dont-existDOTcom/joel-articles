> ## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child
>
> Consider a person who says they're sovereign, says some spirit or doctrine has to be obeyed, and also admits they still enjoy frightening or exploiting vulnerable people. The unusual belief is not what makes child work unsafe. The endorsed harm does. A calm, oriented adult can still be dangerous to the child, and bringing the child forward “just to see what happens” would turn the child's fear into part of the experiment—possibly part of the reward. Disturbing thoughts, unusual beliefs, and a harmful history can all matter without answering the present question of what the person actually endorses now. The contradiction between sovereignty and “I cannot refuse” belongs with the adult. Therapy can stay there, with the person's choices and consequences, without turning into a theology debate or a lesson in better manipulation.
>
> If the person later says the harmful intent is gone, the child still has nothing to prove. The change has to become visible in the person's life, including when exploitation would still be rewarding. That may take time and a sustained relationship with another person, with the child entirely outside the process.
