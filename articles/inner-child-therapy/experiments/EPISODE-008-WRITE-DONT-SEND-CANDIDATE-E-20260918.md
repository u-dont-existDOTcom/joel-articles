## Exact candidate

### Write It. Don't Send It Yet.

Write the text you'd write if your phone didn't have a Send button.

Maybe it says, “You never listen to me. I'm done explaining myself.” Leave it there for a while. When you come back, put the Send button back. “Never” might be heat. “I'm done explaining myself” might be the boundary. Maybe you also see something you actually did wrong and need to apologize for. The job isn't to turn the angry draft into a saintly one; it's to figure out what you genuinely want the other person to receive.

That could be the edited message. It could also be, “I need some time. I'll give you a fuller response tomorrow.” Don't promise tomorrow if you're really planning to disappear. Sometimes the answer is shorter than the draft, and sometimes there isn't an answer worth sending. If protection is needed now, stop editing and protect.

Before you delete the embarrassing version, read it one more time. Why did “you never listen” come out so loudly? What was that writer trying to get heard? Maybe they need reassurance, maybe practical help, maybe somebody to take their no seriously. The message can be wrong for the other person and still tell you something important about the person who wrote it.
