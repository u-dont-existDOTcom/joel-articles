## Broad governing-movement comparison

### Route A — `do not turn deferred judgment into self-conviction`

Start from the exact live pressure created by the owner-final paragraph: the reader has just postponed the `they're evil` verdict. The next human move is to prevent an overcorrection into `therefore I am the whole problem`.

Movement:

`defer the instant verdict -> do not collapse into total self-blame -> repair whatever is genuinely yours without surrendering the rest -> look outward at what repeats -> Gibson enters briefly as support for watching demonstrated behavior rather than solving motive -> let future behavior revise the judgment -> accept that mutual understanding may never close the case -> unresolved words remain for the next exercise`.

Pros:
- inherits pressure directly from the locked upstream prose rather than manufacturing a new example;
- gives the subsection an authorial correction rather than a diagnostic worksheet;
- guilt/repair is inside the live thought rather than a separate balanced-therapy paragraph;
- Gibson can arrive after the problem is already alive;
- naturally hands off to the private draft without a counselor takeaway.

Risk:
- legal/verdict language could become over-crafted if extended into a conceit; do not force a metaphor system.

### Route B — `what can you actually count on?`

Start outward immediately with present relational reliability. One event is weak evidence; recurrence changes what is reasonable to expect. Gibson supports repeated behavior over motive-solving; guilt and misunderstanding enter only as pressures that make reliability hard to see.

Pros:
- very clean fit to the source's demonstrated-capacity function;
- makes motive uncertainty and revisability easy to preserve;
- avoids the R14 apology scenario.

Risk:
- much higher chance of rebuilding `criteria -> caveat -> framework -> rule` counselor architecture;
- invites the same serial Gibson paragraph that survived R12-R14;
- can become a neat relationship-assessment mini-essay even with casual language.

### Route C — `you may never get the shared verdict`

Start with the desire to be understood and work backward into pattern/guilt/reliability.

Pros:
- strong handoff into the unsent-draft exercise.

Risk:
- too close to R14 P3's falsified `illustrate misunderstanding -> authorize ending` route;
- likely to rebuild generalized coaching and tidy closure.
