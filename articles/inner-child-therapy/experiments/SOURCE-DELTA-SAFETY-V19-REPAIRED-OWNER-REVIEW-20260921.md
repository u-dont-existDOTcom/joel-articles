> ## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child
>
> If the present-day you still enjoys frightening or exploiting vulnerability, keep the child out of it. You don't need to bring the child forward “just to see.” Their fear won't tell you anything you don't already know, and it can become part of what the adult enjoys. A promise to behave for the exercise doesn't change that.
>
> And I don't want to start treating weird beliefs or ugly thoughts as proof that somebody is dangerous either. An intrusive thought isn't intent. Neither is anger, an unusual belief, a religious label, an unwanted fantasy, or the fact that you did something harmful before. What are you actually choosing now? If you say you have to obey an urge, doctrine, group, spirit, or master, I care less about what you call it than whether you can say no.
>
> If you can't—or you still want to hurt or exploit someone vulnerable—there's plenty to work on without involving the child. Is the identity you're defending actually giving you the life you expected, or costing you more than it gives? If not, what do you want instead? If you're helping somebody in this situation, you can care about them without flattering cruelty, debating their theology, or helping them manipulate people better.
>
> I'd want to see the change in what you do next, especially when exploiting someone would still feel rewarding. That tells me more than one promise. Until there's something like that to trust, don't go around the boundary with hypnosis, imagery, or a spiritual figure. Sometimes a steady human being is more useful than another trip inside.
