# Turn 39 drafts: When the Urge to Escape Arrives (2026-10-09)

Every version, in order. Pangram results are in `../../tools/PREDICTIONS.md` (rows dated turn 39); the reviews in `REVIEWS.md`.

## first drafts (before the dedup and the first reviews)

**U1**

Sometimes the urge to get away from what's coming up is a lot bigger than a yawn. All of a sudden you want a drink, or you're planning a whole new business. I wouldn't beat yourself up over it. If anything, it might mean you got close to something that really hurts, and a part of you is trying to manage it the only way it knows how. That's kind of progress, honestly! I'd thank it and slow way down. And if what's coming up is more than you can hold right now, get some support.

**U2**

It's also why I'm careful about intensive meditation retreats and plant medicine before there's enough Nurturer and Protector in you. Same with digging into trauma full force. Those can blow right through the protective layers before there's a grown-up in there big enough to hold whatever comes through. Then your little one is out in the open with nobody there yet.

**U3a**

Your own history with psychedelics and other drugs counts here too, more than anybody's trip report. If something ever gave you voices or paranoia, once is enough. Same if it broke your hold on what's real, or you kept seeing things after it should've worn off. I wouldn't take that one again without a professional assessment, even if you've felt totally fine since. I'd be careful with anything else that messes with your sense of what's real, too. That doesn't make every drug dangerous for you, though.

**U3b**

Milder stuff counts too, like getting confused or spaced out again and again. If it's ever severe or risky, or it keeps happening when you're sober, I'd get help right away. And if a prescribed medicine, or anything your body's come to depend on, might be part of the problem, please don't just stop it cold. Coming off some things too fast is dangerous in itself, so get a doctor's help with that.

**U4**

Gentle meditation or self-hypnosis is a different story. It can really calm your nervous system down. A lot of people find that a mantra they feel some heart connection to, repeated in their head or out loud, especially with a nice melody, can lower the walls enough for the heart to open up. It's not something to lean on all day as a way of coping, but used as therapy it can be super effective.
