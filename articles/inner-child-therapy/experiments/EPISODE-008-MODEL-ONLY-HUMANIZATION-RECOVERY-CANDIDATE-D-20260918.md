### Also Look Outward

One ugly conversation can feel like a revelation. Maybe it was. But if the same person comes back later and can actually stay with what you raised, while still disagreeing with you and keeping a boundary you hate, that matters too. One fight isn't a personality test. Then there is the pattern that keeps returning: you bring up something that hurt, and by the end you're carrying their reaction while your own experience has gone missing. That's closer to the territory [Lindsay Gibson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) describes in her work on emotional immaturity.

You may not know for certain whether the other person is or could be capable of a healthier response, and neither do they. If they promise to change, that's probably as useful as their prior promises were. Think about how long it has taken you to become more healthy. Could you have done that from one moment to the next based on a promise? Sometimes we can be a better support for someone when we maintain a realistic boundary, so that we can hold high expectations, and still feel prepared for their failure to meet them.

If you really did hurt them, repair it. You can own what you did and still notice yourself backing away from the complaint because their disappointment is hard to tolerate. Maybe you say it again because the first explanation really wasn't clear. But eventually you may notice you've already said what mattered and said no; what you're still waiting for is their understanding. It may not come. Guilt doesn't tell you how much of the conflict is yours, and it doesn't prove manipulation. The conversation can end with the misunderstanding still there.
