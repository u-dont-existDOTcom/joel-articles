## Exact internal V33

> ## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child
>
> Think about this as if there were an actual child in the room. An adult tells you they enjoy frightening vulnerable people. Would you bring the child over just to see what the child does? No. If the child recoils, all you've learned is that the child is scared—and you may have given the adult exactly the reaction they enjoy.
>
> Now change the adult. They tell you they keep having a horrible intrusive thought, and the thought scares them because they don't want to act on it. That's not the same situation. A strange belief or something harmful in their past can matter, but it doesn't by itself tell you what they want now. Someone can have frightening material in their mind and still be trying not to hurt anybody. The safety question is whether the adult wants the harm and can choose against it.
>
> If later they say that has changed, don't ask the child to prove it. Let the change show up in adult life, over time, especially when hurting or exploiting somebody would still be tempting. Sometimes another person needs to stay with that work for a while without helping the harmful side get better at it. The child stays out of all of it.
