So inner child therapy draws on those higher vantage points more concretely. The inner Guide is the closest thing here to a Higher Self, and it has to work with the Nurturer and Protector and come after them. It can’t act like some transcendent authority that waves everything away. Most reparenting doesn’t do enough with the Guide, which is the opposite extreme from IFS, so I’m trying to bring together the best of both. IFS isn’t exactly one system, so I may be simplifying it here. It has its own branches and disputes, and that’s one more problem I’m trying to remedy by laying all this out systematically in this guide.
IFS calls these wounded young parts “exiles.” I find that pathologizing. I prefer to speak of a child who needs parenting and integration, rather than naming them by their wounded or excluded condition.
And the parts just keep coming: managers, firefighters, exiles, polarized parts, legacy burdens, Self-energy, guides, new subparts... At some point it’s a maze, with a little one somewhere in the middle of it. Some practitioners are gentle about it and walk you through the maze just fine. Others seem to leave people more split up than when they started. A big institute and a pile of certificates won’t tell you whether a model is true, or whether it’s safe for you.
