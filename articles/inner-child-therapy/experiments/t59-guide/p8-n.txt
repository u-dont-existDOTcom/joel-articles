I don't think the Buddha expected anybody to be the spotless-minded type, either. He said kamma is what you intend. He also taught monks several ways to deal with a nasty thought once it's already there, and you wouldn't bother with that if nobody ever had one. And when a king who'd killed his own father to get the throne finally owned up to it, the Buddha told him that seeing it was wrong, making amends and holding back from then on is how a person grows in the practice. Feeling that bad about something you chose to do can sometimes mean something needs your attention. A jingle that just popped up isn't in that league.
