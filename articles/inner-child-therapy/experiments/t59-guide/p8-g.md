I don't think the Buddha would hold the jingle against you, either. He said [kamma is what you intend](https://suttacentral.net/an6.63/en/sujato). For a nasty thought that's already there, he gave monks [a whole set of things to try](https://suttacentral.net/mn20/en/sujato), like thinking about something better instead, or just not paying it any attention. And when [a king who'd killed his own father](https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato) owned up to it, the Buddha said seeing what you did wrong as wrong, making amends and holding back from then on is how a person grows in the practice. None of that asks you to be some pure-minded person. Feeling bad about something you chose to do can sometimes be worth listening to, though a jingle that just showed up is another story.
