I think this is closer to what the Buddha taught, too. [A king who'd had his own father killed](https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato) once confessed it to him, and the Buddha said that seeing a fault as a fault, making it right and holding back from then on is how you grow in the practice. He never asked for a mind where nothing bad shows up. He said [kamma is about what you intend](https://suttacentral.net/an6.63/en/sujato), and he gave monks [several ways to handle a nasty thought](https://suttacentral.net/mn20/en/sujato) once it's there. So it's seeing clearly, practicing and repairing, not proving you've got a pure mind. Feeling bad about something you chose to do can sometimes mean something needs looking at. Feeling bad about a jingle for just showing up is another thing.
