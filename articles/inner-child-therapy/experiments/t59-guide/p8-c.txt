I think this is closer to what the Buddha taught, too. He never expected a mind where nothing bad ever shows up. He said what counts as kamma is what you intend, and he gave monks several ways to deal with a nasty thought once it's already there. When a king who'd had his own father killed confessed it to him, the Buddha said that seeing a fault as a fault, making it right and holding back from then on is how you grow in the practice. So it's about seeing clearly and repairing things, and nobody's asking you to prove you've got a pure mind. Feeling bad about something you chose to do can sometimes tell you something needs looking at. Feeling bad about a jingle just for showing up is a different thing.
