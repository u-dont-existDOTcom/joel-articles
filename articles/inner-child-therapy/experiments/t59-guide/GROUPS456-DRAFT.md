The Guide doesn't need to know how things will turn out, which is lucky, because some things just can't move yet. Say you're waiting on the landlord's signature before you can sign up for the new place. You're not being lazy, whatever the old parent in your head says, and there's no point forging ahead as if you had it. Write down what you're waiting on, so you'll notice when it comes, and go do the next thing that matters to you and can move today.

Say you're still waiting on that signature and the cat's off her food. I'd spend today on the cat, since she matters more to you and you can do something about her. And if it's all one big mess in your head, put it in one sentence and do whatever that sentence says comes first; anything else can wait unless it'd change that.

Forced adulthood can get into your head too, where it wants everything as spotless as that kitchen. At your grandpa's funeral, some dumb jingle starts up in your head and you nearly laugh during the eulogy. That doesn't mean you didn't love him, or that something's wrong with you. Minds come up with ugly and embarrassing stuff all the time, some of it the opposite of what you believe, and bodies do their own thing too. The Guide isn't there to police every thought. It notices the jingle and knows you didn't mean it, and that it isn't who you are. Then it goes with what you'd stand behind, which here is probably squeezing your mom's hand and listening, and lets the jingle go.

I don't think the Buddha expected anybody to be the spotless-minded type, either. He said [kamma is what you intend](https://suttacentral.net/an6.63/en/sujato), and he taught monks [several ways to deal with a nasty thought](https://suttacentral.net/mn20/en/sujato) that's already there, like turning to something better, or just not giving it any attention. Then there's [the king who'd killed his own father](https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato) to get the throne. He felt bad enough about it that he finally owned up, and the Buddha told him that's how a person grows in the practice. You see it was wrong and you make amends, and after that you hold back. Feeling that bad about something you chose to do can sometimes mean something needs your attention, but I wouldn't put a jingle that just popped up in that league.
