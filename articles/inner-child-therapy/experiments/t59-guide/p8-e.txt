I don't think the Buddha would hold the jingle against you, either. He said kamma is what you intend, and he gave monks a whole set of ways to handle a nasty thought that's already there, like thinking about something better instead, or just not paying it any attention. A king who'd killed his own father got a hearing, too. When the king owned up, the Buddha told him that seeing what you did wrong as wrong, making amends and holding back from then on is how a person grows in the practice. None of that asks you to be some pure-minded person. If you feel bad about something you chose to do, that can sometimes be worth listening to; a jingle that just showed up is another story.
