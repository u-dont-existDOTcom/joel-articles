A king who'd killed his own father to get the throne felt bad enough about it that he finally owned up to the Buddha. The Buddha told him that seeing it was wrong, making amends and holding back from then on is how a person grows in the practice. Feeling that bad about something you chose to do can sometimes mean something needs your attention. A jingle that just popped up isn't in that league. I don't think the Buddha expected anybody to be the spotless-minded type. He said kamma is what you intend, and he taught monks several ways to deal with a nasty thought that's already there, like turning to something better, or just not giving it any attention.
