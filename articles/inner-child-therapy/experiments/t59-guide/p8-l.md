I don't think the Buddha expected anybody to start out with a spotless mind, either; that's what the training is for. He said [karma is what you intend](https://suttacentral.net/an6.63/en/sujato), and he taught monks [several ways to deal with a nasty thought](https://suttacentral.net/mn20/en/sujato) that's already there, like turning to something better, or just not giving it any attention. Then there's [the king who'd killed his own father](https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato) to get the throne. He felt bad enough about it that he finally owned up, and the Buddha told him that's how a person grows in the practice. You see it was wrong and you make amends, and after that you hold back. Feeling that bad about something you chose to do can sometimes mean something needs your attention, but a thought that just popped up, however ugly, isn't something you did.
