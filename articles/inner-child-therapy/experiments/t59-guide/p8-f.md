Once, [a king who'd killed his own father](https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato) came to see the Buddha. When the king owned up to it, the Buddha told him that seeing what you did wrong as wrong, making amends and holding back from then on is how a person grows in the practice. So I don't think he'd hold a jingle against you. He said [kamma is what you intend](https://suttacentral.net/an6.63/en/sujato), and he gave monks [a whole set of ways to handle a nasty thought](https://suttacentral.net/mn20/en/sujato) that's already there, like thinking about something better instead, or just not paying it any attention. None of that asks you to be some pure-minded person. Feeling bad about something you chose to do can sometimes be worth listening to; a jingle that just showed up is another story.
