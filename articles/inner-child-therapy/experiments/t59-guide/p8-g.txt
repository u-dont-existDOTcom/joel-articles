I don't think the Buddha would hold the jingle against you, either. He said kamma is what you intend. For a nasty thought that's already there, he gave monks a whole set of things to try, like thinking about something better instead, or just not paying it any attention. And when a king who'd killed his own father owned up to it, the Buddha said seeing what you did wrong as wrong, making amends and holding back from then on is how a person grows in the practice. None of that asks you to be some pure-minded person. Feeling bad about something you chose to do can sometimes be worth listening to, though a jingle that just showed up is another story.
