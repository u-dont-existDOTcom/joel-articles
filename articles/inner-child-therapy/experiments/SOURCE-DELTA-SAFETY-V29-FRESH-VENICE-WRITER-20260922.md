## Exact V29

> ## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child
>
> Most of this guide assumes that the present-day adult wants, at least in some measure, to protect the vulnerable child. That is not always true. Someone may enjoy frightening or humiliating others, want access to vulnerability in order to exploit it, or hope that therapeutic insight will make manipulation easier. If so, do not invite the child forward, even briefly to see what happens. The child’s fear is not a diagnostic test, and the exercise must not become another opportunity for gratification or control.
>
> Keep the conversation with the adult. What help do they actually want? Why are they here? What are they suffering from, if anything, and what do they hope will change? What does this identity provide, what does it cost, and whom does it serve? Is it producing the life or happiness they expected? If someone claims sovereignty while also saying they must obey an urge, doctrine, group, spirit, or master, that contradiction deserves attention. Sovereignty begins with being able to pause, question, refuse, and revise.
>
> This is not a test of religion or conventionality. An unusual belief, an intrusive thought, anger, an unwanted fantasy, a religious label, or even a harmful act in the past does not by itself establish present harmful intent. What matters is what the person now endorses and intends to do. Listen without flattering cruelty, getting drawn into theological arguments, or helping them become more effective at exploiting others.
>
> Promises alone do not make the child safe. Protective credibility develops through repeated non-harmful choices and a demonstrated ability to leave vulnerability unexploited, including when exploitation would feel rewarding. Until that credibility exists, keep the work focused on the present-day adult. Do not use child dialogue, childhood photographs, hypnosis, borrowed-parent imagery, spiritual figures, or any other indirect route to bring the child into contact with an adult position that still intends harm. At this point, real human care may matter more than another internal exercise.
