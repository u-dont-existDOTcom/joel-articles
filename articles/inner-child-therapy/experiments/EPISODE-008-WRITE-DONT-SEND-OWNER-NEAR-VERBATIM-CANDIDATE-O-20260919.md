## P1

Yeah, I mean, this happens all the time. I'll be writing out a reply to someone and, if I'm doing good, I might suddenly think, wait, that's not the best way to put it. It's not like I'm suppressing my feelings; I'm expressing them in a way that can be heard. It's actually way easier to do this with texts than with speaking, of course, but texts are good practice. Owning anything I did wrong is the tricky part because often what I did wrong was mostly just not being very high-IQ and high-EQ in how I communicated. If I actually did do something I know was wrong aside from that, it's easier for me at least. It can be hard to apologize for just being too direct because I associate that with honesty at some level.

## P2 owner anchor

It doesn't mean you need to hide your upset, or apply for a permit to protest, but choose your battles and execute them wisely, so you don't end up regretting how you communicated later. As Lao Tsu might have said (if I could ever have read that whole boring Art of War book), a war with no casualties is the best possible war. When high IQ & EQ communication hits a writer's block, sometimes the best thing is to simply say, "I need some time to think about my response." And if the conflict really requires a response right now, then obviously just do your best rather than wait for the perfect timing.

## P3

And yeah, going back to the writer—which is often my inner child when emotions pop up—is part of the process. It's like I'm holding his hand and showing him a better way, not like I just stole his pencil because he wrote something embarrassing. He still gets to be upset, or angry, or say no. Sometimes what he needs is protection or reassurance, not another edit. I'm trying to help him communicate without making him feel like he was the problem.
