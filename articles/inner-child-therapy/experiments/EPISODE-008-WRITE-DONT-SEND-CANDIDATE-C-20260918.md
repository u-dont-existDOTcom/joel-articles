### Write It. Don't Send It Yet.

Go ahead and write the angry version. The point isn't to send it. It's to stop asking yourself to be reasonable before you've even heard what you're angry about.

When you read it later, there are really two different jobs hiding in the same page. One is the message. Maybe some of it is unfair. Maybe you owe an apology. Maybe the only useful part is one sentence that says no. Edit for the person who's actually going to receive it. “I need some time. I'll give you a fuller response tomorrow” may be plenty—assuming tomorrow is a promise you can keep. Or don't send anything. If somebody needs protecting right now, that comes before the draft.

The other job is easier to miss because nobody else will ever read it. Go back to the version you hated. What was its writer trying to protect? What did they want somebody to understand so badly? Maybe they wanted reassurance; maybe they needed actual help. An angry draft can be a terrible message and still contain a perfectly adult protest. You don't have to send it to take its author seriously.
