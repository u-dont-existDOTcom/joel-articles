They still have to answer for what they did, and it still hurt. Seeing how they got there doesn't make it okay, and in theory they could have done something else. It's just that punishing them now can't reach back and give them what they were missing then, the sense to see it or the help. Or the skills. What's left is fixing what can be fixed, and making it easier for something different to happen now. So a boundary can still be right. So can the law, if it comes to that, or telling them plainly it was wrong (or telling yourself, if they'd never hear it). Keeping away from them for good can be right too, and you can still forgive them from over there. Understanding somebody's limitations should make my expectations more realistic, not make those limitations less consequential. "I understand how you became this way" can sit right next to "I now understand why I shouldn't rely on you for this."
