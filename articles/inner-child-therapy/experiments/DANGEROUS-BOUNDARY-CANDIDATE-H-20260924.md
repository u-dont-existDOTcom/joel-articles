# Before You Try to Go Deep

How much of the grown-up you is actually here right now? Try looking around the room, pausing before you act, making a choice and following through on it. If you can't do that because you're basically the frightened kid, leave the deeper conversation until later. You may need somebody steady there with you, or to do whatever helps you come back a bit before you try to talk to the child.

## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child

Being able to act like an adult and being safe for a child are not quite the same thing. Somebody can be calm, present, and perfectly able to make choices while still wanting to frighten or humiliate what's vulnerable, exploit it, or enjoy the fear. In that situation, bringing the child forward to “see how they react” already breaks the logic of the test. If the child is frightened, all you've learned is that the child is frightened. And if that fear is rewarding to the adult, the exercise has just supplied the reward. The child stays out, not because the adult is too flooded to do the exercise, but because the exercise would put vulnerability in the hands of somebody who is still choosing harm.

That leaves plenty to work on, but it is adult work. Something brought you here, and the identity or stance itself can be examined by what it is doing in your life—what you get from it, what it costs, who it serves, and whether it has produced the life and happiness you expected. A claim of sovereignty belongs in that conversation because it is a claim about agency. If you also say that an urge or an authority has to be obeyed, whether that authority is a doctrine, a group, a spirit, or a master, then the useful question is not which label wins. It is whether you can actually stop, question what is being demanded, refuse it, and change your mind. None of this makes religion or unusual beliefs evidence of danger by themselves. Neither do intrusive thoughts, anger, charged language, an unwanted fantasy, or something harmful you did in the past. Those things can exist without current harmful intent; the relevant issue here is what you endorse now and what you intend to do. If someone is helping you with that, care and listening are still possible without praising cruelty, turning it into a theology fight, or helping you become better at exploitation.

A promise that you will not harm the child does not answer the safety question on its own, because the gate is about what you repeatedly choose when vulnerability is actually available. Protective credibility grows through non-harmful choices, including the times when exploitation would have felt rewarding and you did not do it. Until that has become real in behavior, changing the route does not change who is meeting the child: inner dialogue, childhood photographs, hypnosis, an imagined borrowed parent, or a spiritual figure can all bring the same vulnerable part into contact with the same harmful intent. For some people, sustained care from actual people may be more important at this point than another internal exercise or one done with a bot.

## You Don’t Need an Inner Monologue

“Voice,” “ask,” and “answer” below don't have to mean a literal conversation in your head. If you don't hear a little kid talking in your head, don't invent one. Actually, this is one place where the usual inner-child prompt can get confusing, because “ask” sounds like you're supposed to ask a question and then wait for a second voice to answer. Maybe that's what happens for some people, but you might not be some people.

You might barely have spontaneous words in your mind and yet still be able to think a sentence on purpose. Try it and see. If words are there, speak or write them. You might feel more comfortable that way than just staying in imagination land. And if that starts feeling like homework you're doing for a child who isn't answering, drop it.
