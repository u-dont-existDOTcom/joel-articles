How Much Happiness Do You Know Is Possible?
What do you picture when you wish someone well? Probably them fed and safe, with people around who like them, stuff like that. Wanting that for somebody is love (babies get this part already), and practical love matters a lot. The horizon of what I can wish for someone is partly shaped by the happiness, peace, and freedom I know are possible.
But maybe you’ve had a big spiritual experience, and for a while you felt a peace or a love you didn’t know existed. After that, “I want you to be happy” can mean something much bigger, almost overnight. That’s why loving-kindness meditation can sound comically simple to someone who’s never been near that, and so powerful to someone who has. Saying “may you be happy” over and over won’t zap you into profound love, but it’s one way people find that love and grow it.
If that bigger happiness makes you curious, I’d follow the curiosity and skip pretending to believe anything. Read people from a few different traditions, say a Sufi poet and a Zen teacher. And if you ever meet someone who’s happy in a way that doesn’t add up, watch how they treat people on a day when everything’s going wrong for them.
Sometimes your little one has enough care, and what’s missing is something else. A hot meal can help, and so can saying no to your boss, but is that actually targeting the deeper existential question underneath? Life shouldn’t be just something you endure, like a hamster wheel or a rat race. What would you need to bring zest to a life of discovery?
Curiosity can be enough here, something like “Other people say they’ve felt a depth of love or peace that I never have. I don’t know if I believe their explanation. But what if the experience is possible for me?”
