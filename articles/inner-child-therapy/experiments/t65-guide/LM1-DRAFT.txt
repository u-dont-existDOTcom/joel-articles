If you weren't loved very well as a child, "love your inner child" can sound like nonsense. You might really want the best for your little one, hug them in your imagination, even miss them, and still not feel any of the warm stuff people mean when they say love. You're not broken for that. It just means starting somewhere smaller.

Borrowing still works here. But if picturing even a kind, made-up grown-up makes you feel more threatened, or lonelier, go smaller than that: the least cruel thing a decent adult could say or do. Then do that. None of this is a test of whether you were loved enough. It's scaffolding while the real thing gets built.

Taking the hostility out makes room for the warmth to get in. What people say helps the most, though, is letting their defenses down. If you've never felt safe being loved, the ego can act like a stiff security guard who checks everyone's ID twice, and vulnerability is the opening the feeling needs. Sometimes the warmth isn't gone at all. A cold or cynical part is just standing in front of it.
