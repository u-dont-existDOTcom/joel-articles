Since that was the only move they had, punishment can't go back and create the awareness or support they didn't have enough of. It can't hand them the ability they were missing, either. What's worth doing is repairing what can be repaired, and making a different response easier to reach for now. Seeing how they got there doesn't make what they did harmless, though, or mean nothing else was possible in theory. The harm is real, and they're still accountable. Legal consequences can still be right, if it comes to that, and so can judging them for it. So can boundaries and distance, up to no contact for good. You can understand them and hold them responsible. Forgiving them doesn't require speaking to them ever again. You can think both of these about the same person: "I understand how you became this way," and "I now understand why I shouldn't rely on you for this."
