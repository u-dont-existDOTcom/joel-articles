This one's for when talking to your little one, or picturing them, only gets you silence. Write the scared or hurting thoughts and feelings with the hand you don't usually write with, the clumsy one. Its writing already looks like a child's, which is why it's your little one's hand. Then answer with your usual hand, as the grown-up offering love, protection and guidance. If the clumsy hand just hovers, you might start thinking up what they'd say. Only write what's really there, though, what you actually feel. And if the protective part shows up first, let it use the clumsy hand until it's said all it wants to.

If journaling to make sense of things leaves you more confused, a simpler memory aid is fine. That could be a short note with the date and what happened, or a note of what helped. A reminder or a next step works too. So does a voice note. Maybe you still catch yourself wondering what it all means. A memory aid keeps track of things without you having to work that out at the same time, and it doesn't have to explain your life. Looking for patterns can come later, if it's useful at all.
