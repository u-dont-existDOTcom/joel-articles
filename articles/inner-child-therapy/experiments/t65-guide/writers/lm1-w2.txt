If you weren't loved well as a child, "love your inner child" can sound like nonsense. Maybe you sincerely want the best for your little one. You might hug them in your imagination, or miss them. And you could still feel none of the warmth people mean when they say love, and end up wondering what everyone else is talking about. The door's still open, though. You'd be starting smaller.

If just imagining a caring grown-up makes you feel more threatened, or lonelier, go smaller still. This is all scaffolding, and nobody's using it to test whether you were loved enough. Think of the least cruel response a decent adult could offer. Someone who doesn't love your little one could manage that much.

Taking the hostility out makes room for warmth. What people most often say helped is letting their defenses down, which might sound scary. You'd do it by choice, though, once the part of you that's on guard has been heard. That's different from pushing past it. If you've never been safe being loved, your ego can act like a rigid security guard that won't let that feeling in, and being vulnerable is the opening it needs. That guard is only about letting the feeling in. People still have to earn your trust. Sometimes the warmth isn't missing. A cold part is standing in front of it, guarding something, and that part tends to step aside once it's been heard.
