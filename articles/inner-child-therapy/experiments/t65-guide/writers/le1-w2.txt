In that stretch, you can ask what has actually ended, and what you're still trying to keep going. What isn't known yet? What's your little one afraid will happen in the gap? Maybe it thinks it'll always be like this. What practical act or small ritual would mark the ending, without pretending you know the next beginning? Don't force a new you to show up just because not knowing feels unbearable. The grown-up you can keep your routines and protect your body. You can grieve what ended, and stay with your little one while the next life takes shape.

Missing someone doesn't have to turn into another problem to solve. Sometimes the ache itself can become a cue for love. You're already thinking about them. So don't push the missing away. If love is there, let "I miss you" become "I love you," or offer a simple wish, however you'd say it: "May you be safe. May you be held in love." Picture them, or just feel your care going out to them. It's about what happens in you, so you don't have to believe they receive anything. It doesn't replace grief, contact, boundaries or practical action. If sending love makes you more compulsive or unreal, make it simpler: "I miss you, and I care." Do the same if you can't get back to your life afterward. You can also offer some of the same care to the part of you that's still here.
