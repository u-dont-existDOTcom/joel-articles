For many people, healing eventually includes some forgiveness, or at least getting out from under active resentment. You can't put it on a schedule. And you don't need it first to be safe or to set boundaries. It isn't a condition for holding someone accountable or getting justice, and recovery doesn't wait on it either. While you're actively resenting someone, what you see can narrow, and your little one can stay organized around the injury. So you might want to hurry up and forgive already. But pressure to forgive early gets you an enlightened-looking performance, while the wound goes underground.

What can help is seeing how somebody's conditioning and history shaped the harmful thing they did. Their pain shaped it too, and so did what they could and couldn't handle. Given everything they'd become by that moment, it was the only move they had available. Which might get you wondering who hurt them first. Hurt people often pass on pain they never worked through. When that becomes something you see, and not a moral instruction, you may find yourself forgiving them. The rage, the kind where it's all on them, can soften into something more accurate.

Understanding them doesn't make what they did harmless, or mean they had no other option in theory. Punishment can't go back and give them the awareness or support they didn't have enough of, or the ability. You might wish it could. What's useful is repairing what can be repaired, and making a different response more available now. Accountability stays, and the harm is real. Boundaries and legal consequences can still be right. So can moral judgment and distance, up to permanent no-contact. Understanding them and holding them responsible can go together. So can never speaking to them again and forgiving them. "I understand how you became this way" can sit beside "I now understand why I shouldn't rely on you for this."

Forgiving yourself works the same way, and when it's hard, forgiving other people tends to be harder too. Own what you did ("that one's on me"), and only what was yours. Let yourself feel the remorse. Repair what you can by apologizing and making amends, and by changing the behavior. Then you're allowed to stop holding yourself hostage. That can feel like getting away with something. The responsibility stays, though. Some harms take years. Forgiving too soon is one trap, and the other is living permanently inside resentment. Don't push it. Just keep coming back to how the person who did it became capable of it, until the understanding is real enough to change how you feel.
