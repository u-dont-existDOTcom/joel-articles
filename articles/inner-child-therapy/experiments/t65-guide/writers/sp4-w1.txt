This is for when talking to your little one, or picturing them, only gets you silence. Write the scared or hurting thoughts and feelings with the hand you don't usually write with (the clumsy one). Its writing already looks like a child's, so that's your little one's hand. Then answer with your usual hand, as the grown-up offering love, protection and guidance. If your little one is still silent, it's tempting to make up what they'd say. But write only what's really there, what you actually feel. The protective part might show up first. Then let it have the clumsy hand until it's said its piece.

If thinking things through on paper leaves you more confused, a simpler memory aid is fine. It keeps track of things without you having to work out what they mean at the same time. Looking for patterns can come later, if it's useful at all. A memory aid doesn't have to explain your life. It could be a short note of what happened, with the date, or a note of what helped. A reminder or a next step works too. Or you can leave yourself a voice note.
