In this work, you're steering attention with your words. Unless a painful or negative way of putting it is what needs naming, steer toward what you want to strengthen: an ability or a relationship. Or something you do. "Let's help you build more ways to feel cared for and supported" usually gives the mind a clearer direction than bringing in a label like "dependency" just to deny it or argue with it. Then you're still talking about dependency. In inner-child work and hypnosis especially, make the direction easy to imagine: warmth, protection, steadiness, rest, or whatever is actually needed.

None of this is forced positivity. You know how it feels when somebody rushes you to the bright side. Grief is grief, and danger should be called danger. Harm and anger still need accurate words. So do limits and accountability. Consent too, and whatever practical constraints there are. Once you've named what's real, though, skip any picture of what you shouldn't become if you don't need it, and put most of your energy into what can be protected or practiced. Then there's what can be repaired, and what you can choose or grow.

Before you give advice to someone else or to your little one, it can help to find out what kind of company they want: "Do you want me to mostly listen, help you untangle this, give you ideas, or some mix?" Or however you'd put it. You don't have to ask every time, like a ritual. When it's obvious which they want, go with that. When it isn't, that one sentence can save you from giving advice when they needed to be heard. Or from just listening, when they were asking for help thinking.
