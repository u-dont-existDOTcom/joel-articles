The three grown-up jobs work between people too. A good peer, someone you take turns listening to as equals, can lend you the Nurturer by being there and being warm. They can lend you the Protector by keeping what you say private and sticking to the agreed time. If you say no, they respect it. Maybe your little one is watching for that. They don't supply your Guide, though, or decide what your experience means. A carefully timed question can help your own Guide get heard. They don't interpret or give advice, and the answer is yours. Then you switch roles.

This can be borrowed adulthood at its healthiest. The peer doesn't become your permanent parent. Part of you might wish they would. They show you a capacity and help you take part in it. Then they hand it back to you. Therapy is still useful for what therapy does. Community and disciplined peer support do something different, and a serious healing path can include both.

Community can work in both directions. Sometimes people who love you give you enough safety and belonging that something deeper can open up. Other times the opening comes first, and it changes what kinds of people and relationships you can "manifest." You might wonder what to do with it. Then community helps the opening become something practical and real that your body knows too. There's a loop in all this, and the loop matters more than which door you came in through. Love moves from spiritual experience to your little one, from your little one into the grown-up relationship, from you into other people, and back again.
