For a lot of people, healing eventually includes some kind of forgiveness, or at least getting out from under active resentment. It can't be put on a schedule, though, and you don't need it first to be safe, set boundaries, hold someone accountable or get better. Resentment can narrow what you see and keep your little one organized around the injury. But forcing yourself to forgive early tends to turn into an enlightened performance while the wound goes underground.

What helps me is seeing how someone's conditioning, history, pain and capacity shaped the harmful thing they did. Given everything they'd become by that moment, it was the only move they had available. Hurt people pass on pain they never processed. When you can see that for yourself, instead of being told to, forgiveness sometimes shows up on its own, and the rage that held them as uniquely to blame can soften into something more accurate.

That doesn't make what they did harmless, and it doesn't mean no other option existed in theory. It means punishing them can't go back and create the awareness or support they didn't have. Accountability stays. The harm is real. Boundaries, legal consequences, moral judgment, distance and permanent no-contact can all still be right. You can understand how they got that way, hold them responsible, never speak to them again, and forgive them. "I get how you became this" can sit right next to "and now I get why I can't rely on you for this."

Forgiving yourself works the same way, and when it's hard, forgiving other people tends to be harder too. Once you've faced the responsibility, you're allowed to release it and stop holding yourself hostage. Some harms take years. Forgiving too soon is one trap, and living permanently inside resentment is the other. Now and then, come back to the question of how they became capable of it, without forcing anything, until the understanding is real enough to change how you feel.
