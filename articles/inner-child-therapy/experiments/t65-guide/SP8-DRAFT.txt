If you can't picture your little one without it feeling made up, dig out an actual photo. Then there's no face or posture to invent (it's all there, bangs and everything). Look at it gently and let whatever comes up come up, without deciding what the photo has to prove.

While you look, notice what your body does, and what you feel or want to do, and what that younger you seems to want you to understand. Then sort it into piles, like laundry. What do you remember yourself, and what did someone tell you later? What are you guessing from the picture? And what might be an image, a dream or a metaphor, or just not knowing?

Skip questions like "What abuse must have happened?", "Who did this to me?" or "What memory is my body hiding?" And if you catch yourself grilling the same photo until a story turns up, stop there. Suggestive questions and guided imagery can plant memories of things that never happened. [In one study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03196318), researchers put people's childhood photos into a hot-air balloon ride that never happened, and half of them came to remember it, at least partly.

Remember that somebody chose that moment and framed it. Whatever was happening outside the frame isn't in it, and neither is the reason you looked the way you did. A photo or a jolt in your body can say something real about how you feel today, the way a dream can, and still prove nothing about what happened back then.

If it starts to look like something serious happened, don't confront or accuse anybody based only on what came up here. Write down what came up and how it came up. Then talk it over with someone steady who won't lead you toward an answer. And when you're done, put the photo away. If it keeps pulling you back to search it, or to be certain, stop using it for a while and get yourself regulated in the present first.
