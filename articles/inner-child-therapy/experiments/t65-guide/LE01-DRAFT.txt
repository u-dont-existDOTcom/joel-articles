Your little one often gets loudest in the gap after one life has ended and before the next one has formed. Think of a breakup, a move to another country, an illness, losing your faith or a role, a child leaving home, or leaving a family or community on different terms. Tibetan Buddhism calls an in-between state a bardo. You don't have to buy its teachings about death and rebirth to use the everyday part: endings leave a stretch with no familiar identity and nothing clear to replace it yet.

So ask yourself what has ended, and what you're still trying to keep going. What isn't known yet? What is your little one afraid will happen in the gap? And what small ritual or practical act could mark the ending without pretending you know what comes next? Don't force a new identity just because not knowing feels unbearable. You can keep your routines, take care of your body, grieve what ended and stay with your little one while the next life takes shape.

Missing someone doesn't have to become another problem to solve, either. Sometimes the ache itself can turn into a cue for love. If love is there, let "I miss you" become "I love you," or try a simple wish: "May you be safe. May you be held in love." You can picture them, or just feel the direction of the care. This is about what happens in you. It doesn't depend on them receiving anything, and it doesn't replace grieving, contact, boundaries or doing something practical. If sending love makes you more compulsive or unreal, or you can't get back to your life, make it simpler: "I miss you, and I care."
