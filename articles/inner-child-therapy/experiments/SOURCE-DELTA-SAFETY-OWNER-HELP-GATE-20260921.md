## Exact registered raw-source H2

> ## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child
>
> Most of this guide assumes that the present-day person at least wants to protect the vulnerable part. That cannot always be assumed. Somebody may enjoy frightening or humiliating people, want to exploit vulnerability, or hope therapy will make them more effective at manipulation. In that case, do not invite the child forward—not even briefly to find out how the child reacts. The child’s fear is not a diagnostic test, and the exercise must not become another source of gratification.
>
> Stay with the present-day adult. What help do you actually want? Why are you here? What, if anything, are you suffering from or hoping to change? What does this identity give you, what does it cost, and whom does it serve? Is it producing the life and happiness you expected? If somebody calls themselves sovereign but also says they must obey an urge, doctrine, group, spirit, or master, ask how those claims fit together. Sovereignty begins with the ability to pause, question, refuse, and revise.
>
> This is not a religion test. A religious label, an unusual belief, an intrusive thought, anger, a past harmful act, or an unwanted fantasy does not by itself establish current harmful intent. What matters here is what the person currently endorses and intends to do. Listen without flattering cruelty, arguing theology, or helping somebody become more effective at exploiting people.
>
> A promise is not enough. Protective credibility grows from repeated non-harmful choices and the demonstrated ability not to exploit vulnerability even when doing so would feel rewarding. Until then, keep the work adult-focused and present-focused. Do not use child dialogue, childhood photographs, hypnosis, borrowed-parent imagery, spiritual figures, or another indirect route to put the child in contact with an adult position that still intends harm. Real human care may be more important here than another internal exercise.

## Protected functions that may not be lost

- protective intent is required, not merely regulation/adult capacity;
- current endorsed intent/enjoyment of exploiting or harming vulnerability blocks child contact;
- child is not used as a diagnostic test or gratification source;
- adult-focused work asks what help/change is wanted, what an identity gives/costs, whom it serves, and whether it produces expected life/happiness;
- sovereignty is behavioral capacity to pause/question/refuse/revise under claimed compelled obedience;

- care/listening may continue without flattering cruelty, arguing theology, or improving exploitative skill;

- some people may primarily need sustained real-human care rather than another internal/bot exercise.
