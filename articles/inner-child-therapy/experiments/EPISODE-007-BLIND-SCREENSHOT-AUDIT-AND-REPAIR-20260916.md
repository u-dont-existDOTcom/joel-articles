```markdown
# My Journey

I used to preach [Buddhist enlightenment](http://mindset.u-dont-exist.com/) a lot. Eventually I noticed I could feel spiritually free and still become the same little kid again when an old wound got hit. The spiritual part of me could be free while the kid part was still stuck in the old thing. I came to think this is part of why most people chase spiritual freedom in the first place—some part of us is trying to get above childhood pain, or get away from something in ourselves we don't want to meet. That's [spiritual bypassing](https://goenka.u-dont-exist.com/). I think plenty of seekers and gurus do it too; I've written about the [Dalai Lama as one example](https://substack.com/@udontexist/note/c-245314772?r=5vdc6m).

The way I picture it is almost stupidly simple. Imagine you're broke and starving, and your baby is starving too. *Understandable*: there is no food. Then a food bank opens nearby and suddenly you've got lots of nutritious munchies. But you're eating all of it yourself while baby you is still starving. *Unforgivable.*

Nobody would knowingly leave a baby starving. I just didn't believe the baby was there. Until a few years ago, the whole inner-child idea sounded pretty silly to me. If it still sounds silly to you, I get it. But once I could actually feel him, leaving him out stopped making sense. I had found all this love and freedom and somehow this kid in me was still going hungry.

I don't think that means people who bypass are stupid or crazy. We're doing whatever we've figured out so far. I did the same thing. Reparenting changed for me when it stopped feeling like an idea and started feeling like I was taking care of somebody who was actually there. Instead of trying to get above the pain again, I was finally staying with the one who felt it.

I don't want to spend my whole life staring at the wound either. If inner-child work becomes nothing but managing the hurt kid, the wound is still running the schedule. Spirituality was the thing that showed me my life story wasn't the limit of how much love or happiness I could know.

Maybe ordinary life already gives you enough love and meaning. I didn't feel that way. Conditional love felt radically insufficient to me, and I couldn't stop wondering whether there was something deeper.

### Don’t Give the Inner Adult Away

If you still feel like a kid inside, a person who seems to know what they're doing can have way too much power. You feel small, they feel grown-up, and some part of you wants to go: okay, you drive. Sometimes that's useful. You may really need somebody to hold one grown-up thing with you for a while. That's borrowed adulthood. But if they keep the role instead of helping you take it back, something has gone wrong.

When you feel small, disagreement gets confusing too. A helper can show you something real and still be wrong about you. Even [Pema Chödrön](https://pemachodronfoundation.org/product/taking-the-leap-book/) comes out of a tradition where devotion to a teacher is a big deal, and she still says the teacher should “wean us from dependency altogether” and help us grow up.

If something in your actual life is dangerous, don't ask the kid to meditate their way out of it. Spiritual practice can change how pain feels; it can't make an unsafe situation safe. Sometimes the adult thing is to change the situation or get practical help. Calling real danger merely “your story” doesn't protect the child. The same problem can show up with no-self teachings. If you grew up learning that what you wanted didn't matter, “there is no self” can hit like a trap. You're finally trying to find yourself, and somebody tells you there is no you. Maybe one day that teaching feels freeing. But first you may need enough of a self to stand somewhere. Otherwise it can become one more way to disappear.

Therapy can pull the same trick if every disagreement becomes “resistance.” If you tell a helper “No, this doesn't feel right for me,” they have to be able to consider that you might be right. Otherwise the setup is rigged: agree with them and they're right; disagree and your resistance proves they're right. Sometimes the helper is simply wrong about you. Sometimes their method is trying to take you somewhere you don't want to go. The whole thing should leave me needing them less. If we disagree, I want to be able to keep my feet under me and decide for myself. If I still need them to tell me whether I'm doing adulthood right, I'm still giving them the job. Borrow adulthood if you need to, but don't surrender it.

My own case was weird. I was well-loved as a child, but from age twelve to twenty-six I was depressed and cried every night from feeling the pain of the world. [Loving-kindness (metta) meditation](http://love.u-dont-exist.com/) healed that depression almost instantly for me.

I wasn't lacking love. My parents had given me a lot of it. I found more in [Buddhist teachings—which, it turns out, also include inner-child self-love!](https://substack.com/profile/355021294-u-dont-existcom/note/c-297324975) and later in [loveyhuasca](http://soma.u-dont-exist.com/). Metta gave me a way to turn that love toward myself.

But it didn't reach everything. A lot of the child stuff around my attachment to women was still there. When I later tried [self-directed inner-child therapy](https://youtu.be/yEWFjODXJbI?si=IXUqrhExojOm6Itm), it felt like I was making it up at first. I used San Pedro cactus and CBD tea with sublingual mapacho, and that cracked it open; structured sessions with an inner-child therapist deepened it.

That's where the method stopped being as simple as “find the wounded child and love them.” The map still needs an adult who can do those three jobs. Sometimes protective parts were guarding the door. Other times I was so much the child that there was no adult around to do them. Borrowed adulthood came from that second problem.
```
