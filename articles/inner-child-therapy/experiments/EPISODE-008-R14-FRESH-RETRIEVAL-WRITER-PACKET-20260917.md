## Locked preceding prose — communication calibration

### Catch the Hook Before the Story Takes Over

You might notice the hook before you have any clue what part of you is doing the hooking. Or you might notice it embarrassingly late. The jaw and stomach are already tight, and/or half the text is written, and then suddenly it dawns you: you're supposed to be observing yourself. 😀 Pema Chödrön calls this tightening and urge before the story has finished forming, "shenpa." Maybe forget the parts detective work for a minute. You can figure that out later.

When you catch it, look around and just take a moment to enjoy some free honesty: "Oh! I'm hooked again! That's awesome I could notice it!" If nobody is in danger, take a breath or three before you finish whatever you were doing. Sometimes human beings need to be being, not always doing. 🙃 Maybe then you'll find there's enough adult around to be nice to the kid for a second. Or enough Protector to stop something you'd regret later.

Here's where I want to clearly depart from the new age wisdom that all your problems are self-created: Somebody may really have crossed a boundary and still have hit something old in you. You felt your stomach tighten up, and that somehow meant “They're evil.” But you can defer that judgement for when you're in your happy place.

## Meaning that must survive in the new subsection

The next thought is to look outward too. One ugly conversation, somebody needing time, disagreement, or somebody holding a boundary does not by itself prove emotional immaturity. But what a person repeatedly does with your experience matters: whether there is room for it, whether responsibility is ever possible, and what you can realistically rely on. Keep the link to [Lindsay Gibson's work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) on emotional immaturity, but use it as support rather than turning the subsection into a checklist. You may never know cleanly whether somebody cannot do something, will not do it, or just cannot do it now; protection does not have to wait for perfect motive-reading, and you can update your view when behavior actually changes. Promises alone are not the change, and you do not need another injury merely to prove the pattern.

Guilt can complicate this because you may genuinely have hurt somebody and owe repair while also abandoning your own position to stop their disappointment. Guilt by itself proves neither that you are wrong nor that they are manipulating you. And sometimes the realistic outcome of a conversation is not mutual understanding: conveying something, saying no, or ending the exchange without another round of self-defense can be enough. You do not need to win the whole case before acting. Keep any observing/protective/next-move functions implicit here; do not introduce a capitalized `Witness` concept in this subsection.
