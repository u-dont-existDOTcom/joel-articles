# Repeats check 2: inner child article

Scope notes:
- The prompt's scope sentence came through garbled. It says to report "only repeats and contradictions that involve at least one block inside that h1", and it also says to "check the rest of the article against itself". Sections 1 to 3 below follow the narrow rule: every item involves at least one block from B205 to B231 (the h1 "Sometimes There Isn't a Clear Child Yet"). What I found when checking the rest of the article against itself is in a separate appendix at the end, so it can be dropped if the narrow rule was meant.
- I treated the two chart images (B0, B28) as summaries and did not count them as repeats. B28's "no clear child yet" panel previews this h1 point by point, including B210's "reparent and build who you are at the same time".
- Quotes are copied exactly. Each is shorter than 25 words, and some are cut mid-sentence.

## 1. Repeats involving the h1 (in article order)

**1. B32 and B206–B207**
- B32: “Say you grew up learning that what you wanted didn't matter. Now you're only just finding out what you actually think”
- B207: “"What do I believe?" or "What do I want?" has no clear answer of your own”
- Shared point: someone whose wants didn't count as a child is only now finding out what they think and want.
- Later instance: adds something. B206–B207 add that the self may never have formed and that "love yourself" can feel empty. B32 used the same portrait to show how a "no self" teaching or a therapist can sound like the old message. What repeats is the portrait, not an instruction, but a reader may recognize the same person.
- Better place: B206–B207, because this person is the subject of the h1, while in B32 they are only an example.

**2. B62, B213 and B230**
- B62: “"I feel attracted" or "I feel ashamed" is real as a feeling, but it doesn't settle who you are”
- B213: “Any words you put to them can stay tentative ("I hate cooking... or just for everybody?")”
- B230: “Or that you're not "really" that kind of person, which is a lot to decide from one try.”
- Shared point: one feeling or one attempt doesn't settle who you are, so hold the conclusion loosely.
- Later instances: each adds something. B213 adds a method: tentative wording that you revise. B230 adds where the snap verdict comes from (the old parent) and the beginner setting. Still, B230's "a lot to decide from one try" reads as B62's caution again.
- Better: B62 carries it more fully, with both swings of the pendulum. B213 and B230 sit in the more natural place, the identity h1, while B62 is an identity aside inside a section about hooks.

**3. B62, B215 and B225**
- B62: “what feels authentically yours vs something planted or manipulated into being there from society, influencers, family, friends, religion, etc., etc.”
- B215: “untangling yourself from whatever pulls you away from your own knowing in return for belonging”
- B225: “Questioning what you were programmed to believe while holding the discomfort, and without having to stop loving anyone over it.”
- Shared point: sort out what is your own from what family, religion or society put there.
- Later instances: they add the motive (belonging), the bargains, and keeping your love for people while you question. B62 adds giving it time to see which patterns last. The two are about 150 blocks apart, but a reader who remembers B62 would feel the h1 making the point again at length.
- Better: B215 and B225, because the h1 is the section built for differentiation and B62 is a compressed aside inside "Catch the Hook".

**4. B71, B184 and B225**
- B71: “are you starting to take back the complaint too because they're still upset?”
- B184: “Or saying no to someone, and not texting an hour later to take it back. That second one can be really hard.”
- B225: “Disappointing people and withstanding social pressure, and still being recognizably yourself wherever you are”
- Shared point: keep your no or your position when the other person is upset or disappointed, rather than taking it back. It's hard and slow.
- Later instance: B225 turns it into a general differentiation habit, and its first bullet adds that "Their reasons can still change your mind". Even so, a reader would likely feel this bullet as B184 again, down to the closing tag: B225's "That one can take a while." follows B184's "That second one can be really hard."
- Better: B184, because it ties the point to one concrete act. B225 is the general restatement.

**5. B128, B167 and B230**
- B128: “Calling yourself an idiot for losing your keys again, for one. Your little one was listening”
- B167: “measures whatever you show the world against an impossible ideal, and then attacks you from both sides”
- B230: “The old parent in your head may demand immediate talent, though. Then your first awkward attempt becomes "evidence" that you're stupid or clumsy.”
- Shared point: the inner critic turns a mistake into a verdict on you, and the grown-up you protects the little one from it.
- Later instance: adds the beginner setting and a ready reply ("You're allowed to be bad at this..."). Readers will mostly recognize B230 as a callback to the critic. The part that repeats is the instruction to protect your little one from your own attacks, which B128 already gave.
- Better: B230 for this case, because it shows the attack and the answer in one place. B167 explains the critic once, and B230 applies it.

**6. B166, B206, B209 and B217**
- B166: “your little one learned bit by bit to hide that part of their authentic self which wasn't allowed, so they could keep belonging”
- B206: “if you grew up somewhere you only belonged as long as you played somebody else”
- B209: “when curiosity or mistakes got you punished, or it felt like they loved you less, you got better at the part.”
- B217: “Your little one, with nobody parenting it, takes those bargains to survive, because belonging feels like life or death.”
- Shared point: as a child you hid your own self, or gave it up, to keep belonging.
- Later instances: each adds something. B206 says the real one may never have formed, not just hidden. B209 adds how it happened (attention used up reading danger, curiosity punished) and what it cost (no room for experiments). B217 adds "Even with good parents" and being praised for what you could do. But the origin story itself is told three times inside the h1, at its start, at the start of its first h2, and again in the bargains, after B166 had already told it. A reader would feel told again at B209 and at B217.
- Better: B209, because it gives the mechanism and the cost and leads straight into the practice.

**7. B209, B221 and B229 (with B213)**
- B209: “That left much less room for experiments that show you what you like when nobody's grading you.”
- B213: “what you believe and feel when nobody's around to take your cues from”
- B221: “Would this still be something I'd feel is worth doing if at the end of the day, I didn't get praise”
- B229: “Notice what holds your attention anyway, when nobody is impressed.”
- Shared point: you find out what's yours by what holds up when nobody is grading, praising or watching.
- Later instances: each adds a step. B209 names what was missing, B213 applies it to private reflection, B221 turns it into a check on what you already do, and B229 is the real-life experiment. But the "when nobody's grading / around / impressed" condition comes back each time, and B209 and B229 read as the same line.
- Better: B229, because it's where the reader actually does it.

**8. B210 and B231**
- B210: “Some of this is developmental pl/ork you get to do late: making it safe enough for your curiosity and experimenting to finally grow”
- B231: “which is pl/ork in its most literal form: play becomes part of forming your identity.”
- Shared point: experimental play here is late identity-building pl/ork.
- Later instance: adds the discovery of "a part of yourself that never had permission to exist". The claim that this play is identity-forming pl/ork is the same, though, so a reader may feel B231 announcing what B210 already said.
- Better: B231, because it lands after the concrete practice. B210 states the point before the reader has anything to attach it to.

**9. B212 and B225**
- B212: “If your preferences and beliefs shift a lot with the people around you, that may be the part you've been playing.”
- B225: “Noticing when what you thought you knew suddenly feels like water dripping through your hand because of praise or pity, fear or attraction”
- Shared point: your views give way under other people's influence, and that's a sign to notice.
- Later instance: adds the specific pressures and makes it a moment to catch, as does the bullet before it ("Wait, why was I nodding along?"). B212 treats it as a sign of the part you've been playing. They are close enough that a reader may feel it twice.
- Better: B225, because it gives the triggers and the noticing, while B212 only diagnoses.

**10. B215 and B217**
- B215: “But if going along means betraying yourself, that's the bargain.”
- B217: “Self-differentiation is noticing where you're betraying yourself to belong, and learning to keep your own ground instead.”
- Shared point: the bargain is betraying yourself to belong.
- Later instance: adds the name (self-differentiation) and the alternative (keep your own ground). But the two paragraphs are a block apart and both close on "betraying yourself", so a reader gets the definition twice.
- Better: B217, because it carries both the problem and the way out.

**11. B217 and B222 (with B215 and B223)**
- B215: “Most groups expect some conformity, which is ordinary.”
- B217: “These can all be real gifts. The bargain begins when your worth depends on them”
- B222: “You don't have to stop enjoying pleasure, praise, gain or belonging. The question is whether losing one of them would feel like losing yourself.”
- B223: “You'd be belonging selectively, which still leaves room to belong.”
- Shared point: the good things (gifts, praise, belonging) are fine, and the trouble starts when your worth or self depends on them.
- Later instance: B222 widens it from gifts to the four pairs and turns it into a test question. It has the same shape as B217, a permission followed by a test, five blocks later. A reader would feel told the same thing again. B223 adds staying inside the group.
- Better: B222, because "would losing one feel like losing yourself" is the more usable form. B217 sits where the bargain is defined.

**12. B223 and B225**
- B223: “As a differentiated adult you can stay deeply embedded, free to question things or disagree.”
- B225: “Questioning what you were programmed to believe while holding the discomfort, and without having to stop loving anyone over it.”
- Shared point: you can question and disagree without leaving or loving anyone less.
- Later instance: adds holding the discomfort. Because B224 introduces the list as what differentiation looks like day to day, this bullet reads as B223 again in list form. Mild.
- Better: B223, because it states the claim with its reason (belonging selectively) and with examples from family, faith and relationships.

## 2. Contradictions involving the h1

**C1. B62 and B213: get help to see the pattern, or reflect with no outside input**
- B62: “In that case, sometimes it helps to meditate or talk with a good unbiased therapist who can help you see the pattern.”
- B213: “the practice is finding out who's actually in there, through long reflection with no outside input”
- What pulls apart: B62's case is someone in flux with "nothing solid about you", which is close to the person this h1 is for. B62 suggests an outside helper, and B213 says the early practice is reflection with no outside input.
- Does the article say why: no. A reader could reconcile them: a therapist who reflects your pattern back is different from people whose cues you take. But the text never says whether an unbiased therapist counts as outside input. Mild.
- Better place: B213, because it is the h1's own instruction and B62 is an aside.

**C2. B6 and B212 against B206: is there a little one in there or not**
- B6: “the sweet, innocent, curious, vivacious little one is still inside us, even if hiding.”
- B206: “the real one may never have had room to form.”
- B212: “maybe all you get is a guess, which isn't proof there's no little one in there.”
- What pulls apart: B6 says the little one is still inside most of us. B206 says that for some people the real one may never have formed. Six blocks later, B212 reassures that a guess isn't proof there's no little one.
- Does the article say why: partly. B206's "for a lot of people there is" and B210's "reparent and build who you are at the same time" point to a reconciliation: the child is there, and what never formed is its own sense of who it is. But the h1 never says whether "the real one" means the little one or the self it would have grown into. So B206 and B212 can read as raising a doubt and then denying it. Mild.
- Better place: B212 together with B210, which hold the reconciliation.

**C3. B209 and B212: changing your mind is growth, or a sign of the played part**
- B209: “People grow into who they are by trying things on and then changing their minds”
- B212: “If your preferences and beliefs shift a lot with the people around you, that may be the part you've been playing.”
- What pulls apart: B209 treats changing your mind, including about ideas, as how people grow. Three blocks later, shifting preferences and beliefs are a sign of the performed part.
- Does the article say why: only through "with the people around you". It doesn't say how to tell trying things on from going along. Mild, and a careful reader can reconcile them.
- Better place: B212, which carries the distinction, thin as it is.
