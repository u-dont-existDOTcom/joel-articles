## Exact candidate

### Write It. Don't Send It Yet.

Write the version you're glad nobody else can see yet. Let it be angry if you're angry. Let it say no badly. Let it accuse, complain, beg, whatever was already trying to come out. Then keep your thumb off Send.

When you come back to it, don't make the writer the problem. Some lines may just be trying to hurt back; those can go. But the sentence that says “you can't talk to me like that” might be the sane part. If you owe an apology, put the apology in. If there's a real request or boundary, don't sand it down until it disappears. The better message might be gentler. It might also be two sentences instead of twelve.

You can send the edited version, send less, wait, or say nothing. If you need time, say you need time. If you promise a fuller answer tomorrow, mean it. And if somebody needs protecting now, forget the writing exercise and deal with that first.

Later, read the ugly draft again without fixing it. What was that person trying to make somebody understand? What did they need that the polished message still doesn't give them? Angry is not automatically childish. Sometimes the draft you never send is the first place the protest came out clearly. You can clean up the communication without cleaning that person out of the room.
