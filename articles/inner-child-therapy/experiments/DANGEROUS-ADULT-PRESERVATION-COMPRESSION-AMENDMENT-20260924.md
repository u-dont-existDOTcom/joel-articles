## Why this amendment exists

Repeated candidates B, E, F, H, I, and J kept recreating the same section-scale instruction-manual cadence even when sentence wording and paragraph order changed.

The failure mechanism was not merely prose style. The writer was treating each source function and each source example/subcriterion as an explicit sentence-level obligation.

That conflicts with the active preservation protocol and Joel's voice/recovery rule:

- `docs/HUMANIZATION-PRESERVATION-GATE.md` allows `implicit-equivalent` when explicit source wording is only scaffolding/unpacking/aftercare and the same cognition/function remains unambiguous.
- `project-sources/VOICE-REFERENCE.md` says owner answers/source cognition are not a transcript, sentence bank, or mandatory inventory; preserve the actual thought route rather than question order.

The goal remains full semantic/function preservation. This amendment changes **realization requirements**, not owner meaning.

## Core claims that must remain explicit

These are independent decision-relevant claims and may not be left merely inferable:

1. Adult capacity/presence is not sufficient when present protective intent is absent.
2. Current endorsement/intent to frighten, humiliate, exploit, or harm vulnerability blocks child contact.
3. The child must not be used as a diagnostic test of adult safety; fear/reaction cannot become gratification.
4. While that harmful intent remains, work stays with the present-day adult rather than the child.
5. Beliefs/thoughts/history alone are not the gate; present endorsement and intent are.
6. A verbal promise alone does not establish safety; repeated non-harmful behavior matters.
7. Direct and indirect child-contact routes remain blocked while harmful intent remains.
8. Continued care is allowed without assisting exploitation, and some people may need sustained real-human care.

## Functions that may be consolidated / implicit-equivalent

These source functions remain protected but do not each require their own sentence, list item, or complete explicit inventory.

### Adult-focused inquiry

Source unpacking:
- what help/change is actually wanted;
- what the identity/stance gives and costs;
- whom it serves;
- whether it produces the expected life/happiness.

Allowed compressed realization:
- one reader-clear statement that the adult work examines what the person wants changed and whether the stance is actually serving the life they want, including consequences for themselves/others.

The separate `gives / costs / whom serves / expected happiness` questions are **not mandatory as a four-part inventory** if the surviving sentence carries the same evaluation function.

### Sovereignty

Source unpacking:
- pause;
- question;
- refuse;
- revise.

Allowed compressed realization:
- behavioral ability to disobey/reconsider/change course when an urge or claimed authority demands obedience.

The four verbs are examples of agency, not four mandatory micro-claims.

### False-positive exclusions

Source examples:
- religious identity;
- unusual beliefs;
- intrusive thoughts;
- anger;
- unwanted fantasy;
- charged language;
- past wrongdoing.

Allowed compressed realization:
- a compact distinction covering beliefs/religion, unwanted thoughts/feelings, and past wrongdoing as insufficient by themselves, provided the sentence clearly preserves the controlling contrast with present endorsement/intent.

Every source example need not receive equal explicit space.

### Care without assistance

Source unpacking:
- listen/care;
- do not flatter cruelty;
- do not argue theology;
- do not improve exploitative skill.

Allowed compressed realization:
- care/listening can continue without validating or assisting harm; the person's beliefs do not have to be settled to maintain this boundary.

The three prohibitions do not each need a separate beat.

### Indirect routes

Source examples:
- child dialogue;
- childhood photographs;
- hypnosis;
- borrowed-parent imagery;
- spiritual figures;
- another indirect route.

Allowed compressed realization:
- no direct or indirect contact with the child, with enough representative examples to make clear that proxy/imagined routes are also covered.

The route list is illustrative, not a required exhaustive taxonomy.
