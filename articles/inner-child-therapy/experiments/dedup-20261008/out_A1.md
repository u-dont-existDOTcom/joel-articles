# Repeats and contradictions in the inner child article

Quotes sit inside « » and are copied exactly from article_blocks.md. Each repeat has a strength label: strong (a reader will notice), moderate (a reader will probably notice), light (a near-echo worth knowing about before new text goes in).

## 1. Repeats, in article order

**1. B10, B21, B29, B76. The point: inner-child work or therapy isn't meant to become permanent; the aim is to need it less. (moderate)**
- B10: «Our lives can become small and like a never-ending therapy session when we focus only on caring for ourselves»
- B21: «Spending your whole life processing your wounded inner child isn't freedom either.»
- B29: «she still says a teacher should “wean us from dependency altogether” and help us grow up»
- B76: «Real healing is ultimately measured by reduced need for the therapy, just like real growing up is not something you do forever.»
- What the later ones add: B21 adds the bigger spiritual horizon. B29 applies it to dependence on a teacher. B76 turns it into a test. Still, at B76 a reader hears "not forever" and "grow up" for the third or fourth time.
- Better: B76. It turns the claim into a measure the reader can actually use.

**2. B11–B12, B25. The point: you can be so far inside the child that no adult is left, so you need an adult there before you can be one. (moderate)**
- B11: «What if you’re so far inside the child-state that you can’t become the adult?»
- B25: «get pulled so far into being him that there wasn't a grown-up me around anymore»
- B25: «I started looking for a way to have an adult there before I could reliably be that adult myself»
- What the later one adds: B25 adds the personal account (getting past a protective part, then being pulled in). But it brings in the fix as if it were new, after B12 has already named it «borrowed adulthood».
- Better: B11–B12. They name the problem and the fix. B25 works as the origin story but reads like a second introduction.

**3. B12, B14 (stage 3), B106. The point: do one piece of the adult job yourself while someone else still does the rest. (strong for B14, moderate for B106)**
- B12: «try one piece yourself while they're still helping with the rest»
- B14: «Practice adulting — do at least one part of caring for yourself while support is still there from the previous step.»
- B106: «Ten seconds of sitting with yourself like that is good enough for a start, so try it before you call next time.»
- What the later ones add: stage 3 in B14 adds nothing; it restates B12's sentence two blocks later. B106 adds a concrete case (copy how a friend sits with you without fixing you, and try it before calling them).
- Better: B12 carries the method (watch, copy, imagine them there). B106 is the best example of it.

**4. B14 (stage 1), B33, B49. The point: if you're basically the frightened kid or flooded, don't go deep yet; get present and safe first, maybe with someone steady. (moderate)**
- B14: «You're still fully identified as the child» … «make sure there's enough safety around you to continue»
- B33: «If you can't do that because you're basically the frightened kid, leave the deeper conversation until later.»
- B33: «You may need somebody steady there with you» (which echoes B12: «Sometimes somebody else may have to do one part of the adult role with you or for you first.»)
- B49: «Some people go straight into EMDR on their worst memory before they can even stay in the room.»
- What the later ones add: B33 adds a self-test (look around the room, pause before you act, make a choice and follow through on it). B49 adds the body side (a flooded nervous system, EMDR). B33 still reads as a restart of stage 1 plus B12.
- Better: B33. It gives the test, and it opens "Before You Try to Go Deep", where a readiness check belongs.

**5. B14 (stage 2), B84, B86. The point: Mr. Rogers as an adult to borrow from. (strong for B84 and B86)**
- B14: «remember watching Mr. Rogers or Sesame Street? Those adult role models are what we're looking for at this stage.»
- B84: «Even Mr. Rogers could help here if you felt some nice vibes from his show as a kid.»
- B86: «If nobody comes to mind, you can look to examples like Mr. Rogers, or spiritual love you've felt, if you have.»
- What the later ones add: B86 narrows it to warmth and adds spiritual love. Because it comes two blocks after B84, the same suggestion lands twice in a row.
- Better: B86. Offering Mr. Rogers as the fallback for warmth matches B86's condition that the person «liked you the way you were».

**6. B15, B173. The point: right words in a session aren't enough; a small act out in your life teaches your little one more. (moderate)**
- B15: «saying the right thing when you sit down with your little one helps, but you also have to start actually doing some of it»
- B173: «One small thing like that can teach your little one more than ten more good meditations.»
- What the later one adds: B173 adds the case where the session itself works and daily life doesn't change, plus the misreading "I didn't go deep enough". Both use a self-care-over-overwork example (lying down instead of one more chore; not skipping lunch or saying yes to everything). The same idea also runs through B76 ("it should improve your daily life") and B166 ("mostly out in ordinary life").
- Better: B173. It shows exactly when the point matters and why a session's success doesn't carry over by itself.

**7. B19, B24, B145–B146. The point: it feels silly or made up until you actually feel the child, sometimes first in an altered state. (light)**
- B19: «Inner-child stuff sounded silly to me until I could actually feel him.»
- B24: «it felt like I was making it up at first.» … «and that cracked it open»
- B145: «it's worth meditating first on why it feels fake.»
- B146: «For some people it only stops feeling fake in an altered state, like on MDMA or deep in meditation.»
- What the later ones add: B145–B146 turn the story into guidance and add new steps: memories that feel real aren't proof, and you can carry the felt sense back into ordinary life. A reader who remembers My Journey will recognize the arc, though.
- Better: B146 for the guidance. B19 and B24 sit naturally as story.

**8. B22, B23. The point: I was well loved; my parents gave me plenty of love. (strong, adjacent)**
- B22: «I was well-loved as a child, but from age twelve to twenty-six I was depressed»
- B23: «I wasn't lacking love. My parents had given me a lot of it.»
- What the later one adds: nothing on this point. The reader is told it again in the very next block before B23 moves on to its new point (more love from Buddhism, and metta gave it somewhere to land).
- Better: B22. The contrast "well-loved, but depressed" is where the fact matters.

**9. B22, B40. The point: loving-kindness (metta) got me out of my depression. (moderate)**
- B22: «healed that depression almost instantly for me»
- B40: «I'm biased on loving-kindness, since it got me out of my depression.»
- What the later one adds: B40 uses the fact as a disclosure of bias, which is new, but the claim itself is told again.
- Better: B22. It's the story's home, in My Journey.

**10. B35, B38, B40. The point: for someone with real malice, the work starts with the adult and the inner child stays out of it. (moderate, within one subsection)**
- B35: «For such folks, inner child therapy is clearly not the place to start.»
- B38: «If some of it turns out to be malice they actually want to act on, the inner child can wait.»
- B40: «For these folks it starts with the grown-up they are now, though, and their little one stays out of it.»
- What the later ones add: B38 adds a self-check (real malice, or a critic's voice?), and B40 adds the remedy (loving-kindness for ill will). Even so, "keep the child out, start with the adult" comes three times in six blocks. B41 then adds something new: when the child gets a turn.
- Better: B35. It states the point once at the top of the subsection, with its reason (take apart the adult identity first).

**11. B37, B38. The point: if your own thoughts frighten you, don't bring your inner child into them yet. (moderate, adjacent)**
- B37: «why would you bring your inner child into the middle of them?»
- B38: «they'd do better to meditate on it and find out why they're asking before they bring their inner child anywhere near it.»
- What the later one adds: B38 adds what to do instead (meditate, and check whether it's real malice or a voice like the inherited critic). The instruction itself is the same, one block later.
- Better: B38. It gives the reader something to do. B37's own contributions are the reassurance and «you can wait until you're calm».

**12. B37–B38, B54. The point: a thought or feeling doesn't settle who you are; check whether it's yours or something planted. (light)**
- B37: «Everybody gets scary thoughts they'd rather not have sometimes, and they don't make you a bad person»
- B38: «Are they really feeling malicious, or is it some voice in their head saying so, like the inherited critic?»
- B54: «"I feel attracted" or "I feel ashamed" is real as a feeling, but it doesn't settle who you are, or who you love.»
- B54: «what feels authentically yours vs something planted or manipulated into being there»
- What the later one adds: B54 moves to identity (attraction, shame, beliefs) and adds a time test (see which patterns last). The topics differ, so this is an echo, not a full repeat.
- Better: B54. It's the general statement, and it has the time test.

**13. B39, B118. The point: anger or ill will feels like power but only hurts the one who has it, and seeing that for yourself loosens it. (moderate)**
- B39: «ill will shows up in his mind and he sees it's hurting him» … «Ill will can make you feel powerful.»
- B118: «Anger is a way to get more power when you feel a lack of justice or agency.» … «I saw for myself that it was only hurting me»
- What the later one adds: B118 adds that the anger was a bid to be heard, and that being answered with love took away the need. The situations differ (ill will in an adult, a five-year-old at bedtime), but "power, yet it only hurts me, and I saw it" is the same pair of claims.
- Better: B118. It shows the claim in a lived scene and ties it to the little one.

**14. B43–B45, B156–B157. The point: nothing may "answer", so don't invent a voice; the answer may come as a feeling or a hunch. (strong)**
- B43: «If you don't hear a little kid talking in your head, don't invent one.»
- B44: «Sometimes nothing “answers” and you just know what would be nice to do for yourself.»
- B156: «If nothing comes, that's fine for now. No need to answer for that part just because you'd set this up as a healing session.»
- B157: «it could come in different forms, like as a voice or just a hunch. It's often more like a feeling.»
- What the later ones add: B156–B157 apply it to a protective part rather than the little one, and add a fallback for when nothing answers (notice what sets the reaction off and what it pushes you toward). The core reassurance is told again, though. B42–B45 is a whole subsection on exactly this.
- Better: B42–B45. It's the dedicated subsection. The fallback in B156 is the only new piece.

**15. B44, B77. The point: if the practice starts to feel like an obligation, stop. (light)**
- B44: «And if that starts feeling like homework you're doing for a child who isn't answering, drop it.»
- B77: «If these exercises start feeling like a ritual you have to complete before you're allowed to live, stop there.»
- What the later one adds: B77 widens it from one exercise to the whole practice. Both sentences have the same "if it starts feeling like X, stop" shape, in the same top-level section. B172's «stopping counts» has a different trigger (it's making things worse).
- Better: B77. It's broader and more final, and it closes the checking subsection.

**16. B47, B130. The point: slower exhales for a tight stomach. (light)**
- B47: «try some non-forced, slower exhales and a little gentle self-massage.»
- B130: «If your stomach tightens while your little one waits for an answer, slower exhales may help you stay with them.»
- What the later one adds: B130 adds a new moment (while the little one waits) and a caution (feeling calmer doesn't mean their complaint is wrong). Mostly new; only the technique repeats.
- Better: B47 for the technique, since it's the body subsection. B130's new content is the caution.

**17. B51, B154, B155. The point: don't get stuck working out which part is which. (moderate)**
- B51: «Maybe forget the parts detective work for a minute. You can figure that out later.»
- B154: «How you picture all that is up to you, as long as it helps, and the simpler the better.»
- B155: «I wouldn't spend long deciding what to call each part, though.»
- What the later ones add: B155 adds a reason (every part learns from how the others treat it). B154 and B155 make the point back to back, after B51 already made it.
- Better: B155. It's in the parts section and gives a reason. B51's version suits the in-the-moment hook.

**18. B51–B52, B66–B67, B87. The point: pause before you send the angry message. (strong)**
- B51: «half the text is written, and then suddenly it dawns on you»
- B52: «If nobody is in danger, take a breath or three before you finish whatever you were doing.» … «Or enough Protector to stop something you'd regret later.»
- B66: «But don't send it. Take that breather you earned and then come back to it from a cooler place.»
- B67: «so you don't end up regretting how you communicated later»
- B87: «If you're about to send an angry text, hit pause.»
- What the later ones add: B66–B67 add the line «I need some time to think about my response», an exception for when a reply can't wait, and writing it all out first (where B52 has you pause before finishing). B87 adds only the Protector label. It's a one-liner standing in for a whole earlier subsection, so a reader will feel told again.
- Better: B66. It's the dedicated subsection and the fullest version.

**19. B53, B63. The point: a strong feeling during a conflict isn't a verdict on who's to blame. (moderate)**
- B53: «Just because your stomach tightened up doesn't mean their boundary crossing indicates “They're evil.”»
- B63: «Guilt may keep shouting at you. That doesn't tell you the whole conflict was your fault, nor does it prove they're manipulating you.»
- What the later one adds: B63 applies it to guilt, in both directions (not proof that it was your fault, and not proof that they're manipulating you). It's the same "feeling isn't proof" move in the same section, one subsection later. B54 deliberately extends it to feelings about yourself («Feelings about yourself work the same way»).
- Better: B53. It introduces the idea with the body cue, and the next block extends it.

**20. B53, B104. The point: your own response is yours to change; other people's choices aren't. (light)**
- B53: «Your reaction is what you can control the most, though.»
- B104: «change what's yours to change, and accept what isn't, which is where other people's choices go.»
- What the later one adds: B104 frames it as the Serenity Prayer and applies it to a betrayal by a spiritual teacher.
- Better: B104. It states the point fully. In B53 it's one clause in a crowded paragraph.

**21. B55, B157, B158, B159. The point: the safety exceptions (unwanted touch, real danger, medical warning signs) mean act first and understand later. (strong)**
- B55: «if you don't want to be touched, say no now and work out what it means later.»
- B55: «Same if something might be dangerous right now, or might be a medical warning sign: step away or get checked first.»
- B157: «if you're in danger, get safe first»

- B159: «If a part hesitates about being touched or about sex, that's enough to stop.»
- What the later ones add: B159 adds sex and «It might not be able to tell you why», and extends the stop to going deeper or into altered states. B158 comes at danger through anxiety's predictions. Even so, the same set of three exceptions comes back almost as a unit, and B157's parenthetical makes the danger caution twice in adjacent blocks. Related but different situations: B61 («even once, old stuff or not, get safe first», about a partner who has hurt or threatened you) and B87 («If the room itself triggers your spidey sense, leave, or lock the door and get help.»).
- Better: B158–B159. They're fuller and qualify a specific rule (hear the fear out, then go see). In B55 the exceptions are a short list of examples.

## 2. Contradictions and pulls in opposite directions

**1. B37 vs. B137 and B154. The conflict: you choose when to engage the child vs. you respond whenever the child shows up.**
- B37: «The nice thing about inner child therapy is that you can wait until you're calm and actually ready.» (plus «A real parent doesn't get to do that»)
- B137: «Whenever your little one shows up looking for their parent» … «you go back to being the one they can rely on»
- B154: «If it sounds like your little one, though, hear it out the first time it shows up.»
- Explained? Not fully. The settings differ (B37 is about choosing to bring the child into your frightening thoughts; B137 and B154 are about the child arriving on its own), but nothing says what to do when the little one shows up while you're not calm or ready.
- Which way the article leans: toward B137 and B154. The trust sections all depend on showing up reliably.

**2. B33 vs. B80. The conflict: wait if you're basically the frightened kid vs. just noticing is enough to start.**
- B33: «If you can't do that because you're basically the frightened kid, leave the deeper conversation until later.»
- B80: «That might be the most adult thing you've got right now, and it's plenty to start with.»
- Explained? No. They may mean different starts (the deeper conversation vs. beginning to borrow an adult), but the article doesn't say so.
- Which is more qualified: B33. It gives a concrete test. B80 doesn't say what "start" means.

**3. B86 vs. B111. The conflict: warmth has to come first (borrow it) vs. acts come first and warmth comes later.**
- B86: «Most of us wouldn't open up to somebody who'd only just stopped being mean, or let them take care of us.» … «That takes warmth, and if there isn't any yet, borrow some.»
- B111: «you'd think you have to feel it first before you could do anything for them.» … «the relationship can start with the Protector's plain acts and the warmth can come after»
- Explained? Partly. B111 calls warmth-first a natural but backwards assumption, which is the very position B86 took, without pointing back to it. A possible reconciliation (B86 is about the little one letting you care for them; B111 is about protective acts that don't need their consent) is never stated.
- Which way the article leans: toward B111. B85, B150 and B135 also have the reader act before feeling.

**4. B159 vs. B172. The conflict: a part's "no" is enough to stop vs. hear a protective "nope" out first.**
- B159: «I'd stop there too. It might not be able to tell you why.»
- B172: «if that "nope" sounds like a part of you that's trying to protect you, I'd hear it out first.»
- Explained? No. Both end by respecting a lasting no (B172: «If it still says no, that's your answer»). But in B172 the "but" makes a protective part's no less final than plain not-wanting, while B159 treats it as final with no reason needed. The settings differ slightly (going deeper in a session vs. quitting the practice), and the article doesn't say so.
- Which is better placed: B159. It sits with the other "don't test this" exceptions.

**5. B14 (stage 2) and B87 vs. B145. The conflict: acting the adult before it feels real is fine vs. reaching out when you don't believe the child is real may harm them.**
- B14: «This can almost feel like playing house as a kid where you imagined how you'd take care of a baby doll etc.»
- B87: «Even if this still feels like the child acting like the adult, for now, that's ok.»
- B145: «it could even be like unintentional gaslighting to the little one to ask to connect with them when you don't believe they're even real.»
- Explained? Partly. A few blocks later, B150 separates acts from feelings: «You don't have to pretend to feel anything to let a phone ring.» Nothing says whether B145's caution applies to the pretend play endorsed in stage 2.
- Which is more qualified: B145. It names the risk and the step to take first (meditate on why it feels fake).

**6. B43 vs. B81. The conflict: "ask your little one what they need" is confusing vs. a script that asks exactly that. (minor)**
- B43: «the usual inner-child prompt to “ask your little one what they need” can get confusing»
- B81: «Little Joel is terrified. What does Little Joel need right now?»
- Explained? No. They fit together if B81's question is one you answer yourself (as B44 suggests: «think a sentence on purpose»), but B81 doesn't say so.
- Which is better placed: B43. It explains what "ask" does and doesn't mean.
