## Group 1: depth (would go right after [B33], the first paragraph under "# Before You Try to Go Deep")

### Guide passage

Reparenting needs enough adult capacity to pause, compare options, remain in the present, and act. It also needs enough protective intent that bringing the child forward will not put vulnerability in the hands of somebody who wants to exploit it. When somebody is flooded or identified almost completely with a frightened child-state, those capacities can be hard to reach. The immediate task may be outside support and regulation. Deeper dialogue can wait.

That means turning the depth down, not throwing the relationship away—unless you simply do not want to use the inner-child frame. A no to the frame is not a pacing problem to solve. If you do not want inner-child work, stop using that language and do not keep trying to sneak it back in through “gentler” exercises or by renaming ordinary tasks as reparenting. Care, protection, guidance, and human support can continue in whatever language actually fits you.

If you do want the relationship but a particular depth is too much, preserve the relationship by changing the dose. If you cannot reliably stop, return, stay oriented, or inward attention itself is making things worse, stop the inner exercise and come outward first. Do not test another inward technique just to prove you can tolerate something. Once you are steadier, the next gentlest step might be an ordinary protective action, real human support, or a brief eyes-open acknowledgment before imagery or deeper dialogue.

A complete tolerance pause is a last resort, and a safety problem can require a pause whether or not you want one. In that case, make the pause specific: what made it necessary, what level was last genuinely workable, and what observable change—plus your own agreement—would make a gentle return reasonable. While the pause is still active, do not keep testing gentler child-facing versions such as witnessing, imagery, or symbolic contact. Wait for the return condition instead. When you return, start at or below that last workable level rather than jumping back to where you left off. If a difficult experience still needs a check-in about how it landed or your system is still telling you to step down, settle that before trying to step back up. Changing the dose does not mean the relationship has failed, and this approach does not override your choice about whether to use inner-child framing at all.

### Drafts

[D1] Your little one will still be there later. Some people want out of the whole inner-child idea, though ("this just isn't me"), and that's a different thing. If that's you, I wouldn't keep sneaking it back in through some "gentler" version, or start calling the dishes reparenting. Looking after yourself and keeping yourself safe can happen in whatever words feel like yours. Same with figuring out where you're headed, and getting support from people.

[D2] If you do want it, and it's just too much at this depth, some people try one technique after another, thinking, "I just have to push through this." I'd go shallower instead. If going inward keeps making it worse, or you're not sure you could stop and come back, I'd put the inward stuff down and come back out to the room. Once you're steadier, there's a harm-reduction saying for this: start low and go slow. Low might be doing one protective thing out in your life, or calling someone who's good for you. Or a short hello to your little one with your eyes open.

[D3] A full pause from your little one is the last thing I'd reach for, but sometimes you need one, for safety or because nothing smaller is working. (Looking after yourself, and seeing the people who are good for you, can keep going.) I'd write down why. Then the last level you could still handle, and what would have to change before you'd go back. Some people keep taking little peeks during a pause, just a quick hello, just a bit of imagery. I'd wait. When that change is there and you want to come back, start at that last level you could handle, or lower.

## Group 2: pleasantness (would go right after [B49] and the embedded map, before "### Catch the Hook Before the Story Takes Over")

### Guide passage

Use an eyes-open, present-focused version when imagery pulls you away from the room, time, or body you are actually in. If a session leaves you less able to function, more compulsive, or less oriented to the present, shorten it and bring in grounded support rather than trying to win a contest with your own nervous system.

Do not use pleasantness as the only safety meter either. Learning often includes grief, fear, frustration, difficult dreams, or material you would rather not meet. Difficulty by itself is not harm, just as intensity by itself is not progress. But “I stayed oriented” is not enough by itself either. Ask four things: could you stop and return, do you actually want to go back toward it, did it feel difficult-but-workable rather than simply too much or unwanted, and how did you recover afterward in sleep and ordinary functioning?

If those answers are not known yet, do not rush to classify the experience as healing or harmful. Ask how it landed and how you have been since. Then scaffold the challenge. When you are calibrating, changing one thing at a time can teach you what matters; if distress is climbing, change several things at once. Try shorter duration, less depth or imagery, better timing, more symbolic or indirect representation, eyes open or stronger external anchors, less isolation, and more support. Restore challenge gradually only when you want to continue and recovery supports it. This is not throwing yourself into a cauldron to toughen up; it is finding the edge where you can actually learn.

Dreams and imagery can be useful material about what feels important, frightening, missing, or needed now. They are not evidence that the pictured event happened historically. Do not use them to confirm, suggest, or reconstruct a memory.

### Drafts

[P1] Whatever you try from that map, I wouldn't go by how pleasant a session felt to tell whether it was good for you. This kind of work can bring up grief and fear, and sometimes hard dreams, or stuff you'd rather never have met. A session can also be really intense and get you nowhere. Even staying present the whole time doesn't tell you enough by itself. It's a bit like the gym, where sore and injured can feel pretty similar that evening, and it's the next few days that tell you which one it was.

[P2] So how were the next few days? How did you sleep, and could you get through your day okay? Do you want to go back toward what came up, or is some part of you saying no? (And could you have stopped and come back to the room, if you'd needed to? If not, I'd keep my eyes open next time, before any imagery.) If you honestly can't tell yet, I wouldn't rush to call it healing or harm. I'd just make the next one a bit easier, one change at a time, so you learn what made the difference. If you're getting worse, though, change several things at once, and have someone steady with you.

[P3] Bring the challenge back gradually, and only when you want to and you're recovering well. If you're not sure yet whether you want to, you can wait. Some people try to toughen themselves up in a cauldron, but what you're looking for is the edge where you can actually learn. Those dreams and images that come up can show what feels important or frightening right now. Sometimes they show what feels missing, or what feels needed. They aren't evidence that what you saw in them happened, though, and shouldn't be used to confirm a memory or rebuild one. Or to suggest one.

## Group 3: isolation and honesty (would go right after [B64], the last paragraph of "### Also Look Outward", in the order I1, C2, I2)

### Guide passage

Social practice can also become a performance test. If you are approaching people mainly to prove that you are normal, attractive, lovable, sexually or romantically certain, or capable of intimacy, the other person has become part of the test. Lower the stakes. Practice ordinary reciprocal contact: notice the other person, be curious, let them have their own purposes, and see whether you can be present without turning the interaction into a verdict on you.

Honesty does not mean total disclosure to strangers. Privacy is not fakery. Let people earn access by how they handle smaller truths, boundaries, and vulnerability. But if a relationship is becoming deep while you have to maintain a false normal, happy, or perfect persona to keep it going, either bring more truth in carefully or reduce the depth. Where honesty itself would expose you to coercion, violence, serious stigma, or another concrete danger, protect yourself first; authenticity is not a duty to disclose into an unsafe relationship. Another person is not a diagnostic instrument or reassurance machine. Healthy support and mutual co-regulation are part of relationships, and in a crisis getting a safe human involved can be exactly the right move. Relief at finally being accepted can be powerful; it is not by itself proof of romantic love, compatibility, or earned trust.

If isolation itself is part of the wound, reaching toward people may be one way the adult side provides care. If inner-child language already feels natural to you, you might call that reparenting in action. If it does not, there is no need to label it that way. A decent parent would not tell a lonely child to become perfectly self-sufficient before seeing friends; sometimes the adult move is making the call, arranging the visit, asking the social worker, or helping yourself walk into a room where people share something real.

Look for more than one door: trusted people you already know, reciprocal friendships where possible, peer-support groups, a church, sangha, Unitarian or other spiritual community if that genuinely fits you, a book club, hiking or foraging group, class, volunteer project, professional help, or practical services. Add routes alongside the safe supports you already have rather than treating diversification as a reason to drop somebody good. You do not need every door to open. A network of several imperfect supports is usually sturdier than making one partner, friend, therapist, group, spiritual authority, service, or this app the only place you can belong.

Use the Protector here too. A community is not automatically good because it offers belonging. High-control groups, exploitative authorities, and unsafe or abusive relationships should not be reinforced as “support.”

### Drafts

[I1] Maybe part of what your little one went through was being alone with it. Then reaching toward people can be part of this work too, and you can call that reparenting if you like the word. Some people figure, "I'll get myself sorted out first, and then I'll be fit company for anybody." I don't think a decent parent would ask that of a lonely kid. So sometimes the grown-up thing to do is just to text someone first.

[C2] And reaching out doesn't mean telling strangers everything. Privacy isn't fakery. People can earn more of you by how they handle the smaller things you tell them, and how they take it when you say no. But if a relationship is getting deep and you're still keeping up a fake normal, happy version of yourself to hold onto it, I'd either bring more of the truth in, carefully, or let it be less deep for now. (If telling could put you in real danger, like violence, or being outed where that isn't safe, protect yourself first.) And when someone finally accepts you, it can feel amazing. It still doesn't prove you're meant for each other, or that they've earned your trust yet.

[I2] I'd look for more than one place to belong, too. One partner or one therapist can't be all of it, and if they're busy or gone, you'd be left with nobody. Keep the good people you've got, and then maybe add a spiritual community that fits you, or a class you keep showing up to. Not every one of them has to click. But if a group makes you feel more wanted than you've ever felt, and then someone there starts saying your old friends are holding you back, I'd let the Protector handle that one, and keep your other people, even if it means not going back.
