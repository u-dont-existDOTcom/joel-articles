# Whole-article check of the three groups of new material

I checked every guide point and every draft sentence against the whole article, blocks B0 to B173, not just the spot where the drafts would go.

- SAID means the article already makes the point, closely enough that a reader would feel told twice.
- PARTLY means some of it is there; I say what's missing.
- NEW means it isn't in the article.

Text in backticks is copied exactly from article_blocks.md or guide_items.md. Draft sentences are numbered s1, s2, ... in order. A sentence inside parentheses counts as its own sentence.

## Group 1: depth (D1 to D3, after B33)

### The paragraph the group says B33 carries

Mostly yes. One of its five sentences is carried by the next section, not by B33.

- `Reparenting needs enough adult capacity to pause, compare options, remain in the present, and act.` In B33 as `Try looking around the room, pausing before you act, making a choice and following through on it.`
- `It also needs enough protective intent that bringing the child forward will not put vulnerability in the hands of somebody who wants to exploit it.` Not in B33. B34 to B41 (`When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child`) carry it, e.g. B35 `Some people get a little rush out of making somebody scared or small, though. For such folks, inner child therapy is clearly not the place to start.`
- `When somebody is flooded or identified almost completely with a frightened child-state, those capacities can be hard to reach.` In B33 as `If you can't do that because you're basically the frightened kid, leave the deeper conversation until later.` B14 also has it (stage 1: `You're still fully identified as the child`).
- `The immediate task may be outside support and regulation.` In B33 as `You may need somebody steady there with you, or to do whatever helps you come back a bit before you try to talk to the child.`
- `Deeper dialogue can wait.` In B33 as `leave the deeper conversation until later`.

### Guide points

**G1.1** `That means turning the depth down, not throwing the relationship away`
PARTLY. B33 already puts depth off (`leave the deeper conversation until later`). B37 says waiting is fine in another situation, scary thoughts: `The nice thing about inner child therapy is that you can wait until you're calm and actually ready.` Missing: saying outright that holding back on depth keeps the relationship instead of dropping it. B169 and B170 warn against giving up altogether, but that's about weeks with no change, a different situation.

**G1.2** `A no to the frame is not a pacing problem to solve.` The guide's condition is `unless you simply do not want to use the inner-child frame`, and it says the same thing again at the end: `this approach does not override your choice about whether to use inner-child framing at all`.
PARTLY. B172 already treats not wanting to go on as a valid answer, and keeps it separate from the work being too much right now: `If it's making things worse, or you just can't do it right now, I'd change course sooner (stopping counts). Not wanting to do it anymore is a reason too, but if that "nope" sounds like a part of you that's trying to protect you, I'd hear it out first. If it still says no, that's your answer.` B30 makes the same kind of point about a therapist (`and they call it resistance`). Missing:
- that the no can be to the inner-child idea itself, not just to continuing the steps;
- that it isn't a pacing problem.

B142 to B145 treat the work feeling silly or fake as something to look into (B145 `So, it's worth meditating first on why it feels fake.`). That's about someone who wants the connection but feels fake, a different situation.

**G1.3** `If you do not want inner-child work, stop using that language and do not keep trying to sneak it back in through “gentler” exercises or by renaming ordinary tasks as reparenting.`
NEW. B172's `If it still says no, that's your answer.` ends the work. It says nothing about dropping the language, or about not bringing it back through gentler versions or relabeled chores. Where the article relabels an ordinary act (B87 `"I did that for us. That was the Protector."`), it's for people who are using the frame.

**G1.4** `Care, protection, guidance, and human support can continue in whatever language actually fits you.`
NEW. The nearest is B154, `How you picture all that is up to you, as long as it helps, and the simpler the better.` That's about how you picture parts inside the inner-child work, not about carrying on without it.

**G1.5** `If you do want the relationship but a particular depth is too much, preserve the relationship by changing the dose.`
PARTLY. Three places come close:
- B33 lowers the depth for one case, when you're basically the frightened kid.
- B49 makes the dose point about EMDR: `Some people go straight into EMDR on their worst memory before they can even stay in the room. Then they get flooded all over again and decide EMDR doesn't work.`
- B172: `I'd change course sooner (stopping counts)`.

Missing: going shallower with the same relationship, as a third option besides waiting (B33) and stopping (B172).

**G1.6** `If you cannot reliably stop, return, stay oriented, or inward attention itself is making things worse, stop the inner exercise and come outward first.`
PARTLY. What's there:
- B33 checks orientation and says to come back first: `Try looking around the room` ... `or to do whatever helps you come back a bit before you try to talk to the child`.
- B14 stage 1 sends you into the present: `use your childlike superpowers to come into the "Wow of Now"`.
- B5 says to stop when a practice turns on you, for crying: `If you start getting scared of your own crying, turn the song off for tonight.`

Missing:
- the test of whether you can reliably stop and come back;
- the idea that turning inward can itself be what's making things worse;
- stopping in the middle of an exercise (B33 is a check before you start).

**G1.7** `Do not test another inward technique just to prove you can tolerate something.`
PARTLY. The article warns against pushing in general (B76 `Going at it harder isn't as useful as going at it with more wisdom and compassion.`) and against going too deep too soon with EMDR (B49, quoted under G1.5). Missing: the specific warning against moving from one inward technique to the next to prove you can take it.

**G1.8** `Once you are steadier, the next gentlest step might be an ordinary protective action, real human support, or a brief eyes-open acknowledgment before imagery or deeper dialogue.`
PARTLY. What's there:
- Starting with plain protective acts in daily life: B111 `the relationship can start with the Protector's plain acts and the warmth can come after`, and B150 `the new grown-up can start outside your head`.
- Support from people: B33 `You may need somebody steady there with you`, and B173 `texting a friend that you're having a rough day`.
- A small first contact counting: B80 `That might be the most adult thing you've got right now, and it's plenty to start with.`

Missing:
- the eyes-open version (the article never mentions eyes-open work);
- these as the way back in, after stepping out and before imagery or deeper dialogue.

**G1.9** `A complete tolerance pause is a last resort`
NEW. The article never talks about pausing the relationship with your little one. B169 (`I'd work on that one thing instead of giving up on everything`) is about quitting for good when weeks pass with no change. That's a different situation from a temporary pause because it's too much.

**G1.10** `a safety problem can require a pause whether or not you want one`
PARTLY. B34 to B41 make this point for one case, an adult who might harm the child: B38 `If some of it turns out to be malice they actually want to act on, the inner child can wait.`, and B40 `their little one stays out of it`. Getting safe first also comes up for outside dangers (B61, B157). Missing: the general point that any safety problem can require a pause.

**G1.11** `make the pause specific: what made it necessary, what level was last genuinely workable, and what observable change—plus your own agreement—would make a gentle return reasonable`
NEW. The only return condition in the article is for the harmful-adult case, in B41: `As for when their little one gets a turn, I'd say once they've had some bad days where scaring somebody would've felt good, and they didn't do it.` The article never asks you to note why you paused, the last level that worked, or what would let you come back.

**G1.12** `While the pause is still active, do not keep testing gentler child-facing versions such as witnessing, imagery, or symbolic contact. Wait for the return condition instead.`
NEW.

**G1.13** `When you return, start at or below that last workable level rather than jumping back to where you left off.`
NEW.

**G1.14** `If a difficult experience still needs a check-in about how it landed or your system is still telling you to step down, settle that before trying to step back up.`
PARTLY. B159 covers the step-down signal when a part says no: `And if it says no to going deeper in this work, or to an altered state, I'd stop there too.` B167 has `If it says no, I'd listen.` Missing: settling an open check-in about how a hard experience landed before going back up. No Group 1 draft carries this point.

**G1.15** `Changing the dose does not mean the relationship has failed`
PARTLY. The article says that something isn't failure in other situations:
- B113, Protector acts that don't bring relief right away: `It doesn't mean failure, because the process is dynamic.`
- B173, an exercise that didn't carry over into the week: `I don't think this one failed either`.
- B170, against deciding `reparenting is useless`.
- B49, against deciding `EMDR doesn't work`.

Missing: saying it about turning down the depth. No Group 1 draft carries this point.

## Group 2: pleasantness (P1 to P3, after B49 and the map)

This group has no paragraph that the article is said to carry already.

### Guide points

**G2.1** `Use an eyes-open, present-focused version when imagery pulls you away from the room, time, or body you are actually in.`
NEW. The article never mentions eyes-open work or imagery pulling you out of the present. Two passages are nearby but different:
- B154 `Let a stray thought go as just thinking, and come back to the room you're in.` is about stray thoughts.
- B44 `You might feel more comfortable that way than just staying in imagination land.` is about speaking or writing, for people without an inner monologue.

**G2.2** `If a session leaves you less able to function, more compulsive, or less oriented to the present, shorten it and bring in grounded support`
PARTLY. What's there:
- B172 `If it's making things worse, or you just can't do it right now, I'd change course sooner (stopping counts).`
- B77, for the compulsive case: `If these exercises start feeling like a ritual you have to complete before you're allowed to live, stop there.`
- B33 `You may need somebody steady there with you`.

Missing: these after-session warning signs as such, and the response of shorter sessions plus support. The article's responses are stopping or changing course.

**G2.3** `rather than trying to win a contest with your own nervous system`
PARTLY. The article says not to force your way past or fight your own protective reactions:
- B156 `I'm not going to force my way past you.`
- B162 `If you treat either one like an enemy, or like proof that something's wrong with you, it'll probably just think "See? I knew it," and stay in charge.`
- B76 `Going at it harder isn't as useful as going at it with more wisdom and compassion.`

Missing: saying it about a session that's leaving you worse. No Group 2 draft carries this point; D2 s1 in Group 1 comes closest.

**G2.4** `Do not use pleasantness as the only safety meter either.`
PARTLY. The article says how a session feels doesn't tell you whether it's working. That's the progress measure:
- B76 `Feeling calmer or having deeper insights or explanations for the trauma response are some possible signs of initial progress, but if that's the endgame, it's just a cope.`
- B113 `not feeling better right away is information to learn from. It doesn't mean failure`
- B171 `But I wouldn't decide from it that the problem's over, or even that the exercise did it`

Missing: the safety measure. An unpleasant session isn't necessarily unsafe, and a pleasant one isn't necessarily safe.

**G2.5** `Learning often includes grief, fear, frustration, difficult dreams, or material you would rather not meet.`
PARTLY. Sadness, grief, anger and pain are expected in:
- B4 `although sadness is often the first step to open up`
- B157 `It's often more like a feeling. Maybe grief or anger. If so just listen and be present for it if that's what it wants.`
- B162 `The other one shows up when you get close to something painful`

Missing: difficult dreams (the article never mentions dreams), frustration, and material you'd rather not meet, as normal parts of learning.

**G2.6** `Difficulty by itself is not harm`
NEW. The article doesn't say this about harm. B113's `It doesn't mean failure` is about progress, and about Protector acts in daily life, not sessions.

**G2.7** `intensity by itself is not progress`
SAID.
- B3 `The crying helped me a lot in terms of not going numb inside. It didn't help me develop insight, though. I could have kept crying forever.`
- B165 `that can look convincing, with the right words and maybe even tears, while nothing underneath feels any safer.`
- B173 also has it: `A lot of people's first guess is that they didn't go deep enough. I don't think that's it`.

**G2.8** `But “I stayed oriented” is not enough by itself either.`
NEW. The article treats staying in the room as a precondition (B33; B49 `before they can even stay in the room`), but never says it isn't enough by itself.

**G2.9** `could you stop and return`
PARTLY. B167 names the same ability as what a protective part is watching for: `to find out if there's a grown-up here now who could call it off if things got to be too much`. Missing: asking afterward whether you could have stopped and come back, as a check on the session.

**G2.10** `do you actually want to go back toward it`
PARTLY. Two blocks treat a no as a reason to stop:
- B172 `Not wanting to do it anymore is a reason too` ... `If it still says no, that's your answer.`
- B159 `And if it says no to going deeper in this work, or to an altered state, I'd stop there too.`

Missing: wanting to go back as one of the checks after a session.

**G2.11** `did it feel difficult-but-workable rather than simply too much or unwanted`
NEW. No Group 2 draft carries this point.

**G2.12** `how did you recover afterward in sleep and ordinary functioning?`
PARTLY. The article uses daily life as the test of whether the work is helping:
- B76 `And if the therapy is actually working, it should improve your daily life`
- B173 `And a few days later you're skipping lunch and saying yes to everything again.`

Sleep comes up in B171 only as another reason you might feel better (`especially if you also slept well for once`). Missing: sleep and daily functioning after a session as a sign of whether it was too much. That's a harm measure; the article's version is a progress measure.

**G2.13** `If those answers are not known yet, do not rush to classify the experience as healing or harmful.`
PARTLY. B171 covers not rushing to call it healing: `But I wouldn't decide from it that the problem's over, or even that the exercise did it`. Missing: not rushing to call it harmful.

**G2.14** `Ask how it landed and how you have been since.`
PARTLY. After-the-fact checks appear in B171 (`Write down what changed and how long it lasted if you want, and then leave it alone.`) and B113 (`After the day, look at what actually happened.`, about Protector acts). B92's `Watch how it lands, though.` shares the words but is about how your love lands with your little one, so it doesn't count. Missing: checking in on a hard session you can't read yet.

**G2.15** `Then scaffold the challenge.`
PARTLY. B94 brings in scaffolding for something else, taking advice in small pieces: `Then take a small piece of whatever you'd tell them and hand it to yourself.` ... `The technical term for this is "[scaffolding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instructional_scaffolding)."` B49 points to `a map of what to try when` for somatic approaches. Missing: setting how hard the next session is from how the last one landed.

**G2.16** `When you are calibrating, changing one thing at a time can teach you what matters`
NEW. B171 notes that you can't tell what helped when something else changed too (`or even that the exercise did it, especially if you also slept well for once`). It never says to change one thing at a time.

**G2.17** `if distress is climbing, change several things at once`
NEW.

**G2.18** `Try shorter duration, less depth or imagery, better timing, more symbolic or indirect representation, eyes open or stronger external anchors, less isolation, and more support.`
PARTLY. Some of these levers appear elsewhere, for other purposes:
- less depth: B33 `leave the deeper conversation until later`
- more support: B33 `You may need somebody steady there with you`
- timing: B37 `you can wait until you're calm and actually ready`

Missing: shorter duration, symbolic or indirect representation, eyes open or stronger external anchors, and the levers as a set for adjusting a session.

**G2.19** `Restore challenge gradually only when you want to continue and recovery supports it.`
NEW.

**G2.20** `This is not throwing yourself into a cauldron to toughen up; it is finding the edge where you can actually learn.`
PARTLY. The article already turns away from toughening up, in B145 `I used to be all personal trainer and teach persistence on this as the remedy.` (about feeling fake) and B76 `Going at it harder isn't as useful as going at it with more wisdom and compassion.` Missing: `the edge where you can actually learn`.

**G2.21** `Dreams and imagery can be useful material about what feels important, frightening, missing, or needed now.`
NEW. Dreams aren't in the article. B55's `Transient feelings and thoughts can be meaningful signals.` is a general claim about feelings and thoughts, in another context.

**G2.22** `They are not evidence that the pictured event happened historically.`
PARTLY. The core claim is already in B146, about memories that come up in altered states: `Old memories might come up too. They can feel totally real, but that alone doesn't prove they happened.` What's new is only the reach: dreams, and imagery outside altered states.

**G2.23** `Do not use them to confirm, suggest, or reconstruct a memory.`
PARTLY. B146's `that alone doesn't prove they happened` covers not using them as confirmation. Missing: not using them to suggest or rebuild a memory.
