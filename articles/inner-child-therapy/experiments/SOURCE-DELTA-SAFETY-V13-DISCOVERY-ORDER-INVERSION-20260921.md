## V13 architecture

Invert discovery order.

1. Start from the source-grounded contradiction:
   “I'm sovereign” + “I have to obey X.”
2. Let that contradiction make the author realize that “adult” was carrying two different meanings:
   - capacity to pause/choose;
   - protective intent toward vulnerability.
3. From that realization, distinguish frightening content from current endorsed harmful intent.
4. Only then derive the practical child-contact consequence and adult-level work.

This makes the sovereignty contradiction causal rather than illustrative.

Protected functions remain outside the prose plan and are traced afterward.

## Exact candidate

Central qualification:

> That assumes grown-up you is at least willing to protect vulnerability rather than exploit it.

New H2:

> ## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child
>
> Here is the thought that made me realize I was using “adult” too loosely. Somebody says, “I'm sovereign,” and then, “I have to obey this urge, doctrine, group, spirit, or master.” I don't need to settle the metaphysics. I want to know whether they can pause, question it, refuse, change course. But even if they can, that only tells me something about their ability to choose. It still doesn't tell me what they want to do with vulnerability.
>
> That is why I don't want to read harmful intent straight off the scary-looking stuff. A religious identity, an unusual belief, an intrusive thought or unwanted fantasy, anger, charged language, or something harmful someone did before does not by itself tell me what they endorse now. If you're helping, you can keep caring without flattering cruelty, arguing theology, or teaching somebody to exploit people more effectively.
>
> The line for child work is simpler. If frightening or exploiting vulnerability is something you currently want—or you want therapy to make you better at doing that—don't assume your own vulnerable child-state is exempt because the setting is internal or therapeutic. Don't bring the child forward to find out. Their fear can't be the test, or part of the reward.
>
> With the child out of it, stay with the grown-up problem. What is actually hurting, if anything, or what do you hope help will change? If the wish to harm is tied up with an identity, that identity belongs in the grown-up work too—what does it give you, what does it cost, who does it serve, and if you expected it to make life better or happier, did it? If the intent later changes, don't make the child prove the change. Let it show up in repeated non-harmful choices, including when exploitation would feel rewarding. Until then, no child contact directly or by another side door (dialogue, childhood photos, hypnosis, borrowed-parent imagery, spiritual figures). Some people may need sustained care from a real human being more than another internal exercise or a bot.
