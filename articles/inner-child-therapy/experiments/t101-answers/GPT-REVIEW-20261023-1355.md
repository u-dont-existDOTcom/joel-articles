## What still needs a prose edit

Claude was right to preserve your approved sentences for this first experiment. It lets you judge the effect of rearranging them without simultaneously judging a different voice.

But **“nothing is cut” should describe this first test—not become the rule for the whole reader’s edit.** Otherwise we could reorganize the entire guide and leave much of the over-explanation intact.

For example, this pair remains:

> Helpful advice is great, we all need advice. Let them absorb it at their pace.

The first sentence briefly steps away from the reader’s difficulty to make a general observation. The useful instruction is in the second. My suggested consolidation, replacing those two sentences in the paragraph beginning “If the love still isn’t coming,” would be:

> If you have advice, let them take it in at their own pace.

That keeps the permission to offer advice and the instruction not to rush its reception. It removes a small explanatory detour without adding another teaching.

This is the kind of editing I would favor next: **small, justified reductions in commentary—not an arbitrary word-count target.** I would be much less eager to remove “by choice” or “People still have to earn your trust,” because those qualifications distinguish opening to a feeling from trusting another person.

I also would not automatically remove the final “Oh, so NOW you care?” exchange. It gives the reader an actual response to encounter, rather than another abstract explanation of resistance. Repetition can earn its place by doing something different.
