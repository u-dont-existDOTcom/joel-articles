## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child

Being able to act like an adult is not the same as being safe for a child. This gate is not about religion or unusual beliefs, unwanted thoughts or feelings, or something harmful you did in the past. Those things do not by themselves tell you what you intend now. If the present-day adult currently wants or enjoys frightening, humiliating, exploiting, or otherwise harming what's vulnerable, the child stays out of the work. Bringing the child forward “just to see” does not test whether you're safe: a frightened reaction tells you the child is frightened, and if that fear is rewarding, the test itself has already become part of the harm.

That does not end the work; it changes who the work is with. Stay with the adult and what you actually want to be different, including whether the identity or stance you're living from is serving the life you want or costing you and other people more than you expected. If sovereignty is part of that identity, make it behavioral rather than a title. When an urge or some claimed authority has to be obeyed, can you actually say no or change course? Care and listening can continue while you find out. They do not require agreeing with every belief, validating cruelty, or helping the harm work better.

Safety has to show up in choices, not only in a promise. Repeated non-harmful behavior is what can build protective credibility, especially when exploitation would feel rewarding and the person still chooses not to do it. Until then, no direct or indirect route should put the child in front of the same harmful intent. Dialogue, childhood photos, or hypnosis can all do that, and using an imagined parent or spiritual figure as the intermediary does not change the gate. For some people, sustained care from real humans may be more useful here than another exercise done alone or with a bot.
