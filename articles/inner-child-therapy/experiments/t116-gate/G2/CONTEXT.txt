### What to Make of a Passing Feeling

If what someone did hit something old in you, notice how that feels in your body. And notice how your feelings can also amplify or distort your perceptions of others. Just because your stomach tightened up doesn’t mean their boundary crossing indicates “They’re evil.” You can defer that judgement for when you’re in your happy place.

Feelings about yourself work the same way. “I feel attracted” or “I feel ashamed” is real as a feeling, but it doesn’t settle who you are, or who you love. Making too much out of something momentary is one side of the human psyche’s pendulum. Give yourself time to see what patterns form more durably, and what feels authentically yours vs something planted or manipulated into being there from society, influencers, family, friends, religion, etc., etc.

You may find an exceptional freedom in exactly that in-between place where you don’t have to declare your pronouns on social media, or whether you believe in this or that. If you stay in flux forever, though, it can seem like there’s nothing solid about you. In that case, sometimes it helps to meditate or talk with a good unbiased therapist who can help you see if there’s some avoidance of differentiation (we get deeper into that further on). I myself for a long time was accused of « having no opinions » (which I almost prided myself on🙈), and now it’s often the opposite accusation.

The opposite extreme of our pendulum, throwing everything transient away, as some people do with “thought-noting” practice, also isn’t healthy, except as a meditation exercise. Transient feelings and thoughts can be meaningful signals. For example, if you don’t want to be touched, say no now and work out what it means later.
