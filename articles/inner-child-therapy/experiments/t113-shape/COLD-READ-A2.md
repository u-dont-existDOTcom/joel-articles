# Cold read A2: H-change, H-escape

## H-change

Verdict: FIX AS SUGGESTED

1. **Heading "How many tries would I give it?" sits over a paragraph that holds two cases (checks 1, 4).** The first three sentences answer it ("It's not like we can just put a number on it. Some things shift in a day. Others take about as long as learning guitar."). Then comes a different case: "If it's making things worse, or you just can't do it right now, I'd change course sooner (stopping counts)." A reader whose situation is "it's making me worse" or "I can't do this right now" will skip a heading about the number of tries. "(stopping counts)" is then echoed by the next heading, "If you'd rather stop". The heading also speaks as "I" while its four siblings speak to "you", and it promises a number the paragraph says it can't give. Do not split the paragraph: "sooner" needs "But do notice if progress is stagnating" as its baseline, and that "But" needs the sentences before it. Fix: keep the question form but widen it and use "you": "How many tries before you change course?"

2. **"these steps" lost its referent (checks 3, 5).** NOW: "And if you keep following the reparenting steps correctly without getting the expected results, consider if you have a unique case these steps don't match yet." The dropped clause became the heading "If you're doing it right and it still isn't working", so the paragraph now opens "Consider if you have a unique case these steps don't match yet." Nothing above names any steps: the heading says "it". Also lost: "keep" (persistence over time), "reparenting steps", "expected results". Fix: heading "If you keep following the steps correctly and it still isn't working". That restores "keep" and gives "these steps" a visible antecedent.

3. **"When the words sound empty" is vague (check 1).** Which words, and empty to whom? The first sentence, "Maybe the words are all there, but the loving grown-up behind them isn't, not yet.", never says they are the kind words said to your little one, so a skimmer can't tell. The heading names a symptom, but the paragraph is about the missing loving grown-up. Fix: "When your kind words sound empty" or "When the kind words are there but the grown-up isn't".

4. **Two openers restate their heading (check 2), both minor, leave them.** "If the relief doesn't last" / "Let's say an exercise leaves you feeling lighter for a few hours, but then the old feeling comes back later on." (it is the concrete example, so it earns its place). "If you'd rather stop" / "Not wanting to do it anymore might be reason enough, but..." (it adds a verdict, "might be reason enough").

5. **Unheaded first paragraph is fine (check 4).** It lists three blockers ("not actually implemented", "no alone time", "felt scary"). They are one thought ("look for what exactly is in the way") and work as the lead-in. No change.

6. **Nothing else lost (check 5).** The voice survives and "throw the baby out with the bathwater" is kept. The other splits are clean. The old last paragraph did hold two cases (quitting, and a case the steps don't cover), and now it doesn't.

7. **Overall (check 6): better than NOW.** A skimmer can now find "the relief fades" or "I'd rather stop". Items 1 and 2 are the two places where a heading and its paragraph don't match, and both are quick to fix.

## H-escape

Verdict: FIX AS SUGGESTED

1. **"Your own drug history" is a topic label, not a situation (check 1).** The paragraph is about a bad past reaction: "If you go manic, psychotic (...), get super paranoid, or end up worse afterward than before, those are signs to reconsider trying the same thing again". A skimmer with a long history can't tell if it applies. The drop of "matters too" costs nothing. Fix: "If a past trip went badly".

2. **"Paranoia" is a bare noun, and the opener repeats it (checks 1, 2).** "Paranoia during a rough trip can be a warning sign for psychosis..." Fix: a question the opener answers, "Is your paranoia a warning sign?"

3. **The split of the paranoia paragraph leaves the second half holding two cases (checks 3, 4).** The split puts "Some drugs are known for causing paranoia, like THC, so I'd avoid those." (prevention) in the same paragraph as "If you can't clearly understand what happened yourself, get someone qualified to help. And if you have an idea of what happened but aren't sure, ask someone who knows you well." (after the fact). The "what happened" sentences answer the CIA examples, and the THC sentence now sits between them. Fix: move the split to after the THC sentence, so the second paragraph opens with "If you can't clearly understand what happened yourself" directly under the paragraph about the episode.

4. **"those reactions" points across two headings (checks 2, 3).** Under "If you might be prone to mania or psychosis": "Any of those reactions, from mania to paranoia, can also point to a vulnerability in you". The reactions are listed two headed sections up, and a reader who jumps here from the heading won't have them. Fix: rework the opener, e.g. "Going manic, psychotic or paranoid on a trip can also point to a vulnerability in you, so I'd be careful with other drugs that mess with what's real too."

5. **"By trusted, I mean someone you know is trustworthy..." (check 4).** It sits, unheaded, under the vulnerability heading, but it is a general rule for anyone choosing a guide, facilitator or therapist. It also serves "get someone qualified to help" and "see a psychiatrist". Someone who isn't prone to mania won't open that section and will miss it. It does link back to "trusted advisors" at the end of the paragraph above. Fix: give it its own heading, "Who counts as trusted". The opener "By trusted, I mean" still works under it, or can become "Someone you know is trustworthy from a long relationship with them".

6. **"Coming back from trips confused or spaced out" leans on the dropped sentence (checks 2, 3, 5).** The paragraph now opens "Maybe nothing worse happens, but after several trips like that I'd be concerned." "like that" points at the heading, and "nothing worse" had "Say you keep coming back from trips confused or spaced out." to answer. Lost: "keep" (this is about recurrence). Fix: heading "If you keep coming back from trips confused or spaced out". That restores "keep", matches the "If" headings, and gives "like that" a clear antecedent.

7. **The same paragraph hides the medication and withdrawal warnings (check 4).** "And don't suddenly stop a medication just because you suspect it's involved... Benzos can be dangerous to quit cold turkey. Alcohol withdrawal can be dangerous if you're physically dependent." Someone on benzos or alcohol-dependent, who doesn't come back spaced out, would skip a heading about spacing out and miss a safety line. Fix: split before "And don't suddenly stop a medication" and give it a heading such as "Before you stop a medication (or alcohol)". Drop "And" from the opener.

8. **"What you mix" is vague, and the opener repeats it (checks 1, 2).** "Be careful what you mix, too, and not just prescribed medicines." The paragraph is about interactions, and two of its three examples are ayahuasca ones (cinnamon, acacia confusa with mimosa hostilis, then "a list of things to be cautious about with ayahuasca"). "too" and "not just prescribed medicines" also lean on the medication talk in the paragraph above. Fix: "Mixing: medicines, plants and ayahuasca". The opener can stay.

9. **The opening paragraph still holds two cases (check 4, optional).** First it gives a general rule: "try to select the healthiest ones and use slow and gentle protocols". Then comes a 5-MeO-DMT case ("one of the most abused psychedelics... I have a guide on that"). Someone who never uses 5-MeO-DMT would want to skip the second half. Fix, if the writer wants it: split after "slow and gentle protocols." and head the second part "5-MeO-DMT and huge doses". Keep "I have a guide on that." with the 5-MeO-DMT sentence so the link's "that" stays attached.

10. **Nothing else lost (check 5).** The jokes ("painfully autistic + schizo!", "Sidney the iboga shaman", "crapshoot") are untouched. Lost words: "matters too" (item 1) and "Say you keep" (item 6).

11. **Overall (check 6): better for finding a warning, slightly worse for reading straight through.** Five warnings are now findable. But three openers lean on moved or dropped words (items 4, 6, 8), and three headings are bare labels ("Your own drug history", "Paranoia", "What you mix"). With the fixes above it reads clearly better than NOW.
