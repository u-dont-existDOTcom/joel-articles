Text check: apart from the opening words that moved into headings (H-guard: "If it's been calling all this stupid,"; H-start: "If nothing comes," and "Or if you do get an answer,"), every sentence in both PROPOSED files matches NOW word for word. Heading levels are one below each section's own heading.

## H-guard

Verdict: FIX AS SUGGESTED (light: two heading rewordings; the structure is sound).

1. **Heading 4 reads two ways (check 1).** "If it feels like it's all your fault" sits in a stack of headings where "it" is the guard ("If it won't step back", "If it's been calling all this stupid"), so a skimmer can read it as "if the guard feels it's all your fault". The paragraph is about you blaming yourself: "Blaming yourself can be doing a protective job, too." Fix: "If it all feels like your fault". It has no second "it", and it echoes the quoted "It's all my fault" without repeating "Blaming yourself".

2. **Heading 1 is not a case, and does not say when (check 1).** "Earning its trust" applies to every reader. Per the paragraph it happens "mostly out in ordinary life", not in the session. "Its" has no noun except the section title. The writer's reason ("each paragraph is one thing the guard might do") does not describe it: this one is something you do. Fix: "Earning its trust, day to day".

3. **The roommate image runs across a heading (check 3, low).** Under "Earning its trust": "A new roommate doesn't win you over with a speech on move-in day (hopefully, right?). The rent just has to keep showing up." Under "If it won't step back": "That part doesn't have to move out. It's been living there way longer than the grown-up you." "Move out" and "living there" borrow their house from the roommate paragraph. A reader who skips the trust paragraph loses the image, not a claim; the sentences still parse as "it lives in you". Accept. There is no fix that doesn't change text.

4. **A second case hides in an unheaded paragraph (check 4, optional).** Paragraph 3 holds a temptation ("A harder voice can seem more fitting for something that's been in your way.") and a different situation: "If that voice isn't there in you yet, borrow it from someone who's been safe for you." The reader with no Nurturer voice yet, who needs that line most, cannot find it by skimming. Optional fix: give it its own paragraph under "If you don't have the Nurturer voice yet", body "Borrow it from someone who's been safe for you." (The heading names the Nurturer voice so "that voice" isn't left pointing across the heading.) Skip it if a one-sentence paragraph feels thin.

5. **Checked, no failure (checks 2 and 3).**
   - "It might turn out to be scared of getting its hopes up." connects: heading plus sentence is the original conditional. It opens on a bare "It" that only the heading completes, which is fine while headings are visible.
   - "Blaming yourself can be doing a protective job, too." The "too" pointed back at the "scared" paragraph (a harsh voice doing a protective job). The section's "protective part" covers it for a skimmer.
   - The question "What are you afraid would happen if you stepped back?" and its reply "It might say this will just be one more performance, and you'll fool yourself again." stay together. Leaving that paragraph unheaded is the right call: a heading would cut "It might say" from its question, and its general line ("Usually your little one only comes forward after that part feels heard.") is for every reader. At about 115 words it needs no split.

6. **Lost (check 5):** nothing. "Peek-a-boo!", the Emmy line, "(hopefully, right?)", "(ST-CA, section 9.3.4)" and the voice are intact. Heading 3 keeps Joel's "stupid".

7. **Overall (check 6):** better than NOW. The four trailing cases are findable and skippable, and the opening exchange stays one unbroken flow. Only the two headings above are loose.

## H-start

Verdict: FIX AS SUGGESTED (moderate: one heading misfires, one paragraph needs a split, the stop rule should be easier to find).

1. **"Which thoughts to talk to" does not match its first sentence, and orphans "all that" (checks 1, 2, 3).** The heading promises a filter. The first sentence under it is "How you picture all that is up to you, as long as it helps, and the simpler the better." That is about picturing, not about which thoughts. "All that" pointed at the paragraph above ("It could come in different forms, like as a voice or just a hunch."), which is now across a heading; a reader who skipped "If you do get an answer" has no referent. Fix: rename it "Part, or just a thought?". It names the reader's question and gives "all that" something to point at.

2. **The second paragraph under that heading is a different point (check 4, optional).** "I wouldn't spend long deciding what to call each part, though." rightly stays beside the filter, because the "though" qualifies it. But "Every part of you learns from how the others treat it, your little one included." and "when you hear it out, it doesn't have to get any louder" are another point: treatment of one part carries to the others, and hearing out a protector quiets it. The heading does not cover that. Optional fix: close the filter paragraph with the "I wouldn't spend long..." sentence and give the rest its own heading, e.g. "Hearing a part out". That moves a sentence, so it is the writer's call. If not, leave it; nothing is lost.

3. **"If you do get an answer" holds two cases (check 4).** One is a feeling: "If so just listen and be present for it if that's what it wants." The other is an instruction: "If it tells you to do something, I'd only do it if it's loving and leaves you free." A reader whose part gave only a feeling wades through the cautions. A reader whose part gave an instruction cannot find the cautions ("anything big or hard to undo can wait..."), the section's main safety line after the stop rule. Fix: new heading "If it tells you to do something", body "I'd only do it if it's loving and leaves you free. It should make sense, too. Even then, anything big or hard to undo can wait until you're calm and you've checked it against what you actually know." "Even then" stays with the sentence it limits.

4. **"If nothing comes" can be misread (check 1).** Under a section called "Start With Whatever Showed Up", a skimmer can take it as "if nothing shows up at all". The paragraph is about a part that does not answer: "No need to answer for that part...", "Even without the answer...". Fix: "If no answer comes". It still pairs with "If you do get an answer", and "That's fine for now." connects to it.

5. **"When to stop" (checks 1, 3).** It names no trigger, and it sits after case headings, so it looks like one more optional case when it is the rule that overrides "I'd go see" in the paragraph above. Also "even when it sounds like a prediction" points back at "making a guess about what's coming" in the anxiety paragraph, now across a heading. For a reader who skipped that paragraph, "prediction" is loosely anchored but still understandable. Fix: "When a part says no", which names the reader's situation. Giving the rule its own heading is a clear gain over NOW.

6. **Verify the heading level against H-guard (cross-section).** H-guard's own heading is "###", and its first words ("Whichever protective part showed up") suggest it follows this section. If so, "### Hear the Guard First" becomes a sibling of "### If it's anxiety" and "### When to stop", and reads like one more case in the list. I cannot see the assembled guide; please check the outline.

7. **Checked, fine.** "It could come in different forms..." connects to its heading. The "Or" that paired the two branches survives in the parallel headings. "Anxiety is often a part of you..." repeats the heading's word but carries the claim, so keep it. One thing to know: "A worry that just keeps asking to be reassured can go the same way" (let it go) and "I'd go see" (anxiety paragraph) are different advice for different things, and a reader who jumps to "If it's anxiety" will miss the first. No change needed.

8. **Lost (check 5):** nothing. "pl/ork", "It can be right!" and the voice are intact.

9. **Overall (check 6):** better than NOW once 1, 3 and 5 are fixed. The branches become skippable and the stop rule findable; as proposed, the middle heading misleads and the answer paragraph still buries the cautions.
