# Cold read A3: H-fake, H-therapist

## H-fake

Three headings go over the last three paragraphs. Everything above them stays as the lead-in. Nothing is split.

1. **"In an altered state" (check 1, 2, 3).** It is a fragment, and it never says "fake", so a skimmer can't tell what happens in an altered state or whether the section tells them to go and find one. It is also the tail of the sentence under it ("For some people it only stops feeling fake in an altered state, like on MDMA or deep in meditation."), so the first sentence says it again. It leaves "it" with no anchor. The nearest line above is "...won't prove the whole method, but it's a start.", and a quick reader can take that "it" for the method. Fix: "When it only feels real in an altered state". It is worded differently from the sentence, so it doesn't just echo it, and it puts "fake/real" next to "it".

2. **"The Ideal Parent Figure method" (check 1, 2, 5).** It is a method name, not a situation, so a skimmer who feels fake can't tell what it is for. It gives away the name that sentence two is introducing ("There's a whole method built on it, called the Ideal Parent Figure method."), and that sentence now repeats the heading. It also reads like a how-to, but the paragraph gives one pilot result. Dropping "And" from "And picturing one ideal parent seems to help some people" lost the only link to "you need a bridge" above, so "seems to help" no longer says help with what. Fix: "Another way through: picturing one ideal parent". The "Another" replaces the dropped "And", the name is still introduced in sentence two, and sentence one adds "seems to help some people" to the heading.

3. **"Sometimes kindness feels scary more than fake" (check 1, 3).** This one works. It answers the line above it ("So, it's worth meditating first on why it feels fake."), and it is Joel's own sentence, so claim and voice are intact. The paragraph now opens "One study's title has a word for that: "compassion-threat."", and "that" points at the heading. That resolves only because the heading is the line directly above, so nothing may ever be put between them. No fix.

4. **Check 4, two cases in one paragraph.** The altered-state paragraph holds the experience, a caution about memories ("Old memories might come up too. They can feel totally real, but that alone doesn't prove they happened."), and the keep-it advice. Do not split it. "I'd still hang on to what you felt" is a concession to the memory caution just before it, and "talking to them afterward" needs the "felt" sentence. The new heading doesn't mention the memory caution, and I wouldn't try to cram it in.

5. **Check 5, losses.** Only the "And" in item 2. "Oh my gosh!" and "All of a sudden" are kept. The writer's own headings are plainer than Joel's voice ("The Ideal Parent Figure method" is textbook register), and my suggestions use his phrasing.

6. **Check 6, overall.** Better than NOW for skipping, because the three ways through are now separable and heading 1 is good. Headings 2 and 3 are a label and a fragment that don't tell a skimmer when the paragraph applies.

**Verdict: FIX AS SUGGESTED** (change headings 2 and 3 only).

## H-therapist

Four headings go over paragraphs 2 to 5. Paragraph 1 (start with a somatic therapist) stays as the lead-in. Nothing is split.

1. **"Inner Child Reparenting therapists" (check 2, 4).** The heading is word for word the first words of the paragraph ("Inner Child Reparenting therapists do exist, and you can find them..."). Bigger problem: the last sentence ("I can consult, and if you like the guide, you can ask a therapist to check it out and see what they agree with or disagree with as a great way to vet them.") is a vetting tip that works for any therapist. It now sits under a heading that a reader heading for IFS or Schema will skip. It is one sentence, so it can't be split off. Fix: widen the heading to "Reparenting therapists, and showing a therapist this guide". Alternative, if moving a sentence is allowed: move that sentence to the end of the vetting paragraph (item 3).

2. **"IFS and Schema Therapy" (check 1, 3).** Three problems.
   - "that come close" has lost its referent for a skimmer. In "The best licensed, listed therapists you'll find that come close will be doing Internal Family Systems (IFS), or Schema Therapy." it points at the reparenting paragraph above ("often are not licensed", "none which work with my guide yet"). "Licensed, listed" is the contrast with it. A reader who skipped that section lands here asking "close to what?".
   - The heading reads as an explainer, but the paragraph says "I'll talk more about those in the appendix" and is really about where to find such a therapist (Open Path Collective, the filters, "the official site").
   - "IFS" is unexpanded in the heading.

   Fix: "Licensed therapists who come close: IFS and Schema Therapy". Check 4: "which approaches" and "where to find them" are one case, so keep it as one paragraph. Not caused by this edit: "the official site" doesn't say official site of what, in both versions.

3. **"Before choosing a therapist" (check 1, 3, 4).** The heading doesn't say what the paragraph is (vetting, interview questions). "Get to know the strengths and pitfalls of their approach" now has the heading as the only antecedent for "their", because the dropped "Before choosing a therapist," was it. That works only because the heading sits directly above. Fix: "Vetting a therapist before you choose one". It supplies the noun for "their", names the job, and signals that it applies to every kind of therapist above. The paragraph is long and mixes what to research, what to ask (spiritual experiences, dissociation, challenges and results) and interview practicalities (cost, website). Do not split it. "A certificate on the wall can't tell you any of that." points back over the whole list, and a split would cut "that" off from half of it.

4. **"AI therapy" (check 1).** It is a bare topic. The paragraph is a verdict plus a caution ("pretty bad", "a toss up", "expect it to go sideways"), and the heading hides that. Dropping "Regarding AI therapy," loses nothing. Fix: "AI therapy: a toss-up", which uses his own words.

5. **Paragraph 1 left unheaded (check 4).** This is right. A heading such as "Start with a somatic therapist" would repeat its second sentence ("First and easiest is to start with finding a somatic therapist"). It sits directly under the title, so a skimmer sees it. No change.

6. **Check 5, losses.** Nothing. "Before choosing a therapist," and "Regarding AI therapy," are topic markers that the headings now carry, and the voice is intact. The one risk is the vetting tip buried in item 1.

7. **Check 6, overall.** Better than NOW, because five different topics in a row become findable. As written, though, headings 1 and 2 each strand a line from what a skimming reader needs ("come close" and the vetting tip), and headings 3 and 4 are bare topic labels.

**Verdict: FIX AS SUGGESTED** (all four headings reworded; text unchanged).
