### Also Look Outward

You tell somebody they hurt you, and somehow by the end you're comforting them while the thing you raised has disappeared. One horrible conversation like that doesn't prove emotional immaturity. People need time, they disagree, and sometimes what you're furious about is simply a boundary of theirs. What happens later matters more: maybe they come back and actually think about what you said and own their part, or maybe a completely different subject ends in the same disappearing act. [Lindsay Gibson's work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlNpgFWOLPw) keeps the focus on repeated relational behavior instead of making you solve the motive first.

You may not know for certain whether the other person is or could be capable of a healthier response, and neither do they. If they promise to change, that's probably as useful as their prior promises were. Think about how long it has taken you to become more healthy. Could you have done that from one moment to the next based on a promise? Sometimes we can be a better support for someone when we maintain a realistic boundary, so that we can hold high expectations, and still feel prepared for their failure to meet them.

And sometimes you really did hurt them, which makes the whole thing harder to sort. There is something real to repair. You know that from what you actually did, not simply from the fact that you feel guilty. But while you're trying to repair it, you may notice yourself slowly giving away the rest of your position because their disappointment is hard for you to tolerate. The apology gets bigger and bigger. Maybe now you're apologizing for things you still believe, or agreeing to things you don't actually agree with. And guilt still doesn't tell you whether the other person is manipulating you.

Sometimes you say what you needed to say and still don't get the scene where both people understand each other. They may understand you perfectly well and still think you're wrong. Your no can remain a no while they think it's unfair. At some point you notice you're defending the same thing again. The disagreement can still be there when the conversation ends.
