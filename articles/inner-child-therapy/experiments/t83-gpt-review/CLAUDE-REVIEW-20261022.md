## Notes by ID

**T1, the names.**
- The packet is right that the trouble is the guards, not the roles. Every reader-facing collision I can find involves a protective reaction named with the role's word:
  - Hear the Protective Part First P3.
  - The Inner Guide P3, "No hidden protector".
  - Speak Toward What You Are Building P2, "the Protector & Guide parts".
- So rename the guards and leave the role alone.
- "Adult Protector" would give one role two names in a 27,000-word article that says "Protector" 23 times. The map says "Protector" too. If you want "Adult Protector" (the packet says you agreed it in the earlier discussion, which I haven't seen), it should replace every use, not only the first and the ones next to a guard.
- **Opening primer:** your P3 to P5 of The Chicken-and-Egg Problem already define the one grown-up with three jobs and let readers rename it. Each other figure is defined where it first matters. A three-name forward note there is the glossary the packet warns against.
- **Two cross-references** need the new heading if you rename it:
  - Borrow One Competency at a Time's "(see the section Hear the Protective Part First)".
  - Altered States Can Deepen the Therapy's "the way you did in Hear the Protective Part First".
- No prose refers to Two Common Protective Patterns.
- If you rename to "guards", question 76.1's fix becomes "No hidden guard or old trauma required". The packet would keep your sentence as it is, and 77.1 lets you choose.

**T2, script and pattern.**
- The point is good: the old rule can run with no words.
- It's already made in three places, so a new paragraph would repeat them. One sentence in P2 (mine) would connect them, for example: "Sometimes there's no voice at all, just a sense that needing somebody will end badly, before you can say why."
- "Schema" is clinical vocabulary, so it belongs in the appendix's Schema Therapy section if anywhere. The lane's rule is plain words in the main text.

**C1 to C3, the candy question.**
- Your question found a real gap: what a habit is doing.
- The rest of the packet's material (three coping directions, five candy cases, the arrow sequence) is review scaffolding. It doesn't need to appear in the guide.
- One paragraph after Two Common Protective Patterns P2 can carry all three points. Wording to humanize later, mine, not approved:

  > The pull can also be a habit, with no voice at all. After a criticism you reach for sweets or your phone, and for a while the hurt is harder to feel. That doesn't make sweets or phones bad, and sometimes you just want a sweet (more on that in The Inner Guide). The question is whether the break brings you back to what needs care, or keeps standing in for it.

- C2's loop could be one more sentence: "If you never tell anyone you're struggling, nobody offers help, and that can look like proof that nobody cares."
- The three-style table (surrender, avoidance, overcompensation, renamed resignation, avoidance and inversion in 2021) could be one sentence in the appendix. Arntz and colleagues' paper supports the renaming. It defines all three as ways of handling an activated schema, calls the old fight/flight/freeze link loose, and calls its labels tentative (pp. 1007, 1013, 1018).

**P2, endings of support.**
- The useful part is that a helper stepping back can stir up the old fear of being left, even after real growth.
- When One Life Has Ended already says what to do in a gap. One list item sends a reader there.
- A separate paragraph in Finding a Therapist would repeat it.

**F1, the Schema Therapy appendix.**
- The categorical claim is yours (2026-10-18) in my words. Young's preface contradicts it, so I'd correct it, and it's your call.
- "Usually" in "isn't something a schema therapist would usually offer you" is a hedged inference from a model that doesn't build spirituality in. I'd keep it. The Young reader found no other spiritual material in the book: religion comes up only as the content of rigid rules, and meditation only as a calming technique (Y8).
- P1 is my draft, so its fix is a correction of my prose, but it's in a paragraph that passed Pangram, so you choose (77.8). Wording, mine, to humanize: '…who looks after your "Vulnerable Child," sets limits when your angry or impulsive side takes over, and stands up to the punishing parent in your head. Sound familiar? That's a Nurturer, a Protector and a Guide by other names…'. Young's third function, "Battles or moderates" the coping and critic modes, is already there as "stands up to the punishing parent".
- Keep the IFS opinion and the "a lot depends on who you're seeing" sentence.

**G1, gratitude.**
- The packet states your meaning correctly: appreciating relief or goodness, without needing someone else to be worse off.
- What's open is only the upstream half: #412 is still waiting on the UDA maintainer lane, and nothing in the served map says it yet.

**PGQ-006, my concern.**
- When Escape Leaves Reality Behind treats a past manic or psychotic reaction as a sign to reconsider and take more precautions. Psilocybin studies screen such people out altogether. For example, this trial's protocol excludes a "personal or immediate family history of schizophrenia, bipolar affective disorder" and other psychotic disorders (protocol BACK001, section 4.2: https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/96/NCT05163496/Prot_SAP_000.pdf).
- Your text already says to get professional help when things are severe, and that caution signs mean more precautions of every kind.
- The smallest addition would be one clause: talk it over with a doctor before trying any psychedelic again after a manic or psychotic episode.
- Your wording was a deliberate choice (2026-10-10 and 11), so this is a question, not a fix.

**PGQ-039, my concern.**
- Telling a therapist that a real event was a dream misleads the person you're asking for help. It can send the therapy the wrong way, and your own TalkSpace story shows it doesn't reliably avoid a report.
- P3 also says to ask what the law requires and then "personally I wouldn't even bother with that", which pulls against the sentence before it.
- A version that keeps your point without the misrepresentation: start general and non-identifying ("something I did years ago that I'm ashamed of") until you know what they'd have to report. Your TalkSpace story could stay as the reason to ask first.
- Your last two sentences are the safety core, and every option keeps them: don't hold back a current danger, and get anyone in danger safe.
