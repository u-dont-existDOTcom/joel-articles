## P1

Okay, write it. Write the unfair version too if that's what is there. The only rule is that it stays private for now. Later you can look at it and go, ugh, no, that's just mean. And then maybe two lines down there's the actual no you were trying to say. Keep that. If you owe an apology, put that in too. Sometimes the whole useful response turns out to be three boring lines. Sometimes, after all that typing, you don't really have anything to send.

## P2 owner anchor

It doesn't mean you need to hide your upset, or apply for a permit to protest, but choose your battles and execute them wisely, so you don't end up regretting how you communicated later. As Lao Tsu might have said (if I could ever have read that whole boring Art of War book), a war with no casualties is the best possible war. When high IQ & EQ communication hits a writer's block, sometimes the best thing is to simply say, "I need some time to think about my response." And if the conflict really requires a response right now, then obviously just do your best rather than wait for the perfect timing.

## P3

Then there is whoever wrote the mess. I wouldn't make them disappear just because you managed to write a nicer text. Read the first version again. What were they trying to stop? What did they need somebody to understand? Maybe the answer is protection. Maybe reassurance. Maybe they were just furious and also right about one part of the argument. You can decide the draft should never leave your phone and still take its author seriously. Otherwise you've edited the message and missed the person.
