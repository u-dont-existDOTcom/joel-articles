## 2. What gets in the way

**Headings that don't say what's under them**
- "Allow Céline to explain the “inner child:”" names a person the reader hasn't met, and it hides what is probably the definition section.
- "The Chicken-and-Egg Problem" (615 words) doesn't say which two things are circular.
- "My Journey" (974 words) is the largest block in the first chapter and reads as a memoir. A reader looking for the method will skip it.
- "Five Stages of Reparenting" doesn't say whether the stages are a roadmap or a diagnosis. The five stages are also not visible anywhere else in the contents. Nothing later is labelled Stage 1 to 5, so a reader can't tell where they are.
- "This human life is the most precious opportunity" is a sentence, not a topic. It sits under the suicide section at 456 words, so it is where a person in crisis will land.
- "Signs of Manipulation" (572 words) doesn't say manipulation by whom. Situation 6 only works if you read it next to its parent.
- "Talk to the hand!✋🏿", "Listen To Your Body, Or Else!", "Ask God for a Loan 🫶🏿", "“And So That’s the Way It Happened!”", "You Belong Everywhere and Nowhere", "The Love Has to Move" and "Between Spoiling and Clamping Down" are playful or cryptic. None names its problem. "Ask God for a Loan" (901 words) is the second-largest section and the least findable.
- "Hearthwork: Taking Turns Lending & Borrowing Adulthood" uses a coined term with no introduction. Readers who didn't arrive knowing the word won't know it is about peer support.
- "Everyone has altered states: Prepare now" and "Using spiritual attack for spiritual gain" don't say what happens (sleep paralysis, dark experiences, bad trips). "Spiritual attack" is jargon.
- "The Inner Guide: Becoming Your Own Shepherd" and "Nurturer" both come with no cue on how they relate to "Inner Adult". "The Nurturer" has 0 words of its own, so there is no overview.
- "Is your paranoia a warning sign?", "Who counts as trusted" and "What you mix" are too short to tell what they refer to.
- "If it’s anxiety" (85 words) doesn't say anxiety about what. It is the only child under "What to Make of a Passing Feeling".
- "If it tells you to do something" doesn't say what "it" is. The reader has to come from the parent "Journal With Each Hand", and the contents never says that exercise is an inner-child dialogue.
- "Differentiation" is jargon at H2 level, and nothing in the heading says it means separating from family or group expectations.
- "Discovering and Healing Old Patterns" and "Practicing With Your Little One" are broad chapter names with very few sections in the first case. Together with "Bringing Reparenting Into Real Life", the chapter names don't tell a reader which one holds the daily routine, memory work, or relationships.

**Sections in the wrong place, wrong level or wrong parent**
- "When Therapy Stirs Things Up" and "Notice the Part That Came Here" are H3s under "Your Body Might Need Some Love First". Neither is about the body. Both look like siblings of that H2, and situation 8 needs the first one.
- "Ill Will and Loving-Kindness" sits under "When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child". It could be either a remedy or an unrelated topic, and situation 26 had no clear home.
- The suicide section is the fourth H2 of "Safety and Readiness", after readiness material. It isn't marked as urgent, and its two H3s are 162 and 456 words of spiritual framing. A person in crisis has to scroll past other sections first. "If Someone Has Actually Hurt or Threatened You" (23 words) is another safety item buried at H3 in the conflict chapter.
- Safety content for altered states is far from "Safety and Readiness". "When Escape Leaves Reality Behind" has eight tiny H3s (mania and psychosis, quitting medication or alcohol, mixing substances, confusion after trips) of 29 to 143 words each. They read as a safety checklist but sit in a later chapter.
- "Non-altered states can feel like this too" (104 words) is the last item under the escape section. It addresses people without altered states, so it belongs earlier or in its own place.
- "My Psychedelic Experiences Came A Bit Too Soon" is a personal story attached to the end of the altered-states chapter. It is the same kind of material as "My Journey", but 800 words away.
- "Bringing Reparenting Into Real Life" is a grab bag. Its H2s include play, differentiation, friends and dating, finding a therapist, conflict, and grief after a breakup. "Finding a Therapist to Borrow Adulthood" and the AI therapy item fit with "Safety and Readiness" or with a support chapter, and the grief section ("When One Life Has Ended...") fits better with the difficult-practice chapter. That chapter also has 146 words of intro while "Rediscover Play" and "Differentiation" have 0 words of their own.
- "Finding a Therapist to Borrow Adulthood" and "Finding People and Building Trust" are separate H2s at the same level but overlap in purpose. "Hearthwork" (peer support) sits among friendship items, not next to the therapist section.
- "Don’t Sell Your Inner Adult To Buy An Outer Adult", "Signs of Manipulation", "Borrow One Competency at a Time" and "Noticing Counts" are all under "Borrow the Adult To Become the Adult". "Noticing Counts" doesn't obviously belong there.
- "A Bigger Doorway: Divine Love" is 22 words under "The Nurturer" and looks like a stub for the larger "Guide Yourself To Divine Love" section.
- "Reframing attacks as unskillful attempts to help" is an H4 under "Protecting from the Parent You Inherited", but it could apply to "Protecting them from yourself" as well.
- "Physical Pain Makes It Harder" sits under "When the Urge to Escape Arrives", though pain isn't an escape urge. A reader with chronic pain (situation 24) is unlikely to look there.
- "Listen To Your Body, Or Else!" as its only H4 child adds a level for 166 words.
- "How to Forgive Without Forgetting" and its single child "Forgiving Yourself" (66 words) are the same topic, so there's an extra level for nothing. "Missing Someone" under "When One Life Has Ended..." is also a lone child.
- Appendix: "How This Relates to Similar Therapies" (109 words) has "Schema Therapy" and "Internal Family Systems (IFS)" as siblings, not children. They look like three separate topics. "Development of my framework" and "Original Teachers and Practice Resources" are mixed into the same list.
- "Conclusion and Appendix" is one H1, so the ending ("You Are Worthy of Love. Always.") and the reference material are bundled together. The conclusion is 145 words under a 0-word parent.
- Empty parents: "Safety and Readiness", "Building Your Inner Adult", "The Nurturer", "Practicing With Your Little One", "Discovering and Healing Old Patterns", "When the Practice Gets Difficult", "Altered States and Intensive Practice", "Rediscover Play and Your Own Preferences", "Differentiation" and "Appendix" have 0 words of their own. Readers get no overview of what each contains.
- "If you keep following the reparenting steps correctly without getting the expected results" is a very long heading for 54 words. "How many tries would I give it?" is phrased in the first person, unlike its siblings.

**Things that look like they're in two places**
- Divine or spiritual love appears in three places: "A Bigger Doorway: Divine Love", "Guide Yourself To Divine Love" (with "Ask God for a Loan") and "When the Spiritual Relationship Hurts". "The Spiritual Side of the Question" under suicide adds a fourth. Situation 30 had no clear first stop.
- Hindsight is in "Borrow Hindsight" (Guide) and "Use hindsight to “re-do” traumatic memories in a healthier way" (Discovering). The second is the memory-work version and the first the general one, but a reader can't tell.
- Discipline: "How should discipline have been done right?" and "Answering the Strict Disciplinarian" are in the old-patterns chapter, while "Protecting from the Parent You Inherited" and "Protecting them from yourself" cover the harsh inner voice elsewhere. Situations 4 and 29 could belong in either.
- The "Borrow" motif repeats across headings ("Borrow the Adult...", "Borrow One Competency at a Time", "Borrow Someone Else’s Love", "Borrow Your Love For Someone Else", "Borrow the Guide to Build It", "Borrow Hindsight", "Finding a Therapist to Borrow Adulthood", and "Lending & Borrowing Adulthood"). The word stops distinguishing sections, and "Borrow Someone Else’s Love" and "Borrow Your Love For Someone Else" differ only by a few words.
- Urges and escape: "When the Urge to Escape Arrives", "Altered States Can Deepen Therapy or Escapism" and "When Escape Leaves Reality Behind" all share the escape idea. Alcohol, phone and apps (situations 23 and 36) have no obvious single home.
- Anger and sending texts: "Catch the Hook Before the Story Takes Over", "Write It. Don’t Send It Yet." and "Other Folks Also Have Hooks" all use the hook idea. "Hook" is used for inner reactions and for other people's behaviour, and "Four Pairs That Commonly Hook the Child" uses it a third way.
- Opening up: "Opening Up Honestly to the Right People at the Right Pace", "Before You Tell the Hardest Stuff", "How To Let People In" and "Who counts as trusted" (altered-states chapter) overlap.
- Feelings and body: "Your Body Might Need Some Love First", "Physical Pain Makes It Harder" and "What to Make of a Passing Feeling" overlap for body tension and flare-ups (situations 24, 25 and 35).
- Calm or daily practice: "Gentle Meditation and Mantra", "One Intention in the Morning, One Review at Night", "Show Up When Nothing’s Wrong" and "Close the Session and Return to Life" all fit situation 9, and none is marked as the short version.
- Trust and safety for the child: "Protecting and Building Trust With Your Little One" and "Finding People and Building Trust" share a name pattern and cover different things (inner trust versus other people).
