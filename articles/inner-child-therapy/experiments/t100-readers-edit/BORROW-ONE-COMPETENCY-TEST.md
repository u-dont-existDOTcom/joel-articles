`## Borrow One Competency at a Time`

*(old P1, yours, unchanged)*

A complete ideal parent may be impossible to imagine (unless you saw The Brady Bunch). And imagination is pretty hard for adults anyway since it kinda gets stamped out of kids at an early age, but it's not just a foolish waste of time. It's actually a genius mode unlock. So since the adult is actually a baby when it comes to power to imagine, start small: Borrow one function at a time from a figure who embodies it. Perhaps a therapist or your grandmother really cared for you. Perhaps your teacher at some age was a great guide. Maybe an older sibling protected you well. And there's always your future healed self if you can imagine that.

*(old P6 without its first two sentences; "For warmth," is new)*

For warmth, think of someone you remember fondly. How would they sit beside your little one? Just make sure it's someone who liked you the way you were.

*(old P7, unchanged)*

For protecting, ask what a minimally competent adult would do here. Maybe something simple, like actually smelling that old food you're about to eat and then trashing it if it smells a little weird. If you're about to send an angry text, hit pause. If the room itself triggers your spidey sense, leave, or lock the door and get help. Even if this still feels like the child acting like the adult, for now, that's ok. "Fake it til you make it" isn't exactly it, but yeah. Afterward, you can tell your little one, "I did that for us. That was the Protector." If it sounds funny, laugh!

*(old P8, unchanged)*

The Guide can be you too, just a calmer you. That's a lot easier to imagine than your future healed self. Think back to what (and how) you decided about something like this the last time you were calm. Or ask what the wise person you respect would have you practice today. A wise tradition (like "What would Jesus do?" or "What would Buddha do?") can give some direction, too, until you develop your own internal compass.

`### If the Love Isn't Coming Yet`

*(new heading)*

*(old P2, unchanged)*

If you weren't loved well as a child, "love your inner child" can sound like nonsense. Love them how? Maybe you really want the best for your little one. Maybe you hug them in your imagination, or miss them. And you can still feel none of the warmth people mean when they say love. That doesn't close the door, though, it just means you start smaller.

*(old P3, unchanged)*

Maybe just imagining a caring grown-up makes you feel more threatened, or lonelier. Lonelier is a strange one, with somebody right there. Then go smaller still, to the least cruel response a decent adult could offer your little one. It's scaffolding, and it isn't a test of whether you were loved enough.

*(old P4, unchanged)*

Taking the hostility out makes room for warmth. What people most often say helped is letting their defenses down. I mean by choice, after the part guarding you has been heard. If you've never been safe being loved, your ego can act like a rigid security guard that won't let the feeling in, and being vulnerable is the opening the feeling needs. (This guard is only about letting the feeling in. People still have to earn your trust.) Sometimes the warmth is there, with a cold part standing in front of it, guarding something. That part tends to step aside once it's been heard (see the section Hear the Guard First).

*(old P5, unchanged)*

If the love still isn't coming, you can at least stop judging your little one. Obviously no insults. Helpful advice is great, we all need advice. Let them absorb it at their pace. And don't expect a thank-you for the session, like they booked it. That's enough to start being the adult, even if it isn't enough yet for them to trust you.

*(old P6's first two sentences, now their own paragraph)*

Most of us wouldn't open up to somebody who'd only just stopped being mean, or let them take care of us. That takes warmth, and if there isn't any yet, borrow some.

*(old P9, unchanged)*

The thank-you you weren't waiting for might not come anyway. 😜 More like "Oh, so NOW you care?" Your first instinct might be "But I'm trying!" Some days, just hearing it out like a decent adult is the only thing you'll manage to borrow.
