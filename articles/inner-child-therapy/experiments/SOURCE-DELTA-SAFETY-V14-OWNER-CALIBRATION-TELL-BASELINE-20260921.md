> I was using “adult” a little too loosely above. Looking around the room, pausing, and making a choice tells me some grown-up capacity is there; it doesn't tell me whether vulnerability is safe with you. If the answer is harm, keep the work with grown-up you: what do you want help with, and if that harmful wish is tied to an identity, what is the identity giving you, costing you, who is it serving, and has it made life better or happier in the way you expected? (If you're helping someone rather than doing this yourself, you can care about them without flattering cruelty, arguing theology, or teaching them to exploit people more effectively.)
>
> If you later say the intent changed, don't use the child to prove it. Let the change show up in repeated non-harmful choices, including when exploiting vulnerability would feel rewarding. Until then, child contact stays closed, including the side doors: dialogue, childhood photos, hypnosis, borrowed-parent imagery, spiritual figures, or another indirect route. Some people here may need sustained care from a real human being more than another internal exercise or a bot.
