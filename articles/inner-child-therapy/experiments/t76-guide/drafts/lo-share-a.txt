Then there are the things that are hardest to tell anyone: an urge that scares you, say, or something you did that could get you in legal trouble. Before you tell them, know where it can end up. A post, a group chat, a DM or an AI app can keep a record that gets screenshotted or handed over when a court asks, and a fake name or a deleted message won't make you anonymous. Therapists, doctors, teachers and some others may have to report certain things, depending on where you live, though that doesn't mean every crime you mention gets reported. And the police aren't your therapist.

So ask a therapist or a helpline what they'd have to report, and who can see their notes, before you get into names and details. With an AI, you can start with the kind of help you're looking for and leave the names out. For legal trouble, talk to a lawyer. But don't leave out a danger that's real right now when you talk to the people who can help with it. Sometimes telling, even reporting yourself, is what protects someone, legal consequences and all. And if anyone's in danger right now, get them safe first and sort out the rest later.
