Wishes: exclusion, included; vulnerability, safe; deprivation, cared for or nurtured; the perfectionism wish (accepted as I am, free of self-criticism) is one client's. Evidence: "You wish yourself and others the emotional antidotes to the schema."
- **2. CONFIRMED.** Location: Ch. 14 "The Circle of Life", closing exercise "If You Want to Work with Feelings about Your Childhood". Evidence: "choose a simpler issue that you feel more ready to be with"
- **3. CONFIRMED, framing differs.** Location: Ch. 15 "Stages of Healing", section "The Power of Letting Go". A brief suggestion in a case story, not a scripted exercise. Evidence (Isabel's words, the girl wording is hers): "that little girl in me who tried so hard to be loved"
- **4. CONFIRMED.** Location: Ch. 15 "Stages of Healing", sections "The Integration" and "It's the Same Work". - **6. CONFIRMED.** Location: Ch. 8 "The Many Uses of Mindfulness", section "A Mindful Radar". Evidence: "In the Buddhist tradition this eventually culminates in spiritual enlightenment."

Evidence: "Meditation teaches us to enter being-mode, and to remain there."
- **12. CONFIRMED, verbatim.** Location: Ch. 9 "Participant Workbook", "Follow-up Session 1", "Continued development of a mindful orientation". Evidence: "You cannot expect your newly learned skills to work automatically after just eight training sessions."

## Spiritual or religious practice alongside the work

**EA** is integrative and Buddhist, not theistic. Emotional work comes first, and "Emotional alchemy can be a stage in the journey into spiritual alchemy." (Ch. 15, "From Emotional to Spiritual Alchemy"; someone focused on changing habits may stop there.) The author calls the two a continuum, says each path is complete alone, and warns against assuming spiritual development covers emotional growth. Joining practices: the Ch. 15 exercise and, in Ch. 14 "Expressing Forgiveness", a client using loving-kindness to temper her reactions. Cautions: keep attending to personal needs (Ch. 18); the deeper path needs "a well-qualified teacher" (Ch. 19). Her "prayer" means loving-kindness wishes for oneself and others; Tara is a symbol of compassionate equanimity, with no practice of receiving love from her or from God.

**MST** treats mindfulness as a secular training adapted from mindfulness-based cognitive therapy. Spiritual material is background only (both in Ch. 3): the BPM paragraph, and a remark that long retreats are commonly practised for spiritual purposes while research finds more practice is not always better. It proposes no religious or spiritual practice alongside schema work. Its caring figures are the Healthy Adult mode, the Happy Child mode and an imagined person who listens (Exercise 8.1).
