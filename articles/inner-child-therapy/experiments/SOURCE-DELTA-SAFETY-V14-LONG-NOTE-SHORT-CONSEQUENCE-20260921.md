New H2:

> ## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child
>
> I was using “adult” a little too loosely above. Looking around the room, pausing, and making a choice tells me some grown-up capacity is there; it doesn't tell me whether vulnerability is safe with you. If you still want to frighten or exploit vulnerability—or you want therapy to help you do it better—the child is not the place to test that. Their fear can't be the test, and it can't become part of the reward. I don't want to turn that into a purity test either. A religious identity or unusual belief, an intrusive thought or unwanted fantasy, anger, charged language, even something harmful you did before—none of those tells me by itself what you intend now. “Sovereign” doesn't tell me either. If you say you're sovereign and also that you have to obey an urge, doctrine, group, spirit, or master, I don't need to settle the metaphysics; I want to know whether you can pause, question it, refuse, change your mind. That's about whether there is room to choose. The other question is what you actually want to do with vulnerability. If the answer is harm, keep the work with grown-up you: what do you want help with, and if that harmful wish is tied to an identity, what is the identity giving you, costing you, who is it serving, and has it made life better or happier in the way you expected? (If you're helping someone rather than doing this yourself, you can care about them without flattering cruelty, arguing theology, or teaching them to exploit people more effectively.)
>
> If you later say the intent changed, don't use the child to prove it. Let the change show up in repeated non-harmful choices, including when exploiting vulnerability would feel rewarding. Until then, child contact stays closed, including the side doors: dialogue, childhood photos, hypnosis, borrowed-parent imagery, spiritual figures, or another indirect route. Some people here may need sustained care from a real human being more than another internal exercise or a bot.
