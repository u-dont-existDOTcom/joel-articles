## Borrow One Job at a Time

Good luck picturing a whole perfect parent. Most real people are only good at one or two of the three jobs anyway, so borrow each one from whoever does it best.

If you can't feel any love for your little one yet, I'd start with just not judging them. No rushing them, and no expecting a thank-you for showing up.

That gets you started as the adult, but it might not be enough for them to trust you. For that, think of somebody who took care of you that you remember fondly, and imagine how they'd sit next to your little one. If there wasn't anybody, there's Mr. Rogers, or spiritual love if you've felt it.

Protecting is usually more boring than people expect. Ask what a minimally competent adult would do in the next ten minutes. Probably eat something, or put the phone down, or go to bed. Once in a while it's locking the door.

For guidance, the easiest person to borrow from is you on a calmer day. What did you decide the last time you were thinking straight? That's worth writing down while you're calm, so the upset version of you has something to go on. Or ask what a teacher you respect would want you to practice today.

Your little one might not be thrilled to see you at first. If you get something like "Oh, so now you care?", a decent grown-up wouldn't argue. They'd probably just say, "Fair. I'm late."

You might still feel about six years old while you're doing any of this, and that's fine. Afterward you get to say, "I did that for us."

There's a little research behind this, though nothing that proves the whole method. In a [small pilot in France](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5700488/), 17 adults with complex PTSD from childhood trauma did five weeks of therapy built around imagining ideal parents, and eight months later their symptoms were still down. There was no control group, though. In [another study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25347984/), people who were hard on themselves got more stressed by a compassion imagery exercise than people who weren't, and liked it less. And in a [third](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31301164/), ten minutes of feeling more securely attached calmed that kind of reaction in people with shakier attachment.

So if being nice to yourself feels fake, or even kind of scary, I'd borrow first.
