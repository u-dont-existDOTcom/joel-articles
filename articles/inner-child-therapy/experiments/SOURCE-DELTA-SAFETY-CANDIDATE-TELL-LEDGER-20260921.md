### Safety section

> ## When the Present-Day Adult Is Dangerous to the Child
>
> Being calm and present doesn't make the adult safe for the child. If the present-day adult currently enjoys frightening or humiliating vulnerable people, wants to exploit them, or wants therapy to make manipulation easier, don't bring the child forward—not even briefly to see how the child reacts. The child's fear is not a diagnostic test, and it can't become another source of gratification.
>
> While that intent remains, the work stays with the present-day adult. What do they actually want help with—what is hurting, if anything, or what do they hope will change? If harmful intent is tied up with an identity, that identity is part of the adult work too: what does it give them, what does it cost, whom does it serve, and has it produced the life and happiness they expected? If someone calls themselves sovereign but also says they have to obey an urge, doctrine, group, spirit, or master, leave the metaphysics alone for a minute. Can they pause, question, refuse, or revise?
>
> This is not a religion test. A religious identity or unusual belief does not by itself establish current harmful intent. Neither does an intrusive thought, anger, an unwanted fantasy, charged language, or something harmful they did in the past. Look at what the person actually endorses and intends now. A promise doesn't clear the gate either. It becomes credible through repeated non-harmful choices, including when exploitation would feel rewarding.
>
> Care can continue while the gate is closed. If you're helping, listen without flattering cruelty, arguing theology, or helping the person get better at exploiting people. But don't try to reach the child through dialogue, childhood photos, hypnosis, borrowed-parent imagery, spiritual figures, or another indirect route. Some people here may need sustained care from a real human being more than another inner exercise or a bot.
