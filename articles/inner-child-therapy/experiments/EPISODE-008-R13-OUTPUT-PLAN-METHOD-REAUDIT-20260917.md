## PRODUCING-CHAIN AUDIT

### 1. Plan generation — FAIL

The plan restored the *form* of the Episode 007 method (`literal span -> operation -> positive target`) but not the successful method's actual sparsity.

The frozen R13 instruction artifact was 18,662 bytes for a 265-word candidate. It contained:

- ten local repair jobs;
- a preservation checklist of many required functions;
- extensive gold-standard rules;
- three complete prose predictions;
- a recommendation selecting Prediction A.

That made the plan itself a dense writing environment. The successful late Episode 007 endpoint was very different: detector evidence had localized one 39-word residual, Human/high neighbors were frozen, and the final repair guide was about 2.5 KB. The writer had one live local problem rather than an entire subsection's semantics plus three candidate realizations in active context.

**Causal mechanism:** the plan recreated comprehensive coverage pressure before generation began. It told the writer to compress the framework rather than removing the framework as the organizing structure.

**Process repair:** do not call a plan faithful to the successful local-repair method merely because each row has an operation and positive target. Method fidelity also requires comparable edit scope/sparsity. If most of a subsection is still model-shaped, either localize a truly bounded residual first or use a materially different generation architecture.

### 2. Prediction-stage contamination — FAIL

Prediction A was presented in full and explicitly recommended in the same frozen artifact the later writer read. R13 then follows Prediction A unusually closely, including the same paragraph route and near-identical final handoff idea:

Prediction A: `If the rest of the argument is still yelling in your head, write it there first.`

R13: `If the rest of the argument is still yelling in your head, give it somewhere private to yell first.`

The owner rule requiring three prose predictions was meant to expose assumptions for owner calibration. In this run, the selected prediction became an implicit prose template.

**Causal mechanism:** diagnostic prose and generation instructions occupied the same model context. Saying `do not copy/splice the prediction` did not remove the prediction's information from context.

**Process repair:** preserve the three owner-facing predictions as diagnostics, but do not use the selected full prediction as a writer exemplar in a claimed transfer test. If independence from those probes matters, generation needs a genuinely fresh context containing the frozen semantic target and eligible teaching examples, not the prediction prose. Same-context instructions to ignore a visible prediction are not isolation.
