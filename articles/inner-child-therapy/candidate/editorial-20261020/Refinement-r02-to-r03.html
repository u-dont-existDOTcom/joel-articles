Article sectionRemember When You Already Did It
Context immediately before

My father often suggested folks remember a similar-level (or even harder) challenge that they were able to handle. Maybe you survived a car crash and surgery a few years ago, but now you’re having trouble with emotional eating. You can draw strength from the past. As my sister, a historian, likes to say, “The past didn’t go anywhere.” Your own challenge doesn’t need to be as dramatic as a car crash, as long as it really happened. So who or what helped you get through it? Is one piece of that still within reach? If you catch yourself thinking “I survived a car crash, so why can’t I stop eating when I’m upset?”, that’s the past turned into a stick to hit yourself with. Maybe you’re more worn out now, or missing whatever helped back then. (No example? Try another doorway.)

Inner-Child-r02-candidate.html · r0298

Some people have the opposite problem. It feels like you’re connecting with your little one, but the contact is staying near the surface. Meditating for a long time and then sleeping can open up much more emotionally direct stuff, especially in a second sleep, after you’ve already slept once. What showed me that the daytime pl/ork had stayed shallow was the difference at night. So say you wake up from a dream with far more feeling and need in it than the session came anywhere near. That’s the sign this is you (a session that was good and just didn’t stick around is something else). Then ask what that dream let you feel that the session didn’t, and how you understand it now. And if nightmares start wrecking your sleep, do less of the practice, earlier in the day or for a shorter time, before you write off the whole thing.

Rumi says you have to keep breaking your heart until it stays open. Some people find that deep grieving gives way to warmth, or to feeling connected. Some feel held, in the body. Being loved can open grief from the other side, too. Someone takes care of you, and you think, “Why does this make me sad?” It can be sadness for the care that was missing, and that doesn’t mean the love failed. While you’re present and still choosing, stay with it. Crying that deep can overwhelm you, too. It can retraumatize, mostly when nobody’s with you or you’ve disappeared into the sadness. Once it’s swamping you instead of opening you, slow down (like turning the song off for the night).
