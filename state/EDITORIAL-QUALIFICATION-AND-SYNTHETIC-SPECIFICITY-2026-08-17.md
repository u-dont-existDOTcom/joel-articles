# Editorial qualification + synthetic-specificity owner rules — 2026-08-17

## Trigger

Joel directly corrected two recurring model tendencies during Romance work:

1. **Extreme skeptical qualification sounds AI-written and overexplains.** Use the least qualification necessary to avoid misinformation or a genuinely misleading implication, and assume readers can understand adjacent attribution/evidence roles without defensive aftercare.
2. **Gratuitous `on Tuesday`-style specificity sounds AI-written.** Never invent arbitrary day-of-week or clock-time details as humanization/concreteness. Preserve real source-derived chronology when it matters.

These are general Joel-byline editorial rules, not Romance-only detector tricks.

## Promoted rule

`SKILL.md` now contains:

- `Qualification, skepticism, and reader trust`: minimum necessary qualification; no hostile fact-check voice unless the task is fact-checking; no explanation of non-implications a reasonable reader would not infer; distinguish evidence type economically and continue.
- `Synthetic specificity and fake concreteness`: do not invent `on Tuesday`, `last Tuesday`, `Tuesday afternoon`, arbitrary clock times, scenery, or incidental micro-details to manufacture a human feel.

## Why this matters

The immediate Romance example was a proposed disclaimer after a Komisaruk/Whipple nerve-pathway sentence. The following sentence already separately attributed broader afterglow/healing claims to practitioners. Adding another disclaimer that the laboratory study did not prove those separate claims solved a confusion the prose had not created.

The day-of-week rule is broader: plausible-but-invented calendar detail is pseudo-observation, not human observation. It is especially harmful in memoir/personal prose because it can silently fabricate chronology.

## Scope

- Applies to Joel Rosenblum byline prose, rewrites, source integration, humanization, and cold audit.
- Does not prevent rigorous qualification when the wording would otherwise materially mislead.
- Does not prevent skeptical research notes or fact-check tasks from being skeptical.
- Does not remove genuine source-derived dates, days, times, or chronology.
- Does not change any article authority/master by itself.
