# Section pairs (TV1): published paragraph and rewrite

The section is "The Math of Absorption, and Who This Isn't For", from an essay about founding intentional communities. The rewrite keeps the published order and paragraphs. Text in square brackets in the context is a chart.

## P3pub → P3n3

CONTEXT BEFORE: This does not surprise me, since we are taught to put on a show of competence in modern society. We have a lot of incentives to hide ourselves in our lives and disappear behind our screens the second things get uncomfortable. We have been encouraged by our institutions to be narrow in our skills, and then they act surprised when nobody can sit through a conflict without appointing a subcommittee or blocking somebody.

PUBLISHED:

So the question isn’t whether a person is “good enough” for community. The practical question is how much attention they currently have available for other people, how willing they are to examine their own reactions, and whether the group has enough capacity to support them without being consumed.

REWRITE:

Instead of asking "Is this person good enough?", the practical questions to ask are, "How much attention can this person bring to other people right now? How available is he or she to engage in self-examination? And how available are we to this person, without being consumed by him or her?"

CONTEXT AFTER: Some people have engaged in long practices of contemplation or therapy, or have had very good luck in childhood; other people are just trying to survive some emergency in their life and need care more than responsibility. Most of us are all of these at different times in our lives.

## P6pub → P6m

CONTEXT BEFORE: In the model I’m imagining, some of the functions that are outsourced to therapists in modern society are distributed throughout the community. In other words, there’s a lot of [peer counseling](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling) going on, people listening to each other, being there for each other and helping each other process ordinary emotional stuff.

PUBLISHED:

That does not make the community infinitely absorbent.

REWRITE:

But it’s not infinite.

CONTEXT AFTER: I’m going to use the phrase “[free resource](https://www.rc.org/),” which comes from Re-evaluation Counseling, to refer to the attention, steadiness, skill and practical capacity people have left over for others once they’ve dealt with enough of their own immediate stuff, and “need” to refer to the amount of help people need at a given time.

## P15pub → P15n2

CONTEXT BEFORE: So early on, members usually need to have more resource than acute need, and some practical skills to contribute, and a willingness to learn.

PUBLISHED:

I still don’t know how to keep this language of capacity from becoming a respectable way to exclude people who are disabled, poor, less articulate, unfamiliar with therapeutic language, or simply inconvenient. It’s not going to be solved by pretending it’s unlimited; that’s going to require actually making concrete criteria, allowing for outside review, allowing for re-visiting a “not now” decision, and all of that. And even then, the power is dangerous.
