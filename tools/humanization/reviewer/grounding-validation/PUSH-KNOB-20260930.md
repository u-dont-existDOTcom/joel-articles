# Reader questions and the push knob (2026-09-30, turn 6)

**Joel, 04:44 UTC:** "it's also good to not just base things on the article but on the questions the article generates for the reader which may or may not be satisfactorily answered… of course this could go too far, an article might become a 10 volume set at that rate, so we should brainstorm on how to keep in scope and also give the user some kind of control knob for how much to push the writing. in general we should assume the user doesn't want to massively increase the article size, but small increases or decreases may be fine if warranted. for any uncertainty ask the user."

**Before this, the reviewer had:**
- UNCLEAR: can the reader follow this sentence at this point?
- MISFIRES (turn 5): could this instruction hurt some reader?
- MISSING: is a guide point dropped?

It didn't list the questions a paragraph leaves the reader with.

**What changed (`grounding.txt` step 6, `reviewer.py --push`):**
- **The questions.** The reviewer lists up to five questions a thoughtful reader would stop on at that point. For each, it says where the answer is: in the new text, earlier in the article, later in the guide, or nowhere.
- **Answered later.** A question the guide answers later isn't open, so the article doesn't grow to answer it early.
- **Sorting.** Open questions are sorted by what a wrong guess costs the reader:
  - MUST: it could hurt them, or lead them to act against the article.
  - SHOULD: most readers would get stuck, and nothing later answers it.
  - COULD: extra depth.
- **Size.** Each raised question comes with the smallest answer that would do and its size. A SIZE line totals the words.
- **Too big.** Anything bigger than the push level allows, or belonging to another section, or changing what the section is about, is ASK AUTHOR. Those go to Joel.
- **Parked.** Questions below the push level go on a PARKED line, and from there into `../../../PARKED-READER-QUESTIONS.md`, not into the article.

**The knob.** It's set per section with `"push"` in the target, or with `--push` on the command line. The default is `default`.

| push | raises | a fix may add | the section may change by |
|---|---|---|---|
| tight | MUST | a clause, inside the existing sentences | about +5% |
| default | MUST, SHOULD | about one short sentence per paragraph | about ±10% |
| wide | MUST, SHOULD, COULD | a sentence or two per paragraph, or a suggested new paragraph | more, but anything that changes what the section is about is still ASK AUTHOR |

## Demo: the relationship paragraph (v2) at each push level

The text was "If a relationship keeps making you feel less safe, meditate on why… But if it's been dangerous even once, get safe first, which is not a small thing at all, I know." It ran with the full prompt, as a fresh Opus subagent at each level.

| push | flags | questions raised | parked | size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tight | [2] MISFIRES on "if it's been dangerous even once" | 1 MUST: "What if I'm not sure it was really dangerous, or my own old stuff is part of why it felt that way?" | none | +12 words (3%) |
| default | none | 1 MUST: the same question. Two more were listed as answered later in the guide ("choose a realistic outcome"; the Borrowed Protector's "ask for help"). | none | +15–20 words |
| wide | [2] two QUESTION flags | 2 MUST (the same question, plus "What does 'get safe' mean: do I have to leave, right now, on my own?") and 1 COULD-level one ("Why is once enough here, when you said not to go only by the one fight?") | none | +15–25 words |

**What the demo showed:**
- All three levels found the same MUST. That became the fix: "But if they've actually hurt or threatened you, even once, old stuff or not, get safe first…" It's 100% Human alone (68 words), and Also Look Outward is 100% Human (443).
- COULD questions came up only at wide.
- The runs disagreed on one question. Default said "get safe = help" is answered later (the Borrow section's "ask for help"). Wide called it an open MUST. That's an ASK AUTHOR for Joel.
- The same text, reviewed at default, gave a SIZE of +15–20 words. That's within the budget.
