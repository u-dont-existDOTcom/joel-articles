Before new paragraphs go into an article, check what the whole article already says.

The author of a long article asked that new material from his guide be added only where it's new (Joel, 2026-10-07 01:09: "if it has new stuff add that ... just make sure it's not duplicating stuff"). Until 2026-10-08 the drafts were checked only against the paragraph before them and their own section, and repeats from further away got through (Joel, 23:18: "some of that looked like it was duplicating other stuff from before ... did you make the dedup pass before trying to humanize?"). Your job is the whole-article check.

Below are the article as it stands, with each block numbered [B#] (headings start with #, ##, ###; bracketed notes stand in for embedded posts), and then the new material in groups. Each group has the guide passage it carries, where it would go, and maybe draft paragraphs written from it. None of the new material is in the article.

FOR EACH GROUP

1. Split the guide passage into its separate points: one claim, instruction, caution, permission or example each. Number them (G1.1, G1.2, ... for group 1; G2.x; and so on). If the group says the article already carries part of the passage, check that it really does.
2. For each point, search the whole article, not just the place the group would go. Mark it SAID (the article already makes this point, for the same situation or close enough that a reader would feel told twice: give the block number and a short exact quote), PARTLY (say what's there, with the quote, and what's missing), or NEW.
3. If the group has drafts: for each sentence of each draft, in order, say which guide point it carries and whether that point is SAID / PARTLY / NEW (with the block), or that it carries no guide point (something the writer added). If a draft sentence repeats a sentence in another group's drafts, say so.
4. Then, for the group: the points really left to add (NEW, and the missing part of each PARTLY), in a short list.

Last, list the points two groups share, so each can be said once.

Judge by meaning, not by shared words: two passages that use the same words for different points are not a repeat, and two that make the same point in different words are. Where the article makes a point for a different situation (for example, about a relationship with another person, where the guide is about the relationship with your inner child), say so and call it PARTLY or NEW, with a reason.

Don't suggest rewrites and don't comment on style. Don't invent quotes: every quote must be copied exactly from the text below. {report}
