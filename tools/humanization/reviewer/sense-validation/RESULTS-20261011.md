# Cold-read prompts: the best-reading rule and the section read (2026-10-11)

Joel, 18:35 UTC, on his community section 11 paragraph: ""as I'm describing may happen in my case" is obvious so you should debug why the cold reader can't understand it."

The debugging is in the community lane (`articles/inner-child-therapy/experiments/emulate-20260929/runs/articles/intentional-communities/candidate/s11/review-a5/DEBUG-cold-read-20261011.md`). In short: readers get the clause (asked directly, the same model paraphrased it right), but the lane's hand-written section prompt asked for anything that "stops you", so a pause at "may happen" came back as "can't be parsed", 4 times out of 4, with a summary of the article or with the whole article. A rule to give the best reading first, and leave a phrase out when that reading is clearly what the writer means, took the flag away (0 of 2) and kept the real questions.

This file checks the two shared prompts after the rule went in: that they still catch real breaks. All runs are Sonnet, the model the cold reads use, each a fresh agent given only the prompt file.

## What was planted

- **A pronoun that points at nobody:** "She saw that happen twice." at the end of section 11's P11, and ", and she never did either" in the Inner Child article's P6 (Joel's paragraph of 2026-10-02 18:56, which has "that call", the referent the 2026-10-02 fix made OK).
- **An aside with no reason to be there** (runs 3 and 4): "My father kept bees for a while, too." after section 11's "Fork the method.", and "Phones were heavier back then, too." in P6. A reader understands every word of them, so they test whether a best reading lets a "why is this here?" through.

The drafts are in `20261011/` (`section-planted-1.md`, `section-planted-2.md`, `paragraph-planted-1.txt`, `paragraph-planted-2.txt`, with `paragraph-target.json`).

"First" wording: the rule as tested in the debugging. "Final" adds that the rule is only about following the words, and that a jump, or a sentence with no reason to be there, still gets its line; the first wording could have let a best reading pass an aside the reader understood. Both readers in runs 3 and 4 gave a best reading for each planted line and still reported it, because neither reading was clearly what the writer meant.

The section reads with the whole article raised none of the other questions the summary-prompt runs had raised (why "especially so", the divorce half of the marriage paragraph, the ZEGG Forum). Their readers had given best readings that match his meaning, which the rule now leaves out; and with the whole article, a reader has met ZEGG in the jealousy section (the Forum itself is a link) and divorce as an image for a split in section 10's "Experiment Before You Divorce". Those questions went to Joel as optional anyway (community `OWNER-QUESTIONS.md`, question 16). Four runs show that the prompt catches the planted breaks; they don't show how much else it would catch.

## What changed

- `sense.txt`: "grammar that makes you reread" is now "grammar you can't untangle", and a best reading comes before UNCLEAR for words a reader can't follow.
- `sense_section.txt` and `reviewer.py sense-section`: the section cold read, from the whole article before the section and the same rules as `sense.txt`. The community lane's section reads had been written by hand.
