# The reader's-moment read: does it make sense, say what it points to, say each thing once, unpacked, payoff first?

Tried for lesson bad-trips-2026-11-12h, and not a gate (below). Joel, 2026-11-12 16:48 UTC, after taking my Chest Pain draft, which three fresh readers of my
read against his fixes had passed, through twelve checks to Human, High: "so we need to figure out how you can understand
when things don't make sense, when they're indefinite in reference, when they're logically overlapping and redundant,
when they are compressed, and most of all, when the payoff comes too late. it's like you have trouble understanding
that." His one change that flipped my draft from AI Generated, 100% to Human Written was the payoff moved up: the action,
"I'd also call medics", said before the list of signs instead of after it
(`articles/bad-trips/experiments/t37-answers/JOEL-TRIES-TEACH-CHEST.md`, his try 7).

Earlier reads asked whether a sentence is needed, or whether it looks like the drafts he fixes; a reader who infers
meaning easily lets all five through. This read puts a fresh reader in the reader's moment and has them read one sentence
at a time, saying what they can do after each.

**Not a gate.** Scored before use on seven texts he fixed (`articles/bad-trips/humanize/h47-20261112/CALIBRATION.md`):
it found 5.5 of the nine flags no brief gave away (the read against his fixes 6, the two together 7), added content he
would not, and Sonnet's safeguards stopped it on four of the seven texts. It is kept as a record of what was tried.

---

You are the person this text is for, in this moment:

THE MOMENT: [one line: who you are, where you are, what is happening, what you're trying to decide or do]

THE TEXT:
[the text, one sentence a line, numbered S1, S2, ...]

First, in one line: what question do you bring to this text, in this moment? (For example: "Do I treat this myself or
call an ambulance?")

Then read it one sentence at a time, the way you would on your phone with no time to spare: for each sentence, answer
knowing only that sentence and the ones before it.

- S1: DO: what you now know to do, or "nothing yet". STUCK: any word you can't act on: it doesn't fit your moment (a word
  from another kind of book), you can't tell what it points to (it, this, these, that, another, the second, the same,
  counts, the following, familiar to whom?), or you'd have to stop and work out what it means. AGAIN: anything an
  earlier sentence already said, or that overlaps with it (one sign that is part of another; the same action twice; a
  case the sentence before already covered). PACKED: more than one decision, or more than two things, to hold at once.
- S2: ...

Write "-" where a part has nothing.

Then:

PAYOFF: At which sentence was your question answered, and what did you need before that sentence that it only gave you
later? If the answer to your question, or the thing you do, comes after a list, after the cases, or at the end, say so
and say where it belongs.

WORST: the one change that would help you most in this moment, in one line.
