Anxiety deserves the same kind of listening, but it isn't a fortune teller. It might really be warning you about something, like being overloaded, or a boundary. Most of the time, I'd ask it what it's trying to protect, and then check what it predicted against what actually happens, unless you're really in danger, or it's something in your body like chest pain, which a doctor should look at instead of you waiting to see if the anxiety was right. And when a part of you says no to going deeper, or to an altered state, that no isn't a prediction at all, and it's reason enough to stop. With touch or sex, even a hesitation is enough, and it might not sound like a no at all, more like a part of you going quiet or pulling back a little.
