Make a Simple Vow

If your promises still mean something to your little one, maybe because you've kept a few small ones, a quiet vow can help too. You can say it with or without picturing the two of you playing on a swing set:

"I know you're in there. I love you. You don't have to love me back or trust me yet. I can't promise that nothing painful will happen. Or that I'll always know what to do, or never react badly. But I can promise to tell you the truth and listen when you're angry. I'll make our life together safer where I can, and take the next step I can see to protect you. When I fail, I'll come back to make it right. You don't have to believe these words. Watch what I do."

Only say the "I love you" part if you mean it. If you don't, say the goodwill wish from earlier instead ("May you be loving, peaceful, and free"), if you can mean that one, and don't pretend it's the same thing. Or even just promise your little one you won't be cruel to them on purpose. (Then skip the "love me back" line too.)

Your little one might still answer any of this with "Big fuckity whoopty doo," and that's allowed. One beautiful session wouldn't convince me either, and I'd need to watch you do the same thing over and over. Choose one response small enough to repeat, maybe hearing that sarcasm out without snapping back, or the envelope again. Or something more basic, like keeping an appointment, eating, sleeping, or getting out of a conversation that's turning unsafe.
