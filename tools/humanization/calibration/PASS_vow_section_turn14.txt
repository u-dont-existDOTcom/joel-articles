Make a Simple Vow

If your promises still mean something to your little one, maybe because you've kept a few small ones, a quiet vow can help too. It does really help to imagine holding a baby in your arms to make the vow feel real. Imagine you just had a baby, how would you feel? Would you look at him/her with adoration and joy? Carry that into visualizing yourself with your own inner child:

"I know you're in there. I love you. You don't have to love me back or trust me yet. I can't promise that nothing painful will happen. Or that I'll always know what to do, or never react badly. But I can promise to tell you the truth and listen when you're angry. I'll make our life together safer where I can, and take the next step I can see to protect you. When I fail, I'll come back to make it right. You don't have to believe these words. Watch what I do."

The vow only promises what you can keep, so, unlike with those grown-ups, your little one can actually watch you keep it.

Only say the "I love you" part if you can feel it. If you don't feel the love yet, say you care (with the goodwill prayer from before). That's a first step. Or even before that, just promise your little one you won't be cruel to them on purpose.

Whichever you choose, your little one may still answer with "Big fuckity whoopty doo." That's allowed, and one beautiful session wouldn't convince me either. Pick one thing to do that's small enough to repeat. A big gesture you later drop would only prove them right to doubt you, but a small thing you keep doing can change their mind. Maybe it's one you already tried in "Not Every Hero Wears A Cape," or just keeping an appointment, eating, or going to bed.

Whenever your little one shows up looking for their parent, even if they don't know that's what they are doing, you go back to being the one they can rely on, and you're kind to them while you do it. If they show up to have some innocent fun, don't "put them in their place" as the adult. But sometimes they show up as the one who doesn't want to go to bed. Now you're in my mom's place: you still go to bed, and staying kind about it shows them bedtime isn't a lack of love.
