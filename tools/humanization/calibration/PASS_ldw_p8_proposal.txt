You can see the same move in a dramatized scene from the movie A Beautiful Day in the Neighborhood. Lloyd, a reporter, tells Mr. Rogers it must not have been easy for his sons to grow up with him as a father. He doesn't collapse under that or fight it, but he does answer what's worth answering in it. He agrees with Lloyd, talks honestly about both of his sons, and thanks him for the perspective. Like the two truths at bedtime, a true complaint and real love don't cancel each other out, so you can take the true part without giving up your love or defending yourself.
