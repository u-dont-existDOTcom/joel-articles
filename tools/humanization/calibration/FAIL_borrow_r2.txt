Borrow One Job at a Time

Say it's late, a text just made you feel about six years old, and nobody's around to be the grown-up. You don't need to picture a whole ideal parent right then. Most real people only did one or two of the three jobs well anyway. So pick the job that's missing and think of somebody who's good at that one, as long as being cared for by them never meant making yourself smaller.

If it's love that's missing and you can't feel any, start by not judging your little one. Don't rush them, and don't expect a thank-you for showing up. Non-judgement is enough to begin being the adult, but it's not necessarily enough for them to trust you and open up. So the next step is to find a nurturer in your life that you can remember fondly, and picture how they'd sit next to your little one. If there was none, you can look to examples like Mr. Rogers, or spiritual love you've felt, if you have.

Or maybe you need protecting. Ask what a minimally competent adult would do in the next ten minutes. Ten minutes is about as far ahead as a scared little one can handle anyway. Usually it's something like eating, or not answering that text yet, or going to sleep.

Guidance you can borrow from yourself. Maybe last week, when you were calm, you decided you weren't going to fight with this person over text anymore. That decision still counts even if you can't feel why you made it right now. That's what writing things down is for.

Your little one might not be thrilled to see you, either. If you get something like "Oh, so now you care?", you'll probably want to prove you're a good person. A decent adult would just stick around.

You might still feel about six the whole time, and you can do the grown-up thing anyway. Afterward you get to say, "I did that for us."
