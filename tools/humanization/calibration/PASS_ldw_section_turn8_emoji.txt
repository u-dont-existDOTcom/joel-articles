Love Doesn't Have to Wait for Trust 💛

Sometimes the love is there for your little one and still feels unsafe to them, or safe for a minute and then not. Love and trust don't have to come as a pair, but they got tied together, as if one had to vouch for the other. So your love can sound like you're asking them to trust you. It doesn't need their trust, but it does need you to be honest with them, which isn't the same as perfect. Otherwise it can just be pretend love, the kind a dark empath puts on (someone who reads feelings well and uses that to get something out of people).

The love can go first, though, before their trust is done going over your track record (they've heard what you still call yourself when you mess up, sometimes out loud). Your little one says "I don't trust you." You answer, "I believe you. And I love you."

I was about five when my mom told me it was time for bed (or something equally outrageous to a five-year-old). I cried and screamed "I HATE YOU!" She answered, "But I love you!"

That completely threw me. Fights don't usually go like that. I think that might be exactly why it got through to me. What I screamed was true right then, and what she said back was true too, so I could hate somebody who loved me and she'd still be my mom the whole time.

When it's your little one yelling at you, try not to turn "But I love you" into a line you say on cue, because then it can brush the anger off, and what it's really saying is "so stop being angry." I think you can mean it and still be doing that. Does the love stay afterward, and hear the anger, and take whatever's true in it seriously?

The love can stay, and your little one might still come back with "Big fuckity whoopty doo. I don't believe you." 🙄 You answer, "Okay. I believe that you don't believe me. I still love you. You don't have to love me back. Tell me what feels unsafe."
