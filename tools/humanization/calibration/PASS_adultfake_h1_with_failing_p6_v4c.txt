# When the Adult Voice Feels Fake

A lot of people get stuck right here. You call up the Nurturer and try to talk to your little one, and you get silence or a numb feeling, and all of a sudden your phone, dinner, or the laundry feels urgent. Or some voice says this is silly, and it's hard to tell which part of you is saying it.

My friend Scott said:

> "Half the time when I try to connect to baby me, I feel fake and it seems crazy."

I used to be all personal trainer and teach persistence on this as the remedy. Keeping at it does help, since a little one who learned to expect being dropped won't trust you right away. But I don't think that explains the adult feeling fully. More importantly, I think it could even be like unintentional gaslighting to the little one to ask to connect with them when you don't believe they're even real. Like when my mom pretended to listen to me when I was young, but even less excusable because she was not asking for my time when she did that. So, it's worth meditating first on why it feels fake.

## The Parent You Inherited

I'd look at the parent you inherited: how you learned to treat yourself from how people treated you growing up, long before you started building your Nurturer, Protector and Guide. It can be warm, but it's often more like a critic, or a warden with rules. The rules can even sound reasonable, like "You can come back to the table when you're done crying." If yours was like that, your little one learned bit by bit to hide what wasn't allowed, so they could keep belonging. They might still be doing it.

If it's a critic, the parent you inherited also measures whatever you show the world against an impossible ideal, and then attacks you from both sides: you're not good enough, and anything that does look good is fake anyway. That second one means nothing you do could ever count, and it's one place feeling fake can come from. When a new grown-up voice shows up saying "Trust me," of course your little one doesn't answer.

The new grown-up doesn't have to win your little one over first, or win the argument where the parent you inherited calls it fake. It can start outside your head, as a friend who likes you the way you are and keeps showing up. You can earn trust too, without faking a feeling, by getting off the phone whenever your mom starts comparing you to your cousin, before your little one hears the rest.
