Borrow One Job at a Time

Some people really can imagine one perfect parent. The Ideal Parent Figure method is built on that, and in a small pilot with adults traumatized as children, it seemed to help. Plenty of us try it and get a greeting card, though, or worse, our actual parent. I'd rather borrow one job at a time from real people who did that job well. Just not from anybody whose care needed you to disappear.

For the Nurturer, not judging is where it starts. The inherited critic will call that letting your little one off the hook, which is sort of its whole personality. Your little one doesn't owe you a thank-you for showing up, either. Not getting yelled at is still a long way from feeling wanted, though, and little ones notice. So think of somebody who took care of you that you remember fondly, and picture them sitting beside your little one. If there wasn't anybody, there's Mr. Rogers, or spiritual love if you've felt it.

Your little one might be sarcastic about all this, and honestly, fair. You'd be sarcastic too if some grown-up showed up after all these years acting like your parent. Arguing about how hard you're trying won't help much. A decent adult would let it slide and come back tomorrow.

Protecting can be babysitter level. What would any babysitter do in the next ten minutes? Feed everybody, lock the door, get people to bed, call somebody if it gets bad.

Guidance is the easiest one to borrow, because you can write it down. Whatever you decided when you were calm, or whatever a teacher you trust would want you practicing today, can sit on paper until you can hold it yourself.

You'll probably still feel like the little one the whole time, and that's fine. I don't think children feel brave while they're doing scary things, either. Somebody tells them later. So afterward you can tell yourself, "I did that for us. That was the Protector."
