Also notice what else in you answers your little one, besides what you choose to say. When they say "I don't believe you," something in you might fire back an accusation, and then they can learn your love is only there as long as they take it the right way. That urge to fire back is the bedtime anger again, from the parent's side this time: being doubted after you've tried feels unfair. But there's nothing unfair about them going over your track record, so there's no injustice for the accusation to fix. The part that resents not being believed might be the same one that said "I love you" in the first place, or a different one, or a bit of both. Don't decide those two are the same part just because they both talk in your grown-up voice. If you do, you might take the accusation as proof your love really is conditional, when it could just be another part talking.
