WHAT IT IS: [one line: what kind of text this is, for whom, about what]

Read it the way you really would in that moment: quickly, taking each line at face value, with what you know about your own
situation. For every line (a sign, a step, a claim, a reason, a finding), ask four questions:

- ANYWAY: Does it tell you to look for, worry about or expect something that is already true in your situation when
  nothing is wrong? (Someone on a psychedelic sees things; muscles ache after a hard workout; a new diet changes digestion
  for a while.) Then it can't tell you anything. A line that says to expect such a change, and not to worry about it,
  fits.
- CAN'T TELL: Does it ask you to notice, judge, measure or do something you couldn't, right then, with what you have and
  know? What couldn't you tell?
- FROM: Is it a rule, sign, detail or reason carried over from another kind of text or setting (a general health manual,
  a clinic, a hospital ward, a textbook, an article about something else) that doesn't hold here, means something else
  here, or doesn't fit what this text is about? A danger sign that holds everywhere (lips turning blue, one side of the
  face drooping) fits, and so does a study's finding said with where it was found (in rats, in a dish): that is evidence
  the reader can weigh, not a rule carried over.
- MISREAD: Read quickly, does it sound like the opposite of what it means, or would it alarm or reassure you when it
  shouldn't, until a word more says which?
