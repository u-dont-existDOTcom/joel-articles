# Persona Positive Attractor — Somatic Intro Humanization

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: owner-directed experimental generation protocol; not article authority; no detector authorization.

## Owner correction

Joel proposed combining the existing negative attractor control (`NEAT = DANGER`) with a positive human-personality attractor. The intended purpose is not superficial style mimicry or lexical copying. It is to shift what the writer notices, ignores, interrupts, finds boring, finds funny, distrusts, leaves unfinished, and follows out of curiosity.

## Research boundary

Current external evidence is mixed and must not be overstated:

- Persona prompting can materially improve human-likeness in conversational/Turing-test settings.
- Controlled studies of direct individual-style mimicry show that LLMs still often remain distinguishable from human writing and can remain detector-visible.
- Therefore do not treat `imitate writer X` as a proven detector bypass.

The useful hypothesis for this task is narrower: a coherent persona may provide a *positive alternative attractor* that competes with the model's default completion architecture, while `NEAT = DANGER` rejects the old closure attractor.

## Protocol

1. Persona is a cognitive policy, not a bag of surface tics.
2. Prefer Joel's own owner-supplied prose, corrections, judgments, and demonstrated thought behavior for the positive attractor.
3. Do not invent autobiography, memories, symptoms, chronology, facts, or personal claims to strengthen persona effects.
4. Do not transplant unrelated owner prose or donor wording.
5. Do not imitate another living writer's distinctive phrasing as publication prose. If a non-Joel persona is used experimentally, keep it at high-level behavioral traits (e.g. curious, impatient with repetition, willing to stop mid-thought, skeptical of neatness), not copied syntax/catchphrases.
6. Let persona affect attention and stopping: what is worth saying next, what is boring, what feels overexplained, what gets dropped.
7. Keep `NEAT = DANGER` before acceptance: a candidate continuation that feels satisfying because it closes, balances, summarizes, or resolves is presumptively rejected before output.
8. Avoid replacing every rejected completion with its inverse. Allow silence, topic interruption, mutation, deletion, and later return.
9. Use mutation/recombination of live thoughts to generate new local possibilities, but reject mutations that merely restate already-understood content.
10. During free generation, semantic completeness is not a composition requirement; reconcile against the frozen owner source afterward.
11. Persona does not certify fidelity, authorship, or Pangram status.
12. Any publication candidate still requires normal preservation, architecture, cold-read, owner, and detector gates.

## Working hypothesis for current Somatic Introduction

Use a Joel-like stance only in the bounded sense already evidenced in the current owner interaction:

- curious rather than dutiful;
- impatient with repetition and explanation after the point has landed;
- willing to stop without closure;
- willing to let multiple thoughts coexist or interrupt one another;
- suspicious of polished mini-arguments and neat progressions;
- interested in what is actually strange or unresolved rather than covering every source obligation immediately;
- direct when something seems stupid, artificial, or overworked.

This is not authorization to fabricate Joel's experiences or to turn remembered/corpus mannerisms into detector camouflage.

## Experimental prediction

A useful persona implementation should change *selection dynamics before prose appears*: fewer semantic cards, fewer balanced exceptions, fewer three-beat distinctions, less aftercare, more uneven attention, more local interruption, and more true stopping points. If the output remains a sequence of complete conceptual units with casual wording, the persona has failed to alter the attractor and should not be credited.
