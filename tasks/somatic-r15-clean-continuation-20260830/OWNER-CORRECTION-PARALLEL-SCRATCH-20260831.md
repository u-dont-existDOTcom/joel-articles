# Owner correction — parallel scratch / non-serialized thought

Updated: 2026-08-31
Status: **CURRENT GENERATIVE PROCESS CORRECTION**

Joel corrected the exploratory Somatic Introduction method again.

The prior `follow one live thread until it dies` rule was still too serial. Human writing can keep several partial thoughts active at once. A thought from material that would appear later in the finished article can interrupt, alter, or illuminate an earlier thought before either is complete. Threads can disappear and return. The writer need not finish one idea before another becomes active.

For the current exploratory phase:

- do not serialize thought into one-thread-at-a-time progression;
- allow several incomplete thoughts to coexist without forcing a hierarchy;
- let a later/adjacent thought interrupt an earlier one when it genuinely changes what is interesting;
- do not convert those interactions into a visible outline or comprehensive map;
- do not exhaust all active threads;
- silence, abandonment, return, deletion, and unresolved overlap are allowed;
- do not write toward audience comprehension, length, completeness, or semantic coverage;
- do not infer that all source obligations must appear in the exploratory draft; reconciliation happens later;
- the goal is not to simulate a transcript of every latent thought, but to preserve the small subset that actually becomes salient enough to jot down.

This composes with the owner corrections already recorded for audience removal, free exploratory generation, word/clause-level rereading, and the ban on exhaustive scratch-space coverage.
