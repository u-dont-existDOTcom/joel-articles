`docs/HUMANIZATION-PRESERVATION-GATE.md` requires the changed-scope preservation-unit ledger and authorized-change whitelist to be frozen **before drafting**. Decision 003 instead placed the exhaustive semantic custodian after the blind discourse-motion gate.

### 3. Deletion authority was too weak — FAIL

The writer rule allowed deletion when a function remained `unambiguously present elsewhere`. Repository authority is stricter: `inferable`, `redundant`, and generic reader-availability are not deletion authority. A removed unit requires a pre-authorized disposition such as exact duplicate-function consolidation with a named surviving realization/destination, movement to a named destination, owner supersession, or owner deletion.

The prior wording improperly delegated a substantive preservation judgment to the writer.

### 4. The causal claim for source-only topology surgery was overstated — FAIL AS LAUNCH AUTHORITY

The useful part of the diagnosis remains: a compressed semantic brief can still become a latent coverage plan, and a model asked to realize the whole inventory in one pass can expose that plan as polished explanatory architecture.

But zero-new-token source surgery does not by itself solve the inverse problem: if the AI source realization itself lacks the live intellectual route, epistemic friction, or reader-facing movement needed by the current humanization protocols, deletion and relocation cannot create that missing material. They can only expose or remove relations already present.

The exact Introduction boundary also contains multiple likely unique functions: the local definition of `somatic`, readiness/stop/afterward checks, the non-ladder distinction among regulation/discharge/focused trauma work, and the inner-child connection. The allowed algebra therefore has little obviously legal room without a pre-frozen consolidation ledger.

Prior Somatic surface evidence also contains a topology-only null in another exact boundary. That does not prove topology is universally ineffective, but it lowers the information value of treating topology surgery as a sufficiently justified next production family.

### 5. The outside-validator problem was only contained, not solved — FAIL

The owner had already identified the deeper orchestration problem: a hard rejection can prevent a bad candidate from reaching him, but rejection alone does not cause a better next pass when the writer can simply ignore instructions or reproduce the same architecture.

Decision 003 improved containment with mechanical checks and a two-candidate kill condition, but its blind validators still mostly acted as admission gates. They did not change the information available to the writer or the generation process that produced the failed movement. Therefore the design did not fully answer the owner's objection.
