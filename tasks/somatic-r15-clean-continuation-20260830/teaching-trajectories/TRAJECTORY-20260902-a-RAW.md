# Introduction

Going straight to the deepest memory can look like the serious way to do trauma work. With complex or developmental trauma, it may simply be too much. A traumatic event can be over, and you can know you're safe, while the body still reacts as though the danger is present. If what comes up takes away your ability to remain present or stop—and you are still not basically yourself afterward—I don't think “deep processing” is an honest name for what happened.

That is a reason to work on regulation, not to push harder. Sometimes thinking has gone as far as it can while accumulated survival activation is still there; the body may need a way to discharge or resolve it. A person who already has enough stability may go directly into EMDR or another trauma-focused method.

[Inner-child therapy](http://innerchild.u-dont-exist.com/) depends on there being enough of an adult present to do it. You can understand exactly what a younger part needs and still get pulled so far into the child-state that you can't provide it. Sometimes the body has to become manageable before that adult perspective is available again.
