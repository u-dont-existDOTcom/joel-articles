# Introduction

A person can know perfectly well that the event is over and that they're safe while the body is still responding as if danger is present. Sometimes the story is already understood and accumulated survival activation is still being carried in the body; some bodily way for it to discharge or resolve may reach what more thinking doesn't. In inner-child work ([http://innerchild.u-dont-exist.com/](http://innerchild.u-dont-exist.com/)), someone can know exactly what the younger part needs and still be too caught in the trauma/child-state to give it; sometimes the body has to become manageable enough before the adult perspective is available at all.

With complex or developmental trauma, I don't automatically want the deepest memory first. Once the person is no longer present enough to choose whether to stop, I care a lot less about how “deep” the work is. I'd rather get the choice back, and I also want the person basically back to themselves afterward. Regulation may be what makes that possible; without that much room, “deep processing” may just be a nicer name for overwhelm. If the stability is already there, I wouldn't make somebody do somatic preparation just because it makes a cleaner treatment sequence. Going straight to EMDR or another trauma-focused method may make more sense.
