# Building Enough Safety to Stay Present

## Somatic Experiencing

[Somatic Experiencing](http://traumahealing.org/) is what I reach for when merely getting near the material sends my body over the edge. I can begin with a sensation, orient to the room, notice an impulse or defensive response, and slow down the pace. The whole story can wait.

The useful move is not to beat trauma in a fight. I touch only as much activation as I can honestly tolerate, see what happens, then come back toward something steadier. That is titration and pendulation in practice. Some people spend three to six months building that ability, and severe developmental trauma can take longer. I treat those numbers as orientation, not a countdown. If somebody is already stable and has one clear target, I see no reason to make them wait out arbitrary months before adding focused trauma work.

**[EXISTING SOMATIC EXPERIENCING YOUTUBE EMBED — exact object retained in HTML promotion]**

## Trauma-Sensitive / Restorative Yoga

Power yoga, hot yoga, and somebody announcing, “Open your hips and release your trauma,” are not what I mean here. I mean the slow stuff: trauma-sensitive yoga, restorative yoga, gentle somatic yoga, maybe very gentle yin with props, shorter holds, and an easy way out.

Most of the useful instructions are permissions:

- “You might try…”
- “You can keep your eyes open.”
- “Come out of the shape whenever you want.”
- “Is this useful, neutral, or too much?”
- “There can be several right versions. Pick the one that fits your body.”

I leave out intense stretching, breath retention, forced breathwork, aggressive hip opening, long passive holds, hands-on adjustments without clear consent, and pushing through because suffering is supposedly therapeutic. Once I cannot choose, it is no longer the practice I mean.

## Gentle Shaking / TRE

The shaking here is also the small version. Basic [TRE](https://traumaprevention.com/frequently-asked-questions/), very light neurogenic tremoring, gentle shaking while standing, or a short bit of playful movement may be enough. I am not trying to blast trauma out of my body. If it becomes compulsive, frightening, dissociative, or hard to stop, I have gone past gentle.

Stop, make the movement smaller, support your head, or have an experienced provider help support it.
