# Rules audit: clusters

404 flags: 34 clusters (31 active, 3 historical), 8 stale items, 3 dropped. In rank order; rules, his words and flag ids are in CLUSTERS.json.

**C01 Near-miss step limit: a word or two, 20% / 40% refusals** (active, high, 14 flags, mechanical test). His point was clarifying what a reader could take two ways; the rule keeps the size of his edits. Fix: Start from the near miss and fix what is unclear, however many words that takes; small checked steps are a habit for wording-only tries, never a tool refusal.

**C02 Per-sentence receipts and link-word checks before every Pangram try** (active, high, 12 flags, unasked steps). He asked for lessons to stick and for sentences that follow, not a per-sentence form. Fix: Read each new passage for sense and say a link where the reader would have to work it out; keep any receipt light and never let it block a small change.

**C03 "Payoff first" fixed as a form on every sentence and paragraph** (active, high, 13 flags, mechanical test). He says the editor doesn't understand the term, yet the editor's own definition, read off one or two fixes, is enforced everywhere. Fix: Ask him what payoff first means, with two examples. Until then judge whether the reader knows why they are reading; drop the per-sentence form and the tense, opener and abbreviation tests.

**C04 Nothing below Human High: hard gate, Medium chased, approved changes held** (active, high, 15 flags, always/never from one case). His reason was learning, with "i guess" and "if you can"; the rule is an absolute refusal that conflicts with his Medium ruling, stalls work, and holds back content he approved. Fix: Low is fixed; below High gets a real try and is then listed; his approved changes go in at once; one rule on Medium for all lanes, and windows he called a bug are left alone.

**C05 When a stuck paragraph goes to him: "ask as soon as" against "keep fixing"** (active, high, 12 flags, contradiction). A worker can't obey both, and the "ask" rules hand the work back to him. Fix: Keep fixing up to the budget (restore dropped reasoning, change approach); go to him only for knowledge or decisions that are his, with usually one or two teachable paragraphs and what was tried.

**C06 Cuts and merges made without showing him** (active, high, 17 flags, his words stretched). "Regardless of Pangram" became "without asking"; "said once" became "his paragraph wins". Fix: Cut drafted repeats when the guide reads better, keep anything only one copy says, and list every cut or merge so he can put one back; cuts to his own text or warnings are proposed. Ask him which way the dedup sentence reads.

**C07 Pangram budget: six against ten, paid single paragraphs against free Substack** (active, high, 14 flags, contradiction). "On the right track" became a score comparison that one fickle reading stops; units still say six. Fix: The limit stops blind tests: go on while each try has a named reason. Caps apply to paid checks only; more than a handful of paragraphs go through a free Substack post; a section slightly over six paragraphs passes with a warning.

**C08 Safety, caution and reassurance lines: kept, cut, or sent to him?** (active, high, 8 flags, contradiction). The units contradict each other, so an editor obeying one restores what he cut. Fix: Keep a safety or reassurance line only where it changes what the reader does and isn't already said; ask him only where a caution could harm or contradicts his method; harm-reduction lines stay as he wrote them.

**C09 One content answer stretched to other text or to every article** (active, high, 14 flags, his words stretched). He spoke of the one case. Fix: Change what he named.

**C10 Word-by-word accounting in place of a meaning check** (active, high, 12 flags, mechanical test). His point each time was a lost scope or a changed concept. Fix: Check that each claim keeps its meaning, scope and condition in the same sentence.

**C11 Overused words and shapes hard-failed by the linter** (active, high, 21 flags, overused made banned). He said overused, high frequency, "be aware of". Fix: These are overuse cautions: the linter reports them for a reader to judge, never a required change, and the word stays where it is the one meant.

**C12 Whose words: his typed wording, kept lines, and the editor's own guesses** (active, high, 13 flags, contradiction). The rules pull both ways: some let the editor rewrite or respell wording he typed, others freeze lines (including the editor's guesses) so a real error can't be raised. Fix: Wording he typed or ruled on stays unless it makes no sense, and then he is shown.

**C13 His list fixes made a template for every list** (active, high, 4 flags, fix feature as method). The shape of one paragraph of his is made a recipe. Fix: Unpack a compressed list so each item reads as a real thing said to this reader.

**C14 "Plain word" misreads "converted into"; join and plain word made compulsory** (active, high, 5 flags, fix feature as method). "Converted into" is the exact term, not the plainer one; his objection was an odd paraphrase written to dodge the detector. Fix: Check first where each payoff lands.

**C15 Human tell: defined narrower than he said, two definitions live** (active, high, 5 flags, his words stretched). **C16 Contents order: ask him first, move by move, tree tests** (active, high, 12 flags, unasked steps). **C21 Shapes of one rewrite made recipes** (active, medium, 22 flags, fix feature as method). None of these shapes is a reason he gave; his words were about sense, accuracy and not repeating. Fix: Record each as what one passing fix of his did, to try where it fits.

**C22 Counts standing in for judgment: threes, questions in a row, short lines, contrasts, repeats** (active, medium, 24 flags, mechanical test). **C23 One-case phrases made string tells** (active, medium, 21 flags, mechanical test). His reason each time was meaning in one sentence, or he named no phrase at all.
