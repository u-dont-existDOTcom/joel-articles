# Fixing one of Joel's articles: the working order

**ACTIVE (Joel, 2026-10-21 00:49 UTC):** "update the joel-articles guidance so that next time when i ask GPT to fix an article it can do it as well as you could."

This page is for any worker Joel asks to fix an article: GPT, Codex or Claude. It gives the order the Inner Child lane works in, and the checks that caught what earlier passes missed. The detailed rules stay where they are (`docs/INDEX.md` lists them); this page says when each one comes in. Where it and a gate disagree, the gate wins, and the gap goes to Joel's questions page.

## 1. Before changing anything

1. **Save Joel's message first, word for word,** in the article's `experiments/tNN-answers/` folder, with any screenshot he sent. Everything later quotes it.
2. **Read the article's state:** `CURRENT-STATE.md` (its next step), `OWNER-QUESTIONS.md` (what he has answered and what's open) and `OWNER-EDITS.json` (each of his edits, with the strings that prove it's in). Read its `KEEP-AS-IS.json` too, and keep it open: what he has said stays as it is (a joke, a word he chose, a device, a name changed on purpose) and the cuts he meant, none of which you flag, fix or put back (gate E340). Read the article's handoff note if there is one.
3. **Find his own changes first.** When he sends a version of the article (an upload, a Substack draft, the copy he gave another worker), compare it block by block with the repository's version before anything else. Every difference is a decision of his: moves, new headings, heading levels, cuts, new paragraphs. Put them in word for word and fix only obvious typos, listing each one. In turn 77 this found 28 changed paragraphs, a section move and four typos. **A version of his that runs across what the article has in two places goes in where he wrote it, as one piece, and the copy elsewhere is what goes (gate bad-trips-2026-11-09b; Joel, 2026-11-09 01:24 UTC: "why did you lose my fixes from before? i'm putting them back").**
4. **When he says he tested something in Pangram, don't test it again.**
5. **A file he will edit carries the article's links and pictures, checked before it goes (gate E337).** Run `tools/humanization/file_links_check.py --article ARTICLE FILE` on every .docx, .odt or HTML file you send him to edit: it fails when a link of the article (by address) or a picture is missing. In turn 115 the Inner Child guide went to him as a Word file built from plain text, with no links and no pictures; he reorganized the whole guide in it, and it came back with 6 of its 67 links. When he sends a version back, compare its links and pictures with the article's too (the Inner Child lane's `articles/inner-child-therapy/experiments/t116-joel/links.py` and `t116-review/relink.py`). And before you say which of the post's pictures and videos stay, list them from the live post itself, never from the last guide or memory: save the post's page and run `tools/humanization/post_embeds.py POST.html --article ARTICLE`, which lists every picture, video, audio and post card in it with the words before each, and the ones the article has no note for. Each goes in the article as an IMAGE or EMBED note on its own line where it goes (gate E341: the turn-116 card asked him to keep seven of the Inner Child post's eleven). Instagram embeds are in that list too: run it with `--instagram` and paste the line it prints into an instagram.com page after `tools/humanization/instagram_alive.js`, and each dead one goes to him with its post and Substack's saved snapshot image as the replacement to offer (gate embeds-2026-11-21a).
6. **Look the article up in `articles/BUILT-ON.json`: some of his posts are built on others (lesson toc-2026-11-24b; Joel, 2026-11-20 23:40 UTC: "my hypnosis guide and my hearthwork guide are heavily based on my inner child guide. when i update my inner child guide, those should also be updated (but carefully so as not to break pangram score if they are humanized already)"; 2026-11-24 21:05 UTC, adding the somatic guide: "whenever i change those you should also think to merge the changes into this one (and i shouldn't have to tell you this either, right?)").** A change to a source post (a new point, a changed step, a cut, a meaning made clearer) goes into the posts built on it in the same turn, or to him as a question when it isn't clear where it lands. A lane working on a built-on post first compares its built-on sections with the sources as they are now, so the post is edited once. Each change lands through the humanization rules of the post it goes into: a changed paragraph is checked by itself and goes in only at Human High (`tools/humanization/apply_gate.py`), his own words go to him, and the post on Substack is updated only with his yes. A paragraph that reads Human High now and would only gain a fact may be better left with a link to the source. Record each catch-up's date in the file; `lane_push.py` prints the file's entries for the article it pushes. The Self-Hypnosis guide's September sync with the inner child guide never reached the live post, and two months of changes to both went across to neither while the map still called his answer of 2026-11-20 an open question. **A sentence brought from one of his posts into another comes with its links (lesson toc-2026-11-25f; Joel, 2026-11-25 16:00 UTC, on a card that brought two sentences of the inner child guide into the Self-Hypnosis guide: "i had warmlines linked in the inner child guide tho make sure it's still linked right? always carry the links").** The texts this repository keeps of his live posts are words only, so a copied sentence arrives bare. Before a card or an edit carries a sentence, run `tools/humanization/carried_links.py SOURCE.html "the sentence"` on the source post's body (the post API's `body_html`): it says which of the sentence's words are linked there and to where. The card shows the link, and the edit puts it in. A link elsewhere in the source paragraph, outside the carried words, does not come along.

## 2. His words and his life

- **Whose words: what he wrote himself, not what he humanized (gate E373; Joel, 2026-11-06 17:30 UTC: "'my words' for an AI article, are not my words, unless those were human before we started humanizing (i.e if i wrote that myself inside an ai article) otherwise it's AI words that i humanized").** In an article a model drafted, the rules below for his words cover only what he wrote himself into it. The model's text is yours to fix after he humanizes it too: his versions on cards, the lines he kept, the published post. Keep what he decided in it (a cut, a fact, a meaning he made clear, a reason put first), fix the wording yourself when it reads AI or doesn't make sense, and test the fix. Never hand it back to him as his words to fix: "your job is to do the fixing, that's the whole point here" (the one exception is the teaching paragraphs below).
- **Ask him only where the meaning might change, there's a downside to weigh, or the words are his own (gates gut-2026-11-07b, bad-trips-2026-11-07a; Joel, 2026-11-07 12:08 UTC, after approving all 18 cards of ten rounds: "you're asking me to approve things that are clearly good / you should be learning what you actually need to ask my opinion on, like if meaning might have changed or there's a downside to consider").** Every other tested change goes in, named in the turn's report with its Pangram links: a rewording, a point moved first, a sourced gloss, a repeat cut, a warning said once where its full version stands nearby. A card says why it goes to him in "ask_because" ("meaning: ...", "downside: ...", "his own: ..."), shown to him as "Why I'm asking", and `suggestion_review.py check` refuses one without. A card-by-card pass is only for an author who asks for it ("fine_grained" on the page, with his words and when).
- **Read his record before building a page (gate bad-trips-2026-11-07b; Joel: "make sure you're continuously learning from what i approve vs what i revise or reject so you have a good understanding of what needs asking").** `tools/humanization/approval_record.py --article NAME` tallies his answers by card type, with what he wrote on each card he revised or rejected and each comment on one he approved; the page check prints it for the page's types. A type he has approved every time (five or more, none revised or rejected) goes to him only when the card differs from those in meaning or downside.
- **When no wording you try reads human, he gets the one or two paragraphs he could teach you the most from, with your best tested tries (gate bad-trips-2026-11-07c; Joel: "give me the top most important paragraphs (usually 1 or 2) that youu think i could teach you from that would help you humanize the rest, and give me your best tries on those to fix").** That is AI in every wording tried, after a look across the article (lesson 06a) and the tell panel. Pick the paragraphs whose pattern the most stuck stretches share. Each goes on the marking page as a stretch to edit whose "whose" is "he asked: ..." with his words and when, carrying your best tries with their Pangram readings and links and naming the stretches his fix would teach. Before that, a fresh reader reads the paragraph for sense (unclear words, claims that don't hold up, repeats), and the card's "sense" says what it found and how your tries handle it; the page check refuses the card without it (gate bad-trips-2026-11-07i; Joel, 2026-11-07, on "turn a hard experience into content": "uh, what does "content" mean? you didn't notice that immediately?"). Run `tools/humanization/tells_lint.py` on every try before its Pangram check. The rest stays yours, done the way his fix shows.
- **Sense first, then Pangram (lesson bad-trips-2026-11-21d; Joel, 2026-11-21 15:57 UTC: "my approach requires not even thinking about pangram at first ... once i fully make it look right, then i can chheck if pangram agrees").** The fresh reads run again on each new version of a text of yours, and its check waits until they mark nothing the text hasn't answered: a result on a text still being fixed goes back and forth and decides nothing. When a text that reads right still reads AI, the next change is what in it still doesn't connect, repeats or isn't right, in the same direction, never a new form for the detector (SKILL.md, "Writing for Pangram: his fixes come first"; ways 13 to 17 of `tools/humanization/his_fixes_brief.md`).
- **A repeat goes when cutting it reads better, without a Pangram reason or a card (gate E378; Joel, 2026-11-06 19:43 UTC: "make the cuts regardless of pangram if it makes the guide read better, that was what we were supposed to do already prior").** Where the article's goal is saying each thing once, cut what the article says more fully in its own place, and move the one detail only the repeat had. A sentence he left standing while fixing its wording isn't his decision to keep it; and a check that stays AI is a reason to keep fixing, not to stop and hand him a choice that isn't his.
- His text goes in word for word, with his apostrophes, emoji and capitals as he typed them: a section or a line he writes for the article. Fix only obvious typos, and say which. An answer that explains something to you is his content, not the line (gate E261 (Inner Child)): write the article sentence from it, keeping his facts and the phrases of his that read as article prose, and show it beside his words. Joel, 2026-10-25, on three answers of his I had put in as typed: "you put in some of my things too verbatim".
- Never add a fact about his life that he didn't state. If a paragraph needs one, ask.
- **Generic advice a model added goes; don't tidy it (gate bad-trips-2026-11-09i; Joel, 2026-11-09 20:08 UTC, on a card that moved the Grounding Menu's Room section first: "this is too much boilerplate stuff that ai added in to my guide. help mne locate more boilerplate stuff that could be removed").** In an article a model drafted, before a card moves, reorders or rewords a passage, ask whether it is standard advice any search would give that carries neither his method nor a safety point the reader needs: grounding lists, orientation scripts, comfort objects, evidence notes. If it is, offer the cut instead, shown as the section would read without it, with what it keeps. Safety lines (when to call medics, a warning, a dose) are not boilerplate however generic, and what he wrote himself never is (gate E373). **A caution is not exempt (lesson bad-trips-2026-11-25j; Joel, 2026-11-25 17:42 UTC, on a card whose text kept the post's "Poison control may be useful when ... value of charcoal is unclear" and led with it: "i do not recommend calling poison control to see about giving charcoal ... poison control said they aren't allowed to tell them to take charcoal, so it left them very confused").** For each instruction in a caution that has no version or answer of his behind it, write what happens to the reader who follows it, from the guide's own entries and the source it cites, and ask on the card whether it is what he'd tell someone; the fidelity keys keep a warning from being lost by accident, they don't make a model's warning his. A number from a source goes into a caution only with the cases it was measured in, and the question whether the guide's cases are those (lesson bad-trips-2026-11-25k; the first hour for charcoal is from pure, fast-absorbed drugs: "that's not true for many plant medicines which can take hours to digest"). A list or definition he pastes from a model to say what he means is still a model's: each item is tested against what the reader would see anyway, and a sign goes into a "how to tell" sentence only if it is absent in the ordinary case (lesson bad-trips-2026-11-26b: GPT's symptoms of "neurological disorganization" were mostly what any strong trip brings; the guide's own brain checks were linked instead, and what was left out went to him on a card).
- **What he says stays as it is goes on the article's keep-as-is list the turn he says it (gate E340; Joel, 2026-11-03, on a card that asked about his Lao Tsu joke: "that's the joke yeah you forgot. i guess you need to somehow keep track of the jokes in an article more durably").** When he answers that something you'd flag is meant (a joke, a word, a coinage, a sentence that runs on into its heading, a name changed on purpose, where a video sits) or that a cut was on purpose, add it to the article's `KEEP-AS-IS.json`: the exact words, the kind, why it looks like something to fix and why it isn't, his words, when and where. Something you judge deliberate without asking goes in too, marked "my reading, not asked". Before flagging anything, check it (`tools/humanization/keep_as_is.py is-kept ARTICLE "words"`); the marking page refuses a card on kept words, and the article page shows a red box when kept words go or a meant cut comes back.
- No drug amounts or recipes, even inside a warning.
- Plain words for the guide's therapy words. Never call the inner child "the kid".
- Reassurance lines are content, not scaffolding: this article's readers are often struggling. Don't cut one to tighten a paragraph.
- New wording in his article (a heading, a bridge sentence) is his call. Mark it as yours and ask.
- Every question offers "leave it" and "your words" where they fit, and says which option you'd pick and why.
- Give a question's sub-items numbers (72.1, 72.2) and its options letters (A, B), so an answer like "72.1 B" can't be misread. Letters for both overlapped in question 72 (Joel, 2026-10-21: "your numbering/lettering options are overlapping a bit").

## 3. Moving sections

- **The table of contents first (gate bad-trips-2026-11-09e; Joel, 2026-11-09 17:22 UTC: "maybe it would hlep if you look only at the ToC first, have you tried that? make sure the ToC is optimized then we can make sure the stuff in each section is what belongs there rather than merging or moving?").** Before anything moves or merges between sections, read the contents alone. Give fresh readers only the headings, at their levels with their word counts, and real situations a reader comes with ("a week later, the world still feels unreal"), and ask where each would look first and how sure they are. That's a tree test: a guess or a wrong first pick is a heading that doesn't say what's under it, or a section in the wrong place. Fix the order, levels and names, test the new contents the same way with a fresh reader, and only then check that each section holds what belongs there. The bad trips guide's first run is `articles/bad-trips/humanize/h39-20261109/toc/`.
- **Headings are read in Substack's narrow contents sidebar (gate inner-child-2026-11-16a; Joel, 2026-11-16 02:19 UTC: "we should be optimizing substack posts specifrically for their very narrow toc sidebar so we might need to rethink some headings").** The sidebar lists every h1 to h4 on one line and cuts the rest off with "…": measured in his post's own menu, it leaves 217px for an h1, 205 for an h2, 193 for an h3 and 181 for an h4, at 15px (about 26, 24, 22 and 20 characters); an h5 isn't listed. `tools/humanization/toc_sidebar.py` says how the sidebar shows a heading or a whole contents (fits, tight, or cut, and what's left). Give the contents test's readers the headings as the sidebar shows them, cut-offs included. Pangram's extension, the free route his posts are checked by (item 2 under the Pangram rules below), leaves headings out of what it reads, so a rename alone changes no window of his post there, and a heading's own window may read Low or Medium (gates toc-2026-11-23c and e; Joel, 2026-11-23: "i care more if it shows body text as not high conf"). What has to read as high as before is the body text the contents work changed or moved (a sentence fixed beside a heading, a line set on its own, a moved section): check it in its section in the web app, without the heading, and a moved section in the whole post too, the free way (his extension on the live post as it was and on a temporary draft's secret preview link), never in the web app (gate toc-2026-11-23d); a whole-post check text leaves the headings out, as `tools/humanization/substack_check_text.py` does. A whole post read by his extension is judged by what he sees on the post: when he says it reads all High there, the short pieces its result page lists at Medium or Low between the long High windows are recorded and not raised with him (lesson toc-2026-11-24d; Joel, 2026-11-24 21:35 UTC, on the Nirvāna post's 51 such pieces, 1 to 52 words each: "nirvana post reads all high conf on substack, it's not the same as when you check on pangram gui"). When a heading has to be longer, the words that say what's under it come first; a child heading needn't repeat its parent; keep his words and his playful ones, and ask before replacing them. The inner child guide's first run (`articles/inner-child-therapy/experiments/t130-toc/`): 105 of its 147 listed headings were cut off; in that run, fresh readers' first picks on the cut-off headings were about as often right as on the whole ones, though they called them guesses far more often; the check of 17 November, with three readers a contents and 30 more situations, found the renamed contents ahead on first picks too (about 54 of 68, against about 46).
- **Before a contents goes to him, no two headings in a row say or ask the same thing, and every hard heading has a fate (lessons bad-trips-2026-11-21a and b; Joel, 2026-11-21 13:04 UTC: "why would you put 2 serotonin syndrome headings in a row").** The sidebar doesn't show a parent's text, so a parent and its first child sit on two lines in a row: the parent names the topic once, each child its own part ("Serotonin Syndrome (SS)?", then "How to Recognize It", "What to Do Right Away"). The contents readers write their hardest headings into their read files ("HARD: line | why"), and `tools/humanization/toc_check.py` lists every heading that shares a word with its parent or the heading before it and every heading a reader marked, and fails until each has a fate, changed or kept with its reason (rule 11 and "Before the contents goes to him" in `tools/humanization/headings_brief.md`). Round 47's readers had flagged the serotonin pair in the chat and the page went out with it. A page that saves his picks declares its database before it goes out (lesson bad-trips-2026-11-21c).
- **Check the test before the pick goes to him (gates inner-child-2026-11-17a to g, `docs/lessons/2026-11-17-1807-inner-child.md`; Joel, 2026-11-17 18:07 UTC, on turn 130's contents work: "Check now with fable").** Read the pick itself with fresh readers, not only the versions it was put together from. Have an agent that has seen no contents write the situations from the text with its headings taken out, and a second one read the text for answering sections the key missed. Readers of one model agree with each other, so compare totals and trust a heading's effect only when it repeats. Look at who each renamed heading pulls in, not only who it helps. Rename only where the cut hides the point, in his words and his capitals, and run the keep-as-is list against every rename. Where two headings each find half of a section's readers, offer him a second heading. The inner child guide's check is `articles/inner-child-therapy/experiments/t131-toc-check/`.
- **A model's reorganization is a proposal (gate inner-child-2026-11-20a; Joel, 2026-11-20 16:39 UTC: "which model could get closet (or better) than my order with sufficient universal instructions that could apply to any article?").** Draft one with `tools/humanization/contents_order_brief.md` and put it to him move by move (item 8 below). Ask for them before the sort, never after a miss: in real work there's no outline of his to compare with. - **Name the headings with the headings brief (gate inner-child-2026-11-20b; Joel, 2026-11-20 16:39 UTC: "see if you can make universal instructions that would have produced my heading picks here").** Give Fable `tools/humanization/headings_brief.md`: writing fresh, it came closest to his own picks for the inner child guide (the same or close on 134 of 147, against 129 for Sonnet and for GPT, one run each). The brief's last section lists the kinds of pick all three still missed; read the result against them before it goes to him.
- **Every section mention carries a link (gate inner-child-2026-11-20c; Joel, 2026-11-20 16:39 UTC: "sections mentioned are always linked to").** Where the text names another section or points to one ("more on that in Noticing Counts, further on"), those words link to it: find the mentions with `tools/humanization/section_refs.py`, make each link with `tools/humanization/section_links.py url`, and open it on the rendered page. A mention left unlinked on purpose goes in the article's CURRENT-STATE.md with the reason, so the next pass leaves it. The check runs with the contents test, before the picks go to him, and whatever goes to him (a page, a results file, a chat message) links each section of his post it names to its anchor on the live post (lesson toc-2026-11-23b). **Every heading change fixes the links to it in the same edit (gate inner-child-2026-11-20g; Joel, 2026-11-20 21:49 UTC: "every time a heading changes, we have to fix the anchor links [...] so the anchor links should all be catalogued so this is automatic and easy").** A post with section links keeps `SECTION-LINKS.json` beside its article: every heading with its anchor, every link with where it sits and the section it points to, and the mentions left unlinked on purpose with the reason (`section_links.py build` makes one from the post's HTML). When he changes a heading himself, run the same rename the turn he says so. - **After a rename, read the sentences on either side of the heading (gate inner-child-2026-11-20d; Joel, 2026-11-20 16:39 UTC: "if the heading was shortened and that made the intro sentence no longer make sense, we fix the intro sentence, like with "Confused After Trips?" as it no longer follows").** The sentence that runs into the heading and the first one under it: one that leaned on the old heading's words is fixed with the rename. - **Show each option as it would really look, and say how sure you are (gate bad-trips-2026-11-09f; Joel, 2026-11-09 17:22 UTC: "keep in mind how confident you are that this is the best idea in this case, ask me for teaching cards when unsure about anything, make sure i'm able to see the options and how theyh would really look").** A choice goes to him as the text or the contents each option makes, not as a description of it, with your pick and how sure you are of it. On a contents picks page his own-words box shows the sidebar's cut-off for that heading's level and says as he types whether his words fit (`tools/humanization/contents_picks_page.py`; lesson toc-2026-11-23a; Joel, 2026-11-23: "some visible maker for "your own words" where it would show me how long it can be"). A name offered without the lines under it can't be judged, and he may take it for another group's name: asked to name the group that had been "The inner child, the guards, and the inner adult’s three jobs", he answered with the next group's old name, "Working With Your Inner Child".

What came up when GPT proposed the r03 structure (2026-10-20) and Claude reviewed it (`articles/inner-child-therapy/experiments/t77-answers/GPT-STRUCTURE-REVIEW.md`; applied in turn 78):

1. **Don't undo his own moves.** Compare his latest version with the one before it. A section he moved, and the back-references he wrote for it ("Remember those guards from The Urge to Escape, above?"), are decisions. GPT's M04 sent his altered-states run to the back, undoing the move he had made the day before.
2. **Order carries meaning.** A paragraph that says "don't wait until you're sorted out to reach out" has to come before the get-ready material. Moved to the back, the order says the opposite (his "mess and all" paragraph).
3. **A section stays with the image it extends.** Ask God for a Loan stays in Borrow Love, because the loan is that chapter's picture.
4. **Keep a paragraph with the one it carries on.** When Love Still Feels Missing's third paragraph opens "Taking the hostility out", which picks up the second's "least cruel response". GPT's map split them.
5. **Review every block that changes, not just the move titles.** A move list can hide changes inside it: GPT's M04 also moved two paragraphs within its chapter, and its special-case chapters put the suicide section's help paragraphs first.
