# Supervised Writing Architecture — three-iteration owner checkpoint

Status: **ACTIVE owner-directed workflow, 2026-08-31.**

Purpose: prevent a semantic preservation checklist from becoming the hidden prose outline during difficult Joel-byline humanization/reconstruction. This is an orchestration layer over the existing preservation, architecture, cold-audit, coherence, source-integrity, and thought-shape protocols. It is an **adaptation/composition** of those controls, not a claim that a new writing theory has been invented.

Use this workflow when a writer repeatedly understands the diagnosed AI-shape problem but still regenerates the same proposition → qualification → explanation/closure architecture under different wording.

## Core separation of responsibilities

The same representation must not simultaneously serve as semantic custody, composition outline, and self-certification checklist.

Use four distinct functions:

1. **Semantic custodian / supervisor** — sees the full authoritative source, owner instructions, preservation units, exact strings, and attribution constraints. It does not author the prose realization.
2. **Fresh writer** — writes from a deliberately order-neutral writer packet. It must not receive prior rejected prose, Pangram-localized prose, unrelated Human donor prose, or the original numbered semantic checklist as a rhetorical sequence.
3. **Architecture critic** — reconstructs the candidate's hidden outline, identifies model-shaped thought movement, and requires structural correction before preservation is allowed to influence the realization.
4. **Preservation auditor** — maps the candidate back to the authoritative semantic/provenance units only after the architecture pass. It may reject missing or altered meaning, but may not repair omissions by appending a tidy checklist paragraph.

Where infrastructure allows, the writer for each iteration should be a genuinely fresh child/context. The supervisor persists across iterations. If a fresh writer context cannot actually be created, record `INDEPENDENCE_DEGRADED`; do not pretend that an in-context restart is genuinely cold.

## 1. Authority and source firewall

Before the loop, the supervisor must read current GitHub authority and the minimum task-relevant protocols required by `SKILL.md` and `CANONICAL-REPO-MAP.md`.

For a bounded fresh-writing task, the supervisor freezes:

- the exact owner instruction;
- the exact semantic/function units that must survive;
- certainty, attribution, actor/action/object, chronology, causality, and scope;
- exact strings/links/headings that must survive;
- prohibited inventions and prohibited source donors;
- the changed natural boundary and its neighboring heading dependency.

The writer must not be shown unrelated Joel articles, Pangram-Human snippets, previous AI candidates, detector-highlighted wording, or external prose as style donors unless the owner explicitly authorizes a source for the exact target function.

## 2. Convert the authority into an order-neutral writer packet

The supervisor must transform the source checklist before a writer sees it.

The writer packet contains **semantic nodes and relations, not an article outline**. Use plain, non-publication notes. Do not preserve the original bullet numbering or source order when that order is not itself semantic.

Represent, as applicable:

- states/conditions;
- causal or noncausal relations;
- distinctions that must survive;
- thresholds/readiness conditions;
- conditional actions/options;
- prohibitions on false inference;
- cross-domain dependency;
- exact strings/links as detached protected objects.

The packet must not specify:

- paragraph count;
- paragraph jobs;
- transition wording;
- where each semantic unit should appear;
- a suggested thesis sentence;
- a proposed beginning/middle/end;
- a sequence that merely mirrors the original checklist.

Before dispatch, apply this test:

> If a competent writer could simply walk down the writer packet and turn each item into a sentence or paragraph in the same order, the packet is still too outline-like. Transform it again.

## 3. Iteration contract — exactly three before owner return

Run **exactly three substantive writing iterations**, then stop and return progress to Joel. Do not silently continue to iteration 4.

Each iteration contains all of the following stages.

### A. Fresh realization

Give the iteration's writer only:

- the order-neutral writer packet;
- the section heading/boundary;
- attribution and invention constraints;
- the small hard-prohibition list needed to prevent known failure modes;
- from iteration 2 onward, an **abstract architecture-defect ledger** from prior iterations, never the prior candidate text or sentence-level proposed fixes.

The writer must reconstruct the thought rather than edit a previous candidate.

### B. Mandatory writer self-critique

Immediately after drafting, the same iteration writer must critique its own completed candidate before it can be considered an iteration result.

The self-critique must reverse-outline the actual prose and answer:

1. What is the hidden outline of this candidate?
2. Can its paragraphs be reverse-mapped easily to the semantic packet or original checklist?
3. Does it repeat setup → qualification → closure or proposition → explanation → synthesis?
4. Did conditional possibilities become a neat menu/taxonomy?
5. Did a distinction get introduced and then immediately explained/summarized?
6. Is there a polished cross-domain bridge announcing a relationship the reader could simply encounter?
7. Does any paragraph end by explaining what it just demonstrated?
8. Are paragraph durations suspiciously equalized?
9. Did preservation anxiety create defensive caveats, significance staging, balancing, or explicit sequencing disclaimers?
10. Does the candidate sound like an essay architecture made more conversational rather than a different movement of thought?

The writer must name the strongest credible architecture defects. `No defects` is not acceptable without a reverse outline demonstrating why.

### C. Architecture repair — reconstruct, do not cosmetically edit

If the self-critique finds a real architecture defect, the iteration is not finished.

The writer creates an **architecture repair specification** naming the governing movement that should change. Examples:

- remove nested mini-essay closures and let one thought remain live across paragraphs;
- stop converting conditional possibilities into a menu;
- move a semantic unit into a parenthetical or dependent clause rather than giving it its own conceptual card;
- allow one fact to imply the next without explanatory aftercare;
- remove an abstract bridge and let the next domain arrive directly;
- change an outline-shaped sequence into causal, temporal, conditional, experiential, or threshold movement when that is the actual relation.

Then **discard the realization and reconstruct it from the writer packet plus the architecture repair specification**. Do not perform synonym replacement or line-by-line conversationalization of the failed candidate.

The corrected reconstruction becomes the iteration candidate.

### D. Supervisor architecture gate

The supervisor now reads only the corrected candidate as prose and independently reverse-outlines it.

Automatic rejection before preservation if any of these remain materially true:

- the candidate can be mapped back to the semantic checklist in roughly its original order;
- each paragraph corresponds to one semantic card;
- repeated setup → qualification → tidy conclusion;
- explicit organized menu of conditional responses followed by a disclaimer that they are not steps;
- balanced contrasts used as architecture rather than as the actual thought;
- significance staging (`this matters because...`, equivalent scaffolding) before a point that can simply be made;
- polished cross-domain synthesis followed by explanation of the synthesis;
- paragraph endings that recap or diagnose what the paragraph already showed;
- false symmetry/equalized thought duration;
- old essay architecture with contractions, first person, shorter sentences, casual vocabulary, fragments, or surface irregularity applied on top.

A rejected candidate must be structurally reconstructed within the same iteration. It is not sufficient to rename the sentences.

### E. Preservation audit comes after architecture

Only after the architecture gate passes does the preservation auditor map the exact candidate back to the frozen authority.

Require:

- every unsuperseded semantic/function unit preserved;
- no certainty change;
- no attribution/provenance change;
- no actor/action/object change;
- no causality/chronology change;
- no invented autobiography, examples, evidence, mechanisms, terminology, or authorities;
- all required exact strings/links/headings preserved;
- zero unexplained substantive deltas.

If preservation fails, **do not append missing concepts as a cleanup paragraph or list**. Return the missing unit to the order-neutral graph and reconstruct the affected movement. Then rerun the architecture gate. Preservation repairs are not allowed to reintroduce checklist architecture.

### F. Freeze the iteration result

For each iteration save:

- exact candidate text/hash;
- writer-context identity if available;
- self-critique reverse outline;
- architecture defects found;
- architecture repair specification;
- supervisor architecture disposition;
- preservation disposition and unexplained-delta count;
- remaining weakness.

Candidates are experimental/non-authoritative until owner acceptance. Do not overwrite the registered master.

## 4. Cross-iteration learning without prose anchoring

Iteration 2 and iteration 3 must not receive the prior candidate text.

Carry forward only an **abstract defect ledger**, for example:

- `iteration 1 made readiness into a three-item checklist`;
- `iteration 1 announced the inner-child relation with a polished cross-domain bridge`;
- `iteration 2 still closed every paragraph with a verdict`.

Do not carry forward sentence replacements, favored phrases, detector-highlighted spans, or a previous realization to be polished.

Each new writer must find a new realization from the semantic graph while avoiding the already-observed structural traps.

## 5. Detector boundary during the three iterations

Do not use Pangram as the iterative writer or architecture critic.

Unless Joel explicitly requests detector calls during the three-iteration loop, do not submit the intermediate candidates. Editorial architecture and preservation come first. The owner checkpoint exists so Joel can evaluate whether the thought movement is actually improving before scarce detector work or further rewriting.

## 6. Required owner checkpoint after iteration 3

After exactly three iterations, **stop substantive rewriting and return to Joel**.

Show:

1. the strongest current candidate in full;
2. a compact three-iteration progress table containing:
   - iteration;
   - hidden-outline defect(s) discovered;
   - structural repair performed;
   - architecture result;
   - preservation result / unexplained delta count;
3. the strongest remaining architecture weakness, if any;
4. whether the final candidate is still easily reverse-mappable to the source checklist;
5. whether fresh-context independence was real or degraded;
6. detector status (`not run` unless explicitly authorized/run).

Do not launch iteration 4 until Joel has seen this checkpoint and supplied the next instruction.

## 7. Hard prohibitions

During this workflow:

- no unrelated Human donor prose;
- no transplanting syntax from Cancer, Romance, Community, transcripts, or other articles merely because it scores Human;
- no invented autobiography or synthetic specificity;
- no random roughness, typos, slang, fragments, rhetorical questions, or punctuation tricks as humanization devices;
- no synonym-level repair of a rejected architecture;
- no preservation-by-appendix inside the section (adding a final paragraph that mechanically catches missed bullets);
- no claim that multiple starts in one saturated context are genuinely independent;
- no promotion to article authority before owner acceptance and normal repository reconciliation.

## 8. Task-specific automatic rejection test

For any task supplied as a semantic checklist, run this mechanical pre-delivery question:

> Can I draw a mostly one-to-one arrow from the source units, in their supplied order, to consecutive sentences or paragraphs in the candidate?

If yes, reject the candidate unless that sequence is independently required by chronology, causality, instruction order, or another semantic relation. Mere completeness is not authority for preserving the checklist's rhetorical topology.
