- **romance-2026-11-28a. In a contents run, a link heading with text under it files that text under the link; the text
  gets a heading of its own on the picks page.** My own lesson, from this run's readers, not from his words (his were
  "fix the ToC"). The romance post opens with two headings that are links, "Interactive guide-based convo here." (no
  text) and "Try my Couples card game app based on the guide! (good for singles also!)", and the second sits over the
  opening: his dad's answer, why he wrote the guide, who it's for. In the sidebar the opening is "the card game", so no
  reader of his contents or of Fable's looking for whether the advice applies to them, or how sure he is of it, found it
  (0 of 10 tries); with the opening given its own heading ("Who This Guide Is For") and the card-game link kept as a
  heading above it, every reader of the three proposals did (12 of 12). Fable's notes saw it, but its names kept the
  heading over the opening. So in a contents run, read what sits under each link heading, and where text does, the
  proposal gives that text a heading of its own and the readers test it. It goes on his picks page like every other
  heading (headings are his to pick); the page's question about it is only where the link goes (a heading of its own,
  as now, or a plain line), with the outline shown, since that is his promotion's place in the sidebar. One post so
  far; how often his posts open this way is not known.
