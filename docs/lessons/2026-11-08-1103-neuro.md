- **neuro-2026-11-08d. A science term is read in its current use, checked in recent papers before I reason from it.**
  His words on SC-37: "you seem to use weird/old definitions, GPT says neurotoxicity doesn't mean killing neurons, that's
  an old def." He is right. The papers on glutamate injury use "excitotoxic" for sublethal injury too: dendrites swell
  in beads, spines go, branches pull back, and the cell lives and recovers ([Faddis et al. 1997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6573163),
  "brief sublethal NMDA exposure"; [Hasbani et al. 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6762381)). Through
  three passes of block 37 I had used the cell-death sense (the "stroke-level" excitotoxicity of my notes and of the
  dropped Sattler citation), and the card argued from it.
- **neuro-2026-11-08e. A card that cuts part of a claim says what backs the part that stays, with its sources; a claim
  I think is unsupported is never kept without saying so.** SC-37 cut "swell" from "can swell and injure", and its why
  called the swelling studies "nothing like stress levels". He read that as me doubting the injury itself: "if you were
  right, we'd need to remove that claim, but you're not ... i'm curious why you would have left the research there that
  you thought was wrong." I didn't doubt it, and the card never said why it holds: stress shrinks dendrites and removes
  spines through NMDA receptors ([Martin and Wellman 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3697127): an NMDA
  blocker during stress changed the retraction; [Wellman et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9483990)),
  a stress hormone at stress levels destroys spines through NMDA receptors and calcium-activated calpain
  ([Andres et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3807024)), and one footshock shortened prefrontal
  dendrites for up to two weeks ([Bonifacino et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9938874)). The reason
  "swell" goes is narrower than the card said: the beads form from sodium, chloride and water and form as well without
  calcium (Hasbani et al. 1998, [quoted by Ikegaya et al. 2001](https://cob.silverchair.com/jcs/article-pdf/114/22/4083/1466384/4083.pdf);
  calcium makes the damage last), and no stress study reports swelling. So: the card's why names the source for what
  stays as well as for what goes, and if I think a claim is wrong the card proposes cutting it.
- **neuro-2026-11-08f. Once the science of every section is settled, the suggestions and the protocol are read as one
  whole against it, before the rest is humanized.** His question: "after we settle the science in the various sections

  NAC "vacuum", leucine), doses or timings that disagree between sections, an order or combination the corrected science
  argues against, a warning that sits away from its tool. Order for a guide with a protocol: the science, section by

  humanizing. Paragraphs already humanized are reopened only where the coherence read changes them. Dosing and timing
  lines for illegal drugs stay his: the read can point at a disagreement but doesn't write those lines.
