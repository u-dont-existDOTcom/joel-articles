- **bad-trips-2026-11-20b. A risk says what carries it, as the study that compared the forms found it, not as a summary
  puts it.** Turn 49 named niacin's risk as liver damage and kept "Higher or repeated doses" as what carries it, and my
  answer to him said high doses and "certain formulations" of niacin have been linked to liver injury, from LiverTox's
  summary. His answer: "the niacin risk is for extended release yeah, haven't seen that with IR niacin unless you take it
  thru the day to make it ER. don't make it seem like niacin in general or "certain formulations" causes liver injury.
  that's not true." The randomized trial that compared the forms at the same doses agrees with him (McKenney et al. 1994,
  JAMA: 12 of 23 on sustained-release, none on immediate-release), and slow release is the mechanism, which is why regular
  niacin spread through the day acts the same way. The line now reads "Sustained-release niacin, or repeated doses through
  the day, can create a real risk of liver damage ..." with the trial linked (Human High by itself, b6d71dcc). When a fix
  of mine names a risk, it names the form, the dose or the way of taking it that carries the risk, from the study that
  compared them, and says so in the card or the turn's report.
