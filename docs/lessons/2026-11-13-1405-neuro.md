# What his 1967 DMSO paper taught (the neuro de-armoring lane, 2026-11-13, sent 14:05 UTC)

He sent, without a word, Ramírez & Luza's "Dimethyl sulfoxide in the treatment of mental patients" (Annals of the New
York Academy of Sciences, 1967), the day after my DMSO card said no trial had tested DMSO for anxiety. Its check:
[DMSO-PTSD.md](../../articles/neuro-de-armoring/humanize/fix-20261108/dmso/DMSO-PTSD.md), "His 1967 paper".

- **neuro-2026-11-13a. A search for human evidence uses the condition's older and plain names as well as today's, and
  looks at the decade the compound was studied most, before any card says something was never tested in people.** My
  DMSO search asked for "psychiatric", "anxiety" and "depression" in the title with DMSO, and read the newest and most
  relevant hits. A 1967 paper titled "in the treatment of mental patients", from the year DMSO had a whole New York
  Academy of Sciences volume to itself, gave it to nine people with severe anxiety or obsessive-compulsive disorder among
  42 inpatients. So: search the condition's words as each era used them ("mental patients", "neuroses", "anxiety states",
  "nervous"), search the compound's own heyday (for DMSO, the 1960s and the 1967 and 1975 Annals volumes), follow the
  references of the reviews that cover it, and say "I found no ..." with what was searched, never "no trial has ..."
  (lessons neuro-2026-11-11b and 11c).
