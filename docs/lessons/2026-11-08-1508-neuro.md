- **neuro-2026-11-08g. A card states no benefit its sources don't show, and a guideline's recommendation is reported as
  what it recommends, never as evidence that the thing works.** His words on SC-47: "you can't say "they help some
  people" since you showed zero evidence that they do (helping as much as placebo is not helping, and helping less than
  harming is not helping). the fact that the VA recommends them is not science". My card had ended its corrected claim
  (Prozac no better than placebo in combat veterans, mixed trials since) with "the VA still recommends sertraline and
  paroxetine (VA/DoD, 2023), so they help some people, just not everyone": a conclusion none of its sources showed,
  drawn from a recommendation, and a balancing clause that softened his argument (AGENTS.md, code review rules: "Never
  silently soften, balance, or change the owner's argument"). A benefit is claimed only from a result that shows it
  against placebo, with the harms beside it; his version keeps the recommendation as a fact about the VA ("yet the VA
  still recommends ...").
- **neuro-2026-11-08h. Where the article's argument answers its own question, a science card corrects the support and
  keeps the answer; "nobody knows" goes in only where the evidence says the question is open, and then the card says
  so.** His words on SC-48: "your version sounded absurd, we don't know? yes we know, it's obvious. if we don't know
  anything i wouldn't publish this article." His post answered "Why don't these drugs work on calcified PTSD?" (they
  treat mood, not what trauma changed); my card cut the answer to "Nobody knows for sure", which takes away the reason
  the protocol exists. The answer is the larger argument, his (gate E348); a card may correct its wording (here, the
  "software/hardware" figure) but not drop it. His version says it plainly: even if they work for mood, PTSD is not a mood
  disorder, "a bit like putting some Bengay on a broken leg".
- **neuro-2026-11-08i. Where a science card cuts a claim down to what the studies show, it says what that leaves the
  reader to act on, and asks for what he knows from practice there.** His comment on SC-50: "i'm sure if i wrote this
  article myself it would have been a lot better. i'm strugglin to resurrect it from gemini's version to something more
  useful lol". My card cut "System Reformats" to two animal findings and "hasn't been tested"; his version put the tools
  back in their place (with therapy and the supplements), added what they have been shown to help (addiction, which
  DeltaFosB is part of), and a paragraph of what he knows from use: how iboga trees and mushroom strains differ, which
  he finds more useful, the parasite and toxin view he holds as his own, and his guides. The article's useful content is
  his experience, not Gemini's text, so a card that leaves the reader less to act on asks him for it instead of leaving
  a gap.
