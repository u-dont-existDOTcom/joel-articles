- **neuro-2026-11-11b. Research for a science card goes wide before the card is written, on both sides: trials, cohorts,
  case series and case reports, and firsthand reports marked as such. The top hits of one query are not research. And a
  blood level is not an intake: "a low intake rarely lowers blood potassium" never shows that a low intake does no
  harm.** His words: "your research wasn't good, again", with GPT's research, and "your original point was that
  potassium deficiency would be problematic for brain function based on NHANES, but you said that would only happen if
  someone had diarrhea or malnutrition. well, that's exactly the point. about 40% of US adults were below the 2.3g/d rda
  of k on any given day according to NHANES." My searches for SC-106b to d took the top hits of one PubMed query each and
  read abstracts. They missed a review of 16 children whose steroid-induced mood and behavior problems eased on potassium
  in 13 (Pariury 2019), a cohort of 4,213 older adults (Na 2022), and the studies on the other side (Cherbuin 2014,
  Zingales 2025, the 1991 TAIM trial). A card said eating more helps the nerves "only when something like diarrhea or a
  water pill has drained it", taking the NIH fact sheet's sentence about blood levels as if it settled what a low intake
  does, which no study I had read tested. Meanwhile only about 3 in 10 Americans eat the recommended amount (the 2025
  Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee's report). So before a science card: search several ways (the question's own
  terms, the condition's, "case series", "case report", "randomized", the population the article's reader belongs to),
  follow the best hits' citations, check what is common among the article's readers (here, low intakes, and the people
  who lose more), and give both sides with their size. A thin evidence base is said as thin; it is never written as a
  negative. His figure, 40% below 2.3 g a day, wasn't found anywhere; the national report's figure (about 7 in 10 below
  the recommended amount) is the one the card uses. Reddit is blocked to this lane's web tools, so threads he links are
  asked for as pasted text, not read around.
