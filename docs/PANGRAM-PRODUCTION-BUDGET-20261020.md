# Production Pangram budget, prediction and owner-escalation rule

**ACTIVE — direct Joel correction, 2026-10-20; applies to every Joel-byline article and every production humanization worker, including ChatGPT, Claude, Emulate-assisted, GUI, API and Codex execution.**

This is the normative `joel-articles` production testing policy. The Pangram lab inherits it for Joel article production and keeps the detector transport/cache/accounting implementation. It supersedes the 2026-09-26 "no limit on checks per turn" permission and tighter production ceilings take precedence over older six-call *section* safety caps and aggregate-call accounting exemptions. It does **not** impose production caps on independently authorized, budgeted detector-method research experiments. Explicit fresh owner authorization is required for extra production submissions.

## Why this correction exists

Joel reported on 2026-10-20 that roughly **$30 in Pangram credit disappeared in one day**, and a worker reportedly tested variations on **one paragraph approximately 40 times**. These are owner reports, not independently audited provider billing or submission counts. The goal is to improve prediction and repair reasoning, not make the detector the writing engine.

The article-specific Community prediction scorecard (`articles/intentional-communities/candidate/prediction-review/`) remains the observed score source, not a certificate of universal prediction skill. At the inspected 2026-10-20 checkpoint, the latest recorded ten-batch window (420–429) had **63 correct predictions / 74 measured calls (~85%)**, while the historical aggregate was **1,391 / 1,898 (~73%)**. Keep reporting the denominator/window and recent misses; do not promote a recent window to an unconditional accuracy claim. The working objective is to predict correctly most of the time, with ~85% as the recent reference, and to *notice early* when a specific passage defeats that ability.

## Production scope and submission ceilings

1. **One small active work unit.** Draft/review at most **one natural section or approximately five consecutive paragraphs** at a time. For a long section, work in batches of up to five paragraphs, but do **not** detector-test its entire section during each batch. This rule includes workers running automatic batches and prompts to Emulate or other helpers.
2. **Paragraph first.** Preserve the already-accepted Human text. Record owner-supplied valid Pangram outcomes without resubmission. For changed/untested prose, freeze and test each detector-eligible paragraph *individually* after the editorial preflight. A Human *section* never hides a red individual paragraph. Paragraphs below Pangram's supported minimum are `UNMEASURABLE_ALONE`; do not pad with unrelated Human prose to manufacture an individual pass. Ask Joel about a genuinely unresolved short-paragraph exception.
3. **At most two new paid/credit-consuming submissions for one paragraph in a production repair pass**, counting its first considered test and at most **one meaningfully revised candidate**. No sentence/heading/punctuation/whitespace roulette; the same paragraph family retains its identity across wording variants, sections, batches, model sessions, GUI/API routes, branches and chats. Record an ambiguity that *may* have submitted as a spent slot until History/recovery proves otherwise. Cache/History readback is not a new submission. Stop even earlier if there is no credible, faithful new hypothesis.
4. **Prediction-error brake.** Before *every* new call, predict `Human` or `AI` and explain the actual words/structure that support the prediction. Do not pay to confirm an obviously AI-shaped candidate. If a prediction is wrong, record the miss and diagnose *why*, using Joel's Human tells as seriously as AI tells. **Two prediction misses on the same paragraph require immediate owner escalation**, even if another budget bucket remains. The stricter two-submission paragraph ceiling normally reaches the stop first. A new chat, candidate name, transport or revised punctuation does not reset either counter.
5. **Section certification is downstream, not an editing loop.** Only after the section's detector-eligible paragraphs have individually passed Human (or have explicit recorded Joel exceptions) and semantic/fidelity/architecture gates pass may a worker check the complete natural section once, with its real headings and reader-visible surface. Allow **at most one more section-level check** after a justified, substantive and localized repair; otherwise stop and ask Joel. Prefer reviewing a *specific* two-paragraph interaction offline before the second paid section submission; diagnostic pair measurements are exceptional, not a route to unlimited calls. Do not check the same whole section repeatedly after each paragraph, even if its text changed.
6. **Whole-article certification is final.** Do not run whole-article or large multi-section Pangram checks during ordinary paragraph/section iteration. After all sections and individual paragraphs are editorially accepted and locally measured, make **one final exact reader-visible whole-article** certification call if the task requires it. If it fails, localize the affected boundary using the existing result, repair the local issue, and allow **at most one additional whole-article certification call** for the newly assembled final draft. Further article-wide tests need explicit fresh Joel authorization. An edit invalidates the old *status*, but does not automatically justify a new costly test before the article is again at final readiness.
7. **No batch loopholes.** The two-submission limit counts submissions for the same *paragraph/repair target*, not number of texts in a single API request. A whole section/full article is not recategorized as a paragraph, and large article checks do not become exempt merely because historical section caps did not apply to aggregates. New strategy, new task ID, "scientific test", and GUI/API substitution are not budget resets. If genuine detector research is separately authorized, preserve a distinct frozen experiment design, call budget and evidence plane. Never promote its synthetic probes as article prose.
8. **Transport and spend.** Follow the owner's current GUI preference and cost-routing policy; GUI is not assumed free or immune from measured-count limits. Before submission, check cache, History, pending task IDs, call ledger and exact text identity. Record the precise tested scope, word count, detector route/model/version, prior prediction, reason this call can change a decision, budget slots remaining, and cost estimate only when supported by actual rate data. Do not infer remaining credits from a provider error. Never run a paid detector request just to validate this policy.

These ceilings bound *new production Pangram submissions*. Non-billable editorial reviews, source-preservation and coherence checks still occur as needed; they are not license for endless autonomous paraphrase rounds. If a paragraph remains genuinely hard after two attempts, requesting the missing owner insight is the next productive action, not an algorithmic failure to be hidden.

## Mandatory escalation when blocked

Stop before another Pangram submission; present the **literal paragraph with enough adjacent context**, not a path or paragraph number alone. Include the frozen Human/AI predictions and reasons, first/second measured classifications, exact boundary, any apparent AI tells, **the positive Human tells**, what was learned from the misses, and the substantive claims/functions a rewrite cannot sacrifice.

When no credible AI tell remains, use Joel's requested form, without falsely claiming a pass:

> I can't find AI tells here, and here are the human tells I see, but it doesn't pass. Can you explain?

Then show what the worker sees in that exact prose and ask for Joel's explanation or first-person realization. Await his input on that span; continue independent work elsewhere without spending on the blocked span. If Joel judges a paragraph good enough despite Pangram, record an explicit **owner exception** rather than claiming Human certification.

## Prediction scorecard and reporting

- Before any Pangram call, append an immutable prediction with the exact text/hash, *natural scope*, Human/AI classification, cited tell evidence, intended next decision and try number. Do not fabricate retrospective guesses or call known AI prose a prediction success.
- After a measured call, append the actual result/model/version, billable-status/route, accuracy bit, mistake type and owner teaching. Rebuild the existing article's scorecard and missed-predictions ledger after each check-bearing session; verify the dashboard actually points at its latest data when the dashboard is used. Other articles follow the same schema or explicitly link their own equivalent.
- Report **cumulative and most recent rolling-window accuracy with sample sizes** and comparison to an always-AI baseline; keep 85% a measured rolling reference/goal rather than a boast. Do not pool short-snippet, paragraph and aggregate accuracy without showing the boundary mix. Distinguish Pangram failure from prediction failure and faithfully repaired text from detector camouflage.
- Include the number and scope of *new* Pangram submissions and the per-paragraph/section/article budget remaining in worker receipts. At the stop point, show the concrete owner question without automatically queuing more paid checks.

## Authority and historical coexistence

Preservation, actual article sense, voice, owner locks, semantic traceability, architecture, source integrity, and independently Human paragraphs all remain blocking requirements. A Pangram pass never excuses semantic loss or irrelevant human text. The historical `state/OWNER-CORRECTION-SECTION-ONLY-CALL-CAP-2026-08-21.md` six-call cap in the lab is a broader upper safety bound, not permission to use six iterations routinely on a Joel paragraph or section after this 2026-10-20 correction. The historical 2026-09-19 additional-GUI-call exception for novel discriminating hypotheses is not an automatic override of this newer production owner limit. Request Joel's consent before crossing it.
