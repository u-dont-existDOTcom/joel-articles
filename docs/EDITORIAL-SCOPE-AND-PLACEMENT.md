# Editorial scope, function, and placement

Use this with the humanization architecture gate for Joel article work.

## Protected function does not determine placement

A paragraph may carry an essential rhetorical function—reader agency, invitation, de-escalation, qualification, humor, or emotional pacing—without belonging at the beginning of a section.

Determine placement from the local writing problem: heading promise, paragraph job, reader's live question, chronology, causality, and what the reader actually needs next. Do not move a paragraph earlier merely because an abstract persuasion theory says reassurance should come first.

A useful function and a correct position are separate judgments. Preserve both.

## Owner-approved synthetic architecture remains authoritative thought

Treat interviews, notes, AI-generated drafts, and earlier synthetic prose as source pools rather than as prose authority. But when Joel explicitly says an AI-generated passage is basically what he wants to say, that owner correction promotes its substantive thought architecture and scope into the protected source pool even though its wording remains synthetic.

Recover the intended claims, questions, scope promises, examples, and rhetorical functions; replace model-shaped realization where needed. Do not silently delete a promised question or function because its current wording looks like roadmap prose, generic warmth, therapy language, or another AI pattern.

Owner endorsement of the thought does not make the original syntax owner-authored. Keep provenance separate: semantic/architectural authority and natural authorship are different properties.

## Editorial scope discipline

During a requested editing or humanization pass, do not turn the task into unsolicited fact-checking or claim review. Joel's owner-authored claims remain authoritative for the writing task unless he asks for research/verification or there is a concrete material reason under the article protocol to raise a problem (for example, an internal contradiction, a direct source conflict, unclear severe-claim agency, or another issue that would materially affect the article).

Do not interrupt ordinary prose review merely because a claim is empirical or contestable. Strong claims are allowed to be strong.
