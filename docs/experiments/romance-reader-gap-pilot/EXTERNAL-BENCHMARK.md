# Romance reader-gap pilot — external benchmark

Status: **EXPERIMENT / DIAGNOSTIC ONLY.** This benchmark evaluates whether first-pass reader-gap candidates overlap established relationship-assessment/relationship-education domains and actual reader demand. It does not establish article defects and does not authorize prose changes.

Frozen article under test: canonical Romance r23r2, SHA-256 `f1c2b9a3f0f3d9e123c3870ca5d741af8ed99bbf6f138e68b845de04b1a12a2c`.

## Why benchmark externally

The reader-gap method can manufacture plausible-sounding `what about X?` questions indefinitely. A useful gap candidate should survive at least one independent pressure test:

1. the article itself creates or promises the question;
2. a mature relationship-assessment/education framework treats the domain as consequential;
3. actual readers repeatedly ask the question;
4. research identifies the life transition/domain as consequential.

External prevalence is evidence of reader demand, not a completeness mandate. Joel may still intentionally omit, link out, leave implicit, or reject a question.

## Established baselines scanned

### Gottman Relationship Checkup / conflict work

Official sources describe a broad relationship assessment covering friendship/intimacy, stress and conflict, values/goals, trust/commitment, and areas of concern. Its psychometric domain list includes repair attempts, gridlock on perpetual issues, housework and childcare, financial issues, children, shared roles/goals, and other relationship domains.

Official pages:

- https://www.gottman.com/couples/checkup/
- https://www.gottman.com/professionals/checkup/psychometrics/
- https://www.gottman.com/blog/manage-conflict-the-aftermath-of-a-fight/

### PREPARE/ENRICH

Official assessment materials treat relationship stage, dynamics, commitment, forgiveness and parenting as explicit domains. Its parenting material treats the transition into parenthood as a change in the couple relationship and explicitly distinguishes being partners from becoming only parents.

Official pages:

- https://www.prepare-enrich.com/the-assessment/
- https://www.prepare-enrich.com/the-assessment/parenting/

### Transition-to-parenthood research

Research syntheses consistently treat the transition to parenthood as a major relationship transition. Relevant reviewed domains include relationship satisfaction, communication, intimacy/sexuality, role changes, parenting stress and strain/strengthening of the couple relationship.

Representative sources:

- Mitnick, Heyman & Slep (2009), meta-analysis, DOI 10.1037/a0017004
- Delicate et al. (2018), systematic review/meta-synthesis, DOI 10.1016/j.midw.2018.02.022
- Bogdan, Turliuc & Candel (2022), meta-analysis, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2022.901362
- Lessard et al. (2025), attachment/conflict/intimacy in transition to parenthood, DOI 10.1037/fam0001321

## Actual-reader signal sampled

This was a small falsification-oriented sample, not a prevalence study.

- A 2026 Reddit thread asked how couples actually repair after conflict rather than merely apologizing; replies emphasized accountability, revisiting the injury, changed behavior, and consistent follow-through.
- A 2026 thread described intimacy disappearing after children while the relationship became dominated by logistics, schedules, food, school and daily operations.
- Large marriage threads asking what couples should discuss before marriage repeatedly surfaced children, finances, chores/division of labor, family expectations, sex, communication, jobs, values and goals.

These are useful demand signals, not factual authority.

## Candidate benchmark results

### Tier 1 — independently reinforced

#### G003 — ordinary-conflict repair

**First-pass diagnosis:** partial coverage; the article contains repair ingredients but no compact ordinary-conflict repair model.

**External benchmark:** strong reinforcement. Gottman treats repair attempts and post-conflict processing as explicit, central constructs rather than incidental communication details. Actual readers also ask the literal question `what does repair look like?`.

**Decision:** upgrade from `medium-high / medium confidence` to **high editorial-review priority**. Do not invent a new repair protocol; if the article needs a clearer answer, adapt established repair concepts and Joel's existing material.

#### G006 — keeping the romance alive after children arrive

**First-pass diagnosis:** thin coverage. Children organize the article from the opening onward, but the dedicated Children section largely covers obligations, breakup and co-parenting.

**External benchmark:** very strong reinforcement. Transition-to-parenthood research treats communication, intimacy, relationship satisfaction, roles and conflict as distinct couple-level issues. PREPARE/ENRICH explicitly frames the problem as maintaining the partner relationship while parenting. Actual readers describe the relationship becoming almost entirely logistics after children.

**Decision:** **highest-confidence substantive gap candidate in the current pilot.** This still does not mean the correct repair is a large new section; a short bridge, explicit scope signal, interlink or focused addition could be better.

#### G007 — money, labor, dependency and household responsibility

**First-pass diagnosis:** thin coverage; medium importance.

**External benchmark:** strong reinforcement. Gottman explicitly assesses housework/childcare and financial issues. Marriage-reader threads repeatedly put finances and division of labor among the first things couples wish they had discussed concretely before marriage/children.

**Decision:** upgrade to **high review priority**, especially because the article explicitly asks readers to discuss building a life, children, money and living arrangements. The key question is whether Romance should cover these itself or route them to a more practical companion resource.

#### G001 — how to know whether a relationship is getting healthier over time

**First-pass diagnosis:** partial coverage; high confidence.

**External benchmark:** reinforced at the domain level. Mature assessments are explicitly organized around strengths/challenges across multiple relationship dimensions rather than treating intensity or persistence as success. The Gottman Checkup covers connection, conflict, values/goals, trust/commitment and areas of concern.

**Decision:** keep **high review priority**, but explicitly reject building an unvalidated bespoke numeric `relationship health score`. The reusable established idea is a multidomain check-in; any Joel-specific synthesis should be labeled as such.

### Tier 2 — real conceptual pressure, but likely answerable through composition

#### G002 — repairable trigger/growth edge vs incompatibility vs danger

Gottman distinguishes conflict-management/repair from gridlocked or perpetual issues, while the article itself already provides a strong safety boundary and scattered compatibility tests.

**Decision:** retain as a high-value reader question, but treat it as a **composition problem**, not a request for a universal decision rule. The likely editorial opportunity is to connect already-present tests more explicitly.

#### G004 — how much mismatch can be worked through

Substantially overlaps G002 and established work on perpetual/gridlocked problems, values/goals, roles and compatibility.

**Decision:** likely **merge analytically with G002** unless a fresh reader produces a materially distinct question. Do not create duplicate prose merely because the register contains two phrasings.

#### G005 — durable romantic love vs attachment/idealization/trauma activation/wanting to be wanted

This is unusually central to Joel's article and receives substantial distributed treatment. External assessment frameworks cover intimacy, trust, attachment-related conflict, idealization-adjacent constructs and commitment, but they do not supply one simple discriminant matching Joel's exact question.

**Decision:** retain as **article-specific high-value synthesis question**, but do not force it into a checklist if the distributed argument is intentionally the answer. The Canvas is useful here because it can show that many sections partially answer one question.

### Tier 3 — useful, lower incremental value

#### G008 — healthy boundary vs control/punishment

The distinction matters, but the article already treats consent, coercion, agreements, safety and demands in several places. The current external scan did not establish as strong an independent gap signal as G003/G006/G007.

**Decision:** retain as a lower-priority diagnostic candidate unless a fresh reader or actual-reader signal promotes it.

## What this benchmark changes about the architecture

The experiment should not become a generic question generator. Each candidate should carry an **independent-support status**:

- `article-only`
- `framework-supported`
- `actual-reader-supported`
- `research-supported`
- combinations of the above

This is not a quality score. It is provenance for why the question remains live.

Likewise, use a candidate disposition before any edit:

- `answer-here`
- `connect-existing-material`
- `interlink`
- `scope-signal`
- `leave-implicit`
- `bank`
- `reject`

## Strongest current editorial opportunities

On present evidence, the experiment has produced four candidates worth a real owner/editor look:

1. **G006:** romance/partnership after children actually arrive;
2. **G003:** what successful repair after an ordinary fight actually consists of;
3. **G007:** concrete money/labor/household/dependency alignment before lives merge;
4. **G001/G002 cluster:** how to tell whether the relationship is getting healthier and whether recurring pain is workable rather than merely intense or incompatible.

The next useful falsification is a **fresh-context blind-prefix pass** that does not see this register or benchmark. Compare only after the new reader has independently generated and coverage-checked its questions. If it repeatedly surfaces these same domains, the case for keeping the reader-gap layer strengthens substantially.
