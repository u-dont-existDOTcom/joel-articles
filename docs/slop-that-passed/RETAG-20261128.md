## Patterns across them

- **The tag was put on whole edits that were mixed.** Where he added a fact and also fixed wording (places 4, 5, 9, 11, 15, 17, 22), the fact got the credit and the wording fix went unrecorded ("no new way is drawn from it", place 15).
- **Empty abstract words standing where a thing should be named** are the most common hidden fault: "meaningful", "prepared correctly", "quality mattered enormously", "the power", "capacity", "intervention", "the romantic dyad". Fresh readers often asked about them, and the question was sent to him as a request for facts.
- **A paragraph that describes a situation and never says the point or what to do**, then ends on a closer ("at exactly the point when both people most need a view from outside it"; "It doesn't have to mean the same thing every time."). When his version supplied the point, it was logged as his experience, though it was a general claim (places 2, 6, 16, 26).
- **Caveats and warnings are where it shows most:** a hedge the evidence doesn't back ("they help some people"), the conditions before the consequence (Y-88), a permission for an instruction ("it's fine to"), a risk with no reason given.
- **"Sounds human" was mistaken for his:** casual add-ons of the editor's ("or you just stop caring"), and a model's word kept because it was thought to be his (places 3, 10).
- **Only one of the four cases is confirmed as having read Human High before his edit.** The other three matched a check of his own version, so how many of his sense edits were to text that had already passed is not yet known; the matching has to compare the whole before first.
