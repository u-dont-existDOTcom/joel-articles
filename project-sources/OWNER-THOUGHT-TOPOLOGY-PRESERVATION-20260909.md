# Owner Thought Topology Preservation — Joel-byline Production Rule

Status: ACTIVE GENERAL EDITORIAL RULE / OWNER-DIRECTED / 2026-09-09

This rule is additive to `project-sources/RHYTHM-AND-THOUGHT-SHAPE.md`, the preservation gate, source-integrity gate, and production humanization preflight.

## Trigger

In the Somatic massage/aquatic-bodywork pass, the model took one naturally connected owner thought and reorganized it into separate modality, benefits, protocol, evidence, advice, and safety units. Joel correctly judged the result AI-shaped.

The durable lesson is broader than that article.

## Rule 1 — preserve owner thought topology before editorial categorization

When Joel supplies a connected thought in interview/chat/source form, first recover the actual sequence of cognition:

- what observation or concern came first;
- what caused the next thought to arise;
- where the emphasis changed;
- which complication was minor versus central;
- where one modality/person/source entered because of the preceding thought;
- where the thought genuinely stopped.

Do not automatically reorganize that movement into editorial categories such as:

`benefits -> mechanism -> evidence -> safety -> recommendation`

or

`modality A -> modality B -> evidence -> caveat -> takeaway`.

A polished article architecture can be less faithful and more model-shaped than the source cognition even when every proposition survives.

## Rule 2 — source weighting is part of meaning

If Joel says one issue and then explicitly or implicitly marks another as more important, preserve that asymmetry unless a stronger article-level reason requires a different order.

Do not equalize minor and major complications into neighboring paragraphs of similar rhetorical weight merely because both are protected units.

## Rule 3 — a heading must not create semantic pressure

Adding a modality or idea to an article does not automatically authorize a full subsection.

If the source contains only one live relation involving that modality, let it appear inside that relation. Do not create a heading and then invent enough generic explanation, evidence, personal experience, or safety guidance to make the heading feel complete.

A fuller subsection is earned only when there is enough independent owner/source thought to sustain it.

## Rule 4 — never convert another person's observation into Joel autobiography

Do not turn practitioner, source, or third-party experience into first-person Joel experience for warmth, continuity, or humanization.

Examples of prohibited transformation:

- source: `Hale uses massage after aquatic work to ground people`
- invalid realization: `After I open up emotionally, I want massage to bring my body down.`

First-person experience must have direct owner provenance.

## Rule 5 — do not manufacture epistemic debates the owner did not raise

Accurate attribution may already be sufficient qualification.

If Joel reports that people `feel like they are back in the womb`, attributing that to a practitioner/receiver experience does not require an added rebuttal about prenatal-memory science unless the surrounding prose would otherwise materially imply literal recovered prenatal memory.

Do not add `X, but not Y` skeptical aftercare merely because a prudent explainer could imagine an objection.

## Rule 6 — evidence annotates live thought; source type does not automatically earn a paragraph

Research, practitioner experience, personal experience, and safety guidance are provenance classes, not paragraph jobs.

Attach evidence where it supports the live claim, or route it to citations/further reading. Do not reset the prose into `now the research says...` solely because research exists.

Likewise, do not append a safety paragraph merely to consume the safety ledger if the owner thought places that complication earlier or gives it less weight.

## Rule 7 — distinguish substantive sequence from procedural packaging

An owner-supplied sequence can be real and must be preserved. The mistake is not that steps exist; it is rendering each step as one clean algorithmic slot disconnected from why the previous step led to the next.

When a sequence is causal/experiential, preserve the causal relation between moves rather than serializing it as a compact program.

## Rule 8 — backstage editorial rationale never belongs in reader prose

Sentences about what `the article needs`, what is `enough to mention`, what `we should cite`, or why a detail was omitted are editorial-process material unless the research process itself is the reader-facing subject.

## Production check

Before delivering a substantial reconstruction from owner notes, answer:

1. Can I draw the owner's original thought movement without using the preservation inventory?
2. Does the candidate preserve that movement and its unequal weighting?
3. Did I create any paragraph/heading mainly because a source category or modality needed a slot?
4. Did I invent first-person experience, casualness, or skeptical aftercare?
5. Did evidence enter because the claim needed support, or because the outline expected an evidence paragraph?
6. Did I preserve a real sequence without turning it into a neat program?
7. Does the passage stop where the owner thought stops?

If not, repair the governing topology before editing sentences.
