# 10. Early-Buddhist evidence concerning direct experience, function, and cessation

## Nibbana is a meditative state and attainment

The strongest canonical statement is [AN 9.47](https://suttacentral.net/an9.47/en/sujato), the Sandiṭṭhikanibbāna Sutta. Asked in what way nibbāna is directly visible, the answer is the meditative progression itself: each jhāna and formless attainment is directly visible nibbāna in a qualified sense (*pariyāyena*), and the cessation of perception and feeling with the taints destroyed by wisdom is directly visible nibbāna in the definitive sense (*nippariyāyena*). The surrounding series, [AN 9.46-51](https://suttacentral.net/an9.46/en/sujato), runs the same structure through to nibbāna in this very life in [AN 9.51](https://suttacentral.net/an9.51/en/sujato). This makes nibbāna an attainable meditative event in the canon's own words, and it supplies a canonical gradient of qualified and definitive attainment that parallels my distinction between partial untying and full unweaving.

Arahants are also described entering nibbāna-percipient absorptions. In [AN 10.7](https://suttacentral.net/an10.7/en/sujato), Sāriputta describes a samādhi in which he was not percipient of earth, water, or any ordinary object, with only one perception—“the cessation of becoming is nibbāna”—one perception arising and another ceasing like the flames of a fire. The Buddha describes the same samādhi in [AN 10.6](https://suttacentral.net/an10.6/en/sujato): percipient of “this is peaceful, this is sublime... nibbāna” while percipient of nothing else, with parallels at [AN 11.7](https://suttacentral.net/an11.7/en/sujato) and [AN 3.32](https://suttacentral.net/an3.32/en/sujato). Bhikkhu Brahmāli reads these passages as perception of an idea about nibbāna—the commentarial fruition-attainment model—rather than direct perception of nibbāna ([*What the Nikāyas Say and Do Not Say about Nibbāna*](https://wiswo.org/books/niknib/)); that reading belongs with the Abhidhamma object-model in the research report.

The Buddha ties his own awakening to mastery of the full sequence of deep attainments. In [AN 9.41](https://suttacentral.net/an9.41/en/sujato) he tells Ānanda that he did not claim unexcelled awakening until he had attained and emerged from all nine progressive dwellings, including the cessation of perception and feeling, in forward and reverse order. In [DN 16](https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato), during his final illness, he says his body is only at ease when he enters and remains in the signless immersion of the heart—the aged Buddha routinely resting in a deep sign-free abiding.

Contact language reinforces the same picture. [AN 6.46](https://suttacentral.net/an6.46/en/thanissaro) speaks of monks who touch the deathless element with the body. [AN 9.43-45](https://suttacentral.net/an9.43/en/sujato) describe the body-witness touching the peaceful formless liberations, and define the one liberated both ways by cessation-contact together with the destruction of the taints. [AN 9.34](https://suttacentral.net/an9.34/en/sujato) has Sāriputta declare that nibbāna is pleasant precisely where nothing is felt, structured around the same progression of attainments. [Ud 8.1](https://suttacentral.net/ud8.1/en/anandajoti) names nibbāna *tadāyatanaṃ*—“that sphere”—using *āyatana*, the same category-word the canon uses for the meditative spheres.
