# Facts, Research, Health, Harm Reduction, Links, and Formatting

This file expands the canonical research rules. Facts outrank voice, and the style corpus is never evidence.

## Claim categories

- **Lived experience:** Joel's supplied memory, reaction, experiment, or interpretation. No external citation is required to report that it happened, but do not broaden it into a general effect.
- **Opinion or moral judgment:** state it as judgment; do not launder it through vague expert authority.
- **Externally checkable claim:** names, dates, laws, statistics, study findings, quotations, organizations, prices, species, chemicals, events, and trends require verification when accuracy matters.
- **Mechanism:** biological, psychological, social, or institutional explanation. Plausibility is not evidence that the claimed outcome occurs.
- **Association:** linked variables without established causality.
- **Causal evidence:** evidence that supports an effect under specified conditions.
- **Functional/integrative practice:** what clinicians or communities do, not proof that it works.
- **Anecdote/community signal:** a research lead or lived report, not a rate or universal result.
- **Speculation:** an explicit hypothesis or inference.

## Corpus firewall

## Canon facts and archive links

`CANON-FACTS.md` is authoritative for stable recurring identity, naming, relationship labels, coined-term definitions, and the one-line descriptions Joel has explicitly verified. It is not evidence for broader public claims. Current article facts and Joel's current correction override it. Only Joel may remove `[VERIFY]` from a new entry.

`ARTICLE-INDEX.md` is the URL authority for interlinking. Use exact URLs from it or `CANON-FACTS.md`; never guess a slug. A sitemap establishes that a URL was listed when supplied, not that every old claim on the page is current.

## Verification workflow

1. Extract the exact claim and its implied strength.
2. Find the strongest direct source appropriate to that claim: original research, full dataset, official record, statute, primary document, or named person's own statement.
3. For studies, inspect full text where possible: population, sample, design, comparator, dose/exposure, outcome, absolute effect, uncertainty, attrition, limitations, funding, and conflicts.
4. Follow citation chains; do not trust a secondary characterization without checking the cited source.
5. Check publication date, corrections, retractions, later replications, and whether guidance or product facts have changed.
6. Match wording to the source. Do not collapse biomarkers, symptoms, self-report, response rate, remission, survival, mortality, or another endpoint into one verdict.
7. Put a direct working link inside the article at the claim or immediately after the relevant sentence or paragraph.
8. If support is weak or unavailable, qualify precisely, mark `[SOURCE NEEDED: exact claim]` in a working draft, or remove the claim.
9. Record access level: full text, abstract-only, snippet-only, or secondhand. Do not write that Joel read, noticed, agreed with, or was surprised by a source unless he supplied that personal history.
10. When a newly supplied full source materially changes an abstract-based treatment, retire the old candidate and re-audit its article roles rather than patching a few sentences.

A title, abstract, press release, AI summary, search snippet, or fact-check page is not enough when the full study or primary evidence is necessary. A source can support one component while challenging another; do not force it into a single advocacy role or extrapolate beyond its actual thesis.

## Source hierarchy

Choose by claim type, not prestige alone:

1. primary documents, official records, datasets, and original research;
2. systematic reviews, evidence syntheses, consensus statements, and guidelines for broad claims;
3. high-quality secondary reporting for context;
4. informal sources only for labeled anecdotes, reactions, community signals, or leads.

Search local-language and local institutional sources when place or tradition matters.

## Geographic and population scope

State the narrowest claim the evidence supports. Distinguish a household, village, city, country, region, cultural subgroup, diaspora, continent, and global pattern. Do not broaden an observation merely because the wider noun is rhetorically convenient. When broadening is useful, provide evidence and say what is inference versus direct observation.

## Research-heavy article pattern

When supported by the material, Joel's strongest research articles often move through:

1. why the question matters personally or practically;
2. what the dominant explanation says;
3. what the strongest counterevidence or complication is;
4. what changed Joel's view during research;
5. evidence grouped by strength and mechanism;
6. practical implications, uncertainties, and what would change the conclusion.

Do not force a personal opening, ranking, or recommendation list when the material does not call for it.

## Health and environmental-health standards

Do not diagnose or prescribe. Preserve useful heterodox inquiry while applying skeptical, evidence-weighted safeguards.

Separate mainstream consensus, direct human evidence, functional/integrative practice, mechanism, anecdote, Joel's experience, and speculation. Prioritize acute triage, common and dangerous causes, contraindications, medication/supplement interactions, evidence quality, bias, conflicts of interest, testability, and clinician review.

Rank hypotheses or interventions by:

- base rate or relevance;
- danger if missed;
- human evidence and effect size;
- mechanistic plausibility;
- testability;
- cost and burden;
- reversibility;
- risk of harm;
- interaction complexity.

Avoid protocol stacking, detox/kill-phase assumptions, false precision, and firm causal claims from weak tests, isolated mechanisms, animal studies, or anecdotes. A disclaimer does not repair unsafe guidance.

For high-risk actionable claims, distinguish reporting Joel's field notes from recommending reader action. Put interaction warnings, stop criteria, and emergency escalation near the relevant action. When the format calls for a clinician-review timeline, use 24–72 hours, 1–2 weeks, and 4–12 weeks, plus what not to start, what to track, clinician questions, pivot point, confidence, actionability tier, and main unknowns.


## Population evidence, individual fit, and practical frameworks

The universal aggregate-to-case rule appears in `ARGUMENT-AND-EVIDENCE-ARCHITECTURE.md` and `STRUCTURAL-HUMANITY.md`. The rules below specify its health and therapy application.

Population evidence estimates average effects under specified conditions. It does not, by itself, determine the correct first modality, sequence, intensity, or stopping point for one person. In practical health and therapy articles:

- preserve the evidence hierarchy;
- distinguish evidence volume from individual selection;
- name heterogeneity, tolerability, sequencing, contraindications, and observable response;
- judge fit through functioning, symptoms, sleep, relationships, freedom, ability to stop, and ability to recover;
- audit both errors: proceeding before adequate capacity and using preparation as a permanent waiting room that never reaches the target.

When Joel presents an author-developed composite protocol, staged map, or modality sequence, state its epistemic status beside the framework. Distinguish validated components from the author-developed or model-assisted sequence joining them. Do not hide that distinction in a distant disclaimer, and do not generically weaken a framework beyond its actual evidence status.

Keep biomedical, psychological, traditional, energetic, spiritual, and practitioner explanations as separate explanatory categories unless evidence directly connects them. A traditional term may describe practice or experience without proving a biomedical mechanism.

Match evidence architecture to genre. A practical guide needs direct links at consequential claims and locally decisive comparative evidence; it does not automatically need a modality-by-modality literature-review appendix. Place a study beside the comparison or decision it changes rather than exiling it to a symmetrical evidence section.

When Joel supplies a safety concern, preserve the concern but publish only the strongest supported scope. Do not convert practitioner intuition, an isolated warning, or a plausible mechanism into a universal rule. Record the stronger proposed instruction as unresolved when support is missing.

Consent and scope modifiers are substantive. Preserve distinctions carried by phrases such as `without clear consent`, `forced`, `unsupported`, `high-intensity`, `for this population`, and `when tolerated`. Do not turn a warning about misuse into a blanket ban on the practice.

Do not express agency by claiming there is no correct form or technique. Distinguish multiple valid adaptations from anything-goes instruction. Translate `integration`, `containment`, and `regulation` into concrete actions, observable stop criteria, and ordinary-life outcomes when the reader needs to know what to do.

## Harm-reduction and controversial material

Identify the purpose: warning, crisis prevention, testimony, field notes, practical guidance, or analysis. Preserve that purpose.

Improve safety through precise scope, evidence labels, timing, contraindications, interactions, monitoring, and escalation criteria. Do not replace the useful core with generic institutional language. When a section cannot be safely or accurately rewritten, identify the problem and preserve the splice point rather than pretending it has been solved.

Keep the action recommendation separate from the causal explanation. “Stop driving during unexplained lapses” may be warranted while the absence of prior accidents remains unexplained. Do not turn the safety advice into proof that the outcome was luck, benign, pathological, or produced by a spiritual mechanism.

## Existing-article HTML source

Before substantially rewriting a published article, request its original raw editor HTML when links, headings, emphasis, lists, blockquotes, footnotes, captions, native objects, Share/Subscribe, comments, or paywalls must survive. For Substack, extract the raw editor body only; it is the sole authority for links, object identity/metadata, hierarchy, and media placement. TXT, PDF, screenshots, browser-rendered HTML, prior helpers, and reconstructed previews are analytical aids only. Preserve source markup in archival HTML, build a separate transfer-ready payload, inventory objects by semantic type and anchors, and validate source fidelity, transfer conversion, and destination reconstruction separately.

## Final HTML

Final article text must be delivered as a downloadable clean semantic HTML file. Preserve emphasis, direct source links, human headings, quotations, real lists, punctuation, and intentional emojis. Do not add CSS, decorative wrappers, empty headings, or document-level tags. Working drafts, critiques, and claim ledgers may use Markdown.

## Argument-level research architecture

For research-heavy factual arguments, use `ARGUMENT-AND-EVIDENCE-ARCHITECTURE.md`.

- Identify the central conclusion and every load-bearing premise before drafting.
- Rank evidence as decisive, strongly corroborating, contextual, pattern, texture, or distracting.
- Establish chronology before causality, and keep public availability distinct from demonstrated receipt, reading, understanding, and belief.
- Test the strongest adverse, benign, and mixed interpretations using the same standard.
- Stop research when the decisive dispute is stable, strongest counterevidence is examined, important access failures are documented, and more searching is unlikely to change the conclusion materially.
- Complete the audit before compressing to the minimum decisive facts for an opening, summary, conclusion, or short response.
- Keep practice instruction, phenomenological rationale, analogy, mechanism, human evidence, and proof of the larger claim separate.
- For converging evidence arguments, record whether streams are independent and whether experience preceded the source reading.

## Deterministic provenance and dependency bookkeeping

When the universal argument protocol is threshold-triggered, use `argument_ledger.py` to hash the active source packet and assign stable source-segment, evidence, claim, and destination IDs. Source indexing establishes provenance, not truth. The writer/model must still determine what the evidence proves, whether sources are independent, and how strong the conclusion may be.

Do not infer that a source was read or understood merely because it is indexed. Do not convert an impact report into an article change without the saved permission matrix.
