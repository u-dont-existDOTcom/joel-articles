# Progressive Disclosure for Technical Terms and Linked References

The reader receives the smallest explanation needed **at the point where it becomes necessary**. A term is not defined merely because it is unfamiliar. A link can carry optional background; the article supplies only what the current sentence or argument requires.

## The four levels

### 1. Linked term alone

Use this when the reference is peripheral and the sentence remains understandable without knowing the method in detail.

> We use [Hearthwork](https://ibogaqueen.substack.com/p/hearthwork-an-integrated-peer-counseling) when conflict starts accumulating.

The reader only needs to know that Hearthwork is a relevant practice. The linked guide provides the rest.

Avoid:

> We use Hearthwork, Joel's peer-distributed conflict-processing framework based on equal time, nondirective attention, warmth, discipline, and confidentiality...

That explanation interrupts the current article when the section is not teaching Hearthwork.

### 2. Small orientation gloss

Use one short phrase when the next sentence depends on the reader knowing what kind of thing the term is.

> We use *Hearthwork*, a peer-listening method, before resentment hardens into politics.

The gloss identifies the category. It does not reproduce the manual.

Another example:

> A faint *nāda*-like inner sound sometimes appeared as the sense of a head dissolved.

Here “inner sound” is necessary because otherwise many readers will not know what experience *nāda* refers to.

### 3. Concise functional definition

Use this when the concept performs an active role in the section's reasoning or instructions.

> Hearthwork gives each person equal speaking time while the other listens without interpretation, advice, or fixing. Then they switch.

The reader now understands enough to follow the argument or act. Additional principles can remain in the linked manual.

### 4. Full treatment

Use a full definition, mechanism, sequence, caveat set, or history only when the section is actually teaching that subject.

A section titled **How Hearthwork Works** may explain equal time, listening discipline, confidentiality, facilitator limits, failure modes, and examples. A passing interlink in a community article should not.

## Decision test

Before expanding a term, ask in order:

1. Can the reader understand the present sentence without the explanation?
2. Does the next claim depend on the term's meaning?
3. Does a link already provide optional background?
4. What is the smallest gloss that prevents confusion?
5. Is the proposed mechanism, recipe, biography, or history new substantive content requiring Joel's approval?

## More examples

### Coined term central to the article

First use:

> I call this *pl/ork*: play plus work, because the inner child needs both.

Later uses need no repeated definition.

### Coined term mentioned only in passing

> The full *pl/ork* framework is explained [here](https://community.u-dont-exist.com).

Do not insert the complete four-domain framework unless the current section depends on it.

### Technical acronym

When needed immediately:

> Heart-rate variability (HRV) is one rough measure of variation between heartbeats.

When peripheral and linked:

> The device also reports [HRV](source-link).

The expansion may wait if nothing in the current passage depends on interpreting it.

### Linked medicine or protocol

Peripheral reference:

> I explain loveyhuasca [in the full guide](https://soma.u-dont-exist.com).

Do not append its ingredients, preparation chemistry, or dosing merely because the canon file contains them.

A section actually comparing preparations may need the relevant technical distinction locally, with approval and sourcing.

### Person or book

Peripheral:

> I discuss this more fully in [the book-length study](link).

Locally necessary orientation:

> In his book-length study of early Buddhist contact, [Author](link) argues...

A miniature biography is unnecessary unless the author's identity affects the evidence.

## Timing rule

Do not postpone a definition until after the reader has already needed it. Progressive disclosure means **just in time**, not “eventually somewhere later.” Introduce a central thesis or term before using “this,” “that,” “the same,” or “what I mean by...” to refer back to it.

## Common failures

- **Glossary stuffing:** defining every unfamiliar word at first appearance.
- **Link echo:** linking a guide and then summarizing the entire guide beside the link.
- **Canon-fact dumping:** importing a complete verified definition when a two-word gloss would do.
- **Prestige explanation:** adding jargon that makes the sentence harder rather than clearer.
- **Delayed necessity:** using a central term several times before the reader can understand it.
- **Repeated definition:** explaining the same term again without a new rhetorical function.

## Governing rule

Give the reader enough information to continue accurately, then stop. Expand further only when the article's present job requires it.
