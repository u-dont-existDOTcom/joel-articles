# Visual Editorial Protocol — Images, Charts, Diagrams, Embeds, and Placement

Images are editorial units, not decoration. For every substantial new article or major rewrite, inspect where a visual would clarify, prove, pace, humanize, or make the page easier to scan.

## Joel's current visual grammar

No single community PDF currently controls Joel's visual grammar. The deleted community PDF must not be treated as an approved humanization or mixed-register reference.

For visual work, use the current article's authoritative raw editor HTML and any representative owner-approved HTML/PDF files Joel supplies for that task. Infer visual habits only from clearly labeled owner-approved artifacts. An assistant-produced draft, anti-sample, screenshot set, or PDF retained for factual recovery may show object placement, but it does not establish preferred voice, pacing, caption style, or visual density.

When no approved visual reference is available, use the functional principles below rather than imitating a prior article:

- personal photographs and artifacts for personal or historical claims;
- primary-source screenshots, charts, and embeds for evidence;
- diagrams where a process or framework genuinely needs visual explanation;
- memes or visual jokes only when they arise from the present material;
- generated conceptual images only for clearly illustrative, non-documentary purposes;
- visual relief where dense prose needs pacing, without imposing a quota.

Any page-specific visual map is version-specific and belongs in the active article's visual-opportunity ledger, not in the universal protocol.

Useful visual types:

- documentary/historical image;
- screenshot or social embed;
- chart from cited data;
- explanatory diagram or decision tree;
- process illustration;
- map;
- meme or visual joke already connected to the material;
- generated conceptual image that is clearly illustrative rather than documentary.

## Audience fit

Choose visuals for the article's audience contract, not for the source's original audience or the model's desire to display sophistication. A public explainer may need a simple annotated diagram where a specialist source uses a dense chart; a research article may require the full data display; a personal essay may need an artifact or photograph rather than a generic infographic. When a section deliberately changes audience, adjust the visual explanation and caption accordingly.

## Selection rules

- Prefer a writer's own photo, primary-source image, screenshot, chart, or artifact when the passage makes a factual or personal claim.
- Use generated imagery for concepts, metaphors, or mood—not as fake evidence of a person, place, event, or treatment.
- Avoid generic stock photos that merely repeat the heading.
- Let captions add factual context, voice, or a joke; do not explain what the reader can see.
- Preserve image rights, source links, credit, alt text, dimensions, and aspect ratio.
- In evidence-heavy articles, a chart or diagram is usually more useful than decorative art.
- For health or safety material, visuals must not imply certainty or demonstrate an unproven outcome.

## Onboarding for visual fidelity

Ask for one or two representative article HTML files or PDFs when visual style matters. Extract:

- image density and spacing;
- preferred image types;
- caption voice;
- full-width versus inline placement;
- recurring aspect ratios;
- use of memes, diagrams, screenshots, and embeds;
- whether images lead sections, interrupt dense runs, or close sections;
- alt-text and credit practice.

TXT is not enough to infer visual grammar.

## Delivery

For a new article, advise Joel where images would be most useful and which should be found, supplied, charted, or generated. Do not generate or fetch them unless requested and the tool is available.

For an existing article, preserve media blocks exactly under `INTERLINKING-AND-HTML-SOURCE.md` and use `scripts/html_islands.py` before editing fragile Substack HTML.

## Platform-specific visual propagation

When one article produces Substack, self-hosted, and Ghost versions, treat image/diagram/embed changes as artifact-family dependencies. Confirm the visual survives in every destination or record why it is intentionally omitted.

For Ghost cards, validate diagrams, cards, captions, and responsive navigation inside the uniquely scoped root. Full-width visuals may need an explicit viewport breakout. For self-hosted pages, rehost assets and remove prior-host URLs. Do not treat a source screenshot as proof that either destination renders correctly.
