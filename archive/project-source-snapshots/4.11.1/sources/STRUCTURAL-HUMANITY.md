# Structural Humanity — Genre-Calibrated Rules for Avoiding AI-Shaped Articles

These rules are additive. `MASTER-INSTRUCTIONS.md`, Joel's current request, and article-specific corrections still have higher authority.

## Governing principle

Preserve the irreducible shape of Joel's actual material instead of forcing it into a model's preferred shape. Do not confuse legitimate explicitness with AI-shaped overdetermination. A polemical essay may need a visible thesis; a guide may need explicit sequencing; a research article may need repeated qualifications. The warning sign is **density**: every anecdote proves the thesis, every section restates it, every paragraph lands on a polished takeaway, every heading behaves like a slogan, and every complication is absorbed by the framework.

The goal is not rougher prose. Let the world remain slightly larger than the system explaining it.

## 1. Calibrate by genre before diagnosing AI shape

Identify the form before applying this audit:

- **Polemic or manifesto:** visible thesis, repeated stakes, and sharp contrasts may be necessary.
- **Practical guide:** explicit sequence, headings, lists, and framework language are functional.
- **Research article:** qualifications, definitions, repeated distinctions, and navigational structure may be necessary.
- **Personal essay:** can carry more implication, associative movement, chronology shifts, and unresolved meaning.
- **Hybrid:** judge each section by its actual job rather than one global norm.

Ask whether explicitness exceeds the needs of the genre, not whether explicitness exists.

## 2. Audit architecture before “humanizing” sentences

Before changing diction, inspect:

- thematic unity and thesis-return frequency;
- causal tidiness;
- framework completeness;
- section symmetry and mirrored sentence forms;
- paragraph-ending force;
- heading style;
- psychological or moral closure.

Surface edits cannot repair an over-clean outline. Humanize structure, not grammar: do not add errors, random fragments, fake vulnerability, slang, or quirks merely to appear human.

## 3. Draft from the full human material

Use the fullest available notes, existing prose, links, quotations, memories, contradictions, failed attempts, jokes, uncertainties, images, and neighboring ideas. Do not compress them into a thesis and regenerate a smoother article from the summary.

Privately identify:

- the actual question or point;
- the strongest lived material and evidence;
- inconvenient facts and mixed outcomes;
- missing details that would otherwise require invention;
- claims needing verification and direct links;
- what the piece genuinely asks the reader to see, reconsider, or do.

Ask one to three narrow questions only when missing personal material would otherwise force fabrication or generic filler. When Joel wants immediate output, write around the gap or use `[JOEL: add the specific detail here]`.

## 4. Control epigram density

Preserve the strongest aphorisms, jokes, reversals, and quotable endings. Flatten or remove the lesser ones so ordinary facts, complications, unanswered observations, or simple transitions sometimes end paragraphs.

A strong line is not the problem. A sequence of strong-line candidates is. Watch for consecutive paragraphs or sections ending in antithesis, slogan, elegant moral, or quote-card phrasing. Do not make every paragraph perform a perfect landing.

## 5. Let some evidence remain undigested

After a vivid anecdote, concrete object, quotation, image, or absurd event has made the point, do not automatically add an interpretive sentence. Let at least a few experiences exist before the argument recruits them.

Delete thesis echoes, moral aftercare, and explanations of jokes the reader already understood. A useful test is whether 5–15% of interpretation could be removed without weakening the argument.

## 6. Make frameworks visibly permeable

When presenting a model, state where categories overlap, leak, or fail. Show how one domain can become another: money becoming psychology, jealousy becoming governance, medicine creating status, or child-rearing exposing values the founders never discussed.

Use boxes as diagnostic aids, not reality's natural compartments. Preserve enough stability for the framework to guide action, but do not let it explain everything perfectly.

## 7. Name genuine unresolved problems

Do not manufacture uncertainty to look nuanced. Identify the important danger the model has not solved, why the proposed safeguard may be insufficient, and where power or harm can still accumulate.

One real unresolved problem adds more credibility than five generic caveats. Do not force the article to close every loop merely because the model can produce an answer-shaped sentence.

## 8. Vary rhetorical architecture

Keep a binary contrast when it is genuinely the article's diagnosis. Reduce repeated “X does this; Y does that” patterns elsewhere. Use causal prose, three-way possibilities, concrete scenes, plain statements, questions, lists where useful, and uneven paragraph lengths.

The canonical movement—point/problem → observation → concrete example → evidence/reasoning → practical takeaway—remains useful for some expository passages. It is not a whole-article template. Avoid automatic hook → anecdote → thesis → numbered points → objection → uplift.

## 9. Mix imaginative and navigational headings

Preserve the strangest and most memorable headings when they carry the article's real mission or humor. Let some middle headings be plain and functional so the reader can navigate. If nearly every heading sounds engineered for a quote card, flatten some of them.

## 10. Preserve specificity, self-implication, and inconvenient material

Keep named people, places, institutions, dates, objects, direct emotional labels, chronology shifts, embarrassing memories, controversial values, concrete absurdities, mixed outcomes, and useful digressions when true and relevant. Do not sand them down for broad appeal.

Use actual cultural and factual referents rather than vague literacy. But do not install odd details as “human markers.” The detail must come from the material and do a real job.

## 11. Keep the real causal field

Do not make insight, mindset, acceptance, forgiveness, courage, healing, or one decisive choice the hidden cause or solution to every problem. Track institutions, incentives, power, history, material conditions, relationships, physiology, chance, and other people's agency.

## 12. Use embodied emotion only when true

Do not mechanically convert feeling into caught breath, tightening chests, cold fingers, metallic tastes, or weather mirroring mood. Use bodily or sensory detail only when supplied and relevant. Directly naming grief, shame, anger, love, relief, or fear is often stronger.

## 13. Do not force psychological or moral closure

Do not automatically end with healing, redemption, forgiveness, connection, consensus, uplift, or a stated lesson. End where the material genuinely arrives: a demand, warning, boundary, unresolved cost, sharper question, practical task, final scene, or plain stop. Preserve the rule against prophetic certainty.

## 14. Treat the reader as an intelligent participant

Leave room for inference, disagreement, recognition, and knowledge the reader brings. Do not prescribe the reader's emotional reaction or complete every inference.

## 15. Use samples as diagnosis, not architecture

The corpus reveals decisions, registers, and failure patterns. It is not a phrase bank or a whole-article template. The current article's own material outranks resemblance to the cancer article, research guide, tender transcript, or community article.

Do not mechanically add flashbacks, named references, direct reader address, ambiguity, digressions, or catchphrases. Human shape comes from situated judgment.

## 16. Reconstruct when inherited rhetoric is the problem

When a draft is off-voice or AI-shaped, do not merely remove its loudest slogans. Discard the inherited opening, metaphors, paragraph endings, section sequence, and conclusion where necessary. Rebuild from Joel's actual facts, memories, judgments, humor, uncertainty, links, and images.

Rebuild rhetoric, never biography. Preserve the fact-and-meaning ledger, exact claims, certainty, entity assignment, and first-person provenance.

## 17. Corrections are dependency signals

When Joel corrects a fact, referent, chronology, actor, coined term, or emotional claim, search the whole draft for downstream dependencies. Repair comparisons, pronouns, jokes, captions, transitions, and conclusions. Reordering changes meaning; do not compensate with vague explanatory glue.

## 18. Lock exact compact language and semantic agency

Treat supplied lyrics, quotations, remembered phrases, catchphrases, jokes, and compact formulations as locked unless Joel authorizes change. For specialized methods, record actor → action → object before paraphrasing so compression cannot reverse the idea.

## 19. Give each section one governing movement

Before rewriting, state the section's real pressure or question, strongest concrete material, causal or intellectual route, complication, register, paragraph progression, and honest ending move. A section may legitimately establish experience, explain a mechanism, provide evidence, complicate the model, state a value, offer guidance, and move toward action at once. Do not force one narrow function merely to simplify the outline. Require one intelligible governing movement and a real paragraph chain; reject independent modules, symmetrical source paragraphs, and answer-order traceability.

## 20. Build specificity and motif ledgers

Before generating humor, inventory actual people, objects, money flows, rules, procedures, phrases, places, contradictions, and strange physical details. Draw humor from that inventory. If a joke could migrate unchanged into an essay about startups, churches, or marriage, it is probably portable cleverness.

Track recurring images, metaphors, joke forms, questions, and paragraph-ending devices. A second use must develop or intentionally recall the first.

## 21. Compress scaffolding, not humanity

Cut list announcements, transition previews, thesis echoes, moral aftercare, generic scene-setting, redundant conclusions, and bridges that compensate for bad order. Preserve odd specificity, mixed outcomes, central controversies, exact phrases, source links, images, and functional digressions.

## 22. Enforce first-person provenance sentence by sentence

Classify every first-person statement as supplied memory, supplied judgment, supported inference, or externally checkable fact. Anything else requires a question, placeholder, qualification, or removal. Ban invented epiphanies such as “I only realized this while writing.”

## 23. Preserve central controversial values

Before suggesting removal, ask whether the position determines compatibility, membership, child-rearing, medicine, sexuality, spiritual practice, or another real decision. If so, make its role explicit rather than sanitizing it for reach.

## 24. Protect the true ending and appendix

When the article reaches its real ending, label any subsequent resource list as an appendix. Do not add a second moral conclusion afterward.

## 25. Track completed passes and switch to surgical editing

Maintain a pass ledger for source/fact, links, voice, HTML/media, visuals, and destination transfer. Do not repeat completed audits unless later changes invalidate them. Once the article is strong, broad rewriting creates more risk than value; make targeted changes and show them separately.

## 26. Preserve praise while adding moral complication

Do not create nuance by cancelling genuine praise. When Joel describes a culture, place, person, or institution as forgiving, generous, spiritually alive, funny, or communal, retain that truth and add the limitation at the correct level.

Prefer mixed truth such as: “The otherwise forgiving social culture has other shadows, too.” This preserves the forgiveness while naming additional harms. Avoid a tidy reversal such as “the same forgiving culture has its shadow” when it implies one symbolic dark side, makes the praise feel like setup, or falsely suggests the virtue caused the harm.

## 27. Audit transition words after new material

After inserting or moving a paragraph, re-read the sentences immediately before and after it. Audit `otherwise`, `also`, `still`, `other`, `same`, `too`, `yet`, and `but`. These words encode sequence, contrast, continuation, exception, and judgment. Replace them when the new material changes what has already been introduced or what the sentence is adding.

## 28. Repeat an issue only when its function changes

The same subject may belong in more than one section. Keep it when each appearance has a distinct job—for example, a lived social contradiction in a place-based section and a practical inclusion test in a values/membership section. State the function privately. If both appearances make the same claim for the same purpose, consolidate them.

## 29. Keep geographic scope disciplined

Begin with the actual country, city, community, institution, or population described. Do not turn a Sénégal observation into “Africa,” one commune into intentional communities generally, or one family into a cultural law unless evidence and argumentative purpose justify the expansion.

## 30. Do not idealize belonging

Communal intimacy can provide care, identity, humor, continuity, shared housing, and mutual aid while enforcing conformity through family pressure, law, ostracism, economic dependence, secrecy, or violence. Ask who receives belonging, who is conditionally tolerated, and what someone must hide to keep their place. Preserve the admirable part rather than using the harm to erase it.

This applies to inherited sharing systems as well: a practice can remain morally meaningful while becoming coercive or damaging under modern cash, migration, or status pressures.

## 31. Treat controversial values as membership architecture

Vaccination, sexuality, gender, child-rearing, medicine, religion, relationship agreements, and similar questions determine whether people can raise children, share risk, trust one another, and remain open in daily life. They are not detachable culture-war asides when they affect compatibility or safety. Preserve them and make their design function explicit.

## 32. Let Joel's correction control the judgment

A correction is evidence about the analysis, not merely preferred wording. Infer what distinction Joel is protecting—mixed truth, non-causal coexistence, scope, sequence, praise, or degree—record it, and inspect analogous passages. Do not translate complexity into a more elegant reversal after Joel has rejected that judgment.


## 33. Let argument shape follow evidence shape

Human writing can admit unresolved motives, mixed evidence, and friction between facts. Narrative momentum must not force a causal story the record does not establish. A single thesis should not absorb every fact, and colorful details should not be installed merely because they make the chosen story vivid.

Ask: **Does the architecture reveal what the evidence actually supports, or has the evidence been arranged to imitate a story selected in advance?**

When the record is mixed, preserve a mixed conclusion. When a load-bearing premise fails, rebuild the architecture rather than adding caveats to the old story. See `ARGUMENT-AND-EVIDENCE-ARCHITECTURE.md`.

## 34. Do not let the evidence graph flatten the article

A dependency ledger protects factual architecture; it is not the article's narrative template. Do not make every paragraph correspond to one claim node, arrange prose in database order, or remove scenes and texture merely because they are not decisive evidence.

Use the graph to prevent failed premises, orphaned reasoning, and false compression. Then write from the full human material. Preserve lived sequence, humor, moral tension, useful digression, and evidence that deserves to remain partly undigested.

## 35. Preserve attribution at the right level

Do not attach a person's name to a common developmental or therapeutic idea merely because their notes introduced it during revision. Attribute distinctive terms, original frameworks, quotations, evidence, historical claims, and source-specific interpretations. When the idea is common, integrate it directly. When terminology genuinely comes from a source and conflicts with Joel's vocabulary, name the source term once, explain the adopted term, and then use the adopted term consistently.

## 36. Treat media placement as part of the article's reasoning

A byte-identical image, preview, video, comment, Share object, Subscribe object, or paywall can still become editorially wrong when moved away from its setup, joke, evidence, or section function. Track each object's anchors and rhetorical job. After moving one, audit the surrounding heading hierarchy, table-of-contents nesting, caption, transitions, and destination artifacts. The transfer payload must be rebuilt from the exact final archival HTML.

## 37. Relocate useful material before deleting it

A worthwhile passage may be wrong only because it arrives before the reader has the map, definition, or reason to care. Test movement before deletion or broad rewriting. Place it at the first point where its function becomes necessary, then repair the former and new transitions, antecedents, headings, and media anchors.

## 38. The newer wording is not presumed superior

During comparison, judge original and revision by meaning, clarity, precision, audience fit, and rhetorical function. Domain jargon, compressed prose, or smoother cadence can weaken a passage. Restore the original when it performs the job better.

## 39. Redundancy audits extend beyond annotated passages

Owner comments reveal problems but do not define the full audit boundary. After resolving them, inspect the whole article for repeated frameworks, caveats, summaries, evidence hierarchies, recommendation lists, and conclusions. Consolidate by function and retain unique material.

## 40. Keep decisive evidence beside the decision it changes

A study or source that alters a local comparison belongs beside that comparison when practical. Do not move it into a generic appendix merely to create formal symmetry. Match citation architecture to the article's genre and the reader's decision path.

## 41. Lock the audience contract

Record who the article is for, what that reader knows, how the writer relates to them, what the piece helps them understand or do, the stakes, the intended register, and the publication venue. Preserve that contract across headings, examples, citations, captions, practical steps, and conclusions.

Audience drift is not limited to health writing. A public essay can become an academic paper, a guide can become a literature review, a founder essay can become an investor pitch, a legal explainer can become a lawyer's memo, or a personal account can become an institutional case study. A hybrid may address different audiences in different sections, but the shift must be deliberate, signaled, and useful.

## 42. Prefer audience-fit precision over prestige language

Do not replace clear source wording with clinical, legal, academic, corporate, technical, activist, or managerial jargon merely because the revision sounds more professional. Retain a technical term when it adds precision, is defined when needed, and belongs to the audience contract. Translate a source written for specialists instead of importing its relationship to the reader.

In practical prose, abstract nouns must eventually become actors and actions. Name who implements, aligns, integrates, regulates, supports, governs, includes, builds capacity, or remains accountable; what they do; when; how the reader recognizes progress or failure; and when to stop, revise, or escalate.

## 43. Distinguish general evidence from case-specific application

Evidence about a population, historical class, market, institution type, jurisdiction, or average outcome does not automatically determine what is true or advisable for one person, community, organization, place, or case. State the transfer conditions: relevant differences, baseline, context, constraints, heterogeneity, and what observation would show the general finding does or does not apply here.

This does not downgrade strong evidence. It prevents an average from pretending to be an individualized decision rule.

## 44. Keep explanatory systems and levels distinct

An article may use empirical evidence, mechanism, law, economics, psychology, history, ethics, spiritual tradition, practitioner language, and lived experience. Do not blend them into one mutually validating explanation. State which system is speaking, what it can establish, and where the bridge is evidence, analogy, interpretation, or simply coexistence.

## 45. Audit staged models in both directions

A prerequisite, readiness gate, research phase, capacity-building period, onboarding sequence, or safety step may prevent premature action. It may also become an indefinite waiting room that avoids the actual target. In practical sequences, audit both errors: proceeding before the necessary conditions exist and never proceeding because preparation has become self-perpetuating. Name the transition criteria.

## 46. State the epistemic status of synthesized frameworks locally

When the article presents a sequence, taxonomy, scorecard, model, hierarchy, checklist, decision tree, or combined protocol, state near the framework whether it is established, adapted from a source, author-developed, model-assisted, heuristic, provisional, or speculative. Validated components do not automatically validate the complete synthesis joining them.

Do not weaken every original framework with generic disclaimers. Use the most precise status the evidence supports and place it where the reader encounters the model.

## 47. Keep warnings and accusations no broader than their support

A legitimate concern does not authorize the strongest imaginable wording. Preserve the concern while calibrating the published claim to the evidence, jurisdiction, population, conditions, timing, and severity actually supported. Record a stronger owner intuition or proposed rule as unresolved rather than converting it into a universal statement.

Condition and scope modifiers carry meaning. Preserve terms such as `may`, `when`, `without consent`, `unsupported`, `high-intensity`, `for this population`, `in this jurisdiction`, `under these conditions`, and `when tolerated` when they distinguish the practice, claim, or risk from a blanket judgment.

## 48. Flexibility does not erase standards

Do not express agency, pluralism, or adaptability by claiming there is no correct form, no relevant constraint, or that every interpretation is equally valid. Distinguish multiple acceptable adaptations from anything-goes reasoning. Name the boundaries, required functions, evidence limits, or safety constraints that still apply.

## 49. Preserve causal openness in anomalous lived material

An unexplained event does not become “luck” merely because that is the culturally ordinary cautious explanation. The live possibilities may include luck, a mistaken description of what happened, an unobserved compensatory process, a feature of the state or system, mixed causes, or something else. When the evidence does not discriminate, preserve that uncertainty.

Practical advice can remain decisive while causality stays open. Someone may need to stop driving, seek assessment, or change the setting even though the article cannot yet explain why no accident occurred. Do not use the safety recommendation to smuggle in a causal verdict.

## 50. Preserve contentious specificity when it carries the human logic

A politically charged or externally checkable detail may add little to the narrow proof of a thesis while still carrying the scene's causal history, moral tension, or reason the next response mattered. Weapons or training, a prison setting, institutional backing, a money flow, or an exact organizational role can be the hinge that makes a later transformation intelligible.

Separate three questions: whether the detail is true, whether it needs sourcing, and whether it belongs in the narrative. A source problem may require verification or qualification. It does not automatically justify replacing the detail with a generic phrase or deleting it as a distraction.

## 51. Preserve provisional options without manufacturing a closed system

A list or taxonomy is not automatically AI-shaped. Distinguish provisional author options, uneven examples, open questions, and explicit invitations to invent another answer from a model system that claims to be complete, symmetrical, and non-overlapping. Preserve mixed judgment and unresolved tension when they are the real thought.

## 52. Let hypotheticals expose pressure

A hypothetical belongs when it shows what an option will cost, destabilize, permit, or demand later. Do not add examples merely to make an abstraction concrete. The example must change the reader's understanding of the local mechanism or decision.

## 53. Recover the real audience function of meta-writing

Appendices, prefaces, methodology notes, and “who this is for” passages should name the actual reader relationship, burden, suspicion, and epistemic purpose. Avoid the generic invitation sequence of why detail moved, permission to skip, invitation to continue, and summary of contents.

## 54. Preserve research friction and source access

Research-conversational prose should show why the source matters to Joel's argument, what it supports, what it resists, and what remains unresolved. Match first-person claims to actual source access. A full source that changes the argument's source map requires a reset, not an abstract-based patch.

## 55. Track section provenance before final assembly

Owner-authored, owner-edited, assistant-produced/owner-accepted, scan/PDF-only owner-final, and superseded assistant sections carry different review obligations. Recheck assistant-produced accepted sections in full context before publication. Owner deletion is resolved, not an invitation to restore the material later.

## 56. Verify source boundaries before declaring preservation

A working excerpt or handoff target may end before the source's real rhetorical boundary. Inspect neighboring headings and the complete source when available. Limit “no omissions” and “section complete” claims to the exact span actually reviewed.

## 57. Separate raw-source preservation from publication wording

Archive Joel's exact answers, but do not copy them into publication merely to prove fidelity. Reuse raw wording only when owner-final/locked, evidentiary, identity-bearing, semantically precise, or functionally superior. A clause mosaic can preserve every word and still destroy composition.

## 58. Build a coherence architecture card before prose

Map pressure, reader stake, claim, intellectual/lived route, actor relations, chronology/causality, evidence roles, complication, governing movement, paragraph jobs, stopping point, and exact-language reasons before drafting sentences. An outline inherited from questions, sources, or assistant prose is not neutral.

## 59. Draft in fresh syntax, then restore justified exact language

Compose from the architecture card and claim map rather than line-by-line from raw answers. Return to the source afterward for fidelity, omission, and exact-language restoration. This protects both voice and coherence.

## 60. Audit verbatim dependency

Substantial exact spans from interviews or brainstorm notes require a private reason. Owner-final prose is exempt. Reject traceability wording, answer-order blocks, and copied clauses whose only purpose is avoiding synthesis.

## 61. Test the paragraph chain

Each paragraph needs a main job, a real relation to the prior paragraph, pressure toward the next, and a reason it cannot vanish or move freely. Modular lists remain valid when the genre calls for them.

## 62. Audit the stopping point apart from detector results

Stop where the thought arrives. A practical branch, contradiction, limit, direct addressee, fact, or joke may close the section. Do not append recap, moral, symmetry, or explanatory aftercare merely to complete the form.

## 63. Treat author-intent interviews as source pools

An interview is source recovery, not a transcript, mandatory content inventory, or architecture. Preserve Joel's central judgment, substantive claims and certainty, actor assignments, distinctive language, high-value examples, useful complications, and required context. Select, consolidate, move, synthesize, or omit optional examples, brainstorming, process notes, repeated explanations, distracting detail, and inherited AI scaffolding to create coherent finished prose.

Author-language fidelity forbids softening and generic normalization. It does not forbid competent transitions, paragraph development, synthesis, selection, or clarity.

## 64. Run the semantic-reset gate

Before reconstruction, verify the actual claim, causal model, actor → action → object relation, chronology, attribution, heading, link context, and whether the passage should still exist. AI-shaped questions may carry the AI passage's false assumptions. A direct Joel correction or rewrite supersedes every earlier candidate and detector score.

## 65. Build coherence before testing

State the section's central claim, what causes what, what merely enables or accompanies the result, the strongest complication, intended reader and register, progression, evidence levels, and honest stopping point. Build the outline independently of interview-question order. Assign each retained item a live rhetorical function.

Reject transcript collage, isolated declarations, answer traceability, polished model essays with Joel's nouns substituted, example-plus-moral chains, and tidy taxonomies that absorb every complication. Read the passage without the interview and in its surrounding article context. It must be worth publishing without a detector.

## 66. Select specificity and preserve useful complication

Concrete detail is not automatically human. Keep details that carry the argument, reveal the experience, create necessary humor, or distinguish Joel's judgment. Omit details that merely increase volume. Preserve counterexamples and asymmetries that clarify the real distinction rather than forcing a perfect rule.

Do not repair abstraction by mechanically adding biography, lists, physical nouns, first person, jokes, short sentences, or informal syntax. Exact technical or metaphysical language may be the most human part when it carries the actual thought.

## 67. Use headings, caveats, paragraphs, research, and humor by function

Headings may separate genuine shifts in function or register and reduce the need for model-written bridges. They may not manufacture a tidy taxonomy. State the positive thesis before defensive caveats when possible; group qualifications where their function becomes necessary. Use natural semantic paragraphs rather than one-sentence fragmentation.

Integrate research only for a defined evidentiary or explanatory function. Source provenance is not drafting provenance. Humor must release pressure, expose absurdity, limit a claim, admit uncertainty, deepen a concrete example, or maintain reader contact; do not derive a formula from a successful joke.

## 68. Interpret detector signals at three levels

Separate document/section classification, sentence shading, and split/boundary tests. A green sentence may sit inside generated architecture; an unchanged sentence may turn orange after its boundary changes. Sentence color never establishes authorship, quality, accuracy, or permission.

Before interpretation, identify duplicated text, OCR/PDF corruption, stripped links, missing punctuation, editor overlays, and scan artifacts. Mechanical corruption is not a voice finding.

## 69. Lock exact coherent blocks and boundaries

A lock applies to the exact coherent, owner-approved tested unit and relevant context: wording, punctuation, capitalization, emoji, paragraph breaks, heading, hyperlink and linked phrase, lead-in, exit sentence, and order relative to neighbors. Do not move or polish it casually. When Joel corrects a passing block, his version becomes authoritative. Pure P1 corrections retain prior detector status; substantive visible or boundary changes require retesting before a new lock.

## 70. Preserve candidates and revert on regression

Retain the original, every candidate, every Joel correction, overall detector result, sentence shading, and exact boundaries. If a new candidate becomes less coherent or faithful, stop advancing from it and return to the strongest prior coherent baseline. Newer wording is not presumed better, and a score increase never outweighs semantic regression.

## 71. Enforce the reconstruction ceiling and learning ledger

Use one surgical reconstruction and one coherent post-interview architecture. If that architecture fails globally, one genuinely different architecture test is the maximum. After two global failures, ask Joel to rewrite the block. Do not continue an increasingly conversational paraphrase loop.

After every tested candidate, record boundaries, version, detector result, exact change, coherence, fidelity, locked material, suspected cause, confidence, lesson scope, semantic correction, and next action. Keep one-off findings local until they recur across materially different passages.

## 72. Keep explanatory levels distinct

Map source text, lived experience, author interpretation, proposed mechanism, analogy, external evidence, and uncertainty separately. A source may support one layer without establishing the others. Detector repair that changes a claim, actor, cause, certainty, heading, attribution, or omission is a substantive correction and requires normal propagation.

## 73. Use progressive disclosure across main text and appendix

The main article carries the experience, minimum decisive distinctions, and smallest sufficient support. The appendix carries full source disputes, philology, mechanism, and counterarguments. Repeat only when the function changes.

## 74. Owner judgment and detector humility

A passage may score 100% human and still be wrong in accuracy, causal force, emphasis, grouping, scope, register, or purpose. Joel's correction controls. A classifier may reveal predictable prose, contextual spillover, model-written bridges, or boundary effects; it cannot prove authorship, truth, quality, or approval. Do not add mistakes or distort meaning for a score.

## 75. Separate developmental diagnosis from implementation

A developmental reviewer diagnoses promise, destination, pacing, heading hierarchy, credibility timing, recurrence, skimmer behavior, appendix function, and ending. The controlled implementer applies only accepted moves, trims, pointers, and minimal connective prose. Do not let a diagnosis smuggle in an unauthorized rewrite.

## 76. Make article purpose operational

Record the real problem or mystery, the destination, why the route is necessary, and what counts as payoff. Test every global recommendation against this statement. An editor who misunderstands the purpose may still identify a local problem, but the proposed operation is not controlling.

## 77. Delay revelation without delaying stakes

A thriller or inquiry can withhold the full answer. The opening still tells the reader what the mystery matters for. This is not a demand to state the thesis early; it is a demand to establish the promise and cost of continuing.

## 78. Sequence distinctiveness before credibility taxes

Ask what only Joel can supply and which claims require the skeptical reader to spend trust. Distinctive lived material, exact scenes, or the concrete intellectual problem should usually arrive before generic theory or the article's highest-cost claims. Preserve conviction; change timing when timing is the defect.

## 79. Treat the heading tree as the skimmer's argument

Heading levels and the table of contents must display the real destination, importance, and relationships. A technically correct section filed under the wrong parent is a developmental error even when every paragraph is strong.

## 80. Give the ending an explicit job

Record what the reader should leave doing, reconsidering, feeling, or holding open. Identify which existing passage performs that job and whether ordinary readers will reach it. Humor, deflation, invitation, warning, decision, and unresolved cost are different ending functions.

## 81. Preserve section autonomy without duplicating proof

A reader landing at a heading should understand the local claim, risk, and needed premise. Retain a concise orientation or callback when necessary; use a pointer when the full proof exists elsewhere. Do not compress the article into sections that work only when read linearly from the first word.

## 82. Audit semantic valence, not only repeated words

A distinctive term can recur with opposite or materially different commitments. Record whether each use is endorsed, rejected, quoted, provisional, ironic, or redefined. Lexical deduplication must not erase a conceptual distinction.

## 83. Keep four epistemic planes separate

For each substantial claim, distinguish the world claim, Joel's commitment/certainty, the inferential status, and detector evidence. Neither a citation nor a classifier may absorb or overwrite the other planes.

## 84. Block self-sealing originality claims

For “lost meaning,” “new synthesis,” and unfamiliarity arguments, name what would have stopped publication, what would refute the claim, what was searched, what remains unavailable, and what absence can actually establish.

## 85. Treat abduction as support, not proof

Check whether the model independently predicted the observation, whether the prediction predated the observation, which rival explanations remain, and whether the wording says possible, supported, likely, or proved.

## 86. Use point-and-move when evidence timing is wrong

Keep the author's conviction and early claim, add the smallest forward pointer, move detailed support to the decision point, and verify the destination. Do not manufacture hedging to solve a placement problem.

## 87. Separate experience, mechanism, ontology, and generalization

“This happened,” “this caused it,” “this is what it was,” “this is generally true,” and “this will happen to you” require separate support. A vivid first-person experience can be certain at the experiential layer while all later layers remain provisional.

## 88. Treat credibility as a budget, not a ban

Do not automatically soften rare phenomenology, rebirth, lost-tradition claims, contested readings, or scientific extrapolations. Sequence them so the reader has enough distinctive material and relevant evidence to evaluate the costliest commitments.

## 89. Promote lessons only from labeled recurring evidence

A skill rule needs supporting pairs or triples, genre/section coverage, confidence, counterexamples, scope limits, and retirement conditions. A vivid owner correction can remain a local lesson. Frozen holdout cases control whether a general rule survives.

## 90. Preserve natural under-specification

Do not upgrade an ordinary modifier into an analytical synonym merely because the latter appears more exact. `Just`, `really`, `quite`, `somehow`, `even`, `still`, and `probably` can preserve how the thought occurred: partial, conversational, remembered, or not yet formalized. `Exactly`, `precisely`, `specifically`, and `particularly` can convert that into retrospective explanatory architecture. Make the change only when the increased precision is substantively necessary.

## 91. Audit short prose without content halo

For short human/AI/mixed judgments, neutralize names, doctrines, and technical nouns and inspect the remaining architecture. Keep prose shape, thought provenance, and fidelity/editorial quality separate. A Joel-specific, accurate, cautious sentence may still be a portable model template.

## Final long-article checklist

- Does nearly every example return immediately to the thesis?
- Are there too many polished paragraph endings in succession?
- Has any anecdote been explained after the reader already understood it?
- Does the framework admit overlap, exceptions, and failure modes?
- Is at least one important unknown stated honestly when one exists?
- Are binary and mirrored constructions recurring too often?
- Are nearly all headings slogans?
- Do concrete details feel discovered in life or installed to simulate personality?
- Does the conclusion repeat a meaning already earned?
- Did any cut or reorder orphan a definition, introduction, example, transition, or media anchor?
- Did a small transition word retain a relation that the new material changed?
- Does a repeated issue perform a different function each time?
- Did any local observation expand to a broader geography without evidence?
- Did adding harm accidentally cancel genuine praise?
- Could 5–15% of interpretation be removed without losing the argument?
- Is the audience contract stable, or did the article drift in role, expertise, goal, relationship, scope, action, register, assumed knowledge, stakes, or venue?
- Did a source written for another audience import its jargon or priorities instead of being translated?
- Does practical abstraction identify actors, actions, timing, thresholds, results, and stop/escalation conditions where needed?
- Is aggregate evidence being mistaken for a case-specific decision rule?
- Are distinct explanatory systems or levels being blended without evidence?
- Did a prerequisite prevent premature action, or become permanent avoidance without transition criteria?
- Is a synthesized framework's status stated beside it, and are validated components being confused with a validated whole?
- Are warnings, allegations, or rules broader than the evidence, conditions, population, jurisdiction, or severity supports?
- Did editing drop a condition or scope modifier that carries the actual distinction?
- Did “flexibility” become an anything-goes claim that erased real standards or constraints?
- Did a cautionary sentence settle an unexplained event as luck, coincidence, pathology, or proof when several causes remain possible?
- Did editing remove a controversial specific detail whose historical or moral function survived nowhere else?

## StoryScope caution

Use these patterns as a qualitative editorial lens, not an authorship detector or quality score. StoryScope examined model-generated fiction, not polemical nonfiction, edited human–AI collaboration, or Joel's complete style. A framework-heavy article may resemble AI-associated features for legitimate genre reasons. Diagnose density and fit, not origin.
