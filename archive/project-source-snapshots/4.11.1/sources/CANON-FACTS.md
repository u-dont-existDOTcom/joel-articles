### Source terminology and attribution

Canon facts protect identity and naming; they do not assign ownership of common ideas. Do not credit a reviewer, lecturer, or author merely because they supplied an explanation of a broadly established developmental concept. The inherited/internalized parent, human-doer pattern, experimentation in identity development, externalized worth, and permission to be a beginner may be integrated without attribution unless the passage specifically discusses that person's work.

When an external source contributes a genuinely distinctive term, original framework, quotation, evidence, historical claim, or source-specific interpretation, preserve attribution. If its terminology conflicts with Joel's established vocabulary, record the source term and exact meaning, name it once in the article, explain the adopted article term and distinction, then use the adopted term consistently.
