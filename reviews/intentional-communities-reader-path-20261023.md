## What the article currently does well

The strong movement is personal rather than instructional: Joel's father's film; his own experiences visiting and living in communes; why secular process and spiritual authority each failed him; the four-part alternative; practical tests involving medicine, relationships, children, the Zapatistas, power, membership, economics and exit; and finally the invitation to start doing community without buying land. The narrative is unusually rich in first-person observation, humor and hard cases. The problem is **access to the invitation**, not a shortage of ideas. The reader encounters almost the whole article's cases, caveats and organizational design before the most accessible invitation appears.

A good edit should retain competing pressures rather than make the article simpler by discarding them. In particular, early healing without claiming that anyone is ever finished; individual practice alongside shared obligations; optional medicine with competence/safety; children's freedom with effective protection; freedom to leave with real resources; and founders' responsibility without founder veto power remain protected distinctions.

### R2 — Move the detailed money progression out of the four-part overview — HIGH PRIORITY, OPTIONAL

### R8 — Check one genuine authorial boundary without silently resolving it

Two exact claims may look incompatible when skimmed:

- §4 “Founded on Freedom”: **“Each of us guides our own practice”** (personal agency);
- §11 “How My Own Model Could Become a Cult”: **“if a member is avoiding the therapy process and becoming hard to deal with, they will naturally not belong at a certain point.”**

Any change here is an authorial decision, not an editor-inferred new rule.

## Repetitions that should NOT be deleted

1. **Power:** §3 diagnoses capture; §11 tests the founder; §13 asks who holds the deed and who can remove a member. These are cause, accountability and implementation, not three interchangeable paragraphs.
2. **Healing:** §4 defines inner/relational practice; §12 describes the group's capacity to support members; §16 explains what readers can do immediately. These serve distinct reader moments.
3. **Escuelita:** §1 introduces the inherited aim of spreading the method; §9 supplies the Zapatista lived/history/evidence and cautions; §10 explains federated extension; §16 pays off the father's film. Repetition here makes the ending earned.
4. **Exits:** §11 tests whether founder power can be overridden, §13 protects a departing individual's material interests, §14 plans the dissolution of an entire institution. Deleting one would lose a different case.
5. **Children:** The §8 safeguards, reporting independence, and adult right to leave are essential even though the broad freedom principle appears earlier. Keep the protections where readers need to act.

The authorial jokes (e.g., goat relocation, communal showers, “Heavy Flow Maxi Pad,” bureaucratic God-complex critique) should not be removed just to flatten tone. They frequently provide a break in a demanding explanatory chain.
