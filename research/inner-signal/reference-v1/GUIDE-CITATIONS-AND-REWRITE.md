## Recommended citation design

### Spoken practice

No source codes, footnotes, citations, URLs or researcher surnames used as evidence markers inside an induction, deepener, cue-setting passage or return. Necessary delivery directions such as a pause remain separate from spoken text. Do not mistake removing a citation for removing a useful instruction. The archive retains provenance outside the spoken passage.

A short note before or after the complete practice can say that the approach combines Joel's method with the traditions described in the source appendix. The narration itself should remain an experience. No bracketed B4, chapter references or academic asides should be voiced by an app.

### Explanatory prose

Use natural attribution when it answers a reader's question, and occasional unobtrusive linked words where a specific consequential factual claim needs immediate support. Avoid author-year chains and a source marker after every practical sentence. Do not create scientific claims merely to justify an exercise that is transparently an invitation to experiment.

Preserve necessary uncertainty beside the relevant claim. A source appendix cannot repair an overstatement in the main text. Distinguish a personal judgment, a source's theory and an empirical result in ordinary prose. For instance, an account of a useful somatic shift need not promise a molecular mechanism.

Existing links with navigational, evidentiary or personal functions are not automatically disposable. Review their purpose individually rather than applying a global strip-links operation.

### Source appendix

Prefer one compact appendix called **Sources and further reading**, organized by the guide's section names. It should tell the interested reader what each source contributes, give the useful chapter or page range, and state when a passage is Joel's adaptation. A generic list of six books would not adequately map evidence to claims.

Keep research-access limitations in the working ledger unless they materially affect the reader-facing claim.
