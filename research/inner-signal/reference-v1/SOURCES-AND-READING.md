# Hypnosis reference foundation v1: sources and reading coverage

Date: 2026-09-10. This is an editorial/reference foundation, not clinical training, a validated treatment, or an installed app knowledge base. Book descriptions below preserve the authors' accounts; application decisions are separately identified in TEACHING-REFERENCE.md. No copyrighted books or full extractions are committed.

## Source identities and access

B4 page references mean printed pages; the supplied PDF page is printed page + 28. B3 references mean the displayed page label in this electronic rendition; PDF page is displayed page + 1. EPUBs do not supply dependable print pagination, so chapter and subheading are primary locators. Local spine-unit/word locators supplement them where useful. Existing text layers were used without new OCR. Extraction or a table-of-contents survey does not count as close reading.

### B1 — Peter A. Levine and Maggie Phillips, Freedom from Pain (2012)

Supplied EPUB SHA-256: `38c3dd74bee378e75653b88b1dd9fedcdd637db52d93c97dc33bc20e32d0e334`.

Earlier close reading: introduction; chapter 4 on tracking, pendulation, compassion, journaling and resources; chapter 5 affirmations; chapter 7 resilience. This pass adds the complete chapter 3, including dissociation, grounding, breathing alternatives, anxiety, helplessness and anger (local spine unit 8, approximately 8,000 words).

Source role: self-regulation practice, individually useful resources, deliberate imagery and actual bodily response. The chapter explicitly recognizes that breathing exercises may increase anxiety for some people. Its stronger physiological generalizations, electrical-grounding analogies, and mechanism explanations are not treated as independently established findings. Medical-treatment chapters and their underlying evidence were not comprehensively reviewed.

### B2 — Peter A. Levine, Healing Trauma (supplied 2008 edition)

Supplied EPUB SHA-256: `4af8f530c620e7076cd2b503dea8d552bd5de678adb1f53ab9e328e0e3c43458`.

Earlier reading included the complete chapter 4 twelve-phase program and relevant body-effects/tips material. This pass adds complete chapters 5 and 6 on sexual trauma and spiritual transformation (local spine units 9 and 10).

Source role: progressive learning rather than a compulsory twelve-step session; resourcing, pacing, and the author's sexual/spiritual framing. Some developmental and spiritual generalizations are source-specific, not universal descriptions. Companion audio was not supplied or heard. In particular, the missing audio-dependent exercises in phases 5 and 10 must not be reconstructed and attributed to Levine.

### B3 — Peter A. Levine, In an Unspoken Voice (2010)

Supplied PDF SHA-256: `f2d43ac03c8851a5d550521785217cf5f13f13d8996772f5df815d260d686eb6`.

Earlier reading covered origins, nine building blocks, major therapy-map/SIBAM passages, selected cases and containment. This pass adds displayed pp.276–296 (part of chapter 13) and pp.312–317 (beginning of chapter 14). Neither range constitutes the whole corresponding chapter.

Source role: felt experience, containment, intention, emotion, and spirituality. The author distinguishes feeling an impulse from acting it out and, on p.316, resists the equation that trauma and suffering necessarily yield transformation. His neuroanatomical, evolutionary, free-will and spiritual explanations remain identifiable theoretical claims. New diagrams were not used as independent evidence in this pass.

### B4 — Maggie Phillips and Claire Frederick, Healing the Divided Self (1995)

Supplied PDF SHA-256: `34b9f3b84925c73a2e44b127e636512f5bf2722909ff993ae0443bbcd20551e3`.

This is explicitly a professional clinical text, not a self-help book (p.xviii). Earlier passes covered preparation and interactive trance, the SARI diagram, sensory training/utilization, selected ego-strengthening/renurturing/SE material, memory uncertainty, the Kristi mismatch case, interpersonal trance, cues, hostile states and generativity.

This pass closely read complete ranges pp.50–97, 101–127, 130–132, 157–162, 212–216, 245–249 and 294–299. Portions of pp.128–129 were also examined, but are not counted as complete new page readings. Search excerpts additionally exposed nearby passages, including pp.165–169 and spiritual material at pp.327–328; these are bounded excerpts rather than whole-chapter completion.

Important locators:

- pp.50–52: ideomotor/ideosensory communication and explicit limits on signal validity.
- pp.52–60: affect/somatic bridging, utilization, metaphor, indirection, positive regression and possible difficulties.
- pp.61–81: ego-state assumptions, metaphor, access routes, cooperation, nonverbal work and externalization.
- pp.82–100: direct suggestion/self-hypnosis, evocative resources, future orientation, metaphor and associational cues. Pp.98–100 were already read in the preceding pass.
- pp.101–127: distancing, regression, ideodynamic approaches, memory uncertainty, abreaction and alternatives. The text contains both caution and instructions that presuppose hidden origins or repeat questions until agreement; do not erase that tension.
- pp.157–162 and retrieved pp.165–169: integration, overcoupling, undercoupling, cooperation and distinctions from merging/fusion. Preserve the authors' actual 1995 position rather than assigning it a contemporary identity-policy consensus.
- pp.212–216, earlier pp.222–225 and 229–233: clinician reactions, overprotection, embodied relationship and mismatch repair.
- pp.245–249: current relationships and forgiveness; the authors do not require forgiveness as the cure. Their legal/family recommendations are not general legal advice.
- pp.294–299: bodywork, consent, coordination, adverse responses and the book's then-experimental framing of EMDR.

Source role: a detailed account of this clinical tradition, not proof that its cases validate an app. Diagnostic, prescribing, crisis-contract, legal and memory-retrieval claims require separate contemporary verification before current clinical guidance. Most disorder-specific cases, medication discussion and references have not been closely reviewed.

### B5 — Peter A. Levine, Trauma and Memory

Supplied EPUB SHA-256: `45e435c633dd916d496854cfa760a5a37cd7c4f7d2aa482e57140b5caab559d2`.

Earlier close reading: chapter 4 emotional/procedural memory and chapter 7 the veracity trap. This pass adds complete chapter 2, The Fabric of Memory (spine unit 10, approximately 2,854 words), and chapter 8, The Molecules of Memory (unit 16, approximately 6,378 words).

Source role: the author's memory taxonomy and account of reconsolidation, plus his warning that compelling experience does not establish historical accuracy. His grouping of declarative/episodic material must be explained in his terms when discussing the book. His extrapolation from laboratory memory research to clinical transformation is not direct verification of the molecular mechanism in this guide. The cited Nader Nature paper could not be opened in this pass; its full methods were not independently reviewed. The chapter's memory-erasure fiction and hypothetical ethics are not descriptions of a routinely available treatment.

### B6 — Peter A. Levine and Maggie Kline, Trauma Through a Child's Eyes (2006)

Supplied EPUB SHA-256: `ff519a2278d17dd301e5f2c99ff92aeb1c86d9e978e30bfe5c07aea698dbdcfc`.

Earlier close reading: selected chapters 4–5 material on attunement, sensations, resources, stories and following the child's lead. This pass adds complete subsections Sensations and Emotions Are Not the Same! and Tending to Emotional Needs by Following Children's Lead (spine units 49–50), and the chapter 10 material on developing relationships, match/mismatch/rematch and thriving/surviving (unit 97, words 1900–3742 only).

Source role: actual children and caregivers. Use of these ideas in adult younger-state work is an explicit adaptation, not proof of developmental equivalence. Phrases about the body's truthfulness do not establish a diagnostic body-language dictionary. The neuroscience and developmental causal claims were not all independently checked.

## Additional first-party and professional reference sources

All URLs below were opened on 2026-09-10. Publication dates are not replaced by the access date. Reading an authored reference entry is not the same as reading every study in its bibliography. These entries were used chiefly to represent their authors' theories, terminology and professional guidance.

## Coverage decision and remaining lookup domains

Reuse and compose the supplied clinical traditions with authored general-hypnosis references; use W11 to check topic coverage. Do not invent a new clinical taxonomy or confuse a syllabus with training.

The core reading now supports drafting the guide and a source-linked educational/consultation reference. It does not close specialist questions about particular disorders, medication, pediatric treatment, forensic law, every commercial method, or competing neurobiological theories. Those need question-triggered primary/professional retrieval, with uncertainty stated if access fails. Specific outcome statistics require inspection of the relevant studies rather than extrapolation from these notes.

The stopping condition is coverage of essential reader tasks and known high-consequence distinctions, not reading every book page or claiming that no unfamiliar future question can arise. No additional general book upload is required before the guide rewrite. Actual app retrieval, source access, answer quality and voice behavior remain to be implemented and tested.
