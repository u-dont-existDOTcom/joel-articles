### B1. Peter A. Levine and Maggie Phillips, Freedom from Pain

Supplied EPUB SHA-256: `38c3dd74bee378e75653b88b1dd9fedcdd637db52d93c97dc33bc20e32d0e334`.

Close reading concentrated on chapter 4, The Journey Back from Unmanageable Pain; chapter 7, Resilience, Continued Recovery, and Restoring the Deep Self; the introduction; and the Practicing Affirmations passage in chapter 5. Other chapters were surveyed rather than all cases closely read.

Important locators and contributions:

- Chapter 4, Further Mastery in Tracking Sensations and related tracking discussion: experience can be approached through a thought and its bodily response. The authors also present claims about sensations versus thoughts that should be kept as their theoretical framing, not silently upgraded to established neuroscience.
- Chapter 4, the Dorothy example: a felt pulsing quality becomes a participant-generated butterfly image, then an exploration of constriction and a small possibility of opening. Useful precedent for moving between sensation, image, and a compassionate response rather than requiring verbal interpretation first. This clinical story is an illustration, not validation of a new app sequence.
- Chapter 4, The Promise of Pendulation and Pendulation and the Rhythms of Pain: pacing, contraction/expansion, and access to more manageable experience. Guided language and imagery are present; the method is not guidance-free observation.
- Chapter 4, Self-Love and Compassion: loving-kindness phrases, self-directed intention, and a two-hand practice involving heart and diaphragm. Check how the words register bodily. Joel's heart/compassion language therefore need not be postponed until after all somatic work. The guide's specific solar-plexus/child symbolism remains Joel's own map, not a universal anatomical fact established by this book.
- Chapter 4, A New Approach to Journaling: reconstruct an episode's sequence, including thoughts, emotions, imagery, and sensations, and notice possible intervention points. Adapt this as present-process inquiry, not proof that a discovered association caused a symptom.
- Chapter 4, Focusing on Pathology Instead of Skills: capacity and workable responses matter; every episode need not become an investigation into hidden pathology.
- Chapter 5, Practicing Affirmations: deliberately chosen phrases and actual bodily resonance can coexist. This is counterevidence to a blanket claim that top-down suggestions are incompatible with the authors' somatic work.

### B2. Peter A. Levine, Healing Trauma

Supplied EPUB SHA-256: `4af8f530c620e7076cd2b503dea8d552bd5de678adb1f53ab9e328e0e3c43458`.

Close reading included the body-effects discussion, the complete chapter 4 twelve-phase program, and Helpful Tips and Techniques for Preventing Trauma. Remaining explanatory chapters were surveyed rather than all passages audited.

Chapter 4 is an explicit self-practice program with ordered initial learning, companion recordings, optional pauses, resources, felt sense, activation, pendulation, defensive responses, orientation, and settling. It initially suggests one phase per day or two. Do not collapse this learning course into twelve tasks that must all be completed in one short session.

Phase 4 uses a positive memory, person, image, or resource and then examines its bodily effects. Phase 5 includes noticing what happens bodily after a thought. Phases 6 and 8 use guided situations and imagery; phase 12 includes affirmations. These are direct counterexamples to the earlier assistant's claim that a somatic method should necessarily have almost no prewritten evocative guidance.

Some movement exercises have partner requirements or are intended for particular stages. They do not automatically become safe generic solo instructions. Phases 5 and 10 contain audio-dependent material not available in this upload.

### B5. Peter A. Levine, Trauma and Memory

Supplied EPUB SHA-256: `45e435c633dd916d496854cfa760a5a37cd7c4f7d2aa482e57140b5caab559d2`.

Chapter 4 supports attention to procedural tendencies, sensory-affective experience, movement, and multiple SIBAM channels without requiring a coherent verbal history first. Its sequence is not rigidly linear. The False-Positive Bias discussion is useful against treating alarm as proof of current danger.

Chapter 7 warns about compelling explanations, leading questions, and the felt truthfulness of strong experiences. Both terrifying and ecstatic experiences can feel convincingly true. Redesign implication: distinguish material noticed before a suggestion from material arising after an intentional experiment. Neither unprompted appearance nor vivid bodily resonance establishes external truth. A generated resource may be helpful without being discovered historical evidence.

## Corrections to the earlier assistant proposal

1. Reject the categorical implication that prewritten scripts are incompatible with bottom-up responsiveness. Books themselves use recordings, structured imagery, and affirmations. The problem is continuing a predetermined response path despite contrary experience.
2. Replace the rigid sensation-first-then-imagery rule with observation-before-attribution and ongoing attention to bodily response. SIBAM includes images, behavior, affect, and meaning; resources may start through any workable channel.
3. Retain hypnosis as more than an attentional container: chosen suggestions can intentionally evoke resources, rehearse actions, and develop new experience. This is a proposed bidirectional integration, not a claim that all such influence is physiologically bottom-up.
4. Direct/indirect language and top-down/bottom-up processing are different distinctions. A softly phrased suggestion can impose an explanation; a direct sensory question can leave experience open.
5. Reducing written-guide length does not require eliminating a rich induction, shortening every spoken practice, or asking for more frequent reports.
6. Interactive primarily means responsive inner practice. It need not mean dependence on an app. Any app is limited to available reports and observations and does not read a nervous system or replace a therapeutic relationship.

## Provisional architecture to explore

Preserve an explicitly chosen hypnotic entry and responsive indirect language throughout. Before absorption, choose one manageable present-day intention and a plain stopping/reorientation method. Develop an embodied or imaginal resource that actually fits. Let one tolerable sensation, image, feeling, gesture, question, or younger-state concern become the focus. Offer one invitation or consciously chosen experiment, then allow enough quiet to notice its result. The next move depends on the actual result. A session may end with greater capacity or clearer limits without a new story or insight.

Separate two functions inside the same practice:

- Inquiry: what is present; how it changes; what preceded it; what is uncertain. Avoid planting a cause, identity, memory, or obligatory emotion.
- Deliberate experiment: try a chosen image, supportive phrase, small voluntary movement, distance, or rehearsal, and notice what follows. Useful changes are real experiences, but their occurrence is not independent confirmation of a proposed cause or external truth.

Use the Nurturer, Protector, and Leader as available functions rather than requiring a roll call of every character. A welcoming whole, Higher Self, loving-kindness phrase, or borrowed model may be an entry resource when it actually helps. Some users may prefer neutral witnessing, external contact, or no imagery.

Maintain explicit exit: end the waking session, restore ordinary orientation and judgment, open eyes, make voluntary movement appropriate to the person, and confirm alertness before other activities. Sleep practice remains a separate goal and module.

This is a design proposal, not an established treatment sequence. It should not make solo traumatic-memory recovery, abreaction, forced discharge, or dissociative regression a default. Clinical crisis management, medical diagnosis, and medication guidance remain outside the proposed self-practice guide/app.

## Cheapest useful next test

Prototype one hypnotic entry plus one response-contingent present-day exploration, not a replacement manuscript. Review whether it still feels genuinely hypnotic, whether the next prompt follows what was reported, whether the person can revise or stop it, and whether it avoids inventing explanations. Include no-change, imagery-first, sensation-first, unwanted-softening, and emerging-younger-state cases. Keep a simple self-led/cue-card version as a usability comparison; do not assume generative chat is automatically better than recordings or self-direction.

Success is not depth, a dramatic release, universal calm, or a plausible story. For the prototype, the relevant outcomes are usable absorption, actual responsiveness, preserved choice and orientation, learning that can be used outside the session, and no worsening of stability or function. A usability test is not evidence of clinical efficacy. No tests have yet been run.
